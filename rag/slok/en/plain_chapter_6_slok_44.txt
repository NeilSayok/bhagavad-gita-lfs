Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
pūrvābhyāsena tenaiva hriyate hyavaśo.api saḥ .
jijñāsurapi yogasya śabdabrahmātivartate

Word Meanings:
पूर्वाभ्यासेन - by previous practice
तेनैव - that same
ह्रियते - is drawn
ह्यवशोऽपि - even instinctively
सः - he
जिज्ञासुरपि - even a seeker
योगस्य - of yoga
शब्दब्रह्मातिवर्तते - transcends Vedic rituals

Life Application:
Allow your innate spiritual inclination to carry you forward, rising above mere ritualistic rules.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
By that former practice alone he is borne on, even helplessly. Even he who merely wishes to know Yoga goes beyond the Sabdabrahman.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
By that former practice alone he is borne on, even helplessly. Even a inquirer after Yoga transcends the Sabdabrahman.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Impelled helplessly by that very practice of his former birth, he is irresistibly drawn toward divine realization; and even a seeker who merely desires to know Meditation-Yoga transcends the realm of Sabdabrahman (ritualistic Vedic actions and their rewards).

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Driven by past practice, the seeker is drawn helplessly to Yoga; even a seeker inquiring into Yoga surpasses the rewards of ritualistic karma.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: There in that new body he naturally regains the intellectual impressions acquired in his former body, and he strives more than before for perfection, O son of Kurus. By that very former practice he is borne on helplessly, for even a mere seeker of Yoga transcends the realm of ritualistic Vedic sound.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "By that former practice alone he is borne on, even helplessly. Even he who merely wishes to know Yoga goes beyond the Sabdabrahman"—highlights the transcendent power of Yoga where a mere inquirer surpasses all Vedic ritualistic fruits (*Shabda-Brahma*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The Samskara of past practice is so potent that it shields the practitioner from material temptations, steering him naturally toward God.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The magnetic pull of Krishna-remembrance releases the seeker from worldly desires, drawing him irresistibly to the Lord"s feet.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Transcends Sabdabrahman"—even a mere inquirer of Yoga surpasses Vedic ritualistic rewards to become eligible for Kaivalya Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of being drawn by past spiritual habits and going beyond Vedic ritualistic karma.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The inclination of one meditating on Lord Vasudeva naturally settles into exclusive devotion to God.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Impelled by past practice, the seeker crosses all worldly temptations to reach Lord Krishna"s lotus feet.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Goes beyond Sabdabrahman"—desire for non-dual Brahman-knowledge alone, transcending ritual fruits, grants Mukti.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
By virtue of the divine consciousness of his previous life, he becomes involuntarily attracted to the yogic principles—even without seeking them. Such an inquisitive transcendentalist stands always above the ritualistic principles of the scriptures.
