Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Lord Krishna will not only mention the things comprising the Field, but will also describe the attributes and properties of the Field, its modifications, and what effect is produced from what cause. Similarly, He will also explain in this chapter the nature of the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna) and His powers associated with the limiting adjuncts. Hear all this from Me in brief.

Merely repeating the adjectives describing the nature of the infinite Self or emphasizing them does not bring any special benefit to a sincere seeker, nor does it aid in his development. Turning one's eyes away from the causes that give rise to the problems of our life does not mean solving the problem. We must closely observe and study this world around us, which we ourselves have perceived, as well as the very processes through which we function and observe the variety of countless objects, emotions, and thoughts. To ignore this means depriving oneself of vast, essential, and fundamental knowledge. This is self-deception.

To formulate a battle strategy against enemies, it is necessary to have at least a general knowledge of the enemy's strategy. Similarly, in order to fight against the Field, conquer it, and free oneself from its bonds, it is essential to know what the Field is and how these adjuncts function and behave in specific situations.

Thus, anatomy, biology, psychology, and other branches of natural science also contribute to understanding life. The path of knowledge in spirituality is the pinnacle and fulfillment of all secular sciences. This fact is confirmed by the truth that even on the battlefield, while imparting this knowledge to Arjuna, the Lord does not forget to emphasize that having complete knowledge of this Field is of great importance. We must study it closely.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
'Yadvikari' means that which is endowed with modifications. 'Yatas ca yat' means from which it moves or functions. 'Sa ca' refers to the promoter (instigator). The statement 'yatas ca yat' indicates from what the field functions, while 'sa ca yah' refers to the essential nature alone.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anandagiri explains the purpose of the upcoming verse with the words beginning with 'tat'. 'Vivaksitam' means that which is desired to be known. He states the fruit of praise with 'srot' etc. The true nature of the Field etc. is established not only by the statement of trustworthy authorities, but also from Vedic statements; hence it is said 'chandobhih' etc. The diversity of the four Vedas, starting with Rig, is accepted due to the differences of branches (shakhas). The true nature is not only proved by Sruti and Smriti, but is also logical; hence 'kinca' is added. Anticipating the question as to what those aphorisms (sutras) are, he mentions 'atmetyeva' etc. By the word 'adi' (etc.), sutras concerning knowledge and ignorance (vidya and avidya) such as 'brahmavid apnoti param' and 'atha yo 'nyam devatam' are meant. The term 'atma' is used to denote the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna), which also implies the Field. Aphorisms like 'athato brahma-jijnasa' are also included here; otherwise, there would be tautology with 'chandobhih', thinking thus he qualifies it with 'hetumadbhir' etc.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary: Tat-ksetram — The word 'tat' denotes two things — the subject matter previously mentioned and distance. That which was referred to as 'idam' (this) in the first verse of this chapter is here referred to as 'tat' (that). The field is not present in all space, nor in all time, and even now it is perishing every moment — this is the distance of the field (from one's True Self).
Yacca — The essential nature of that field, which has been described in the fifth verse of this chapter.
Yadrik ca — The natural disposition of that field, which has been described in verses 26 and 27 of this chapter by showing it to be subject to birth and destruction.
Yadvikari — Although the twenty-three elements mentioned in verse 5 are also called modifications being effects of Nature (Prakriti), yet here by this term only the modifications such as desire, hatred, etc., born in the field due to the assumed relationship between the field and the Knower of the field are meant, which are described in verse 6.
Yatas ca yat — That from which this field is produced, i.e., the seven modifications and three gunas born of Prakriti, which are described in the latter half of verse 19 of this chapter.
Sa ca — The Knower of the field described in the latter half of verse 1, is here denoted by the word 'sah', and it is about Him that listening is enjoined.
Yah — The essential nature of this Knower of the field, which has been described in the latter half of verse 20 and in verse 22 of this chapter.
Yat-prabhavas ca — The power possessed by that Knower of the field, which is described in verses 31 to 33 of this chapter.
Tat samasena me srnu — Here under the word 'tat', both the field and the Knower of the field should be included. The point is: hear from Me in brief four things regarding the field — what it is, of what nature it is, what modifications it possesses, and from what it is produced; and two things regarding the Knower of the field — who He is, and what His power is.

Although in the beginning of this chapter, in the first two verses, the field and the Knower of the field were described in brief, which the Lord also called 'knowledge', yet the detailed analysis of the distinction between the field and the Knower of the field (the field with modifications and the modification-less Knower of the field along with His power) begins from this fourth verse. Therefore, the Lord commands Arjuna to listen attentively.
In this verse, the Lord has commanded to hear four things regarding the field, but only two things regarding the Knower of the field — His essential nature and power. This may raise a doubt: why was the influence/power of the field not mentioned, and why were the nature, modifications, and origin of the Knower of the field not mentioned? The answer is that what power can the field have, which does not remain stable in one form even for a single moment? In the mind of an ignorant worldly person attached to Prakriti, insentient objects like wealth hold importance; hence to him the influence of the field (insentient objects) appears in the world. In reality, independently, the field has no power at all. Hence no description of its power is given.

The nature of the Knower of the field is free from birth and destruction; therefore His disposition is also free from birth and destruction. Hence the Lord did not describe His disposition separately but included it within His essential nature. Only due to assuming a relationship with the field do modifications like desire and hatred appear in the Knower of the field; otherwise the Knower of the field is in essence entirely free from modifications. Therefore, describing modifications of the modification-less Knower of the field is impossible. The Knower of the field is non-dual, beginningless, and eternal. Hence regarding Him, the question of who was produced from what does not arise.

Connection — Where the detailed description of what was commanded to be heard in brief in the previous verse takes place, is stated in the following verse.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Someone (Shankaracharya) explained 'yadvikari' as 'that whose modification it is', which is incorrect, because in a Bahuvrihi compound the suffix 'ini' becomes redundant. But with the sense that 'whatever modification it is, that is yadvikara, and he who possesses it is yadvikari', he says 'yat' etc. Here by 'yena vikarena' (by which modification) a Karmadharaya compound is indicated. 'Yukta' (endowed with) is the meaning of the suffix 'ini'. Someone explained 'yatas ca yat' as 'from which cause what effect arises', which is improper, because it is already conveyed by 'yadvikari' and requires unwarranted ellipsis (adhyahara), and verse 13.20 ('vikarams ca gunams ca') is explained otherwise. Another interpreted it as 'from which qualities like humility the Knowable is attained', which is also incorrect due to ellipsis. Since 13.8 ('amanitvam') and 13.13 ('anadimat') are established otherwise, he explains differently with 'yatas ca' etc. 'Yatah' means by whose impulsion. 'Yat' is the present participle form of the root 'in'. Since 'in' and 'ya' have essentially the same meaning, it is stated as 'yati' (goes). Since physical motion is not possible for the entire Field, relying on secondary/metaphorical sense he clarifies as 'pravartate' (functions/moves). The commentary that 'sa ca yah' is a statement about the individual jiva is wrong, because that is contextually irrelevant and the term Kshetrajna does not refer to it; with this intention he says 'sa ca' etc., because the promoter/impeller from whom the Field functions is under discussion. Now if so, it should be asked: are 'yatas ca yat' and 'sa ca yah' a single statement or two? Not the first, because two 'ca' words cannot be justified, and in connection with 'tatsamasena', 'sa ca yah' would become redundant. Nor the second, because there is no difference in meaning. Accepting the second option he explains: 'yatas ca yat' is a declaration that 'from such a entity endowed with attributes the Field functions', whereas 'sa ca yah' is a declaration to state the essential nature of the promoter alone — thus there is a difference in meaning.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
He presents the summary verse of the chapter on the Field taught via 'this body' etc., for ease of comprehension: 'tat' etc. That which was indicated as 'this body' is referred to by the word 'tat'. And this indicated Field, which by nature is insentient, divided into movable and immovable forms, and of the nature of being perceptible, is meant by 'yac ca'. How it is and of what manner due to its own attributes is 'yadrik'. What modifications belong to it is 'yadvikari'. 'Yatah' means from which cause what effect arises (to be supplied); or from which cause, i.e., from the union of Prakriti and Purusha, it arises. As for 'yat' meaning divided into movable and immovable forms, the Acharyas (such as Shankara) did not explain it, deeming the word 'yat' redundant since its meaning is already included in 'yac ca'. Here all the 'ca' words are used in the conjunctive sense. And that Knower of the field who is indicated is by nature of the essence of Existence-Consciousness-Bliss; He whose powers (prabhavas) are such is 'yat-prabhavas'. Hear in brief from My words that true nature of the Field and the Knower of the Field characterized by the aforesaid attributes, that is, hear and comprehend.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Even while Arjuna is already listening, 'srnu' (hear!) is said again to draw his full attention with 'tat ksetram' etc. Referring to what will be stated further from 'mahabhutani' (13.6) to 'sanghata' (13.7), the first and last 'yat' words refer to the insentient substance and its aggregate respectively, hence there is no repetition; with this intention he says 'which substance'. In accordance with being the substratum of the senses to be described, he states the meaning of 'yadrik' as 'that which is the substratum of those things'. Those modifications which exist as its effects constitute 'yadvikari'; to clarify that the intention lies in the sense specified by 'yat', he states 'and what its modifications are'. 'Yatah' does not refer to the material cause etc., since that was already stated first; with this intention he says 'from what cause/reason'. Mentioning the specific reason to be stated later in 'cetana dhrtih' (13.7), he says 'for what purpose'. Being present in the mind of the Lord who creates the field, the purpose itself is also a cause/reason, just as in the usage 'adhyayanena vasati' (he stays for the sake of study). 'Yat-svarupam' refers to the aggregate. For a specific structural organization constitutes body-hood etc. Thus the first 'yat' word serves to determine the specific insentient or sentient substance, while the second, once insentience is established, serves to determine whether it is one among many insentient substances or an aggregate — this is the idea. To avert any confusion of referring to the Supreme Self when the word 'mam' (Me) appears, he says 'sa ca ksetrajnah' (and that Knower of the field). By 'yo yat-prabhavah', both the essential nature and its attributes are indicated. 'Prabhavas' means wondrous and sublime specific traits of nature.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Now please hear My brief description of this field of activity and how it is constituted, what its changes are, whence it is produced, who that knower of the field of activities is, and what his influences are.

 The Lord is describing the field of activities and the knower of the field of activities in their constitutional positions. One has to know how this body is constituted, the materials of which this body is made, under whose control this body is working, how the changes are taking place, wherefrom the changes are coming, what the causes are, what the reasons are, what the ultimate goal of the individual soul is, and what the actual form of the individual soul is. One should also know the distinction between the individual living soul and the Supersoul, their different influences, their potentials, etc. One just has to understand this Bhagavad-gītā directly from the description given by the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and all this will be clarified. But one should be careful not to consider the Supreme Personality of Godhead in every body to be one with the individual soul, the jīva. This is something like equating the potent and the impotent.
