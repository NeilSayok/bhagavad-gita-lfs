Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Those for whose sake we desire kingdom, enjoyments and pleasures, they themselves stand here, having abandoned the hope of their own lives and wealth, ready for battle.

Explanation: 'Yeshamarthe kankshitam no rajyam bhogah sukhani cha' -- whatever kingdom, happiness, enjoyment and the like we desire, we do not desire them for our own personal happiness; rather, we desire them for the sake of these very kinsmen, loved ones, friends and others. That our teachers, fathers, grandfathers, sons and the rest may attain comfort, may be served, may remain happy -- it is for this alone that we wish to fight and win the kingdom, to gather the means of enjoyment.

'Ta ime.avasthita yuddhe pranamstyaktva dhanani cha' -- but these very people, all of them, having abandoned the hope of their own lives and wealth, stand here before us on this battlefield ready to fight. They have resolved: we have no attachment to our lives, nor any craving for wealth; we may well die, but we shall not withdraw from battle. If all of these are indeed going to die, then for whose sake do we need a kingdom? For whose sake do we need happiness? For whose sake do we need wealth? That is, for whose sake should we even desire all this?

'Pranamstyaktva dhanani cha' means that they stand having abandoned the hope of life and of wealth -- that is, having given up the desire that we shall live and that we shall gain wealth. If they held any desire for life or wealth, why would they stand in battle ready to die? Hence here the abandoning of life and wealth means precisely the abandoning of the hope for them.

Transition: who these people are, for whose sake we desire kingdom, enjoyment and happiness, Arjuna describes in the next two verses.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said -- Sanjaya said: The high-souled Partha, supremely compassionate, seeing there, stationed in both armies, his fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends -- his own kinsmen arrayed for battle -- was overcome, and spoke thus to Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
'Na cha shreyah' etc. up to 'mahikrite': those wrongly conceived as objects to be slain, with the individualizing notion 'these are my teachers and the rest,' would necessarily generate sin thereby. Likewise, the act of slaying, even of those who truly deserve to be slain in battle, if undertaken with the thought 'this battle is being waged for visible ends such as pleasure, happiness and the like' -- that too necessarily generates sin. This notion lies hidden within Arjuna's objection. It is for this very reason that the Lord's reply will be given: 'you must perform actions solely as your own duty, not with the individualizing notion [of who is being slain, or of what is to be gained].'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The words 'prana' (life) and 'dhana' (wealth) here denote the hope for these, respectively. Even though one might abandon one's own life, a hope for wealth could still arise for the sake of one's own kinsmen's enjoyment -- to rule out that possibility, wealth is mentioned separately.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
If asked, do you not see any benefit such as victory? To this he answers, in 'Na kankshe...'. He elaborates on this very point, in 'Kim no...' across two-and-a-half verses: for whose sake we need kingdom and the rest, those very ones stand arrayed for battle, having abandoned their lives and wealth, accepting sacrifice. Hence, what use do we have for kingdom and the rest?

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Those for whose sake -- for our own kinsmen's sake -- kingdom, the means of enjoyment, is desired, and pleasures, the means of happiness, are desired -- those very ones, now directly perceived before our eyes, stand in battle having abandoned their lives and their wealth. Though the verb 'to abandon' would ordinarily be expected, the past-participle form 'tyaktva' (having abandoned) is used instead, to suggest this: even though they have engaged in this battle where life and the rest are the very means being abandoned, one need not doubt that, out of excessive love for those things, they will flee -- this is ruled out; they stand firm, having truly renounced hope.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
One might object: though you alone have no such craving, still, might not one, for the sake of all one's own kinsmen, kill the enemy and make the kingdom one's own? Anticipating this doubt, he says, in 'Yeshamarthe...': those very ones -- for whose sake kingdom is desired by us, and pleasures and happiness are desired -- all of them stand here in this battle for the sake of dying, having abandoned their lives and wealth. Hence, even by killing them, no worldly attainment remains for us either -- this is the sense.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
when all those for whom we may desire them are now arrayed on this battlefield?

 Arjuna has addressed Lord Kṛṣṇa as Govinda because Kṛṣṇa is the object of all pleasures for cows and the senses. By using this significant word, Arjuna indicates that Kṛṣṇa should understand what will satisfy Arjuna's senses. But Govinda is not meant for satisfying our senses. If we try to satisfy the senses of Govinda, however, then automatically our own senses are satisfied. Materially, everyone wants to satisfy his senses, and he wants God to be the order supplier for such satisfaction. The Lord will satisfy the senses of the living entities as much as they deserve, but not to the extent that they may covet. But when one takes the opposite way -- namely, when one tries to satisfy the senses of Govinda without desiring to satisfy one's own senses -- then by the grace of Govinda all desires of the living entity are satisfied. Arjuna's deep affection for community and family members is exhibited here partly due to his natural compassion for them. He is therefore not prepared to fight. Everyone wants to show his opulence to friends and relatives, but Arjuna fears that all his relatives and friends will be killed on the battlefield and he will be unable to share his opulence after victory. This is a typical calculation of material life. The transcendental life, however, is different. Since a devotee wants to satisfy the desires of the Lord, he can, Lord willing, accept all kinds of opulence for the service of the Lord, and if the Lord is not willing, he should not accept a farthing. Arjuna did not want to kill his relatives, and if there were any need to kill them, he desired that Kṛṣṇa kill them personally. At this point he did not know that Kṛṣṇa had already killed them before their coming into the battlefield and that he was only to become an instrument for Kṛṣṇa. This fact is disclosed in following chapters. As a natural devotee of the Lord, Arjuna did not like to retaliate against his miscreant cousins and brothers, but it was the Lord's plan that they should all be killed. The devotee of the Lord does not retaliate against the wrongdoer, but the Lord does not tolerate any mischief done to the devotee by the miscreants. The Lord can excuse a person on His own account, but He excuses no one who has done harm to His devotees. Therefore the Lord was determined to kill the miscreants, although Arjuna wanted to excuse them.
