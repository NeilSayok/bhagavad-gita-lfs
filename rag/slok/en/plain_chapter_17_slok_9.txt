Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
kaṭvamlalavaṇātyuṣṇatīkṣṇarūkṣavidāhinaḥ .
āhārā rājasasyeṣṭā duḥkhaśokāmayapradāḥ

Word Meanings:
कट्वम्ललवणात्युष्णतीक्ष्णरूक्षविदाहिनः - those that are bitter
आहाराः - foods
राजसस्य - of the Rajasic
इष्टाः - are liked
दुःखशोकामयप्रदाः - are productive of pain

Life Application:
Foods bitter, sour, salty, excessively hot, pungent, dry, burning—desired by Rajasic, causing pain, grief, sickness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The foods that are bitter, sour, saline, excessively hot, pungent, dry and burning, are liked by the Rajasic and are productive of pain, grief and disease.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The foods that are bitter, sour, saline, excessively hot, pungent, dry and burning, are liked by the Rajasic and are productive of pain, grief and disease.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Those in whom Passion is dominant like foods that are bitter, sour, salty, over-hot, pungent, dry and burning. These produce unhappiness, repentance and disease.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Active and passionate individuals with Rajasic tendencies prefer foods that are bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry, and burning. Although such food seems to provide energy initially, its ultimate results are pain, disease, and grief. Once a habit for such food is developed, it becomes difficult to control. It is important to note that outer dietary control alone cannot change thoughts, but rather a change in inner thoughts naturally alters one's dietary preferences.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The foods that are killed by men of the Rajas (Strand) are those which are bitter,  sour, saltish, very hot, harsh, dry, and burning; and which cause pain, grief and disease.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Foods that are bitter, sour, very salty, exceedingly heating, very pungent, dry and burning, are all dear to Rajasika men; they produce pain, sorrow and disease.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, very hot, pungent, dry and burning, and which production pain, sorrow and disease, are dear to one having rajas.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that 'bitter' refers to pungent tastes, 'dry' means without any oil or fat, and 'burning' means causing heat and inflammation in the body. The prefix 'excessively' applies to all these qualities. 'Pain' is immediate suffering, 'grief' is sorrow following the meal, and 'disease' is illness caused by imbalance.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Foods that are too bitter, too sour, too salty, too hot, too pungent, too dry, and too burning are dear to a Rajasic person, and they produce pain, grief, and disease.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'bitter' includes items like bitter gourd; 'sour' refers to tamarind or lemon; 'salty' means excessive salt; 'hot' refers to piping hot foods; 'pungent' refers to red chillies; 'dry' refers to oil-less roasted items; and 'burning' refers to highly inflammatory things like mustard. These items are loved by Rajasic people, showing their passionate nature. However, such foods result in pain (discomfort while eating), grief (mental worry and regret afterward), and diseases in the body.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The foods that are bitter, sour, very salty, over-hot, very pungent, dry and burning, are those that they the taste (Rasa) of bitterness and sourness, that are inordinately salty, hot, pungent, and that are dry and burning. Pungent foods are those which are unsuitable and difficult to be taken by others because of their being ver cold, ver hot etc. Dry things are those which cause the feeling of dryness in the eater. Burning foods are those which cause burning sensation. Foods of this kind are relished by men of Rajasik nature. They promote pain, sorrow and disease.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.7–17.10 jointly): Sri Abhinavagupta explains that food is of three kinds according to Gunas, just like faith, sacrifice, penance, and charity. 'Yatayamam' refers to food for which three hours (one yama) have elapsed after cooking.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Foods that are katu-amla-lavana-atyusna-tiksna-ruksa-vidahinah, bitter, sour, salty, very hot (-'very' is to be connected with all, viz bitter etc.; that is very bitter, very sour, and so on-), pungent, dry [Without fat.] and burning; and duhkha-soka-amaya-pradah, which produce pain, sorrow and disease; [Pain, immediate suffering; sorrow, grief arising from not having that desired food.] are rajasasyaistah, dear to one having rajas.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that foods that are excessively bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry, and burning are loved by Rajasic people. These foods increase passion and produce pain, sorrow, and disease.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that the prefix 'excessively' must be applied to all seven qualities. These foods are excessively bitter (like neem), excessively sour, excessively salty, excessively hot (causing physical burns), excessively pungent (like black pepper), excessively dry (devoid of fat/oil), and excessively burning (causing internal heat). They produce immediate pain, mental distress (grief) afterward, and physical illness (disease) by imbalance of Gunas. Sattvic people should avoid them.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the prefix 'excessively' applies to all seven qualities: too bitter, too sour, too salty, too hot, too pungent (like chillies), too dry, and too burning. Such foods are liked by Rajasic individuals. They produce pain (immediate discomfort), grief (mental sorrow afterward), and disease.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that foods that are too bitter (like neem), too sour, too salty, over-hot, too pungent (like black pepper), too dry (without any oil), and too burning are dear to Rajasic people. They cause immediate pain, mental distress (grief), and disease. Seeking souls must avoid them.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that foods that are excessively bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent (causing watering of eyes/nose), dry (causing dryness in the mouth), and burning (causing internal heat) are dear to Rajasic people. They promote Gunas of passion and lead to physical pain, grief (regret and worry afterward), and diseases.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that foods prepared for oneself without performing sacrifices, which are too bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry, and burning are dear to Rajasic people. They cause pain during consumption, grief (stomach upset and regret) afterward, and disease.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that 'excessively' applies to all qualities. Foods that are too bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry (without fat), and burning cause immediate pain, mental distress (grief) afterward, and diseases by creating imbalance in bodily humors (Dhatus).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Foods that are too bitter, too sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry and burning are dear to those in the mode of passion. Such foods cause distress, misery and disease.
