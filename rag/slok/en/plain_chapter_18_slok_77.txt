Life Application:
And remembering again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, great is my amazement and I rejoice again and again.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
And remembering again and again that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
And, remembering again and again, also that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my wonder, O King; and I rejoice again and again.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
As memory recalls again and again the exceeding beauty of the Lord, I am filled with amazement and happiness.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Lord Krishna had shown His Cosmic Form (Vishvarupa) to Arjuna, which Sanjaya is remembering here. For a sensitive person, this Cosmic Form is as impactful as the knowledge of the Gita is unforgettable to an intellectual. The vision of the Virat Purusha described in the Vedas is shocking, and in the Gita, it is undoubtedly extremely powerful. It is not merely a poetic imagination of Sage Vyasa. When the intellect is delighted by the silence of wisdom and the heart is ecstatic in the embrace of love, man is flooded with a sense of inspired gratitude. Language is a weak medium to describe the satisfaction of fulfillment. Therefore, without further expanding on the strongest emotion of his mind, Sanjaya declares his firm conviction in summary in the final verse of the Gita.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
And remembering again and again that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again. The cosmic vision remains ever fresh and awe-inspiring in Sanjaya's consciousness.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
And remembering again and again that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again. Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains Sanjaya's response to the Cosmic Form: 1. Most wonderful form of Hari (rūpamatyadbhutaṃ hareḥ): This refers to the Cosmic Form (Vishvarupa) shown in Chapter 11. It was extremely marvelous, beyond anything in the material world. 2. Great is my amazement (vismayo me mahān): Recalling that form fills Sanjaya with deep awe and wonder at the Lord's infinite power and glory. 3. Rejoice again and again (hṛṣyāmi ca punaḥ punaḥ): The remembrance of the Cosmic Form makes Sanjaya experience the highest spiritual ecstasy repeatedly, showing the ever-joyful nature of the Divine.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Great amazement is caused in me, stirred by joy born of the repeated remembrance of that most marvellous and sovereign form of the Lord revealed to Arjuna and directly witnessed by me. I rejoice again and again.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.74–18.78 jointly): Ityaham etc. upto matir mama While concluding the [Krsna-Arjuna]  dialogue with Sanjaya's speech, the [sage Vyasa] teaches this :  What leads to the Absolute Brahman is nothing but the recollection of the purport of the dialoguea recollection that is led finally to the status of the highly vivid, direct cognition admitting no differentiation  [between its subject and object],  resulting from the continuity helped by the series of incessant contemplations [on the purport of the dialogue] according to the method of firmly fixing.  Thus, only through the recollection of the dialogue of the Bhagavat and Arjuna, the Reality could be reached and due to that come fortunes, voctories and prosperity.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Furthermore: 'tacca...'. 'Tat' refers to the Cosmic Form (Vishvarupa). The rest is clear.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Remembering that highly wonderful form of Hari, which the Lord showed Arjuna to remove his doubt, I experience great amazement and rejoice again and again. By addressing Dhritarashtra as 'O King' (Rajan), Sanjaya hints: 'Even after hearing of this Cosmic Form which removes all doubts, it is a wonder that you do not abandon your hostility and seek peace.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Sanjaya uses 'that form' to distinguish the extraordinary Cosmic Form shown to Arjuna from the ordinary form of Krishna as the son of Vasudeva. Remembering it brings immense wonder and spiritual reward.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Remembering that highly wonderful form of Hari, the destroyer of all grief, I experience great wonder: 'How could a small soul like me see such a form?'. Addressing Dhritarashtra as 'Rajan', Sanjaya suggests: 'You too know the joy of seeing the Divine, yet you remain blind to it.'

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sanjaya refers to the Cosmic Form (Vishvarupa) in which he saw space and time transcended, seeing the destruction of Bhishma and others clearly. This shows the world is like a magic show (Indrajala), and only through spiritual vision does one see the pure Reality. Remembering it brings great joy and wonder.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O King, as I remember the wonderful form of Lord Kṛṣṇa, I am struck with wonder more and more, and I rejoice again and again.

 It appears that Sañjaya also, by the grace of Vyāsa, could see the universal form Kṛṣṇa exhibited to Arjuna. It is, of course, said that Lord Kṛṣṇa had never exhibited such a form before. It was exhibited to Arjuna only, yet some great devotees could also see the universal form of Kṛṣṇa when it was shown to Arjuna, and Vyāsa was one of them. He is one of the great devotees of the Lord, and he is considered to be a powerful incarnation of Kṛṣṇa. Vyāsa disclosed this to his disciple Sañjaya, who remembered that wonderful form of Kṛṣṇa exhibited to Arjuna and enjoyed it repeatedly.
