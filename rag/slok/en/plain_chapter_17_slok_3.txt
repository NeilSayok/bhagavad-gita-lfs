Life Application:
Faith of each accords with his nature; a person consists of his faith; whatever his faith is, that indeed he is.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
O Bharata, the faith of everyone is in accordance with their internal nature (constitution/impressions). A person consists of their faith; therefore, whatever a person's faith is, that they indeed are (i.e. their character and form correspond to their faith).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The faith of each is in accordance with his nature, O Arjuna. The man consists of his faith; as a man's faith is, so is he.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The faith of every man conforms to his nature. By nature he is full of faith. He is in fact what his faith makes him.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The faith that sustains each person's personality in the world varies. This is because every individual's faith corresponds to their inherent nature and past impressions (Samskaras). Faith and nature determine each other interactively, but the Gita declares nature (Svabhava) as the primary determinant. Faith represents the inner strength by which a person remains committed to their chosen goal. A person's capacity determines their confidence, which in turn reinforces their capacity. Thus, capacity and faith are complementary. A person's character is shaped by the qualities (Gunas) acquired from previous lives. A devotee attains the object of worship in accordance with their faith. Every person is what their faith is; their achievements and actions depend entirely on this faith.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Corresponding to one's own sattva everybody has faith, O descendant of Bharata !  The person predominantly consists of the faith.  What one has faith in, that he is (becomes) certainly.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The faith of everyone, O Arjuna, is in accordance with his internal organ (Antahkarana). Man consists of faith. Whatever his faith is, that verily is he.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
O scion of the Bharata dynasty, the faith of all beings is in accordance with their minds. This person is made up of faith as the dominant factor. He is verily what his faith is.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that 'Sattva' here refers to the mind (Chitta). One's faith is in accordance with the mind. A person is what their faith is: if one possesses Sattvic faith, one is Sattvic; if Rajasic, Rajasic; if Tamasic, Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that our latent impressions (Vasanas) are the efficient cause of faith, while the mind (Antahkarana) is its material cause. Therefore, faith varies according to the qualities predominant in the mind. Since a person acts in accordance with their faith, they are said to consist of faith. Anandgiri describes how a person's life and goal conform to the nature of their faith.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Bharata, the faith of every man is in accordance with his internal constitution. This man consists of faith. Therefore, whatever his faith is, that verily he is (i.e. his standing is according to his faith).

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'Sattva' here refers to the mind (Antahkarana). The latent impressions (Samskaras) in the mind determine a person's faith, whether it is Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic. This principle applies to all human beings, regardless of their knowledge of scriptures, caste, creed, or nation. A person is made of their faith, and their actions and destination are governed by it. Sattvic faith points toward God (divine nature), while Rajasic and Tamasic faith point toward the material world (demonic nature). The Lord emphasizes that one should cultivate Sattvic faith by associating with pure environments, scriptures, and thoughts.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'Sattva' means internal organ (i.e., mind). The faith of everyone is according to his internal organ. The meaning is that with whatever Guna his internal organ is conjoined, one's faith corresponds to that Guna (i.e., Guna as object). The term Sattva covers here body, senses etc., already mentioned. Man consists of faith, viz., is the product of his faith. Of whatever faith he is, viz., with whatever faith a man is possessed, that verily he is; he is a transformation of faith of that nature. The purport is this:  If the person is associated with faith in auspicious acts he becomes associated with fruit of these auspicious acts. Conseently, attainment chiefly follows one's faith.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that since faith is a function of the mind (Antahkarana), one's faith conforms to the state of one's mind, which is shaped by past impressions. A person is made of faith; whatever one's faith is, that indeed is the person's nature. This clarifies why different individuals exhibit different kinds of faith.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that the variation in faith is due to both its efficient cause (past latent impressions) and its material cause (the mind, which is constituted of the three Gunas). In all beings who lack scriptural wisdom, faith conforms to the state of their mind—being Sattvic in a mind dominated by Sattva, Rajasic in a mind dominated by Rajas, and Tamasic in a mind dominated by Tamas. Since a person is made up of their faith, their character and final destination are determined by it.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that although faith is technically a product of Sattva (purity), it becomes threefold because the minds of beings are colored by the mixture of Rajas and Tamas. Faith conforms to the proportion of these qualities in the mind. A worldly person is a product of their faith; they are shaped by it. Those with Sattvic faith, reinforced by Sattvic impressions, remain Sattvic, while those with Rajasic or Tamasic faith remain in those states. For those with scriptural wisdom, their faith is single and purely Sattvic because they conquer their lower nature.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that the faith of all beings is in accordance with their minds. A person is made of their faith; whatever their faith is, that they indeed are. The character and the spiritual path of a person are therefore determined by this faith, which is built upon past Samskaras.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the Lord classifies faith to show that the result of actions depends on the qualities of the performer. The word 'Dehinaam' (of the embodied) indicates that faith is conditioned by the type of body and mind they possess. This faith is 'Svabhavaja' (born of nature), meaning it arises from रुचि (taste or predilection) which is built upon past latent impressions (Vasanas). He urges Arjuna to listen to understand how these qualities manifest.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the definition of faith is given here. The faith of all three classes of beings conforms to their primary nature (Sattva). The transmigrating soul is made of faith; whatever faith one holds (Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic), one takes on that very form. This shows that the nature of their faith dictates their spiritual standing and achievements.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that faith is in accordance with the quality of the intellect (Sattva) shaped by past deeds. If one holds Sattvic faith, one is Sattvic; if Rajasic, one is Rajasic; and if Tamasic, one is Tamasic. Their worship and behavior will reflect this faith. For example, if a devotee of their father considers him divine due to past merits, the gods bless him as Sattvic. But if one worships spirits or seeks petty gains out of delusion, one is Tamasic and falls into hell.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O son of Bharata, according to one’s existence under the various modes of nature, one evolves a particular kind of faith. The living being is said to be of a particular faith according to the modes he has acquired.

 Everyone has a particular type of faith, regardless of what he is. But his faith is considered good, passionate or ignorant according to the nature he has acquired. Thus, according to his particular type of faith, one associates with certain persons. Now the real fact is that every living being, as is stated in the Fifteenth Chapter, is originally a fragmental part and parcel of the Supreme Lord. Therefore one is originally transcendental to all the modes of material nature. But when one forgets his relationship with the Supreme Personality of Godhead and comes into contact with the material nature in conditional life, he generates his own position by association with the different varieties of material nature. The resultant artificial faith and existence are only material. Although one may be conducted by some impression, or some conception of life, originally he is nirguṇa, or transcendental. Therefore one has to become cleansed of the material contamination that he has acquired, in order to regain his relationship with the Supreme Lord. That is the only path back without fear: Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If one is situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, then that path is guaranteed for his elevation to the perfectional stage. If one does not take to this path of self-realization, then he is surely to be conducted by the influence of the modes of nature. The word śraddhā, or “faith,” is very significant in this verse. Śraddhā, or faith, originally comes out of the mode of goodness. One’s faith may be in a demigod or some created God or some mental concoction. One’s strong faith is supposed to be productive of works of material goodness. But in material conditional life, no works are completely purified. They are mixed. They are not in pure goodness. Pure goodness is transcendental; in purified goodness one can understand the real nature of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. As long as one’s faith is not completely in purified goodness, the faith is subject to contamination by any of the modes of material nature. The contaminated modes of material nature expand to the heart. Therefore according to the position of the heart in contact with a particular mode of material nature, one’s faith is established. It should be understood that if one’s heart is in the mode of goodness his faith is also in the mode of goodness. If his heart is in the mode of passion, his faith is also in the mode of passion. And if his heart is in the mode of darkness, illusion, his faith is also thus contaminated. Thus we find different types of faith in this world, and there are different types of religions due to different types of faith. The real principle of religious faith is situated in the mode of pure goodness, but because the heart is tainted we find different types of religious principles. Thus according to different types of faith, there are different kinds of worship.
