Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The Bhagavad Gita, being a practical dynamic text of religion, does not stop with merely expounding theories. In it, after discussing every theory, the exact method of practice is also described, by which a seeker can attain the state of perfection.

WHO SERVES ME WITH UNSWERVING DEVOTION-YOGA — Supreme love for God is called devotion (Bhakti). Our mind effortlessly dwells upon the beloved object. Our entire nature is nourished by our thoughts. 'As we think, so we become' is the law. Therefore, by contemplating with a single-pointed mind upon the infinite nature of the Self, the finite mortal ego is ended and one abides in one's True Nature.

It is true that continuous contemplation on the Supreme Self with uniform devotion and intensity is not easy. In the condition in which we find ourselves today, we lack the capacity to keep the mind steady in meditation for long periods. Knowing this inability of seekers, the Lord suggests a practical method by which we can maintain the remembrance of God for a long time — and that method is SERVICE. It was described in Chapter 3 that actions performed with a spirit of dedication to God become worship (Yajna). This makes it clear that mere ritual worship or singing hymns alone is not enough. The Teacher of the Gita expects His devotees not to confine their religion merely to the puja room or temples. They must follow religion in their daily life, field of work, and dealings with people.

Continuous remembrance of God and the discipline of service remove the distractions of the mind and make it fit for the subtler practice of meditation. The proportion of Tamas and Rajas decreases, and Sattva-guna increases in equal measure. Such a Sattva-predominant seeker becomes qualified for the practice of meditation. For such a seeker, Self-realization is not far away.

A noble practitioner experiences the nature of Brahman and himself becomes Brahman — just as a dreamer, upon waking up, himself becomes the waker. Listen to how this seeker becomes Brahman...

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
'Brahmabhuyam' means attaining dearness to the Lord like Brahman (the Supreme Lord) or Prakriti. But it does not mean being as dear as Lakshmi, but simply attaining the state of being dear to Him. In the Padma Purana it is said: 'Whether bound or liberated, no soul is as dear to Hari as Ramaa (Lakshmi).' 'Bhuyaya' means for becoming (bhavaya).

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Having answered the first two questions, he now answers the third question — 'Now...'. He excludes the worldly interpretation of the word 'Me' (Mam) by explaining it as 'Ishvara' (God). By using the word 'Narayana', any distinction among forms is excluded. He removes any idea of His being indifferent or distant by saying 'residing in the hearts of all'. There is an alternative application depending on whether one is a primary or secondary qualified seeker. To rule out any accidental nature of Bhakti Yoga, the word 'avyabhicharena' (unswerving) is used, which is explained as 'never wavering'. 'Bhakti' means supreme love for God, and since one unites through it, it is called 'Yoga'. 'Serves' means meditating constantly without outward distraction of mind. 'He' means the wise person who, endowed with right knowledge inspired by the Lord, attains this state while still living.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He who serves Me through unswerving Yoga of Devotion, having transcended these modes (gunas), becomes fit for attaining Brahman.

Commentary: [Although the Lord had already explained the means to transcend the modes in verses 19-20 of this chapter, Arjuna nevertheless asked for the means to become transcendent in verse 21. This indicates that Arjuna wished to know another means besides that one. Therefore, considering Arjuna eligible for devotion, the Lord tells him devotion as the means to transcend the modes.]

'Maam cha yo'vyabhichaarena bhaktiyogena sevate' — In these words, the worshipper, the worshipped, and worship — all three are present. Namely, by 'yah' the worshipper, by 'maam' the worshipped, and by 'avyabhichaarena bhaktiyogena sevate' the worship is expressed. The term 'avyabhichaarena' implies that there should be no reliance on anything else. Leaving aside worldly reliance, there should not even be reliance on other yogas like Jnana Yoga or Karma Yoga. And the term 'bhaktiyogena' implies relying solely on God — having only God as refuge, hope, strength, and faith. Thus, by forbidding reliance on others through 'avyabhichaarena', reliance on God alone is enjoined through 'bhaktiyogena'.

The term 'sevate' means worshipping God, adoring Him, surrendering to Him, and acting according to His will through unswerving devotional service.

'Sa gunaansamatityaitan' — He who surrenders to God alone with exclusive devotion does not have to make effort to transcend the modes; rather, by God's grace, transcendence of the modes happens automatically (Gita 12.6-7).

'Brahmabhuyaya kalpate' — Having transcended the modes, he becomes qualified (eligible) for attaining Brahman. When the Lord has spoken of devotion here, why did He mention attaining Brahman instead of attaining Himself? The reason for mentioning Brahman-attainment here is that Arjuna had asked for the means to transcend the modes (attaining Nirguna Brahman). Therefore, the Lord described His devotion as the means to attain Brahman.

Secondly, it is stated in the scriptures that one who worships the Lord does not need to perform any other discipline or effort to achieve the stages of knowledge; rather, the stages of knowledge are realized automatically for him. Referring to that, the Lord says here that one who serves Him through unswerving devotion-yoga does not need any other means to become eligible for Brahman-realization; he automatically becomes eligible. However, such a devotee is not satisfied merely with Brahman-realization. His only feeling is how to please the Lord; his happiness lies solely in the Lord's happiness. The conclusion is that for one who is solely devoted to God and attracted to Him, Brahman-realization is self-evident. Whether he gives importance to Brahman-realization or not is a different matter, but he automatically becomes qualified for it.

Thirdly, the Ultimate Truth that is realized through Jnana Yoga, Karma Yoga, etc., is realized through Devotion as well. Even though there are differences in the paths, there is no difference in the realization of that Truth.

Connection: How is it that one worships God yet becomes qualified for realizing Brahman? The answer to this is given in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The state of transcendence of Gunas is not attained merely by reflecting on the difference between the Prakrti and the self as declared in the text such as, 'When the seer beholds no agent of action other than the Gunas, then he transcends the Gunas, for it is liable then to be sublated by contrary subtle impressions (Vasanas) which have continued from beginningless time. He who, with unswerving Bhakti Yoga, namely, one-pointed Bhakti Yoga, serves Me of true-resolve, supremely compassionate and the ocean of parental affection for supplicants - such a man crosses over the Gunas of Sattva etc., which are otherwise invincible. He becomes worthy for brahmabhuya, the state of brahman i.e., he becomes alified for the state of brahman. The meaning is that he attains the self as It really is, immortal and immutable.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Expecting that interpreting 'brahmabhuyaya' as 'for becoming Parabrahman' is contradictory to scriptural authority, Sri Madhvacharya explains it differently — 'Brahmavat...'. Here the word 'Brahman' does not mean Parabrahman, but rather Prakriti as referred to in 'Mama yonir mahad brahma' (14.3), and supported by the subsequent statement 'Brahmano hi pratishthaham' (14.27). And 'bhuyam' (becoming) does not mean identity (tadaatmya), but rather becoming dear to the Lord like Brahman (Prakriti), because one object cannot literally become another. To clarify whether the dearness of the transcendent person is equal to that of Prakriti, he quotes authority — 'Na tu...'. To explain why the word 'bhuya' implies dearness, he states — 'Bhuyaya...'. By grammatical rule, 'bhuya' means state or becoming. Becoming like Brahman is 'brahmabhuyam'. Incorporating the sense of comparison (vati), it signifies attaining dearness like Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Furthermore, the transcendence of modes for a practitioner of the path of devotion (Bhakti-marga) is accomplished solely through devotion to the Supreme Lord who is Himself beyond the modes. Thus He says — 'Mam ca...'. He who serves Me, the Supreme Being (Purushottama), with unswerving, whole-hearted, exclusive devotion-yoga (the yoga of love), having transcended these modes, becomes qualified for 'brahmabhuyaya' — the state of Akshara-Brahman. By this it is stated that for those who serve through the discipline of unswerving devotion, the realization of Brahman is an incidental result of devotion. As stated in the Shruti: 'Know Him alone as the Self and relinquish all other speech; this is the bridge to Immortality' (Mundaka Up. 2.2.5). Here, knowledge of Him as the Self and the object of loving devotion excludes wavering devotion, signifying that this alone is the bridge to liberation.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Now he gives the answer to the third question, 'How does he transcend these three modes?'. The word 'ca' is used in the sense of 'tu' (however/emphasis). He who serves — i.e., constantly meditates upon — Me alone, the Supreme Lord Narayana, the indweller of all beings, who through Maya has assumed the state of the individual soul (Kshetrajna), the embodiment of supreme bliss, the Lord Vasudeva, through unswerving Yoga of Devotion characterized by supreme love as described in Chapter 12 — that devotee of Mine, having fully transcended these previously mentioned modes by sublating the perception of duality, becomes qualified for 'brahmabhuyaya', i.e., for becoming Brahman, for Liberation. The meaning is that constant remembrance of the Lord is the sole means to transcend the modes.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He gives the answer to the question, 'And how does he transcend these three modes?' — 'Mam ca...'. The word 'ca' is used for emphasis. He who serves Me alone, the Supreme Lord Sri Narayana, through unswerving single-pointed Yoga of Devotion, having completely transcended these modes, becomes qualified for 'brahmabhuyaya', that is, for becoming Brahman, for Liberation.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having solved the first two questions, he now answers the third question: 'How does he transcend these three modes?' — 'Maam...'. The word 'ca' is for combining the characteristics of the transcendent person that are to be achieved through effort by a seeker of liberation. Be he a renunciate or a man of action, he who serves Me — God, Narayana residing in the hearts of all beings — through unswerving, desireless Bhakti Yoga characterized by supreme love (worship itself being devotion and yoga, which generates Knowledge of Truth), meditating constantly while abandoning thoughts of sense objects — he, endowed with right knowledge granted by the Lord's grace, while still living, completely transcends these modes as described, and becomes qualified for 'brahmabhuyaya', i.e., for becoming Brahman, for Liberation.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
To refute the doubt regarding a separate independent means in the verse 'Mam ca...', Sri Venkatanatha comments — 'Atha...'. After stating the secondary causes for transcending the modes, the principal cause is now declared. Merely reflecting on the distinction between Prakriti and the Self ('Nanyam...') is not independently effective due to the overwhelming presence of obstacles in the form of beginningless latent impressions (vasanas). By the word 'Mam', He implies the attributes suitable for appeasing these obstacles, describing Him as 'true of resolve, supremely compassionate, an ocean of affection for supplicants'. As per Brahma Sutras (3.2.5), the Lord's resolve for liberation is infallible. 'Avyabhichara' means exclusivity characterized by abandoning other deities. The word 'ca' combines the previously mentioned disciplines. 'Etan' refers to the otherwise invincible modes. 'Kalpate' means becoming qualified for the state of Brahman — that is, realizing the self as it really is, immortal and immutable.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He answers the question, 'How does he transcend the modes?' — 'Mam ca...'. The word 'ca' is in the sense of 'tu' (however/emphasis). He who serves Me, for My sake, through unswerving, exclusive Bhakti Yoga, having completely transcended these modes, becomes qualified (capable) for 'brahmabhuyaya', i.e., for the state of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Desiring to answer how one transcends the three modes, he describes 'Tanumanasa', the third stage among the three stages of spiritual discipline — 'Mam ca...'. The practitioner who worships Me, the inner Self (the word 'ca' being in the sense of 'tu', indicating the uniqueness of this seeker compared to those in earlier stages), through unswerving Bhakti Yoga uninterrupted by other mental modifications — i.e., through the yoga of mental concentration with an unbroken stream of thought directed at Me, the Lord, like a continuous stream of oil — meditating thus with a subtle mind, having transcended these modes and at the fruition of meditation even sublating Sattva-guna, becomes qualified for 'brahmabhuyaya', i.e., for the state of Brahman.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who engages in full devotional service, unfailing in all circumstances, at once transcends the modes of material nature and thus comes to the level of Brahman.

 This verse is a reply to Arjuna’s third question: What is the means of attaining to the transcendental position? As explained before, the material world is acting under the spell of the modes of material nature. One should not be disturbed by the activities of the modes of nature; instead of putting his consciousness into such activities, he may transfer his consciousness to Kṛṣṇa activities. Kṛṣṇa activities are known as bhakti-yoga – always acting for Kṛṣṇa. This includes not only Kṛṣṇa, but His different plenary expansions such as Rāma and Nārāyaṇa. He has innumerable expansions. One who is engaged in the service of any of the forms of Kṛṣṇa, or of His plenary expansions, is considered to be transcendentally situated. One should also note that all the forms of Kṛṣṇa are fully transcendental, blissful, full of knowledge and eternal. Such personalities of Godhead are omnipotent and omniscient, and they possess all transcendental qualities. So if one engages himself in the service of Kṛṣṇa or His plenary expansions with unfailing determination, although these modes of material nature are very difficult to overcome, one can overcome them easily. This has already been explained in the Seventh Chapter. One who surrenders unto Kṛṣṇa at once surmounts the influence of the modes of material nature. To be in Kṛṣṇa consciousness or in devotional service means to acquire equality with Kṛṣṇa. The Lord says that His nature is eternal, blissful and full of knowledge, and the living entities are part and parcel of the Supreme, as gold particles are part of a gold mine. Thus the living entity, in his spiritual position, is as good as gold, as good as Kṛṣṇa in quality. The difference of individuality continues, otherwise there would be no question of bhakti-yoga. Bhakti-yoga means that the Lord is there, the devotee is there, and the activity of exchange of love between the Lord and the devotee is there. Therefore the individuality of two persons is present in the Supreme Personality of Godhead and the individual person, otherwise there would be no meaning to bhakti-yoga . If one is not situated in the same transcendental position with the Lord, one cannot serve the Supreme Lord. To be a personal assistant to a king, one must acquire the qualifications. Thus the qualification is to become Brahman, or freed from all material contamination. It is said in the Vedic literature, brahmaiva san brahmāpy eti . One can attain the Supreme Brahman by becoming Brahman. This means that one must qualitatively become one with Brahman. By attainment of Brahman, one does not lose his eternal Brahman identity as an individual soul.
