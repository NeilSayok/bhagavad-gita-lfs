Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Doubtless all the warriors were under great tension, but as soon as they heard the conch-blast, each raised his own conch and sounded it. After that, the instruments of war -- conches, kettledrums and the rest -- began to sound as a declaration of war. Sanjaya describes this uproar as 'the sound became tumultuous.' But further on, describing the Pandavas' answering conch-blast, he says that sound was so terrible that heaven and earth resounded with it and it rent the hearts of the Kauravas. This description makes clear that Sanjaya was opposed to this act of Duryodhana. Hence we can, with full confidence, accept the Lord Krishna's teaching given on that battlefield as narrated by him.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
After that, conches, kettledrums, drums, mridangas and horns all sounded together at once; their sound became tremendous.

Explanation: 'tatah shankhashcha bheryashcha panavanaka-gomukhah' -- although Bhishma had not blown the conch to declare the start of war, but only to cheer Duryodhana, still the Kaurava army took Bhishma's conch-blowing as itself the declaration of war. So, as soon as Bhishma blew his conch, all the instruments of the Kaurava army -- conches and the rest -- sounded together at once.

'Shankha' (conches) come from the ocean; they are kept in the worship of the Deity and used in performing arati and the like; on auspicious occasions and at the start of battle they are blown by mouth. 'Bheri' is the name for kettledrums (large ones are called naubat); these are made of iron and covered with buffalo hide, and are beaten with a wooden stick; they are kept in temples and in kings' forts, and are played especially at festivals and auspicious occasions -- in royal households they are played daily. 'Panava' is the name for a drum, made of iron or wood and covered with goat-hide, played by hand or with a wooden stick; in shape it resembles a dholak but is larger; playing panavas at the start of a task is considered as auspicious as worshipping Ganesha. 'Anaka' is the name for the mridanga, also called pakhawaj; in shape it is like a wooden dholak, made of clay and covered with hide, played by hand. 'Gomukh' is the name for the horn (narsingha); curved like a serpent in shape, with a mouth like a cow's, blown by mouth.

'Sahasaivabhyahanyanta' -- the Kaurava army's enthusiasm was very high, so as soon as Grandsire Bhishma's conch sounded, all the instruments of the Kaurava army sounded together at once, spontaneously, without delay and without effort. 'Sa shabdastumulo'bhavat' -- the sound of the conches and other instruments of the Kaurava army, standing arrayed in their separate divisions and units, became terrific, i.e., it resounded very loudly.

Transition: at the very start of this chapter, Dhritarashtra had asked Sanjaya what his sons and Pandu's sons did on the battlefield. So from verse 2 to verse 13, Sanjaya has answered 'what Dhritarashtra's sons did.' Now, from the next verse, Sanjaya answers 'what Pandu's sons did.'

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said  Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within.

Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory.

Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra:  Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Venkatanatha then glosses the verse's grammar: from 'tasya sanjanayan' up to 'tumulo 'bhavat' (verse 13's close), he explains that the word 'janayan' (producing), being a present participle in the sense of purpose or cause of an ongoing action (per Panini's grammar), is glossed as 'in order to produce.' 'Simhanadam vinadya' (having roared a lion's roar) is explained on the pattern of 'cooking the rice' -- hence the added word 'having done' (krtva) is used to convey the sense 'having also done the conch-blowing,' illustrating that roots like 'kr' (do), 'bhu' (be), 'as' (be) denote action in general. In the phrase 'tatah shankhah' (then, the conches...) of the following verse, the word 'tatah' (then) is said to indicate that it was Bhishma the commander who caused the conches to be sounded, in order to signal eagerness for victory -- hence the gloss 'caused to be done.' 'Shankha-bheri' (conch and kettledrum) is a synecdoche for all such instruments; so too the naming of a few specific instruments in the verse is meant to represent the whole category. The second 'cha' (and) joins the combined sound of conches, kettledrums etc. to the lion-roar and conch-blowing; and the word 'krtva' (having done) is a third element joining in the sense of 'caused to be done.'

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Thus, hearing the conch-sound made by the commander to inspire battle-enthusiasm, the musicians, in order to alert everyone, played the kettledrums and other instruments -- so it is said. As soon as they heard it, the conches, kettledrums, panavas, anakas and gomukhas were struck and played. The word 'eva' (only) indicates they played only upon hearing that sound, not merely as a display of their own valor at the approach of battle. That sound of the conches and the rest became tumultuous and great.
