Life Application:
Recognize that of all forms born in any womb, great nature is the mother and God the seed-giving father.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
A mere glance at creation reveals that there is a continuous generation of living beings. The place of dead beings is constantly taken by countless newborn creatures. In all these wombs—human, animal, beast, vegetation—this process goes on continuously. All these living beings are made of the combination of matter and consciousness. The diversity or distinction among them is due to material limitations (upadhis), whereas the conscious principle is one and the same in all of them. This material nature itself is indicated by the term 'Mahadbrahma'. Lord Krishna, identifying Himself with His nature of Existence-Consciousness-Bliss (Sat-Chit-Ananda), says, 'I am the father who deposits the seed in this womb of nature.' His statement is metaphorical. As we have seen in the commentary on the previous verse, the manifestation of the Supreme Self as consciousness in nature is the sowing of the seed by Him, as a result of which that material nature becomes conscious and functional. Just as an engine moves only when powered by steam, otherwise it is just a piece of iron in a particular shape; the same is the case with the body, mind, and intellect limitations without consciousness. A single man cannot be called a father just because he has the capacity to reproduce. For that, he has to deposit his seed in the womb after marriage. Similarly, without nature (prakriti), the Spirit (Purusha) alone cannot manifest itself. The Lord states this principle here in brief, that He is the eternal Father of the entire universe, who arranges the staging of the drama of life on the world stage. Although followers of other religions try to convince us that the fatherhood of God was first recognized and accepted only by Christianity, the facts refute this notion, because the Gita was taught to Arjuna thousands of years before Jesus Christ. At most, we can say that Jesus Christ must have taken this idea from pre-existing religions. Hindus did not place much emphasis on the fatherhood of God. Although this concept is poetic, from a theoretical point of view it cannot be said to be very logical. However, because this concept is easily comprehensible to the general public, later religious founders generously accepted it from earlier religions. Introducing the main subject of this chapter, Lord Krishna explains what those qualities of nature are and how they bind the Self to the non-Self.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains: Objection: How is it said that the birth of all beings takes place through the birth of Hiranyagarbha in accordance with the stated cause, when other specific causes are possible for different bodies among the classes of gods and so on? To this, he (the Lord) replies: 'In all wombs' (sarva-yonisu). Lest it be doubted as to how this (the great Brahman/Prakriti) can be the cause when other different causes appear in those respective cases, he says: 'In all states' (sarvavastham), because it exists in those very forms.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary -- sarvayoniṣu kaunteya mūrtayaḥ sambhavanti yāḥ -- Jarayuja (viviparous: born from womb/placenta like humans, animals, etc.), Andaja (oviparous: born from eggs like birds, snakes, etc.), Svedaja (born from sweat/moisture like lice, nit, etc.), and Udbhijja (sprouting: born from earth like trees, creepers, etc.) -- these are the four sources (khani) of birth for all living beings. From each of these four sources, millions of species (yonis) are born. Even among the creatures born in each of these millions of species, the shape of each creature is unique. Even within the same species or race, there is a gross or subtle difference in the shapes of the beings; that is, no two shapes are exactly alike. For instance, in the human species, billions of bodies have been born over billions of years, but to this day, no two human faces/shapes are identical. In this regard, a poet has said: "Turban, destiny, voice (throat), nature, form, speech, intellect, and handwriting — these characters do not match even between two individuals, despite seeing many lands." That is, the turban, fortune, voice, temperament, shape, words, reasoning power, and handwriting of two individuals never match. Thus, of all the bodies that have been born since time immemorial in the eighty-four lakh species, the shape of each is different. Apart from the eighty-four lakh species, gods, manes (ancestors), Gandharvas, ghosts, spirits, etc., should also be included under the term 'sarvayoniṣu' (all wombs).
tāsāṃ brahma mahadyonirahaṃ bījapradaḥ pitā -- The four sources mentioned above, i.e., eighty-four lakh species, are the birthplaces of bodies, and the origin (in place of the mother) of all these species is Mahadbrahma, i.e., the primordial nature (mula prakriti). I am the father who deposits the seed in the form of individual souls (Jivas) in that primordial nature.
The Lord deposits the seed of His conscious part into various bodies of different colors and shapes — this proves that the part of the Supreme Lord residing in each creature appears different only due to the difference in bodies. In reality, the same Supreme Self exists in all beings (Gita 13.2). This is explained with an illustration. Although the illustration does not apply in all aspects, yet it helps in bringing the intellect closer to the illustrated truth. In both cloth and earth, the same element (matter) is dominant. If cloth is put in water, it sinks to the bottom. Cloth is woven from warp (long thread) and weft (cross thread). Between every warp and weft, there is a microscopic hole. There are many such holes in a cloth. When immersed in water, water fills all the fibers and separate holes of the cloth. Even after taking the cloth out of water, the same water remains uniformly present in its fibers and countless holes. In this illustration, the cloth is Nature, the countless separate holes are the bodies, and the water filling the cloth and its holes is the Supreme Self. The implication is that from a gross perspective, the Supreme Self appears separate in each body, but from a subtle perspective, the same one Supreme Self is fully present in all bodies, in the entire universe.
Connection -- The discussion on how the individual souls, produced by the union of the Supreme Self and His power (nature), get bound by the qualities born of nature, begins in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains: Thus, this creation of Prakriti consisting of conscious (chit) and unconscious (achit) entities in the form of effects is created by Me alone, as stated in the verse beginning with 'sarva-yonisu'. This is not being said as something entirely new, but rather this is how it is seen everywhere in the world; hence it is said that one is the father and another is the field (womb). It should be understood that of those physical forms of beings, the great Brahman is the womb (mother), and I am the seed-giving father. For this very reason, in scriptures like the Vishnu Purana, Lakshmi-Narayana and Gauri-Shankara are everywhere depicted as having assumed the forms of female and male.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains: Objection: How is the birth of all beings from that (union of Prakriti and Purusha), since other causes are possible for the specific bodies of gods etc.? Anticipating this, He says: 'In all wombs' (sarva-yonisu). O son of Kunti, whatever bodies (forms) of diverse and unique structures are born in all wombs — of gods, manes, humans, cattle, beasts, etc. — through the divisions of viviparous, oviparous, sweat-born, and soil-born, the great Brahman itself, having attained those respective causal states, is the womb (mother). And I, the Supreme Lord, am the father who deposits the seed, the agent of impregnation. Since the other causes are merely specific states of the great Brahman, it is rightly said that the birth of all beings takes place from that.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains: This manner of the birth of beings from Prakriti and Purusha supervised by Me does not occur only at the beginning of creation, but rather it happens at all times; this is what is said in: 'In all wombs' (sarva-yonisu). In all wombs, such as those of humans etc., whatever forms (bodies) of moving and non-moving creatures are produced, the great Brahman (Prakriti) is the womb (mother) of those forms. And I am the seed-giver, the father who performs the act of impregnation.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains: Not only at the beginning of creation is Nature the womb (mother) and I the one who performs the impregnation, but it is always so; this He says in: 'In all wombs' (sarva-yonisu). In all wombs of humans and others, whatever forms characterized by the structure of bodies are born, O son of Kunti (just as Kunti is your mother, similarly) the great Brahman existing in those respective causal forms is their womb or cause. And I, the Supreme Lord, am the seed-giver, the father who performs the impregnation. Thus, since the Supreme Lord as the impregnator is everywhere present in other causes which are merely specific states of Nature, it is appropriate to say that the birth of all beings takes place from that.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains: Thus, by this verse, the Lord's control over the primary (Prakrita) and incidental (Naimittika) creations is declared. Now, that even the daily continuous creation (Nitya-srishti) is carried out by Him alone is stated by the verse beginning with 'sarva-yonisu'. To avoid the defect of tautology between the two verses, he says 'karyavastho'pi', meaning even within the continuous creation. Just after the primary fourfold creation starting from Hiranyagarbha up to the dissolution, whatever progeny continues even among the tiny creatures, even there the Lord does not remain indifferent and let others do it; with this intention the word 'sarva-yonisu' is used. Hence he says 'devagandharva...' (gods, Gandharvas, etc.). The diversity characterized by the distinct causes and objects proved by means of knowledge is recalled by the well-known reference 'yah' in order to make known the unique power of the Creator; with this intention he says 'tattanmurtayah'. To rule out the meaning of mere possibility for the word 'sambhavanti' and to indicate the relation of material cause and effect, he says 'jayante' (are born). Due to the absence of immediate material causality, it is referred to as 'cause'. Considering the impossibility of mere insentient nature (Prakriti) being the cause without connection with consciousness and without the control of the Supreme Lord, he says 'maya samyojitacetanavarga' (the group of conscious souls conjoined by Me). How can the single primordial nature be the womb (cause) in the final individual creation (Vyasti-srishti) indicated by 'sarva-yonisu', which appears to have many wombs? To this he replies: 'mahadadivisesantavastha' (the state beginning from Mahat and ending with the elements/Visesas). What is the meaning of the word 'seed' here, and what is its giving, which is the cause for designated fatherhood? From 'tasam brahma mahadyoniraham bijapradah pita', it appears that nature alone is the material cause and the Lord is merely the efficient cause, as in the Theistic Samkhya system; but that is contrary to the Vedas. Furthermore, what is the cause of the diversity among those born in the same womb and from the same unbiased father? To this he replies: 'tatra tatra' (in those respective cases). The nature mentioned in the previous verse as 'mahadbrahma yonih' is referred to here by its own word. The word 'seed', which occupies the place of semen/womb here, signifies the conscious self (Jiva) suitable for that respective creation. This is the sense.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains: Objection: Since various kinds of objects are perceived in diverse ways in many wombs, how can there be a single womb (cause) for all of them? To this, he says: 'In all wombs' (sarva-yonisu). First, there is the general creation in the form of the birth of all wombs; then, in all wombs, O son of Kunti, whatever forms (bodies) are produced, their womb (origin or place of mother) is the great Brahman, i.e., Prakriti, and I alone am the father (producer) who deposits the seed in the form of desire and knowledge. This is the meaning. It means that the same Brahman, by My will, manifests in the form of various wombs.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains: Moreover, in all wombs which act as material causes, just as plants grow in the earth, whatever four types of forms (bodies) of gods, humans, animals/birds, and non-moving entities are produced, the great Brahman (the previously mentioned Maya) is the womb (cause) of those forms. Here in the word 'brahma-mahat', the secondary member is placed last according to grammatical rules. The meaning is that Maya alone is the womb. And I am the seed-giving father who deposits My reflection in them. Just as a man deposits semen mixed with the impressions of past deeds in his wife's womb, which leads to the formation of the body from the wife, and the manifestation of consciousness in it from the semen's part; similarly, I am the father and Maya is the mother of that body endowed with consciousness. This is the meaning.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
It should be understood that all species of life, O son of Kuntī, are made possible by birth in this material nature, and that I am the seed-giving father.

 In this verse it is clearly explained that the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is the original father of all living entities. The living entities are combinations of the material nature and the spiritual nature. Such living entities are seen not only on this planet but on every planet, even on the highest, where Brahmā is situated. Everywhere there are living entities; within the earth there are living entities, even within water and within fire. All these appearances are due to the mother, material nature, and Kṛṣṇa’s seed-giving process. The purport is that the material world is impregnated with living entities, who come out in various forms at the time of creation according to their past deeds.
