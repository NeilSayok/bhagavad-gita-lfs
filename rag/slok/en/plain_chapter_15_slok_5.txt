Life Application:
Free from pride and delusion, victorious over attachment, devoted to Self, desires stilled, freed from dualities—reach eternal goal.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Those who are free from pride and delusion, who have conquered the evil of attachment, who are constantly established in the Self, whose desires have completely turned away, and who are liberated from the dualities of pleasure and pain, those undeluded wise ones reach that imperishable goal.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Free from pride and delusion, victorious over the evil of attachment, dwelling constantly in the Self, their desires having completely turned away, freed from the pairs of opposites known as pleasure and pain, the undeluded reach the eternal goal.

निर्मानमोहाः free from pride and delusion? जितसङ्गदोषाः victorious over the evil of attachment? अध्यात्मनित्याः dwelling constantly in the Self? विनिवृत्तकामाः (their) desires having completely turned away? द्वन्द्वैः from the pairs of opposites? विमुक्ताः freed? सुखदुःखसंज्ञैः known as pleasure and pain? गच्छन्ति reach? अमूढाः the undeluded? पदम् goal? अव्ययम् eternal? तत् That.Commentary Wherever there is pride there is stiff egoism. Absence of discrimination between the Real and the unreal is Moha. Perversion is Moha. Infatuation is Moha. Those who are free from likes and dislikes even when they attain pleasant or unpleasant objects have triumphed over the,evil of attachment. Kartritva Abhimana or the idea I am the doer is Sanga. Likes and dislikes are the Doshas or the evils. Heat and cold? pleasure and pain? honour and dishonour? censure and praise? etc.? are the pairs of opposites. Only those who have destroyed ignorance and who have attained the knowledge of the Self reach the eternal goal.Adhyatmanityah Ever engaged in the contemplation of the nature of Brahman or the Supreme Being.Vinivrittakamah All the desires vanish in toto without leaving any trace or taint behind. They who have reached this stage become Yatis or Sannyasins. In the fire of wisdom all desires are burnt. As the birds fly away from a tree which has caught fire? so do desires go away from him.Tat That (the goal) described above.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The wise attain Eternity when, freed from pride and delusion, they have conquered their love for the things of sense; when, renouncing desire and fixing their gaze on the Self, they have ceased to be tossed to and fro by the opposing sensations, like pleasure and pain.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Free from pride and delusion, victorious over the evil of attachment, dwelling constantly in the Self, their desires having completely turned away, freed from the pairs of opposites known as pleasure and pain, the undeluded reach the eternal goal.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains the qualifications necessary for Self-realization. One must be free from pride (the false evaluation of one's own importance) and delusion (the inability to perceive the reality of life). The seeker must conquer attachment to the body and sense objects, turning the mind to the Self (Adhyatma-nityah). Desires must be completely renounced because they agitate the mind. By remaining unaffected by dualities like pleasure and pain, the undeluded seekers reach the imperishable state of Brahman.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Those who are rid of pride and delusion; have put down the evils of attachment; remain constantly in their own nature of the Self; have their desires completely departed; and are fully liberated from the pairs known as pleasures and pains-these undeluded men go to that changeless Abode.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Without the delusion of perverse notions (concerning the self), victorious over the evil of attachment, ever devoted to the self, turned away from desires and liberated from dualities called pleasure and pain, the undeluded go to that imperishable status.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
The wise ones who are free from pride and non-discrimination, who have conered the evil of association, [Hatred and love arising from association with foes and friends.] who are ever devoted to spirituality, completely free from desires, free from the dualities called happiness and sorrow, reach that undecaying State.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that the path to liberation requires these virtues: freedom from pride and delusion, victory over attachment, constant contemplation on the Supreme Self, complete cessation of desires, and freedom from the dualities of pleasure and pain. By these means, the undeluded Jiva reaches the eternal abode of Lord Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri states that to reach the supreme abode of Vishnu, one must possess these qualities. Pride refers to egotism, and delusion is lack of discrimination. Conquering attachment means being free from likes and dislikes even in the presence of friends or enemies. Constant devotion to the Self means being established in hearing, contemplating, and meditating on the scriptures, leading to non-dual knowledge.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Those who are free from pride and delusion, who have conquered the evil of attachment, who are constantly established in the Self, whose desires have completely turned away, and who are liberated from the dualities of pleasure and pain, those undeluded wise ones reach that imperishable goal.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that pride arises from identifying oneself with the physical body and its honors. When a devotee belongs solely to the Lord, this ego-identity vanishes. Attachment to worldly things brings various defects like possessiveness, greed, and hope. Conquering attachment removes these defects. Constant engagement in the Self means identifying oneself as an eternal part of the Lord. Desires arise from assuming connection with transient objects; when this illusion is broken, desires disappear. The Jiva is then freed from the pairs of opposites (pleasure-pain, heat-cold, etc.) by seeing every situation as the Lord's grace. Free from delusion, they realize the ever-attained supreme abode.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Thus, when they have taken refuge in Me, become free from 'perverse notions concerning the self', namely, the misconception of the non-self as the self, who have conquered the evil of attachment to enjoyments composed of the Gunas, who are constantly devoted to the contemplation of the Self, whose desires for other things have completely departed, who are freed from dualities of pleasure and pain - these undeluded ones reach that imperishable goal.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Amudhah, the wise ones, who are devoid of delusion; who are nirmana-mohah, free from (nir) pride (mana) and non-discrimination (moha); jita-sanga-dosah, who have conered (jita) the evil (dosa) of association (sanga)-association itself being the evil; those who have conered that; adhyatma-nityah, who are ever devoted to spirituality, ever engaged in reflecting on the nature of the supreme Self; engrossed in that; [Engrossed in hearing, reflecting and meditating on the Self.] vinivrtta-kamah, who are completely (vi) free from (nivrtta) desires (kamah), whose desires have completely gone away without trace (ni), the men of self-control, the monks; vimuktah, who are free from, have got rid of; dvandvaih, the dualities-likes, dislikes, etc.; sukha-duhkha-sanjnaih, called happiness and sorrow; gacchanti, reach; tat, that; avyayam, undecaying; padam, State, as has been described above.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that the Jiva attains the eternal state of Vishnu by cultivating freedom from pride and delusion, conquering attachment, meditating on the Supreme Self, becoming free from worldly desires, and rising above pleasure and pain.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that the state of non-return (Avyaya Pada) is obtained by those Jivas who have surrendered to the Lord, whose pride of doership (Kartritva-abhimana) and delusion of ownership are gone, who are devoted to the Lord's sport (Lila), and who are free from worldly dualities.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states that those who possess these qualifications reach the imperishable state of Brahman. One must be free from pride (egotism) and delusion (false identification). Attachment is the impurity of mind that leads to rebirth. Dwelling in the Self means constant contemplation of the non-dual Self. Desires must be completely burned in the fire of knowledge, and one must remain tranquil in both pleasure and pain.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the seekers who go to the eternal goal are those who are free from pride (the idea of one's own superiority) and delusion (perverted understanding). They have conquered the evil of attachment to objects. They are Adhyatmanityah, meaning they are ever devoted to the knowledge of the Supreme Self, and their desires have completely departed. Freed from the dualities of pleasure and pain, they reach the changeless state.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that those who are free from pride (egotism) and delusion (lack of discrimination) reach the imperishable state of Brahman. They have conquered the defects arising from attachment. They are always contemplating on the non-dual Self. Their desires are completely gone, and they are free from the dualities of pleasure and pain, being undeluded about the nature of the Self.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states that those who have surrendered to the Lord and are free from false identification of body with self (Nirmana-moha), who have conquered the attachment to worldly objects, who are ever engaged in meditation on the Lord (Adhyatma-nityah), whose desires for material enjoyments have ceased, and who are liberated from pleasure and pain, reach the undecaying status of liberation.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the path to the Lord's supreme status is attained by those who are free from worldly pride and delusion, who have conquered the defect of attachment, who are eternally established in the devotional service of the Lord, whose desires for material pleasure are gone, and who are free from the dualities of pleasure and pain. Being undeluded, they enter the eternal pastimes of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth states that those who are free from pride (the idea of agency) and delusion (seeing the world as real) reach the imperishable Brahman. They have conquered the evil of attachment to actions. They are established in the contemplation of the non-dual Self. Their desires for external objects have ceased, and they are free from dualities like pleasure and pain. Being undeluded, they realize their oneness with the Supreme Self.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Those who are free from false prestige, illusion and false association, who understand the eternal, who are done with material lust, who are freed from the dualities of happiness and distress, and who, unbewildered, know how to surrender unto the Supreme Person attain to that eternal kingdom.

 The surrendering process is described here very nicely. The first qualification is that one should not be deluded by pride. Because the conditioned soul is puffed up, thinking himself the lord of material nature, it is very difficult for him to surrender unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead. One should know by the cultivation of real knowledge that he is not lord of material nature; the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the Lord. When one is free from delusion caused by pride, he can begin the process of surrender. For one who is always expecting some honor in this material world, it is not possible to surrender to the Supreme Person. Pride is due to illusion, for although one comes here, stays for a brief time and then goes away, he has the foolish notion that he is the lord of the world. He thus makes all things complicated, and he is always in trouble. The whole world moves under this impression. People are considering the land, this earth, to belong to human society, and they have divided the land under the false impression that they are the proprietors. One has to get out of this false notion that human society is the proprietor of this world. When one is freed from such a false notion, he becomes free from all the false associations caused by familial, social and national affections. These faulty associations bind one to this material world. After this stage, one has to develop spiritual knowledge. One has to cultivate knowledge of what is actually his own and what is actually not his own. And when one has an understanding of things as they are, he becomes free from all dual conceptions such as happiness and distress, pleasure and pain. He becomes full in knowledge; then it is possible for him to surrender to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
