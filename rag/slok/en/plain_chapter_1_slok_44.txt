Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
utsannakuladharmāṇāṃ manuṣyāṇāṃ janārdana .
narake niyataṃ vāso bhavatītyanuśuśruma

Word Meanings:
उत्सन्नकुलधर्माणाम् - whose family customs are destroyed
मनुष्याणाम् - of men
जनार्दन - O Janardana
नरके - in hell
नियतम् - definitely
वासः - dwelling
भवति - is
इति - thus
अनुशुश्रुम - we have heard

Life Application:
Respect wisdom passed down through generations, letting established moral guidelines steer you away from reckless actions.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The wise say, my Lord, that they are forever lost, whose ancient traditions are lost.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Even after this, the Lord says nothing whatsoever. By now Arjuna's condition had become such that he could neither remain silent nor come up with any fresh argument. Yet the effect of the Lord's silence was itself quite extraordinary. In this verse Arjuna merely cites a traditional saying.

For Hindus, dharma itself is culture. Hence sufficient light has already been thrown on the importance of kuladharma. For this very reason, Arjuna once again draws attention here to the evil consequences of the destruction of kuladharma.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Since the enjoyment of the pleasures gained through attaining a kingdom would involve engaging in violence against our own kinsmen, this is exceedingly grievous for us who possess the discernment to distinguish virtue from vice -- so says Arjuna, his heart utterly broken, in 'Aho bata...'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Janardana! We have heard that those men whose kuladharmas are destroyed dwell in hells for a long time.

Explanation: 'Utsanna-kuladharmanam... anushushruma' -- the Lord has given man discernment, and the authority to perform new action. Hence a man is free, in performing or not performing an action, in doing it well or doing it poorly. Therefore he ought always to perform his prescribed duty with careful discernment. But men, falling into greed for the enjoyment of pleasures and the like, disregard their own discernment and become overpowered by attachment and aversion, as a result of which their conduct begins to run contrary to scripture and family propriety. As a consequence, they are condemned, insulted, and disgraced in this world, and in the next world they attain misfortune and hell. Because of their sins, they must endure the suffering of hell for a very long time. This is what we have heard, by tradition, from our elders and teachers.

The word 'manushyanam' (of men) here includes all the men of the family-destroyer and of his family -- that is, it includes the ancestors who came before the family-destroyers, the family-destroyer himself, and the descendants (lineage) yet to come.

Transition: what effect did this description of the chain of calamities resulting from war have on Arjuna himself? This is stated in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said -- Sanjaya said: The high-souled Partha, supremely compassionate, seeing there, stationed in both armies, his fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends -- his own kinsmen arrayed for battle -- was overcome, and spoke thus to Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Nihatya etc. upto anususruma. Sin alone is the agent in the act of slaying these desperadoes. Therefore here the idea is this : These ememies of ours have been slain, i.e., have been take possession of, by sin. Sin would come to us also after slaying them. Sin in this context is the disregard, on account of greed etc., to the injurious conseences like the ruination of the family and the like. That is why Arjuna makes a specific mention of the [ruin of the] family etc., and of its duties in the passage 'How by slaying my own kinsmen etc'.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Even the mere resolve to wage war, culminating in the killing of one's kinsmen, is in every way most sinful -- what then of the war itself -- to say this, grieving over his own resolve to act, he says: 'if this is indeed your understanding, then why did you come here fixed upon fighting?' -- this need not be said; the sense is that I acted rashly, without due deliberation.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Then he states what results, in 'Utsanna...': for those men whose kuladharmas and jatidharmas are destroyed, dwelling in hell becomes fixed, certain -- thus have we heard, from scripture and from our teachers. Since we, the doers of this war that causes the destruction of the family, are bound for hell without doubt, withdrawal from it is indeed the better course -- hinting this, and suggesting that just as people seeking protection from hell pray to You, so too I am praying to You for that same protection, he addresses Him, saying 'O Janardana.'

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Thus, since all dharmas are lost, hell becomes the destination for all -- he says this, in 'Utsanna-kuladharmanam...': O Janardana, we have heard that men whose kuladharmas are destroyed dwell in hell for certain -- 'we have heard' means: we have heard this, is the sense. By the address 'Janardana,' it is conveyed that those devoid of connection to You come to such a state on account of their connection to ignorance.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Alas, how strange it is that we are preparing to commit greatly sinful acts. Driven by the desire to enjoy royal happiness, we are intent on killing our own kinsmen.

 Driven by selfish motives, one may be inclined to such sinful acts as the killing of one's own brother, father or mother. There are many such instances in the history of the world. But Arjuna, being a saintly devotee of the Lord, is always conscious of moral principles and therefore takes care to avoid such activities.
