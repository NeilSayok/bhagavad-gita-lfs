Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
jitātmanaḥ praśāntasya paramātmā samāhitaḥ .
śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu tathā mānāpamānayoḥ

Word Meanings:
जितात्मनः - of the self-controlled
प्रशान्तस्य - of the peaceful
परमात्मा - the highest
समाहितः - remains balanced
शीतोष्णसुखदुःखेषु - in cold, heat, pleasure and pain
तथा - so
मानापमानयोः - in honour and dishonour

Life Application:
Remain serene and self-possessed in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor, centered in divine awareness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The Supreme Self of him who has conquered his lower self and is peaceful is steadfast in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Supreme Self of him who has conquered his lower self and is tranquil remains deeply steadfast in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The Supreme Self of a person who has conquered his mind and senses, and who remains tranquil, abides serenely steadfast in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "The Supreme Lord abides in Samadhi"—in the mind of the self-controlled devotee unaffected by dualities, direct meditation on Lord Vishnu remains unbroken.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Only when pairs of opposites fail to disturb the mind does the direct intuitive experience of Supreme Brahman become unshakeable in the self-controlled sage.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: For him whose mind is thoroughly serene and free from attachment, his higher Self remains in unbroken samadhi through all dualities of heat and cold.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Supreme Self of him who has conquered himself and is deeply tranquil remains perfectly steadfast amid heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor. The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with spiritual knowledge and realization, who is unwavering and master of his senses, to whom a clod of earth, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be fully integrated.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The Supreme Self of him who has conquered himself and is tranquil abides steadfast in heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor"—explains that for the serene, self-governed sage, the Supreme Brahman is experienced as ever-present and unaffected by dualities.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Enduring dualities is the hallmark of Samadhi; one remaining unshakeable in heat and cold is fit for Vishnu"s abode.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: For the devotee absorbed in Krishna-love, worldly honor and dishonor, pleasure and pain become entirely insignificant.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The Supreme Self is settled"—upon cessation of all dualities, direct intuitive knowledge of Sat-Chit-Ananda Brahman is accomplished.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the self-conquered sage abiding in unbroken absorption in the Supreme Self amid all dualities.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A seeker with a serene mind remains absorbed in meditation on Lord Vasudeva under all conditions.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the mind transcends all dualities, the constant presence of Lord Krishna is directly experienced.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "The Supreme Self is settled"—only in a mind devoid of dualities does the Supreme Self directly shine forth.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
For one who has conquered the mind, the Supersoul is already reached, for he has attained tranquility. To such a man happiness and distress, heat and cold, honor and dishonor are all the same.
