Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
viṣayendriyasaṃyogādyattadagre.amṛtopamam .
pariṇāme viṣamiva tatsukhaṃ rājasaṃ smṛtam

Word Meanings:
विषयेन्द्रियसंयोगात् - from contact of senses with objects
यत् - which
तत् - that
अग्रे - at first
अमृतोपमम् - like nectar
परिणामे - in the end
विषम् - poison
इव - like
तत् - that
सुखम् - pleasure
राजसम् - Rajasic
स्मृतम् - is declared.

Life Application:
Happiness arising from sensory contact, nectar at first, poison in end—is Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That happiness which arises from the contact of the sense-organs with their objects, which at first is like nectar, but in the end is like poison, is declared to be Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
That happiness which arises from the contact of the sense-organs with the objects, which is at first like nectar, and in the end like poison  that is declared to be Rajasic.

विषयेन्द्रियसंयोगात् from the contact of the senseorgans with the objects? यत् which? तत् that? अग्रे at first? अमृतोपमम् like nectar? परिणामे in the end? विषम् poison? इव like? तत् that? सुखम् pleasure? राजसम् Rajasic? स्मृतम् is declared.Commentary Sensual pleasure is mixed with pain? fear and sin. A small grain of sensual pleasure is mixed with a mountain of pain. He who indulges in sensual pleasures will have to experience pain also? side by side. He is afraid of losing the objects that give him pleasure. He is attached to them. Attachment is death. It brings him again and again to this world of death. Fear and attachment coexist with sensual pleasure. He has to exert a lot to get money. He can obtain the objects through money. During exertion he commits many sinful acts and he will have to suffer in hell. The next birth will be of a very low nature. He tells lies and cheats people to obtain money. The senses also lose their vigour through indulgence in sensual pleasure. He loses his strength? vigour? wealth and energy. His intellect becomes dull? weak? impure? turbid and perverted. He loses his money and proper understanding. (Cf.V.22)

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
That which as first is like nectar, because the senses revel in their objects, but in the end acts like poison - that pleasure arises from Passion.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Rajasic happiness is the exact opposite of Sattvic happiness. It is born of the direct contact between the sense-organs and their objects. Since both the objects and the senses are transient and changing, their contact cannot be permanent. Furthermore, even during enjoyment, there is anxiety and fear of losing the pleasure. Therefore, Rajasic happiness, which is momentary and leads to mental exhaustion, ultimately acts like poison.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
[The happiness]  which is like nectar at its time due to the contact between the senses and sense-objects; but which is like poison at the time of its result-that is considered to be of the Rajas (Strand).

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That pleasure which arises from the contact of senses with their objects, which is like elixir at first but like poison in the end, is said to be Rajasika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
That joy is referred to as born of rajas which, arising from the contact of the organs and (their) objects, is like nectar in the beginning, but like poison at the end.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sensory happiness is temporary and ultimately like poison because it diminishes physical strength, energy, beauty, wisdom, memory, and enthusiasm, and leads to unrighteousness and hell.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
That happiness which arises from the contact of the senses with their objects, which at first is like nectar and in the end like poison, is declared to be Rajasic.

Commentary: Sensory happiness requires no effort; it is common to all creatures. It seems like nectar in the beginning because of the intense desire to enjoy. However, as the sensory capacity is exhausted, it turns into indifference and pain. Furthermore, the memory of past wealth makes a poor person miserable. The endless loop of one desire leading to another creates a constant sense of lack, which acts like poison. Material attachment binds the soul to rebirth and can lead to hellish states if it drives one to sin. Thus, it is declared to be Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
That which at the 'beginning,' i.e., at the time of experience looks like elixir because of the contact of senses with their objects agreable to them, but 'at the end,' i.e., when satiation or further incapacity to enjoy due to over-indulgence in them occurs, looks life poison - that pleasure is said to be Rajasika. In this latter state these so-called enjoyments cause the misery of Naraka.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.36–18.39 jointly): Explains the threefold classification of happiness. Sattvika joy feels like poison initially during arduous mental discipline, but yields the nectar of liberation in the end through the tranquility of Self-knowledge. Rajasa joy stems from sense-object contact, tasting like nectar at first but ending in sorrow like poison. Tamasa joy is delusive from start to finish, born of sleep, indolence, and negligence.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Tat, that; sukham, joy; is smrtam, referred to; as rajasam, born of rajas; yat, which; visaya-indriya-samyogat, arising from the contact of the organs and (their) objects; is amrtopamam, like nectar; agre, in the beginning, in the intial moments; but iva, like; visam, poison; pariname, at the end-at the end of full enjoyment of the objects (of the senses), because it causes loss of strength, vigour, beauty, wisdom, [Prajna, the capacity to understand whatever is heard.] retentive faculty, wealth and diligence, and because it is the cause of vice and its conseent hell etc.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
The happiness which arises from the contact of senses with objects, like sight meeting beauty, which is at first like nectar but in the end like poison (painful), is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The happiness which is born of the contact of senses and objects, rather than the clarity of intellect, and which is like nectar in the beginning due to lack of effort, but is like poison in the end because of temporal and spiritual harms, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The happiness born of the contact of organs and objects, like association with women, is like nectar in the beginning, but like poison in the end because it leads to misery in this world and the next.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
The happiness arising from sensory contact, which is like nectar at the very first moment because of no need for mind-control, but is like poison in the end because it weakens the body, energy, and wisdom, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The pleasure from food, drink, and other objects is only temporary, existing while the hunger or cold lasts. In the end, it causes decay of health and leads to hell (naraka) due to sinful actions committed for its sake. This is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
The happiness born of sensory contact, free from connection to the Lord, is sweet in the beginning but acts like poison in the end because it causes forgetfulness of the Divine, which leads to spiritual death.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The happiness born of sensory contact is like nectar during the enjoyment, but like poison during separation or loss, causing misery in this world and the next.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
That happiness which is derived from contact of the senses with their objects and which appears like nectar at first but poison at the end is said to be of the nature of passion.

 A young man and a young woman meet, and the senses drive the young man to see her, to touch her and to have sexual intercourse. In the beginning this may be very pleasing to the senses, but at the end, or after some time, it becomes just like poison. They are separated or there is divorce, there is lamentation, there is sorrow, etc. Such happiness is always in the mode of passion. Happiness derived from a combination of the senses and the sense objects is always a cause of distress and should be avoided by all means.
