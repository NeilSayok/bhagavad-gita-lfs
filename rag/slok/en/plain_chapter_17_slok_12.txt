Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
abhisandhāya tu phalaṃ dambhārthamapi caiva yat .
ijyate bharataśreṣṭha taṃ yajñaṃ viddhi rājasam

Word Meanings:
अभिसंधाय - seeking for
तु - indeed
फलम् - fruit
दम्भार्थम् - for ostentation
अपि - also
च - and
एव - even
यत् - which
इज्यते - is offered
भरतश्रेष्ठ - O best of the Bharatas
तम् - that
यज्ञम् - sacrifice
विद्धि - know
राजसम् - Rajasic.

Life Application:
Sacrifice offered seeking fruit or out of ostentation—know that to be Rajasic sacrifice.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
But the sacrifice which is offered, O best of the Bharatas, seeking fruit (reward) and also for the sake of ostentation, know that sacrifice to be Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The sacrifice which is offered, O Arjuna, seeking a reward and for ostentation, know thou that to be a Rajasic Yajna.

अभिसंधाय seeking for? तु indeed? फलम् fruit? दम्भार्थम् for ostentation? अपि also? च and? एव even? यत् which? इज्यते is offered? भरतश्रेष्ठ O best of the Bharatas? तम् that? यज्ञम् sacrifice? विद्धि know? राजसम् Rajasic.Commentary If anyone performs a sacrifice in order to obtain? heaven? son? wealth? or name and fame? then it is a sacrifice of a Rajasic nature. The performer of this kind of sacrifice has the motive of increasing his own importance? for popularising his own name in the world? for gaining some reward? for showing himself off as a great? pious and learned man? for making an exhibition of his riches for his own glorificaion. He has no aspiration for attaining the knowledge of the Self.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Sacrifice which is performed for the sake of its results, or for self-glorification - that, O best of Aryans, is the product of Passion.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Desire is the core characteristic of Rajas. Rajasic people perform sacrifices driven by desire for fruits. They are constantly worried about the outcome. Some perform sacrifices merely to show off their wealth or knowledge. Such ostentatious actions are Rajasic and do not bring peace of mind.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
What is offered aiming at fruit and also only for the sake of display-know that sacrifice to be of the Rajas (Strand) and to be transitary and  impermanent.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
But that sacrifice which is offered with the fruit in view and for the sake of ostentation, know it, O Arjuna, to be Rajasika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
But that sacrifice which is performed having in veiw a result, as also for ostentation,-know that sacrifice to be done through rajas, O greatest among the descendants of Bharata.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that the sacrifice offered with the aim of attaining heaven, etc., and to publicize one's religiosity, is Rajasic in nature and should be abandoned.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
But know that sacrifice, O best of Bharatas, which is performed seeking reward or for ostentation, to be Rajasic.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that performing sacrifice to gain desired objects (like wealth, progeny, health, or heaven) or to avoid undesired situations (like disease, enemies, or insult) is Rajasic. Similarly, performing rituals so that others look upon one as pious, virtuous, and charitable is ostentatious (Dambha) and therefore Rajasic. In such sacrifices, scriptural methods are followed mainly to avoid the failure of the fruit, but there is no real faith.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
That sacrifice, performed to gain fruits, full of ostentation and with fame as its aim, know that sacrifice to be characterised by Rajas.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.11–17.13 jointly): Sri Abhinavagupta explains that a sacrifice performed contrary to scriptural injunctions, devoid of proper rituals, where no food is distributed, and which is done without stabilizing the mind in faith, is declared Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that 'phala' refers to desired rewards like heaven, and 'dambha' refers to displaying one's piety. A sacrifice performed seeking such fruits, or for the sake of show without desiring spiritual purification, is Rajasic. The address 'best of Bharatas' implies Arjuna is qualified for Sattvic sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that a Rajasic sacrifice is one performed with the objective of achieving results like heaven, or for ostentation (Dambha) to show off one's greatness.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that a Rajasic sacrifice is performed for attaining heaven, etc., and for showcasing one's religious standing in the world. Arjuna is addressed as 'best of Bharatas' to indicate he is above such lower motivations.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that a Rajasic sacrifice is done with expectation of results and characterized by pride, aiming at securing fame. A purely ostentatious sacrifice without any rule is Tamasic, but a Rajasic one has fruit as its main focus. 'Best of Bharatas' denotes Arjuna's fitness for the higher Sattvic sacrifices.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that a Rajasic sacrifice is performed seeking worldly or heavenly fruits (like heaven) and for ostentation (Dambha) to gain reputation in the world. Performers of such sacrifices are Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that a Rajasic sacrifice is one performed with desire for rewards and for the sake of public reputation, showing off one's religious activities.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
But the sacrifice performed for some material benefit, or for the sake of pride, O chief of the Bhāratas, you should know to be in the mode of passion.

 Sometimes sacrifices and rituals are performed for elevation to the heavenly kingdom or for some material benefits in this world. Such sacrifices or ritualistic performances are considered to be in the mode of passion.
