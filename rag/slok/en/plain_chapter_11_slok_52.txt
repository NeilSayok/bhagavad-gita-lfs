Life Application:
Realize how rare and precious it is to behold this gentle divine form which even celestial beings long to see.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Sri Bhagavan said — The vision of this form of Mine which you have seen is extremely rare to obtain. Even the gods are ever eager to behold this form.

Commentary — 'sudurdarśamidaṁ rūpaṁ dṛṣṭavānasi yanmama' — Here, the term 'sudurdarśam' (very difficult to see) refers only to the four-armed form, not to the Cosmic Form (virat-rupa) or the two-armed human form. Because why would the gods even imagine the Cosmic Form? And since the human form was easily accessible to humans, how could it be rare for the gods? Therefore, by 'sudurdarśam', we must understand Lord Vishnu's four-armed form, for which the terms 'deva-rupam' (11.45) and 'svakaṁ rūpam' (11.50) have been used.

'devā apyasya rūpasya nityaṁ darśanakāṅkṣiṇaḥ' — The Lord has said here that the vision of this four-armed form of Mine is extremely rare. In the subsequent fifty-third and fifty-fourth verses, it is stated that the vision of this four-armed form cannot be attained through means like the Vedas, sacrifices, austerities, charity, etc., but rather it can only be attained through unswerving devotion (ananya-bhakti). Now a doubt arises here: if the gods also constantly desire (crave) the vision of this form, then why do they not obtain this vision, since a constant desire for the Lord's vision is indeed unswerving devotion? The solution to this is that in reality, the constant desire of the gods is not unswerving devotion (ananya-bhakti).

To have a constant desire means that one continuously yearns only for the Supreme Lord and has no other desires. A person with such a desire, even if he were the most wicked, becomes a devotee of the Lord and attains Him. However, the gods do not possess such unswerving desire; because they have generally become gods to enjoy pleasures, and their purpose is mostly the enjoyment of pleasures. Then what kind of desire do they have? It is the kind of desire (wish) that almost all believers have — that they may get a vision of the Lord and be liberated. They do have such a wish, but their interest in enjoyment and accumulation remains as it is. That is to say, just as someone walking on a path might happen to find a gem, similarly, if we get liberated (as a secondary matter), it would be nice — just as the desire for liberation is secondary in ordinary men, similarly, if the Lord reveals Himself, we too will behold Him — in this way, the desire for vision in the gods is secondary.

The gods merely make a superficial wish: 'We are in such a high position, our worlds, bodies, and enjoyments are divine, we are very virtuous; hence, we should have the vision of the Lord' — therefore they will never obtain the vision. The reason is that they possess pride in their godhood, status, etc. The vision of the Lord cannot be attained through pride or by the power of status, etc. Therefore, Arjuna said in the fourteenth verse of the tenth chapter: 'O Lord, neither the gods nor the demons know Your manifestation.' In this way, Arjuna placed the gods and the demons in the same category regarding their ignorance of the Lord. The meaning of this is that just as the gods possess opulence, similarly, the demons possess various illusions and mystic powers, but they cannot know the Lord by their strength. In the same way, even if the gods desire the vision of the Lord, they cannot obtain it by their power of godhood; because godhood is not the cause for the vision of the Lord. The point is that the Lord can neither be seen by the power of godhood, nor can He be seen by auspicious deeds like sacrifices, penance, charity, etc. (11.53). Both gods and humans can see the Lord only through unswerving devotion (ananya-bhakti). The phrase 'devā api' (even the gods) means that by the strength of those virtues due to which the gods obtained a high position and divine enjoyments, they cannot see the Lord. The implication is that virtuous deeds can grant higher worlds and divine enjoyments, but they do not have the power to reveal the vision of the Lord. In the vision of the Lord, this material importance has no value at all.

Connection — In the next verse, the Lord reinforces the statement made in the preceding verse.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Under these circumstances, while describing the extreme difficulty of seeing His form, the Lord, in three verses (beginning with 'sudurdarsam'), concludes that He can be attained only through grace and devotion. He says: This imperishable Cosmic Form of Mine which you have seen, since I am extremely difficult to behold, then what to say of this form of Mine! That is why He says, 'devah api' (even the gods). Even though they are satvika, they always desire a vision, but they have not seen it due to the absence of the seed of grace.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Showing the extreme rarity of the grace He has bestowed, the Blessed Lord said in four verses (beginning with 'sudurdarsam'): This Cosmic Form of Mine which you have just seen is extremely difficult to see, absolutely impossible to behold. The idea is that even the gods are always desirous of a vision of this form, yet they have neither seen it before as you have, nor will they see it in the future. The word 'nityam' (always) is used in relation to their desire for the vision.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Showing the extreme rarity of the grace He has bestowed, the Blessed Lord said: This Cosmic Form of Mine which you have seen is extremely difficult to see, completely impossible to behold. Therefore, even the gods always desire a vision of this form, not that they actually see it.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Hearing this, in order to prevent any sense of futility regarding the extremely rare grace He has bestowed (i.e., to show its great value), the Blessed Lord spoke. This form of Mine which you have seen by My grace is 'sudurdarsam' for others, meaning that which is beheld only with extreme difficulty. With the intention that even the highly exalted, satvika gods like Indra and others, who long for this vision, have neither seen it as you have nor will they see it, the Lord says 'devah' (the gods).

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Since the word 'yat' (which) refers to something well-known, [the commentator] describes its well-known attributes as 'established in the administration of all', etc. Thinking it inappropriate to connect 'nityam' (always) with only a part of the term (seeing), and implying that the constancy refers to their desire while there is a complete absence of vision, [Ramanuja] states 'but they have not seen it'.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Stating the extreme rarity of His form, which can be attained solely by His own grace, the Blessed Lord said: This visible form of Mine which you have seen is 'sudurdarsam', meaning impossible to behold even with great difficulty. Even the gods, who are fit for My divine play and are My own parts, remain desirous of seeing it every day. The inner meaning here is: when Brahma and other gods departed after offering prayers at the house of Sri Devaki, the Lord's manifestation was only within the womb, not outside. After manifesting outside, upon the mother's request, He concealed that form and established it as an object of meditation. Since the gods were unaware of that event and worshipped Him according to Vedic rituals, they only obtained the vision of that meditative form. And this form is full of divine emotion, inaccessible through the Vedas, etc., and having heard about it from the mouths of devotees, they remain longing for it.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Showing the rarity of the vision of this Cosmic Form, the Blessed Lord said: 'sudurdarsam...'. 'darśanakāṅkṣiṇaḥ' means that they only desire the vision, but they do not obtain it.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: My dear Arjuna, this form of Mine you are now seeing is very difficult to behold. Even the demigods are ever seeking the opportunity to see this form, which is so dear.

 In the forty-eighth verse of this chapter Lord Kṛṣṇa concluded revealing His universal form and informed Arjuna that this form is not possible to be seen by so many pious activities, sacrifices, etc. Now here the word su-durdarśam is used, indicating that Kṛṣṇa’s two-handed form is still more confidential. One may be able to see the universal form of Kṛṣṇa by adding a little tinge of devotional service to various activities like penances, Vedic study and philosophical speculation. It may be possible, but without a tinge of bhakti one cannot see; that has already been explained. Still, beyond that universal form, the form of Kṛṣṇa with two hands is still more difficult to see, even for demigods like Brahmā and Lord Śiva. They desire to see Him, and we have evidence in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that when He was supposed to be in the womb of His mother, Devakī, all the demigods from heaven came to see the marvel of Kṛṣṇa, and they offered nice prayers to the Lord, although He was not at that time visible to them. They waited to see Him. A foolish person may deride Him, thinking Him an ordinary person, and may offer respect not to Him but to the impersonal “something” within Him, but these are all nonsensical postures. Kṛṣṇa in His two-armed form is actually desired to be seen by demigods like Brahmā and Śiva. In Bhagavad-gītā (9.11) it is also confirmed, avajānanti māṁ mūḍhā mānuṣīṁ tanum āśritam: He is not visible to the foolish persons who deride Him. Kṛṣṇa’s body, as confirmed by Brahma-saṁhitā and confirmed by Kṛṣṇa Himself in Bhagavad-gītā , is completely spiritual and full of bliss and eternality. His body is never like a material body. But for some who make a study of Kṛṣṇa by reading Bhagavad-gītā or similar Vedic scriptures, Kṛṣṇa is a problem. For one using a material process, Kṛṣṇa is considered to be a great historical personality and very learned philosopher, but He is an ordinary man, and even though He was so powerful He had to accept a material body. Ultimately they think that the Absolute Truth is impersonal; therefore they think that from His impersonal feature He assumed a personal feature attached to material nature. This is a materialistic calculation of the Supreme Lord. Another calculation is speculative. Those who are in search of knowledge also speculate on Kṛṣṇa and consider Him to be less important than the universal form of the Supreme. Thus some think that the universal form of Kṛṣṇa which was manifested to Arjuna is more important than His personal form. According to them, the personal form of the Supreme is something imaginary. They believe that in the ultimate issue, the Absolute Truth is not a person. But the transcendental process is described in Bhagavad-gītā , Chapter Four: to hear about Kṛṣṇa from authorities. That is the actual Vedic process, and those who are actually in the Vedic line hear about Kṛṣṇa from authority, and by repeated hearing about Him, Kṛṣṇa becomes dear. As we have several times discussed, Kṛṣṇa is covered by His yoga-māyā potency. He is not to be seen or revealed to anyone and everyone. Only by one to whom He reveals Himself can He be seen. This is confirmed in the Vedic literature; for one who is a surrendered soul, the Absolute Truth can actually be understood. The transcendentalist, by continuous Kṛṣṇa consciousness and by devotional service to Kṛṣṇa, can have his spiritual eyes opened and can see Kṛṣṇa by revelation. Such a revelation is not possible even for the demigods; therefore it is difficult even for the demigods to understand Kṛṣṇa, and the advanced demigods are always in hope of seeing Kṛṣṇa in His two-handed form. The conclusion is that although to see the universal form of Kṛṣṇa is very, very difficult and not possible for anyone and everyone, it is still more difficult to understand His personal form as Śyāmasundara.
