Life Application:
Inquire whether those who worship God in a personal form or those who contemplate the unmanifest are more versed in yoga.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Although the philosophical discourses of the Bhagavad Gita are in dialogue style, the logical flow of thoughts is never ignored. There is consistency not only within each chapter, but also between chapters. The previous chapter ended with the assurance that single-minded devotion allows one to experience the Lord's cosmic glory directly. This inspired Arjuna to ask a practical question: which form of the Supreme Self should he worship? Seekers are generally of two types, both striving for the same goal: some worship the manifest, personal (Saguna, Sakara) form, while others meditate on the unmanifest, impersonal (Nirguna, Nirakara) form. Both are sincere and progress on their paths. Arjuna asks who among them is more established in Yoga. In philosophy, what is perceptible to the senses is manifest (Vyakta), and what is not is unmanifest (Avyakta). Arjuna had been taught that Brahman is unmanifest and all-pervading, but having just seen the cosmic form, he asks which seeker is superior. This debate of Saguna versus Nirguna remains active today. Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Salutations to the Lord who loves worship! In this chapter, the superiority of the worship of the personal Lord over the worship of the Unmanifest (Avyakta/Prakriti/Sri) and its means are shown. The Vedas also speak of the worship of the Unmanifest as a means of liberation (e.g., Rig Veda 7.4.4.4, Katha Upanishad 3.15). Although knowing, Arjuna asks to obtain the knowledge of subtle reasoning: which of the two—those who worship You in this manner (evam) and those who worship the Imperishable-Unmanifest—are superior. The Unmanifest is Prakriti, and it is Akshara. The Supreme Brahman is not different from the Lord, and His personal form is the primary object of meditation. Descriptions of His solar form, etc., are not merely figurative. Thus, the worshippers of the Lord are superior.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
From the second to the tenth chapter, the knowledge of the unconditioned Brahman was expounded, and meditation on the conditioned Brahman was also taught for the grace of dull and average seekers. At the end of the eleventh chapter (11.55), devotion to the personal Lord was instructed. To determine the comparison between these paths based on the qualifications of seekers, Arjuna asks: who among those who take refuge in You and worship You, and those who worship the attributeless, imperishable Unmanifest Brahman, is the best knower of Yoga?

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna asked: Those devotees who, ever steadfast, thus worship You (the personal God), and those who worship the imperishable, the unmanifested—which of them are the best knowers of Yoga?

Commentary: The words 'ye bhaktah' refer to the personal worshippers described in 11.55. 'Satatayukta' means to live with the constant attitude that 'I belong to God alone' in all spiritual and worldly actions. A seeker should understand that if his purpose is sole God-realization, all his actions become a means to it. 'Tvam paryupasate' refers to the worship of all personal forms of the Lord. 'Ye capyaksaram avyaktam' refers to the worshippers of the Nirguna (attribute-free) Brahman, which is beyond the senses. Arjuna asks who among these two is superior. The Lord's detailed response from chapter 12 to 14 highlights the importance of this question, which brings out the confidential and supreme truths of spiritual realization.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said  These are two types of spiritual aspirants who are contrasted thus:  (1) 

On the one hand there are those devotees who adore You 'thus'; namely, in the way taught in such text as 'Whosoever works for Me' (11.55), and who are  desirous of being ever 'integrated' with You, namely, considering You as the supreme end. They adore You in utter devotion - You, the ocean of boundless attributes of limitless excellence like grace, affability, omniscience, true resolve etc., and endowed with all glory. (2) On the other hand there are those who meditate on the 'Imperishable', (Aksara) namely, the individual self in Its true nature, which is the same as the 'Unmanifest' (Avyakta), namely that whose nature cannot be grasped by organs such as the eye etc. The estion posed is:  Which of these two classes of devotees have greater knowledge of Yoga?  Who would reach their respective goals sooner?  Such is the meaning of the estion. Sri Krsna clearly states later on, 'O Arjuna, I become before long their redeemer from the fatal sea of recurring births and deaths' (12.7), with reference to the speed with which the latter kind of devotees reach Him.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
This chapter establishes the superiority of the worship of Lord Vishnu over the worship of the Unmanifest (Avyakta/Sri). The Vedas also accept the worship of the Unmanifest as a means of liberation, which causes doubt. Arjuna, though knowing, asks to clarify this and understand the subtle reasoning: who among the worshippers of You in the personal form (evam) and those of the Imperishable-Unmanifest is superior. 'Avyakta' here refers to Prakriti and 'Akshara' is its adjective. Both refer to the Lord, but the worship of the personal form is the true and direct means of liberation. Formlessness means the absence of material form.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Bhakti is the object of love (prameya) and the object is Purushottama (Hari); hence in the path of grace, His service alone is proper. In previous chapters, because the Imperishable was called the Lord's abode, a distinction seemed to appear. To know the difference between their worshippers, Arjuna asks: who among those who worship Your personal, blissful form associated with Shri, Bhu, Lila powers, and those who worship the Imperishable-Unmanifest, is the 'yuktatama' (most perfect) in Your opinion?

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
In previous chapters, the worship of the formless, imperishable Brahman as well as the personal God was taught. After the Cosmic vision, the verse 'he who works for Me...' was stated. To know which of the two—unconditioned (Nirvishesha) or conditioned (Savishesha)—worship is superior, Arjuna asks: who among those devoted to Your personal form (evam), and those who renounce all actions and worship the imperishable-unmanifest Brahman, is the superior knower of Yoga? The word 'evam' cannot relate to the latter part as they are renouncers of action.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The display of the Cosmic form in Bhakti Yoga is to show the ease of devotion and the speed of its fruit. In this chapter, the question and answer compare the two types of worshippers. Arjuna asks: who among those devoted to Your personal form (evam) who are the ocean of infinite auspicious attributes, and those who worship the 'Akshara' (the true nature of the individual self) which is 'Avyakta', is the better knower of Yoga (meaning, who will reach the goal faster)? In response, the Lord clarifies the superiority of the Saguna worshippers by promising their swift redemption.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Arjuna inquired: Which are considered to be more perfect, those who are always properly engaged in Your devotional service or those who worship the impersonal Brahman, the unmanifested?

 Kṛṣṇa has now explained about the personal, the impersonal and the universal and has described all kinds of devotees and yogīs . Generally, the transcendentalists can be divided into two classes. One is the impersonalist, and the other is the personalist. The personalist devotee engages himself with all energy in the service of the Supreme Lord. The impersonalist also engages himself, not directly in the service of Kṛṣṇa but in meditation on the impersonal Brahman, the unmanifested. We find in this chapter that of the different processes for realization of the Absolute Truth, bhakti-yoga, devotional service, is the highest. If one at all desires to have the association of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, then he must take to devotional service. Those who worship the Supreme Lord directly by devotional service are called personalists. Those who engage themselves in meditation on the impersonal Brahman are called impersonalists. Arjuna is here questioning which position is better. There are different ways to realize the Absolute Truth, but Kṛṣṇa indicates in this chapter that bhakti-yoga, or devotional service to Him, is the highest of all. It is the most direct, and it is the easiest means for association with the Godhead. In the Second Chapter of Bhagavad-gītā , the Supreme Lord explained that a living entity is not the material body; he is a spiritual spark. And the Absolute Truth is the spiritual whole. In the Seventh Chapter He spoke of the living entity as being part and parcel of the supreme whole and recommended that he transfer his attention fully to the whole. Then again in the Eighth Chapter it was said that anyone who thinks of Kṛṣṇa at the time of quitting his body is at once transferred to the spiritual sky, to the abode of Kṛṣṇa. And at the end of the Sixth Chapter the Lord clearly said that of all yogīs, one who always thinks of Kṛṣṇa within himself is considered the most perfect. So in practically every chapter the conclusion has been that one should be attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa, for that is the highest spiritual realization. Nevertheless, there are those who are not attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa. They are so firmly detached that even in the preparation of commentaries to Bhagavad-gītā they want to distract other people from Kṛṣṇa and transfer all devotion to the impersonal brahma-jyotir . They prefer to meditate on the impersonal form of the Absolute Truth, which is beyond the reach of the senses and is not manifest. And so, factually, there are two classes of transcendentalists. Now Arjuna is trying to settle the question of which process is easier and which of the classes is most perfect. In other words, he is clarifying his own position because he is attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa. He is not attached to the impersonal Brahman. He wants to know whether his position is secure. The impersonal manifestation, either in this material world or in the spiritual world of the Supreme Lord, is a problem for meditation. Actually, one cannot perfectly conceive of the impersonal feature of the Absolute Truth. Therefore Arjuna wants to say, “What is the use of such a waste of time?” Arjuna experienced in the Eleventh Chapter that to be attached to the personal form of Kṛṣṇa is best because he could thus understand all other forms at the same time and there was no disturbance to his love for Kṛṣṇa. This important question asked of Kṛṣṇa by Arjuna will clarify the distinction between the impersonal and personal conceptions of the Absolute Truth.
