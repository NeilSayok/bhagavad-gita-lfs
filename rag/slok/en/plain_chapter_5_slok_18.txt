Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
vidyāvinayasampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini .
śuni caiva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ samadarśinaḥ

Word Meanings:
विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने - endowed with learning and humility
ब्राह्मणे - in a brahmana
गवि - in a cow
हस्तिनि - in an elephant
शुनि - in a dog
चैव - and also
श्वपाके - in an outcaste
च - and
पण्डिताः - the wise
समदर्शिनः - see with equal vision

Life Application:
Treat all living beings with equal respect and compassion today, looking past external appearances to the shared spirit within.

Sages look with an equal eye on a Brahmin endowed with learning and humility, on a cow, on an elephant, and even on a dog and an outcast.

The enlightened sages, beholding the self-same Supreme Brahman everywhere, look with an equal eye (Samadarshi) upon a learned and humble Brahmana, a holy cow, a majestic elephant, an unclean dog, and an outcaste.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "The wise look with equal eye"—sages who behold Lord Vishnu indwelling all living entities as the Prototype (Bimba) possess true equal vision.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: True sages, seeing beyond the veils of gunas, behold the single non-dual Brahman with equal vision in a Brahmin, cow, elephant, dog, or outcaste.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Sages look with an equal eye on a Brahmin endowed with learning and humility, on a cow, on an elephant, and even on a dog and an outcast.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Sages ignore bodily differences of caste or species, beholding the essential equality of all souls as pure knowledge in nature.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: So, regarding a Brahmana endowed with learning and humility, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcast, the wise look with an equal eye.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Sages look with an equal eye on a Brahmin endowed with learning and humility, on a cow, on an elephant, and even on a dog and an outcast"—illustrates *Samadarshitva* where the wise perceive the self-same Brahman in all bodies irrespective of Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic adjuncts.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Equal vision does not mean ignoring physical distinctions, but recognizing the one indwelling Vishnu in all creatures.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A devotee seeing his Beloved Krishna in all living beings naturally develops equal vision toward all.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The wise possess equal vision"—a true scholar sublates all guna-based inequalities to focus on the non-dual substratum, Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how enlightened sages perceive the one undivided Brahman in diverse outer forms.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A practitioner of Vasudeva-worship disregards physical differences to behold the eternal equality of all souls.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: For an unswerving devotee of Krishna, the entire moving and non-moving universe becomes a divine manifestation of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Sages possess equal vision"—beholding the single Consciousness amidst multi-hued guna-born forms is true scholarship.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The humble sages, by virtue of true knowledge, see with equal vision a learned and gentle brahmana, a cow, an elephant, a dog and a dog-eater [outcaste].
