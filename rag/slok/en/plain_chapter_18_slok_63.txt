Life Application:
Thus has wisdom more secret than secret been declared to you by Me; reflect on it fully and act as you choose.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
This verse can be considered the final verse of the Gita discourse given on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. In Sanskrit, the word 'iti' denotes the completion of a statement or quotation. From this perspective, Lord Krishna completes His teaching here. 'More secret than all secrets' (guhyat guhyataram): A secret is that which is unknown to most people, but known to a select few. After presenting all the principles, facts, and paths of life to Arjuna, Lord Krishna invites him to make an intelligent decision after reflecting on it. Spiritual teachers should not force their disciples in any way. In India, religion has never been propagated by compulsion. Lord Krishna continues in the next verses.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Thus has wisdom, which is more secret than all secrets, been declared to you by Me. Reflecting on this fully, do as you like.

Commentary: The Lord has explained all paths—Karma, Jnana, and Bhakti. He calls this teaching 'more secret than all secrets' because it reveals the deepest truth of life, which is otherwise hidden. The Lord does not impose His will on Arjuna; instead, He gives him the freedom of choice ('do as you like') after he has analyzed the entire teaching. This shows that the Lord values human intellect and free will. True surrender is not blind obedience, but an act of conscious choice and intelligence.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Concluding the teaching of the Gita, the Lord says: 'iti...'. The wisdom taught by the Lord on the battlefield is the essence of all Vedanta conclusions. Therefore, reflect on it fully and then act as you wish.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Concluding the essence of the Gita, the Lord says: 'iti...'. By Me, the omniscient and the supreme well-wisher, this wisdom which is the means to liberation and pertains to the Self, has been declared to you, who are extremely dear to Me. Reflect on this Gita scripture fully, recognizing its overall unity, and then act according to your eligibility and not whim. The path involves performing duties without desire for results to purify the heart, then taking up sannyasa to study Vedanta under a guru for self-realization, leading to liberation. For Kshatriyas who are not eligible for sannyasa, acting under the Lord's command for the welfare of the world, with sole shelter in Him, leads to the dawn of knowledge either in this life by His grace or in a future life as a Brahmana after sannyasa.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Intending to conclude the scripture, the Lord says: 'iti...'. Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Concluding the three paths of Karma, Jnana, and Bhakti, the Lord says: 'iti...'. By Me, the omniscient and the supreme benefactor, this wisdom has been declared to you, who surrendered to Me as a disciple. Here, 'guhyam' refers to Vedic knowledge about celestial rewards, and 'guhyataram' refers to the Upanishadic knowledge about the means to liberation. Reflect on this entire scripture fully according to your capability, and then act as you wish. The statement 'act as you wish' does not mean you can choose not to fight, because the duty of a Kshatriya is unavoidable. Rather, it means you should reflect on which path (Karma, Jnana, or Bhakti) you are qualified for at present, and perform your duty in that stage.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Concluding the entire Gita, the Lord says: 'iti...'. In this manner, this wisdom, which is the essence of all scriptures, more secret than even the secret mantra seeds, has been declared to you by Me, the creator and soul of all. Reflect on this Gita scripture fully, considering the connection between the preceding and succeeding verses, and then act as you wish. The idea is that upon reflecting on this, your intellect will naturally incline towards obeying My command.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Thus I have explained to you knowledge still more confidential. Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do.

 The Lord has already explained to Arjuna the knowledge of brahma-bhūta. One who is in the brahma-bhūta condition is joyful; he never laments, nor does he desire anything. That is due to confidential knowledge. Kṛṣṇa also discloses knowledge of the Supersoul. This is also Brahman knowledge, knowledge of Brahman, but it is superior. Here the words yathecchasi tathā kuru – “As you like, you may act” – indicate that God does not interfere with the little independence of the living entity. In Bhagavad-gītā , the Lord has explained in all respects how one can elevate his living condition. The best advice imparted to Arjuna is to surrender unto the Supersoul seated within his heart. By right discrimination, one should agree to act according to the order of the Supersoul. That will help one become situated constantly in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the highest perfectional stage of human life. Arjuna is being directly ordered by the Personality of Godhead to fight. Surrender to the Supreme Personality of Godhead is in the best interest of the living entities. It is not for the interest of the Supreme. Before surrendering, one is free to deliberate on this subject as far as the intelligence goes; that is the best way to accept the instruction of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Such instruction comes also through the spiritual master, the bona fide representative of Kṛṣṇa.
