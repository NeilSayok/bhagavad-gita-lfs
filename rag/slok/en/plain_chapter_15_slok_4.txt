Seek that goal from which none return, surrendering to the primeval person from whom ancient activity streamed.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Beyond lies the Path, from which, when found, there is no return. This is the Primal God from whence this ancient creation has sprung.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Then That goal should be sought for, whither having gone none returns again; and I seek refuge in that Primeval Purusha Whence streamed forth the ancient activity or energy.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that the seeker must withdraw attention from the body, mind, and intellect, and redirect it toward the Supreme Brahman, the source of all. This state of detachment from the world and attachment to the Divine is the 'sword of detachment'. The practical way to achieve this is total surrender and devotion (Sharanagati) to the Primal Person (Adya Purusha), from whom this ancient projection of the universe began. Surrender purifies the ego and merges the individual back into the Supreme Source.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Then that Abode must be sought, having reached Which one would not return.  [The Yogin] would attain nothing but that Primal Person from Whom the old activity (world creation) commences.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Then, one should seek that goal attaining which one never returns. One should seek refuge with that Primal Person from whom streamed forth this ancient activity.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Thereafter, that State has to be sought for, going where they do not return again: I take refuge in that Primeval Person Himself, from whom has ensued the eternal Manifestation.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that one should take refuge in Lord Vishnu, who is the Primeval Person, to attain liberation. Seeking refuge in Him is the only remedy for cutting the bonds of Samsara. By taking refuge in Him, one overcomes all grief and death, and achieves the state of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that after cutting down the tree of Samsara, one must seek the supreme state of Brahman which is beyond the tree. This is achieved by taking refuge in the Primeval Purusha, who is all-pervading and the source of the entire universe, just as magic tricks emanate from a magician.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Thereafter, that state should be sought after, going where they do not return again. I seek refuge in that Primeval Purusha from whom streamed forth this ancient activity.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that to search for the Supreme state (Paramapada), one must first cut off attachment to the transient world. The Lord is ever-attained (Nitya-prapta), but our attachment to the world hides this reality. Seeking the Lord does not mean finding something absent, but turning away from the unreal and taking shelter in the real. Surrender (Sharanagati) is the key. A devotee must feel 'I belong to the Lord, and the Lord belongs to me.' By this absolute surrender, the divine love is awakened, which frees the Jiva from the cycle of birth and death forever.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The form of this tree... is not understood by people immersed in Samsara... One should seek refuge in the Primal Person alone in order to overcome this ignorance. One should seek refuge in Him who is primal, namely, the beginning of all entities... Surrendering to Me is the only way to cross over My Guna-composed Maya.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that one must seek refuge in Lord Vishnu, who is the Primeval Person, to attain the supreme state. Surrendering to Him is the source of all spiritual activity and knowledge that dismantles ignorance and attachment to the Gunas.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that to seek the supreme abode, one must completely surrender to the Primal Person, who is the source of all creation and sport (Lila). The Jiva must renounce its self-made ego-world and recognize that all energy and activity flow from the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that after cutting the tree of Samsara, the seeker must seek the Supreme Brahman, realizing 'I am that Brahman.' One must take refuge in the Primal Person, Vishnu, who is the source of the ancient illusion of Samsara. By His grace alone, ignorance is destroyed, and the seeker attains the state of non-return.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the state of Vishnu, whither having gone none returns, should be sought. The way to find it is to surrender to the Primal Person, who is the Lord Himself, from whom this beginningless flow of Samsara has emerged like magic from a magician. By His grace, one crosses the ocean of worldly life.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that after cutting down the tree of Samsara, one must seek the state of the Supreme Self. The weapon of detachment leads to the surrender to the Primal Person, who is the cause of all projection. By surrendering to Him, the beginningless ignorance is removed, and one realizes the non-dual Self.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the goal to be sought is the Supreme Abode of Vishnu. The means is surrendering to the Primal Person (Prasada-prapatti). The ancient path of seekers has always been to take refuge in the Lord, which destroys all past sins and cuts the root of Samsara.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the seeker must search for that supreme place of the Lord. The Jiva should take refuge in the Primeval Purusha, Purushottama, who is the source of all divine play. Through devotion and surrender, the devotee becomes eligible to participate in His eternal pastimes.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that after discarding the false appearance of the world, the non-dual reality of Brahman should be sought. The seeker surrenders to the Primal Purusha, who is the substratum of all projections. This surrender is the realization of identity with Him, which dissolves the beginningless ignorance.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Thereafter, one must seek that place from which, having gone, one never returns, and there surrender to that Supreme Personality of Godhead from whom everything has begun and in whom everything is abiding since time immemorial.

 It is now clearly stated that the real form of this banyan tree cannot be understood in this material world. Since the root is upwards, the extension of the real tree is at the other end. When entangled with the material expansions of the tree, one cannot see how far the tree extends, nor can one see the beginning of this tree. Yet one has to find out the cause. “I am the son of my father, my father is the son of such-and-such a person, etc.” By searching in this way, one comes to Brahmā, who is generated by the Garbhodaka-śāyī Viṣṇu. Finally, in this way, when one reaches the Supreme Personality of Godhead, that is the end of research work. One has to search out that origin of this tree, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, through the association of persons who are in knowledge of that Supreme Personality of Godhead. Then by understanding one becomes gradually detached from this false reflection of reality, and by knowledge one can cut off the connection and actually become situated in the real tree. The word asaṅga is very important in this connection because the attachment for sense enjoyment and lording it over the material nature is very strong. Therefore one must learn detachment by discussion of spiritual science based on authoritative scriptures, and one must hear from persons who are actually in knowledge. As a result of such discussion in the association of devotees, one comes to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Then the first thing one must do is surrender to Him. The description of that place whence having gone one never returns to this false reflected tree is given here. The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is the original root from whom everything has emanated. To gain the favor of that Personality of Godhead, one has only to surrender, and this is a result of performing devotional service by hearing, chanting, etc. He is the cause of the extension of the material world. This has already been explained by the Lord Himself. Ahaṁ sarvasya prabhavaḥ: “I am the origin of everything.” Therefore to get out of the entanglement of this strong banyan tree of material life, one must surrender to Kṛṣṇa. As soon as one surrenders unto Kṛṣṇa, one becomes detached automatically from this material extension.
