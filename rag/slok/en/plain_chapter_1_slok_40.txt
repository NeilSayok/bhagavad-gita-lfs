Life Application:
Protect the positive traditions and ethical foundations of your community, ensuring they are passed on intact.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Just as a storyteller adds some new elements each time he narrates an old tale, so too Arjuna's inventive mind keeps producing fresh arguments to bolster his mistaken conviction. No sooner does he finish one argument than another new one occurs to him, behind which he seeks to hide his own weakness. Now his argument is this: when many families are destroyed in war, all manner of social and religious traditions will come to an end, and unrighteousness will soon spread on every side.

Our ancestors always took special interest in undertaking new experiments in the field of civilization and culture. They knew that the family's culture is the fundamental unit of a nation's culture. This is why Arjuna specifically brings up the destruction of the family's dharma here -- because its destruction can have grave consequences.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When the family is destroyed, its eternal family duties (dharmas), followed since time immemorial, perish; and once dharma is destroyed, unrighteousness overtakes the entire remaining family.

Explanation: 'Kulakshaye pranashyanti kuladharmah sanatanah' -- when war occurs, it brings about the destruction (decline) of the family (lineage). Ever since the family began, its dharmas -- that is, its sacred traditions, sacred customs, proprieties -- have likewise continued by tradition. But once the family is destroyed, those dharmas which had always accompanied the family also perish -- that is, all the scripturally-ordained sacred rites performed at birth, at the sacred-thread ceremony, at marriage, at death, and after death -- rites that bring welfare to both the living and the departed souls, in this world and the next -- perish. For once the family itself is destroyed, upon what would the dharmas dependent on the family continue to rest?

'Dharme nashte kulam kritsnam adharmo'bhibhavatyuta' -- once the family's sacred proprieties, its sacred conduct, are destroyed, then the failure to observe dharma and the doing of what is contrary to dharma -- that is, unrighteousness, consisting in not doing what ought to be done and doing what ought not to be done -- overtakes the entire family; that is, unrighteousness spreads throughout the whole family.

Now a doubt arises here: once the family is destroyed, once the family no longer even exists, then whom would unrighteousness overtake? The answer is this: those men fit for battle are indeed slain in war; but those unfit for battle -- the children and women left behind -- are overtaken by unrighteousness. For when the men who are knowledgeable and experienced in weapons, scripture, worldly conduct and the like are killed in war, there remain no one to properly instruct or govern those left behind. As a result, lacking knowledge of propriety and proper conduct, they begin to act according to their own whims -- that is, they fail to do what ought to be done and begin doing what ought not to be done. This is how unrighteousness spreads among them.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said -- Sanjaya said: The high-souled Partha, supremely compassionate, seeing there, stationed in both armies, his fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends -- his own kinsmen arrayed for battle -- was overcome, and spoke thus to Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Nihatya etc. upto anususruma. Sin alone is the agent in the act of slaying these desperadoes. Therefore here the idea is this : These ememies of ours have been slain, i.e., have been take possession of, by sin. Sin would come to us also after slaying them. Sin in this context is the disregard, on account of greed etc., to the injurious conseences like the ruination of the family and the like. That is why Arjuna makes a specific mention of the [ruin of the] family etc., and of its duties in the passage 'How by slaying my own kinsmen etc'.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
One might reason: if our own husbands, transgressing dharma, have brought about the destruction of the family, what fault would there be if we too engage in misconduct? -- corrupted by such false reasoning, the women of the family would become defiled, is the sense. Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
What exactly is that fault caused by the destruction of the family? Anticipating this expectation, he answers, in 'Kulakshaya...': for with the destruction of the family, the eternal, age-old dharmas belonging to the family, the duties proper to the family, perish utterly, for lack of anyone to perform them. And what results once dharma is destroyed? He answers this, in 'Dharma...': once dharma is destroyed, due to the destruction of the family that upheld it, unrighteousness overwhelms the entire remaining family of the one who caused the family's destruction -- that is, his family becomes overwhelmingly steeped in unrighteousness, is the sense.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having said this, anticipating the doubt that perhaps this withdrawal stems merely from worldly attachment, not from true knowledge of the nature of sin and a righteous frame of mind, he now expounds, across five verses, the fault caused by the destruction of the family, beginning with 'Kulakshaya...': the eternal, ancient family dharmas, received through generations, utterly perish -- that is, they cease to exist, with no possibility of their revival -- once the family is destroyed, or once such destruction occurs. Hence, our devotion to You, O Partha (a name derived through Kunti's bond with You), our own traditional dharma inherited through generations, being accepted by You -- turning away from the sin that would destroy it is indeed proper, is the sense. One might object: even now, though dharma is being destroyed, if in the future some devotee, like Prahlada, should arise within the family, dharma would spring up again; hence, since it is heroic Kshatriya-dharma that we would be destroying by refusing to fight, engaging in war is indeed proper. Anticipating this, he says, in 'Dharme nashte...': or, once dharma is destroyed, unrighteousness overwhelms, pervades, the entire remaining family as well, is the sense.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When irreligion is prominent in the family, O Krishna, the women of the family become polluted, and from the degradation of womanhood, O descendant of Vrishni, comes unwanted progeny.

 Good population in human society is the basic principle for peace, prosperity and spiritual progress in life. The varnashrama religion's principles were so designed that the good population would prevail in society for the general spiritual progress of state and community. Such population depends on the chastity and faithfulness of its womanhood. As children are very prone to be misled, women are similarly very prone to degradation. Therefore, both children and women require protection by the elder members of the family. By being engaged in various religious practices, women will not be misled into adultery. According to Canakya Pandita, women are generally not very intelligent and therefore not trustworthy. So the different family traditions of religious activities should always engage them, and thus their chastity and devotion will give birth to a good population eligible for participating in the varnashrama system. On the failure of such varnashrama-dharma, naturally the women become free to act and mix with men, and thus adultery is indulged in at the risk of unwanted population. Irresponsible men also provoke adultery in society, and thus unwanted children flood the human race at the risk of war and pestilence.
