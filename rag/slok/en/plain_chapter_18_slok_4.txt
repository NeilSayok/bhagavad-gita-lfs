Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
In this verse, Lord Krishna promises Arjuna that He will give a complete exposition of the nature of renunciation. For an ordinary human being, performing any kind of renunciation is not an easy task; accumulation and prosperity are, as it were, the very life of the mind. Therefore, it is natural that, in order to awaken the noble qualities of Arjuna, the Lord addresses him as the 'best of the Bharatas' (bharatasattama) and 'tiger among men' (purushavyaghra). From the standpoint of study, renunciation has been classified into three categories. This threefold classification is found throughout the Gita, and those three categories are Sattvic, Rajasic and Tamasic. The Lord says:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
And He explains the manner of that in the verse beginning with 'niscayam'. The division of sacrifices has been spoken of in 'dravyayajnah' (4.28) etc. In charity, the giving of fearlessness (abhaya-dana) is included. The meaning is that among these, whatever sacrifice etc., are prescribed must indeed be performed. Otherwise, there would be a contradiction with the Vyasa Smriti: 'Whether a celibate student, a householder, a forest-dweller, or an ascetic—whoever desires to attain liberation should take recourse to the highest stage of life (ashrama).' For, those (actions of the ascetics) indeed consist of the sacrifice of knowledge, the imparting of knowledge and fearlessness, and austerities like celibacy, etc. Therefore, whatever statement appears to be contrary should be applied according to the difference in qualifications (adhikara). Otherwise, there would be no recourse for the others.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Even though the aforementioned alternative views apply only to those who are qualified for actions, how is the final decision established? To this, the Lord says, 'tatra' (there). In order to show that very decision, He first states the subdivision within relinquishment (tyaga) by saying 'tyāgo hi' (relinquishment indeed).
Objection: Since both relinquishment (tyaga) and renunciation (sannyasa) are equally under discussion, if only the subdivision of relinquishment is mentioned, would it not lead to the neglect of renunciation?
He (Shankara) replies, 'No,' [implying that they are identical in meaning] by using the word 'tyaga' (relinquishment).
Objection: Even if the threefold division of the subject matter into Sattvic, Rajasic, and Tamasic is stated, since it is impossible to arrive at the decision by oneself, what is the need of the Lord's decision here?
Anticipating this, he (Shankara) says, 'Since (yasmāt)...'
He concludes with, 'Therefore (tasmāt)...' showing that since no one other than the Lord can determine the truth of this division, the decision of the Lord must be heard.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, best among the descendants of Bharata! Listen to My decision regarding relinquishment, the first of the two—renunciation and relinquishment. Because, O best of men, relinquishment has been declared to be of three kinds.

Commentary:

**niscayam srnu me tatra tyage bharatasattama** — O Arjuna, best among the descendants of Bharata, now I shall give My opinion regarding relinquishment, the first of the two—renunciation and relinquishment; listen to that.

**tyago hi purusavyaghra trividhah samprakirtitah** — O tiger among men, relinquishment has been declared to be of three kinds—Sattvic, Rajasic and Tamasic. Actually, in the Lord's view, only Sattvic relinquishment is true relinquishment. But the purpose of describing Rajasic and Tamasic relinquishment along with it is that without them, the excellence of the Lord's desired Sattvic relinquishment cannot be clearly shown; because to prove the excellence of any object by examination or comparison, other objects must be placed alongside it.

The purpose of stating three kinds of relinquishment is also that the seeker should adopt Sattvic relinquishment, and discard Rajasic and Tamasic relinquishment.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Regarding contradictory versions on Tyaga among disputants, listen from Me My decision. Tyaga has been described by Me in respect of actions prescribed by the scriptures from three points of view:  (1) as referring to fruits, (2) as referring to acts themselves and, (3) as referring to agency. It is contained in the statement, 'Surrendering all your actions to Me with a mind focussed on the self,' and 'Free from desire and selfishness and cured of fever - fight' (3.30). The renunciation of fruits consists in the following manner. 'Heaven and such other results arising from acts do not belong to Me.' Renunciation of acts is complete abandonment of the sense of possession in regard to one's acts. This sense of possession is of the following nature:  'Those acts are mine on account of their being the means for fruits which are to be mine.' Renunciation referring to agency is the renunciation of agency of oneself by ascribing the agency to the Lord of all.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.4–18.11 jointly): Niscayam etc.  upto abhidhiyate.  The conclusion here is  this :  Due to the manifoldness of the nature of the Strands, that have been defined earlier, the act of relinishment itself is performed with a certain mental disposition which is a modification of the Sattva, the Rajas and the Tamas (the Strands).  Because it reflects  (is contaminated by)  the nature of the person having the same (the said mental dispositon),  what is called the real (unalloyed)  relinishment is the performance of the actions  by the knowers of the Supreme Brahman by giving up desire to achieve fruits and by avoiding the craving and hatred on account of their eanimity to [the pairs of opposites like]  success and failure etc.  That is why [the Bhagavat] says :  'By the act of relinishment born of the Rajas or of the Tamas  (Strands), no connection with the fruit  [of relinishment] is attained'.  However, for an act of relinishing, born of the Sattva (Strand), there is the fruit in the form of honouring the purport of the scriptures.  The application of the term  'relinishment'  stands to reason, in fact, only in the case of a sage who has relinished his holding on the multitude of the Strands.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
This is said not merely by the strength of the adjective 'wise ones' (manishinah), but because the Lord also expounds the manner of non-contradiction; with this intention, he states the purport of the subsequent sentence by saying 'tat' (that).
Objection: But since the Paramahamsas (ascetics) do not perform sacrifices and charity, how can it be said, 'It should not be abandoned, but must indeed be performed' (18.5)?
To this, he says 'yajña' (sacrifice) etc.
Objection: By taking sacrifice to mean Jyotistoma etc., and charity to mean donating wealth, why is the state of Paramahamsa itself not rejected?
To this, he says 'anyatha' (otherwise).
'yati' means the Hamsa class of ascetics, etc. 'asthatum' means to attain. 'uttamashramam' means the highest stage of life (ashrama), i.e., the state of Paramahamsa.
Objection: Even under this explanation, how is the performance of sacrifice etc., possible for Paramahamsas?
To this, he says 'jnana' (knowledge) etc. This is a Karmadharaya compound comprising three Bahuvrihi compounds. They are Paramahamsas whose charity consists of imparting knowledge (vidya) and giving protection from fear (abhaya).
Objection: But it is mentioned in the Puranas (Bhagavata 5.1.11-19) that Priyavrata, who was entering the Paramahamsa state, was dissuaded by Hiranyagarbha. How then can the Paramahamsa state be a duty?
To this, he says 'atah' (therefore).
Objection: Since all statements are equally authoritative, why should there not be a contradiction of the Smriti by the Purana statement itself?
To this, he says 'anyatha' (otherwise). The meaning is that if this connection is not made, since there would be no other recourse for the others like the celibate student (brahmachari) etc., the loss of authority of the scriptures would result. Thus, by establishing the obligatoriness of sacrifice etc., even for Paramahamsas, the view of some is also refuted, namely: 'This section is begun with reference to the ignorant who are qualified for action, and not to the Paramahamsa ascetics.'

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Such is indeed the Vedic decision; since they (the disputants) do not determine it, the Lord states His own decision by saying 'niscayam srnu' (listen to My decision). He says that if that very relinquishment (tyaga) is done otherwise, it becomes secondary (gauna). It has been declared to be of three kinds: Tamasic, Rajasic, and Sattvic. Or, it is declared to be of three kinds in the verse: 'Renouncing all actions in Me' (3.30). There, being 'nirashih' means free from desire; 'nirmamah' means devoid of the sense of possession (mamata) in the fruit and action. Thus, renouncing and offering all actions to Me, contemplating the agency as belonging to Me, with the intellect: 'I perform this action with the power dependent on Him'—the relinquishment of agency, the sense of possession, and the fruits in the actions performed is what is called sannyasa (renunciation).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
**niscayam** etc. Amidst such difference of opinion, regarding that relinquishment (tyaga) which is under discussion, which is performed by one qualified for action, and which is denoted by the words 'sannyasa' and 'tyaga'—which is the abandonment of actions preceded by the desire for fruits—hear My decision, which has been established by the ancient teachers.
O best of the Bharatas! If you ask, 'What is so difficult to understand here?' the Lord says: **purusa...** O tiger among men, best of men, **hi** because relinquishment performed by one qualified for action, which is the abandonment of actions preceded by the desire for fruits, has been declared in the scriptures to be **trividhah**, of three kinds, based on the divisions of Tamasic etc.
Alternatively, relinquishment, which is of the nature of the absence of a qualified entity, is declared to be of three kinds due to: the absence of the qualification, the absence of the qualified object, or the absence of both.
To explain: the abandonment of action preceded by the desire for fruits is of three kinds:
1. The abandonment of the desire for fruits even while performing the action—this is the first kind.
2. The abandonment of the action itself even while having the desire for fruits—this is the second.
3. The abandonment of both the desire for fruits and the action—this is the third.
Among these, the first is Sattvic and should be adopted. The second is to be rejected; it is of two kinds: Rajasic (done out of a sense of trouble) and Tamasic (done out of delusion).
This much relinquishment, which is performed by one qualified for action, is the subject of Arjuna's question.
The third kind of relinquishment, however, which is performed by one not qualified for action (the knower of the Self) and is of the nature of transcendence of attributes (nairgunya), is not the subject of Arjuna's question. That too is of two kinds, based on the division of means (sadhana) and fruit (phala).
There, through the Sattvic relinquishment—which is the performance of action without the desire for fruits—one whose mind is purified and who has developed the desire to know the Self (vividisha) engages in the study of Vedanta (shravana), which is the means to Self-knowledge. When the mind is purified, there is abandonment of the action which was the means, just as husking is abandoned once the rice is obtained. This is the first kind, which is the means, and is called Vividisha-sannyasa (renunciation of the seeker). The Lord will refer to this later as 'the supreme state of actionlessness' (18.49).
The second is the natural abandonment of both the desire for fruits and the action by one who has attained Self-knowledge in this very life at the very beginning, due to the maturity of practice in past lives, and is thus fulfilled. This is the fruit, and is called Vidvat-sannyasa (renunciation of the wise). This has been explained earlier in the two verses starting with 'He who is devoted only to the Self' (3.17-18) and has been elaborated in detail through the characteristics of the person of steady wisdom (sthitaprajna), etc.
Since the true nature of relinquishment is so difficult to comprehend, and you have said that you want to know the truth, therefore, understand it from My all-knowing words—this is the intent.
By the two addresses (best of Bharatas and tiger among men), the superiority due to family lineage and the superiority due to personal valor are highlighted to indicate Arjuna's exceptional capability.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having thus separately stated the true nature of the meanings of the words 'sannyasa' and 'tyaga' according to different opinions, the Lord speaks to show His own view, which is their identity: **niscayam** etc. Regarding that relinquishment—that is, the alternative views on relinquishment and renunciation—hear and comprehend My decision from My words. The Lord speaks with the intention that the meaning denoted by the words 'relinquishment' and 'renunciation' is indeed one and the same. Relinquishment has been declared to be **trividhah**, of three kinds, according to the divisions of Tamasic etc., in the scriptures. **hi** Because the meaning denoted by the words 'relinquishment' and 'renunciation' in the case of one who is qualified for action but is ignorant of the Self can possibly be of three kinds, which is clearly declared in the scriptures, and it cannot be expounded by anyone other than the Lord, who is the knower of all scriptures. Therefore, in this matter which is difficult to understand, listen to the divine decision and resolve concerning the supreme truth of the scriptures. By addressing him as 'best among the descendants of Bharata' (bharatasattama), the most virtuous among the Kshatriyas, the Lord implies: 'Listen to the decision spoken by Me regarding the relinquishment and renunciation that ought to be performed by the foremost Kshatriyas.' By addressing him as 'tiger among men' (purusavyaghra), the Lord implies: 'The decision regarding the meaning of relinquishment and renunciation is being declared by Me not only for the foremost Kshatriyas, but also for other excellent men who are qualified for action but are ignorant of the Self.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
With the intention that the word 'tatra' (there) indicates the doubtfulness of the subject to which logical reasoning is applied, by restating the status of the dispute under discussion, he (Ramanuja) says: 'Regarding contradictory versions...'.
By the words 'My decision', in order to dispel the doubt about the rise of other opinions, he says: 'Listen to the decision regarding relinquishment from Me.' 'From Me' means from Me who am free from defects such as delusion.
The statement 'tyago hi' (relinquishment indeed) etc. does not refer to the threefold division of Sattvic relinquishment etc. to be described later, but rather to the internal divisions of Sattvic relinquishment. This is because the word 'samprakirtitah' (has been declared) naturally refers to what has been said previously. Also, the word 'hi' indicates the listener's agreement. With this intention, he (Ramanuja) says: 'Tyaga has been described by Me in respect of actions prescribed...' etc.
The single verse 'Surrendering all your actions to Me' (3.30) is the one that describes the threefold relinquishment. Here, in order to refute the abandonment of actions themselves (which would result in fruitless performance) and to counter the doubts of the Sankhya school, he distinguishes the nature of the three types by saying: 'heaven and such other results...' etc.
The phrase 'as being the means for fruits which are to be mine' etc. implies that the seeker of liberation contemplates: 'The Lord impels me solely for His own purpose, for His own pleasure.' The contemplation of agency in the Lord of all is due to one's own agency being dependent on Him and requiring His permission. The relinquishment of agency, which means contemplating that one's own agency is directed by another in an action where there are multiple agents, will be clarified later.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus stated the opinions of others due to their ignorance of the truth, the Lord speaks to declare His own determined opinion for the sake of imparting knowledge of the truth among those opinions: **niscayam** etc. Regarding that relinquishment, which has been explained in many ways by many people, O best of the Bharatas, you who are fit to listen by virtue of being born in a noble family, hear My determined and final decision. Turning his attention thus, the Lord says: **tyaga** (relinquishment). O tiger among men, best of men! Since man has the qualification for worshipping the Lord, by calling him the best of men and using the word 'tiger', the Lord indicates Arjuna's capability to demonstrate valor by putting it into practice after listening. He says: **tyago hi...** Relinquishment is indeed declared to be of three kinds, i.e., expounded in a proper manner.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
**niscayam** etc. There being difference of opinion regarding the relinquishment of actions, hear My decision from My words on the subject of relinquishment, which is the first to be discussed. **hi** Because, O tiger among men, relinquishment has been declared in the scriptures to be of three kinds, according to the divisions of Sattvic, Rajasic, and Tamasic. Sannyasa (renunciation) of actions accompanied by firm dispassion is Sattvic; relinquishment of them out of fear of physical strain is Rajasic; and relinquishment of them due to ignorance/delusion is Tamasic. Therefore, the meaning is that due to its profound nature, relinquishment must be considered with certainty.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O best of the Bhāratas, now hear My judgment about renunciation. O tiger among men, renunciation is declared in the scriptures to be of three kinds.

 Although there are differences of opinion about renunciation, here the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, gives His judgment, which should be taken as final. After all, the Vedas are different laws given by the Lord. Here the Lord is personally present, and His word should be taken as final. The Lord says that the process of renunciation should be considered in terms of the modes of material nature in which it is performed.
