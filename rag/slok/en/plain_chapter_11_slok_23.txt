Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He concludes what was said, that 'the three worlds are terrified', by saying 'since...' (yasmāt). The construction is: 'Since Your form is of this kind, therefore, having seen it...'. He says 'tathā' (so also) to mean: 'Like ordinary people, I too have become terrified due to fear, experiencing distress, pain, and agitation of the body and senses'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O mighty-armed one! Seeing Your great form, which has many mouths and eyes, many arms, thighs, and feet, many bellies, and many terrible teeth, all living beings are distressed, and I too am distressed.

Commentary—[From the 15th to the 18th verses, the 'Deva' (divine) form in the Cosmic Form is described; from the 19th to the 22nd verses, the 'Ugra' (fierce) form is described; and from the 23rd to the 30th verses, the 'Atyanta Ugra' (exceedingly fierce) form is described.]

'bahu-vaktra-netram' — Your mouths do not resemble one another. Some mouths are gentle and some are fierce. Some mouths are small and some are large. Similarly, Your eyes also do not all appear the same. Some eyes are gentle and some are fierce. Some eyes are small, some are large, some are long, some are wide, some are round, some are squint/crooked, etc.

'bahu-bāhūru-pādam' — The structure, color, shape, and functions of the hands are extraordinary and distinct. The thighs are strange/varied, and the feet are also of different kinds.

'bahūdaram' — The bellies are also not identical. There are various kinds of bellies—some large, some small, some terrifying, etc.

'bahu-daṁṣṭrā-karālaṁ dṛṣṭvā lokāḥ pravyathitās-tathāham' — There are many types of terrifying tusks/teeth in the mouths. Seeing such a great, terrifying, and fierce form, all living beings are distressed, and I too am distressed.

In the verses prior to this one, mention has also been made of many mouths, eyes, etc., and of everyone being terrified. Why, then, is Arjuna repeating the same thing? The reason is:

(1) Whatever aspect of the Cosmic Form comes before Arjuna's vision, he sees in it new and extraordinary uniqueness and divinity.

(2) On seeing the Cosmic Form, Arjuna became so frightened, astonished, bewildered, and distressed that he lost track of what he had said and what he was saying.

(3) Earlier Arjuna spoke of the three worlds being distressed, but here, along with all living beings, he speaks of himself also being distressed.

(4) Repeating the same thing is a sign of Arjuna being terrified and astonished. It is commonly observed in the world that when someone experiences fear, joy, grief, surprise, etc., they naturally repeat a word or sentence; for example, if someone sees a snake, they fearfully repeat 'Snake! Snake! Snake!'. If a noble person arrives, one says joyfully, 'Welcome! Welcome! Welcome!'. If a beloved one dies, one says in grief, 'I am ruined! Ruined! It is all dark, all dark!'. If a sudden calamity strikes, it comes out of one's mouth, 'I am dead! Dead! Dead!'. Similarly here, in the vision of the Cosmic Form, due to fear and joy, certain words and sentences were repeatedly uttered by Arjuna. Arjuna has also acknowledged this fear and joy: 'adṛṣṭapūrvaṁ hṛṣito'smi dṛṣṭvā bhayena ca pravyathitaṁ mano me' (11.45). The point is that in states of fear, joy, grief, etc., repeating a statement is not considered a redundancy error (punarukti-doṣa).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
He concludes what was said, that 'the three worlds are terrified', with the verse starting with 'rūpam' (form) — O mighty-armed one, seeing Your form, all living beings are terrified, and so also am I terrified due to fear. What kind of form is Yours? 'Mahat', meaning of immense size. That which has many mouths and eyes. That which has many arms, thighs, and feet. That which has many bellies. 'Bahu-daṁṣṭrā-karālam', meaning extremely terrifying due to many tusks. The meaning is that simply by seeing it, all the worlds, including myself, are afflicted with fear.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Moreover — O mighty-armed one, seeing Your 'mahat', i.e., extremely powerful form, all the worlds are 'pravyathitāḥ', i.e., very frightened, and so am I terrified. Seeing what kind of form? That which has many mouths and eyes; that which has many arms, thighs, and feet; that which has many bellies; and that which is terrifying (deformed) due to many tusks, meaning it is fierce.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
If it is asked why Rudras and others are filled with wonder/astonishment, he explains the reason, saying it is because all the worlds are afflicted on seeing Your terrifying form, and thus speaks the verse beginning with 'rūpam'. Your form is 'mahat', meaning of immense size. He explains that very form: That which has many mouths and eyes; many arms, etc. (All creatures are) 'pravyathitāḥ', i.e., trembling/agitated with fear. If it is objected, 'What does the distress of the worlds have to do with you? Surely you are not distressed?', he answers: 'tathāham api vyathitaḥ' (so also am I distressed).

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
By the verse 'rudrādityāḥ...' (11.22) etc., wonder/astonishment was expressed; and by the verse 'rūpaṁ mahat te...', fear is being described. To show that the term 'three worlds' mentioned earlier and the term 'worlds' (loka) used here refer to the same subject, (Sri Ramanuja) says: 'the worlds mentioned earlier'. By the words 'idam' (this) and 'īdṛśam' (of this kind), reference is made to the subject and its attributes. Since the prefix 'pra-' in words like 'prasthāna' (departure) or 'prasmarana' (forgetting) is sometimes seen to imply negation or departure, in order to rule out such an interpretation in the word 'pravyathitāḥ', (Sri Ramanuja) has said: 'atīva' (exceedingly/intensely). 'vyathitāḥ' means agitated (shaken) or frightened.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Moreover — O mighty-armed one (endowed with the great power of grace)! Seeing Your form, all the worlds established within Your very form are 'pravyathitāḥ', meaning they are terrified. And so also am I terrified. He describes this form as a source of fear using adjectives starting with 'bahu' (many): That which has many mouths and eyes; that which has many arms, thighs, and feet; that which has many bellies; and that which is terrifying (karālam) due to many tusks. The abundance of mouths indicates the capacity to swallow; the abundance of eyes indicates the capacity to see in all directions, making hiding impossible; the abundance of arms indicates the capacity to seize; the abundance of thighs and feet indicates the capacity to run, making escape impossible; the abundance of bellies indicates the capacity to digest; and the abundance of tusks indicates the capacity to chew (devour). Therefore, seeing such a form, if even the worlds within Your form are terrified, then what doubt is there about my terror! This is indicated by the word 'tathā' (so also).

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Again, he expresses the distress of the worlds and of himself with the verse beginning with 'rūpam'. 'Mahat' means without beginning, middle, or end. O mighty-armed one, seeing Your terrifying great form, the worlds are distressed, and so also am I distressed — this is the construction.
