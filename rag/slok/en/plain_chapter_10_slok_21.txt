Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ādityānāmahaṃ viṣṇurjyotiṣāṃ raviraṃśumān .
marīcirmarutāmasmi nakṣatrāṇāmahaṃ śaśī

Word Meanings:
आदित्यानामहं - among Adityas I am
विष्णुर्ज्योतिषां - Vishnu, among luminaries
रविरंशुमान् - radiant sun
मरीचिर्मरुतामस्मि - Marichi among Maruts I am
नक्षत्राणामहं - among stars I am
शशी - moon

Life Application:
See God as Vishnu among light-givers, the sun among luminaries, Marichi among winds, and the moon among stars.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among lights I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the Maruts, and the Moon among the stars.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among lights I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the Maruts; among the stars I am the Moon.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the storm gods, and among the stars I am the Moon.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Among the twelve Adityas, Vishnu (who manifested as Vamana) is the Lord's chief manifestation. Among all illuminating orbs, the Sun; among the wind gods, Marichi; and among nocturnal lights, the Moon symbolizes divine glory.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among Maruts; among stars I am the Moon.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the Maruts; among the stars I am the Moon.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the Maruts; among the stars I am the Moon.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Among the Adityas, 'Vishnu' signifies the All-pervading and Inner-entering Lord. As stated in the Mokshadharma (Mahabharata), God is named 'Vishnu' because He pervades all worlds, is the ultimate goal of all creatures, and possesses supreme effulgence.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The Lord begins enumerating His chief divine opulences. Among the twelve Adityas, the all-pervading Vishnu; among illuminators, the Sun; among wind gods, Marichi; and among stars, the Moon are the Lord's chief Vibhutis.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among the Maruts, and the Moon among the stars.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Among the Adityas, I am the foremost Vishnu (Vamana); among luminaries, I am the supreme radiant Sun; among wind gods, I am Marichi; and the Moon, king of stars, is My divine Vibhuti.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Lord describes His infinite divine opulences in prominence. The Supreme Self alone abides in the hearts of all living beings; whatever is glorious, beautiful, or mighty in the universe manifests from a fragment of the Lord's divine splendor.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: Among the twelve Adityas, I am the Aditya named Vishnu; among luminaries, I am the ray-bearing Sun; among the Maruts (wind gods), I am the Marut named Marichi; and among stars, I am the Moon.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The Lord's opulences are twofold—direct (manifest) and hidden (unmanifest). Here among the Adityas, the word 'Vishnu' establishes God's direct omniscience, all-pervading entry, and supreme effulgence as the ultimate goal and refuge of all beings.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Among the Adityas, Vishnu appearing as an Avatar is the Lord's own supreme Vibhuti. The Sun, Marichi, and the Moon carry the Lord's divine light and vitality.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Among the twelve Adityas, the supreme Vishnu; among radiant lights, the effulgent Sun; among wind deities, Marichi; and the Moon, sovereign of stars, is My divine manifestation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Among the twelve Adityas I am Vishnu; among luminaries I am the radiant Sun; I am Marichi among Maruts, and the Moon among stars.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Among Adityas, the Vamana incarnation Vishnu; among illuminators, the Sun; among wind deities, Marichi; and among stars, the Moon are the Lord's divine Vibhutis.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: In describing His Vibhutis, the Lord declares the most exalted entity of each class as His manifestation, such as Vishnu among Adityas and the Sun among lights.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Among Adityas I am Vishnu; among lights I am the Sun; I am Marichi among winds, and the Moon among stars.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: To encourage perceiving God in all exalted entities, the Lord states that Vishnu among Adityas, the Sun among luminaries, Marichi among winds, and the Moon among stars represent Him.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Of the Ādityas I am Viṣṇu, of lights I am the radiant sun, of the Maruts I am Marīci, and among the stars I am the moon.
