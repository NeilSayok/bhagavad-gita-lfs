Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
sāṅkhyayogau pṛthagbālāḥ pravadanti na paṇḍitāḥ .
ekamapyāsthitaḥ samyagubhayorvindate phalam

Word Meanings:
साङ्ख्ययोगौ - Sankhya and Yoga
पृथग्बालाः - as distinct, the ignorant
प्रवदन्ति - declare
न - not
पण्डिताः - the wise
एकमप्यास्थितः - established even in one
सम्यगुभयोर्विन्दते - obtains of both
फलम् - the fruit

Life Application:
Recognize that honest spiritual study and selfless daily work ultimately lead to the same deep inner peace.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya and Yoga as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya and Yoga as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Children (the spiritually ignorant) speak of Samkhya (Jnana Yoga) and Karma Yoga as leading to different fruits, but not the enlightened wise; for he who is firmly established in either one obtains the fruit of both (ultimate Liberation).

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Obtains the fruit of both"—the ultimate destination of both Samkhya and Karma-Yoga is identical, namely Lord Vishnu"s supreme abode.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Samkhya (knowledge-renunciation) and Karma-Yoga are not distinct in their ultimate goal; when practiced properly, either path yields the single fruit of Liberation.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya and Yoga as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Both Samkhya and Karma-Yoga share the single aim of Self-realization; only the spiritually unrefined view them as opposing or yielding separate fruits.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya (Knowledge) and Yoga (Action) as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both. The supreme state which is reached by Samkhya-yogis is also reached by Karma-yogis; therefore he who sees Samkhya and Yoga as one in fruit, sees truly.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya and Yoga as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both"—refutes the ignorant illusion of inherent conflict between Knowledge and Action.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Both Samkhya and Yoga assist in indirect and direct Knowledge of God, bestowing the single reward of Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The Yoga of Krishna-devotion automatically perfects the fruit of Samkhya knowledge; hence both are non-different.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The wise do not speak thus"—only childish fools view Samkhya and Yoga as separate, for both culminate in Self-realization via mind-purification.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Children (the ignorant) speak of Samkhya and Yoga as distinct, but not the wise. He who is firmly established in even one obtains the fruit of both.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how the ignorant alone preach division between Samkhya and Yoga, whereas the wise see unity of their ultimate fruit.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: In Vasudeva-devotion, both Samkhya and Karma-Yoga combine to grant the practitioner supreme bliss.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker"s devotion is surrendered to Lord Krishna, both Knowledge and Action yield the self-same supreme fruit.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Established firmly in even one"—whoever faithfully practices either path naturally achieves the fruit of the other.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Only the ignorant speak of devotional service [karma-yoga] as being different from the analytical study of the material world [Sankhya]. Those who are actually learned say that he who applies himself well to one of these paths achieves the results of both.
