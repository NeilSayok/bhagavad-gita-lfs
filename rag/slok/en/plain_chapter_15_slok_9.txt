Life Application:
Presiding over hearing, sight, touch, taste, smell, and mind, the embodied soul experiences sense objects.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Presiding over the ear, the eye, touch, taste and smell, as well as the mind, it enjoys the objects of the senses.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Presiding over the ear, the eye, touch, taste and smell, as well as the mind, it enjoys the objects of the senses.

श्रोत्रम् the ear? चक्षुः the eye? स्पर्शनम् the (organ of) touch? च and? रसनम् the (organ of) taste? घ्राणम् the (organ of) smell? एव even? च and? अधिष्ठाय presiding over? मनः the mind? च and? अयम् this (soul)? विषयान् objects of the senses? उपसेवते enjoys.Commentary Here is a description of how the subtle body remaining in the gross body enjoys the objects of the senses.The individual soul uses the mind along with each sense separately and enjoys or experiences the objects of the senses such as sound? touch? colour (form)? taste and smell.It sits on the marvellous car of its mind? passes through the gateway of the ear in the twinkling of an eye and enjoys the various kinds of music of this world. It holds the reins of the nerves of sensation? enters the domain of touch through the portal of the skin and enjoys the diverse kinds of soft objects. It roams about in the hills of beautiful forms and enjoys them through the windows of his eyes. It enters the cave of taste by the avenue of the tongue and enjoyes dainties? palatable dishes and refreshing beverages. It enters the forest of scents through the door of the nose and enjoys them to its hearts content.It makes its abode in the ears? the eyes? the skin? the tongue and the nose? as also in the mind and enjoys the objects of the senses. It gains experiences of the outer world through the mind?,intellect? subconscious mind? egoism? the ten senses and the five vital airs.Ghranameva cha The word cha (and) indicates that we shall have to include the five organs of action? and also the fourfold inner instrument (mind? intellect? subconscious mind and egoism). In the Katha Upanishad it is said आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तं भोक्तेत्याहुर्मनीषिणः।।The Self? the senses and the mind united? the wise call the enjoyer.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He is the perception of the ear, the eye, the touch, the taste and the smell, yea and of the mind also; and the enjoyment the things which they perceive is also His.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Presiding over the ear, the eye, touch, taste and smell, as well as the mind, he enjoys the objects of the senses.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that the pure Consciousness (Atman) by Itself does not experience any sensory objects because It is without attributes. However, when It reflects in the intellect-mind equipment, It becomes the Jiva, the enjoyer (Bhokta). Presiding over the five sense organs and the mind, the Jiva experiences the material world. The mind acts as the coordinator of all sensory inputs. It is because of the Jiva's presence that the inert body appears to hear, see, feel, taste, and smell. Since this divine spark is the animator, one who is ignorant fails to recognize It, thinking that the physical organs themselves have the power to perceive.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Presiding  over the ear, the eye, the touch-sense the taste-sense and also the smell-sense and the mind, He enjoys the sense objects.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Presiding over the ear, the eye, the sense of touch, the tongue and the nose, and the mind, It experiences these objects of senses.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
This one enjoys the objects by presiding over the ear, eyes, skin and tongue as also the nose and the mind.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya comments that the Lord also experiences or illumines the sensory experiences through the senses and the mind of the Jiva. It is by the power of the Lord dwelling within that the Jiva is able to experience anything, as supported by the Upanishads: 'He who sings on the lute, sings of Him'. The Lord experiences only auspiciousness, as no sin or sorrow can touch Him.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that this verse details the specific senses (ear, eye, touch, taste, smell) along with the mind as the sixth, which the Jiva presides over within the physical body. By ruling over these instruments, the Jiva interacts with and enjoys the outer objects of sound, form, touch, taste, and smell.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Presiding over the ear, the eye, touch, taste and smell, as well as the mind, it enjoys the objects of the senses.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that the Jiva can experience sensory pleasures only by presiding over the mind and senses. Without the mind's attention, the senses cannot perceive their objects. The soul is inherently pure and transcendental, but by identifying with the mind and senses, it binds itself to samsara. The pleasure derived from sense objects is temporary and illusionary, leading to attachment, rebirth, and suffering. A wise seeker should redirect their love and attention from these changing sense objects to the eternal Lord.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Presiding over these sense-organs, of which the mind is the sixth, the lord of the body drives the organs towards their corresponding objects like sound and the rest and enjoys them.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 15.9–15.11 jointly): Srotram etc. upto asetasah.  Mind :  the internal organ is hery implied.  Hence, due to His association of  the bodily existence, when He remains  [in the body], or rises up to seize another body, or enjoys the sense objects - at that time the deluded persons do not perceive Him, because they are not well enlightened.  But, considering everything as manifestation of the  [Self] awareness, the highly enlightened men recognize Him.  Thus they are men of unbroken contemplation.  Because they are engrossed in their exertion.
 
But in the case of those who have not mastered their self (mind),  even their effort does not  yield any fruit, because their passion has not yet been fully decayed. Indeed the seeds of corn, sown during the autumnal season are not capable of yielding a rich harvest, even though the means like water etc., get collected together. Therefore, it (the water etc.) does not constitute a collection of means.  Indeed totally different is the water  sent forth by the mass of  clouds that had been filled up in the month of Madhu.  Altogether different is the earth that  had been under the grip of the Cold-season (Sisira)  and has (now) brilliance  by the simple touch of the sun-rays.  In the same way, the effort of the men of uncontrolled  self, never gets to the stage of fulfilment of all reisities .  That is why those  who, even after receiving the means like the inititation etc.  necessary for the realisation of the Supreme Lord, continue to have their inner vision filled with the collection of the knots (granthi) of anger, bewilderment etc., in the manner as before-in their case, even the means does never bear any fruit.  This must be borne in mind.  For, it is has been said :
 
'When anger etc., are seen, then even the initiated one never gets emancipation'.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Seated in the body, it upasevate, enjoys; visayan, the objects-sound etc.; adhisthaya, by presiding over; srotram, the ear; caksuh, eyes; sparsanam, skin, the organ of touch; rasanam, tongue; eva ca, as also; the ghranam, nose; and manah, the mind, the sixth-(presiding over) each one of them along with its (corresponding) organ.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that the Jiva is able to experience sensory objects only because of the inner presence of the Supreme Lord, who regulates the senses. The Jiva uses the senses under the Lord's control to perform actions and experience fruits.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that the Jiva uses the senses and the mind, which are creations of the material ego (Prakrita-ahankara), directing them towards their respective objects to experience the sensory world under the influence of worldly desires.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that the Jiva, residing in the physical body, experiences sensory objects like sound and touch by presiding over the five senses and the mind as the sixth. The mind acts as the supervisor, without which the senses cannot function, and the Jiva enjoys the objects of the senses through this combined instrumentation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the Jiva presides over the external senses (ear, eye, touch, taste, smell) and the internal organ (mind) to experience their respective objects. Through this presiding, the Jiva acts as the enjoyer within the physical tabernacle.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that the Jiva, by presiding over each sense organ along with the mind as the sixth, experiences objects like sound and form. The inclusion of 'cha' suggests the coordination of the inner instruments (mind, intellect, ego) in processing these sensory experiences.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the Jiva, as the ruler of the senses, directs them towards their corresponding objects. Presiding over the mind as the coordinator, it experiences worldly objects, though in its essential state it is free from all material enjoyments.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the Jiva, presiding over the physical senses and the mind, experiences sensory objects. This experiencing is meant for both worldly enjoyment and ultimately for realizing the divine play, though under the influence of Maya it is perceived as material bondage.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the Jiva activates the senses and the mind to project and experience sensory objects. Just as a lamp requires oil and a wick but reveals objects by its own light, the Jiva relies on the mind and senses but experiences objects by its own consciousness.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The living entity, thus taking another gross body, obtains a certain type of ear, eye, tongue, nose and sense of touch, which are grouped about the mind. He thus enjoys a particular set of sense objects.

 In other words, if the living entity adulterates his consciousness with the qualities of cats and dogs, in his next life he gets a cat or dog body and enjoys. Consciousness is originally pure, like water. But if we mix water with a certain color, it changes. Similarly, consciousness is pure, for the spirit soul is pure. But consciousness is changed according to the association of the material qualities. Real consciousness is Kṛṣṇa consciousness. When, therefore, one is situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he is in his pure life. But if his consciousness is adulterated by some type of material mentality, in the next life he gets a corresponding body. He does not necessarily get a human body again; he can get the body of a cat, dog, hog, demigod or one of many other forms, for there are 8,400,000 species.
