Life Application:
That supreme abode is not illuminated by sun, moon, or fire; reaching it, seekers never return to mortal darkness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Neither does the sun illumine it, nor the moon, nor the fire. That is My supreme abode, having gone whither they do not return.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Neither doth the sun illumine there nor the moon, nor the fire; having gone thither they return not; that is My supreme abode.

It existed before the sun? the moon and the fire came into existence during creation. It remains even after they dissolve into the Unmanifested during the dissolution of the world.This verse is taken from the Kathopanishad The sun does not shine there? nor do the moon and the stars? nor does this lightning shine and much less this fire. When It shines? everything shines after It? by Its light? all these shine (Chap.II?5.15). The same idea occurs in the Svetasvatara Upanishad (6.14) and the Mundaka Upanishad (II.2.10). The sun? the moon? etc.? derive their light from Para Brahman. Nothing else is needed for illuminating the Supreme Being because It is selfluminous.Dhama paramam Supreme abode or superexcellent seat or Para Brahman.Though the sun is endowed with the power of illumining all? it cannot illumine the Supreme Being.यत् धाम वैष्णवं पदं गत्वा प्राप्य न निवर्तन्ते यत् च सूर्यादिभिः न भासयते तत् धाम पदं परमं मम विष्णोः।That abode? to which having gone? none returns? and which the sun? moon? stars? lightning and fire do not illumine? is the highest abode of Vishnu.(Cf.VIII.21)

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Neither sun, moon, nor fire shines there. Those who go thither never come back. For, O Arjuna, that is my Celestial Home!

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Neither the sun nor the moon nor fire illumines That. That is My supreme Abode, reaching which they do not return.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that the supreme state of Brahman cannot be illumined by physical sources of light like the sun, moon, or fire. Rather, these physical sources are objectified and illuminated by the Light of Consciousness (Chaitanya). The subject (knower) can never be illumined by the object (known). This self-luminous Consciousness is the Lord's supreme Abode. Reaching this state of realization, the Jiva transcends all ignorance and never returns to the sorrowful cycle of birth and death.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The sun does not illumine That; nor the moon and  nor the fire;  That is My Abode Supreme, having gone to Which they (Yogins) never return.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That supreme light (i.e. the individual self), reaching which they do not return any more, is Mine; the sun does not illumine It, nor moon, nor the fire.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Neither the sun nor the moon nor fire illumines That. That is My supreme Abode, reaching which they do not return.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that the Supreme Abode of Lord Vishnu is completely self-luminous and spiritual, transcending the physical elements of sun, moon, and fire. Those who attain this spiritual state never return to material existence, as they are fully liberated.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that the Supreme State is non-dual and self-luminous. It cannot be known as an object because it is the Subject itself. The sun, moon, and fire are objective lights that cannot illumine their own source. Reaching this state, the seeker's ignorance is destroyed, and thus there is no return.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Neither does the sun illumine it, nor the moon, nor the fire. That is My supreme abode, having gone whither they do not return.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that the physical light of the sun, moon, and fire, which belongs to the material world (Prakriti), has no access to the self-luminous spiritual realm of Brahman. The physical senses, mind, and intellect, which are also material, cannot grasp or reveal the Supreme Self. The Jiva is an eternal fragment of the Lord. Just as rivers find rest and merge into the ocean, the Jiva finds eternal peace upon returning to its source—the Lord's supreme Abode. Having realized this oneness, there is no returning to the transient world of birth and death.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The sun cannot illumine the light of the self, nor moon, nor fire. For, knowledge is indeed that which illumines them all... That supreme light, reaching which they do not return any more is the self, which is My glory (Vibhuti) and therefore belongs to Me and is a part of Myself.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Na tat etc.  There is no scope for the sun etc., in  [illumining] That. For, they are conditioned by time etc., because they are objects of  knowledge,  [and] because they are the helpers of the sense organs.  On the other hand,  That  [Absolute] is unrestricted by space, time etc.  It is the knower, the one inducer of the sense organs and also the one transcending them.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Na suryah, niether the sun-though possessed of the power of illumining everything; so also, na sasankah, nor the moon; na pavakah, nor even fire; bhasayate, illumines; tat, That [-this (word) refers to the remote word dhama (Abode) at the end of the verse-], that Abode which is of the nature of light. That abode, the State of Visnu, gatva, reaching, attaining; yat, which; they na, do not; nivartante, return, and which the sun etc. do not illumine; tat, that; is mama, My, Visnu's; paramam, supreme; dhama, Abode, State.
Objection: It has been said, 'reaching which they do not return'. Is it not well known that all goings end, verily, in returning, and unions are followed by separations? How is it said that there is no return for those who come to that Abode?
Reply: As to that, listen to the reason:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that the Supreme Abode of Vishnu is of the nature of pure Consciousness and is therefore self-luminous. Unlike material light, which depends on sensory contact, this supreme state is the source of all illumination and does not require sun, moon, or fire.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that the Supreme Abode (Dhama) of Purushottama is the ultimate spiritual light and the substratum of all creation. It is beyond the reach of physical luminaries like the sun, moon, and fire, and once the Jiva enters this divine abode, it never returns to the relative world.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that the supreme state of Vishnu is self-luminous, and it is the illuminator of all material luminaries like the sun, moon, and fire. The Vedas declare that everything shines by His light. Since It is the Subject, It cannot be objectified or illumined by anything else. Reaching this state, the yogis are freed from the illusion of duality and never return.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the state of Vishnu, which the sun and other lights do not illuminate and from which there is no return, is His supreme nature. Because it is beyond the reach of physical light, it is free from all material limitations and dualities like heat and cold.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that the supreme state of Vishnu is self-revealing and cannot be illumined by objects of sight like the sun (representing eyes), moon (representing mind), or fire (representing speech). It is the source of all light, as Upanishads declare. By attaining this non-dual state of Brahman, one is freed from the cycle of birth and death forever.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the 'Dhama' referred to is the self-luminous individual self (Jiva) in its purified state of liberation. External physical lights only remove darkness to facilitate sensory perception, whereas knowledge itself is the ultimate illuminator. This supreme light of the self belongs to the Lord as His glory (Vibhuti).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the supreme abode of the Lord is the highest destination where the Jiva enters into the eternal relationship with Purushottama. It is self-luminous and cannot be accessed by material elements. Reaching this abode through pure devotion, the Jiva never falls back into the material world.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the supreme abode is the non-dual Brahman, which cannot be objectified by the senses or mind. The sun, moon, and fire represent the external lights and mental concepts, which cannot illumine the self-luminous Subject. Once the Jiva realizes its identity with this Brahman, all cycles of rebirth cease.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
That supreme abode of Mine is not illumined by the sun or moon, nor by fire or electricity. Those who reach it never return to this material world.

 The spiritual world, the abode of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa – which is known as Kṛṣṇaloka, Goloka Vṛndāvana – is described here. In the spiritual sky there is no need of sunshine, moonshine, fire or electricity, because all the planets are self-luminous. We have only one planet in this universe, the sun, which is self-luminous, but all the planets in the spiritual sky are self-luminous. The shining effulgence of all those planets (called Vaikuṇṭhas) constitutes the shining sky known as the brahma-jyotir. Actually, the effulgence is emanating from the planet of Kṛṣṇa, Goloka Vṛndāvana. Part of that shining effulgence is covered by the mahat-tattva, the material world. Other than this, the major portion of that shining sky is full of spiritual planets, which are called Vaikuṇṭhas, chief of which is Goloka Vṛndāvana. As long as a living entity is in this dark material world, he is in conditional life, but as soon as he reaches the spiritual sky by cutting through the false, perverted tree of this material world, he becomes liberated. Then there is no chance of his coming back here. In his conditional life, the living entity considers himself to be the lord of this material world, but in his liberated state he enters into the spiritual kingdom and becomes an associate of the Supreme Lord. There he enjoys eternal bliss, eternal life, and full knowledge. One should be captivated by this information. He should desire to transfer himself to that eternal world and extricate himself from this false reflection of reality. For one who is too much attached to this material world, it is very difficult to cut that attachment, but if he takes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness there is a chance of gradually becoming detached. One has to associate himself with devotees, those who are in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. One should search out a society dedicated to Kṛṣṇa consciousness and learn how to discharge devotional service. In this way he can cut off his attachment to the material world. One cannot become detached from the attraction of the material world simply by dressing himself in saffron cloth. He must become attached to the devotional service of the Lord. Therefore one should take it very seriously that devotional service as described in the Twelfth Chapter is the only way to get out of this false representation of the real tree. In Chapter Fourteen the contamination of all kinds of processes by material nature is described. Only devotional service is described as purely transcendental. The words paramaṁ mama are very important here. Actually every nook and corner is the property of the Supreme Lord, but the spiritual world is paramam, full of six opulences. The Kaṭha Upaniṣad (2.2.15) also confirms that in the spiritual world there is no need of sunshine, moonshine or stars ( na tatra sūryo bhāti na candra-tārakam ), for the whole spiritual sky is illuminated by the internal potency of the Supreme Lord. That supreme abode can be achieved only by surrender and by no other means.
