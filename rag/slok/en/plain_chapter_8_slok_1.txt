Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
arjuna uvāca .
kiṃ tad brahma kimadhyātmaṃ kiṃ karma puruṣottama .
adhibhūtaṃ ca kiṃ proktamadhidaivaṃ kimucyate

Word Meanings:
अर्जुन - Prince Arjuna
उवाच - said
किं - what
तद् - that
ब्रह्म - Brahman
किमध्यात्मं - what is Adhyatma
किं - what
कर्म - action
पुरुषोत्तम - O Supreme Person
अधिभूतं - the material realm
च - and
किं - what
प्रोक्तमधिदैवं - declared as Adhidaiva
किमुच्यते - what is called

Life Application:
Inquire earnestly about Brahman, the Self, action, material realm, divine realm, and the Lord of sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "What is Brahman"—Arjuna inquires with the desire to comprehend the various manifestations of Lord Vishnu and their meditation methods.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Arjuna poses these questions to understand the exact philosophical definitions of Brahman, Adhyatma, Karma, Adhibhuta, and Adhidaiva introduced at the end of Chapter 7.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna said: What is that Brahman? What is Adhyatma? What is Karma, O Supreme Person? What is declared to be Adhibhuta, and what is called Adhidaiva?

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "What is Brahman"—Arjuna inquires to clarify the nature of Brahman to be attained by seekers, Adhyatma for seekers of Kaivalya, and Karma for seekers of prosperity.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: In response to Arjuna"s seven queries (Brahman, Adhyatma, Karma, Adhibhuta, Adhidaiva, Adhiyajna, and divine remembrance at death), the Lord explains that the Supreme Imperishable is Brahman, one"s essential nature is Adhyatma, and the creative offering that originates cosmic existences is Karma.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: Arjuna asks to clarify the six metaphysical terms mentioned by the Lord at the conclusion of Chapter 7 (7.29-30), inquiring into the true natures of Brahman, Adhyatma, Karma, Adhibhuta, and Adhidaiva.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Arjuna"s inquiry aims at delineating the divine opulences and forms of the object of worship, all of which primarily denote Supreme Lord Sri Hari.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: To understand the divine play, Arjuna inquires into the mystery of the seven terms spoken by the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "What is Brahman"—Arjuna poses these seven questions to hear from Lord Krishna"s holy lips the exact resolution of the terms indicated at Chapter 7"s end.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Arjuna said: What is that Brahman? What is Adhyatma? What is Karma, O Purushottama? What is called Adhibhuta, and what is Adhidaiva?

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Introduction of the seven inquiries at the beginning of Chapter 8 to remove the doubts of seekers of liberation.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Arjuna inquires to delineate the differences in object of worship and practice for the three types of devotees: the wise, seekers of Kaivalya, and seekers of wealth.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Arjuna desires to understand the seven esoteric forms of the Supreme Lord through unselfish love.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Seeker Arjuna inquires into the seven metaphysical principles to attain Brahma-Vidya (Supreme Knowledge).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Arjuna inquired: O my Lord, O Supreme Person, what is Brahman? What is the self? What are fruitive activities? What is this material manifestation? And what are the demigods? Please explain this to me.
