Life Application:
Approach your obligations with a win-win mindset, knowing that noble effort brings spiritual value regardless of outcome.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
If killed, thou shalt attain Heaven; if victorious, enjoy the kingdom of earth. Therefore arise, O Son of Kunti, and fight!

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Since Arjuna's cause in this war was righteous, fighting was proper for him from every point of view. If he dies fighting, that hero will attain heaven, and if victorious, he will enjoy the wealth and sovereignty of the earth. Even after death, a valiant warrior who fights for dharma will likewise enjoy the happiness of heaven. Therefore, the conclusion of all the arguments given so far is contained in this one sentence: rise up resolved to fight.

In the particular situation in which the Gita's teaching was given, the advice to fight is entirely justified; but in general circumstances this divine call of Sri Krishna means that a person should give up every kind of mental weakness and face the challenges arising in the struggles of life with courage and firmness, striving for victory. Thus the teaching of the Gita is useful and beneficial not for one particular individual but for the whole of humanity.

Now hear the attitude of mind with which one should fight.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anticipating the objection that if he kills his elders and others in battle he will be censured by the wise, while if he withdraws from battle he will be censured by his enemies -- a rope that binds him on both sides -- the Lord says 'in the battle, again...' Since gain is certain whether one wins or loses, rising up for the sake of battle is necessary; this is conveyed by the word 'therefore'. For a Kshatriya of pure lineage who has already engaged himself for battle, it is by no means proper to withdraw from it; this is shown by the address 'O son of Kunti'. That both victory and defeat are said to bring equal benefit, even though victory itself is not guaranteed, while the gain is certain, is explained by the word 'since'. The words 'I shall conquer' make this firm resolve altogether clear.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
If you are killed in this battle, you will attain heaven; and if you win, you will enjoy the sovereignty of the earth. Therefore, O son of Kunti, rise up resolved to fight.

Explanation -- In the sixth verse of this chapter Arjuna had said that they did not even know whether they would conquer the enemy or the enemy would conquer them. Taking up this doubt of Arjuna's, the Lord now clearly tells him that even if he is slain in battle by Karna and others he will go to heaven, and if he wins the battle he will enjoy the kingdom of the earth right here. Thus, in both cases the gain is entirely his -- it is a win-win either way. The point is that by fighting there is only gain on both counts, and by not fighting there is only loss on both counts. Therefore Arjuna should engage himself in the battle.

'Therefore rise up, O Kaunteya, resolved to fight' -- The address 'Kaunteya' (son of Kunti) here implies that when Krishna had gone to the Kauravas with a proposal of peace, Arjuna's mother Kunti had sent this very message for him, that he must fight. Hence Arjuna should not withdraw from the war, but should stand up firmly resolved to fight.

Arjuna had resolved not to fight, while the Lord had already commanded him to fight in the third verse of this chapter. This gave rise to doubt in Arjuna's mind as to whether fighting or not fighting was right. So here the Lord, in order to remove that doubt, tells him to make one firm resolve to fight, without harbouring any doubt about it.

The Lord's purport here appears to be that a person should never give up the duty that has come to him under any circumstance, but should carry out his duty with enthusiasm and diligence. It is in the performance of duty alone that the true humanity of man lies.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
If you are slain in a righteous war by enemies, you shall thery attain supreme bliss. Or, slaying the enemies, you shall enjoy this kingdom without obstacles. As the duty called war, when done without attachment to the fruits, becomes the means for winning supreme bliss, you will attain that supreme bliss. Therefore, arise, assured that engagement in war (here the duty) is the means for attaining release, which is known as man's supreme goal. This alone is suitable for you, the son of Kunti. This is the purport.

Sri Krsna then explains to the aspirant for liberation how to conduct oneself in war.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Atha ca etc., upto krta-niscavah.  Accepting what the opponent has stated, this pentad of verses is narrated as an argument : 'If your goodself prefers to abide by the generally accepted practice, even then this [fighting] must be undertaken necessarily'.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Again, by undertaking the fight with Karna and others, va, either; hatah, by being killed; prapsyasi, you will attain; svargam, heaven; or jitva, by winning over Karna and other heroes; bhoksyase, you will enjoy; mahim, the earth. The purport is that in either case you surely stand to gain. Since this is so, Kaunteya, O son of Kunti; tasmat, therefore; uttistha, rise up; krta-niscayah, with determination; yuddhaya, for fighting, i.e. with the determination, 'I shall either defeat the enemies or shall die.'

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
In answer to what was said earlier, 'nor do we know which of the two is better for us' (2.6), the Lord says 'if slain...'. You shall attain heaven, or, having conquered, you shall enjoy the earth. The sense is that in either alternative there is gain for you.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Anticipating the objection that in battle, if elders and others are killed, neutral onlookers will censure him, while if he withdraws his enemies will censure him -- a rope binding him on both sides -- the Lord shows that since gain is certain whether one wins or loses, rising up for the sake of battle alone is necessary. The first half of the verse is clear in meaning. Since you gain either way, rise up for battle resolved thus: 'I shall either conquer the enemies or die' -- for even though the outcome on either side is uncertain, the duty to fight is altogether certain. By this, the doubt raised earlier, 'nor do we know which of the two is better for us,' stands answered.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
In reply to what was said, 'nor do we know which is better for us, whether we should conquer them or they should conquer us,' the Lord says 'if slain...'. The meaning is that in either alternative there is only gain for you.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having pointed out the fault in the contrary course, the Lord now shows that engaging in battle brings gain in every way, saying 'if slain...'. Slain by Karna and others, you attain heaven; or having conquered Karna and others, you enjoy the earth. Since this is so, rise up resolved: 'I shall either conquer the enemies or die.' The address 'O son of Kunti' indicates that, having conquered the enemies and gained the kingdom, you must surely give happiness to your mother Kunti.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The verse 'if slain...' is introduced as continuing the sense of the preceding verse, by the word 'therefore'. The idea is that in either case there is gain for you. Though the words 'you shall attain' and 'you shall enjoy' are addressed to Arjuna in particular, the general reference to 'the hero' indicates that the killing of Bhishma and others, being itself the cause of their own good, is not a cause for grief. By the word 'good' (shreyas), the Lord includes at will heaven, kingdom, and other joys, up to final liberation, since mere death by itself does not constitute the highest human goal; hence the qualification 'in a righteous war' is understood. The phrase 'from that very act' means that since the killing enjoined by scripture is itself the means to the highest good, that highest good is thereby attained. The word 'heaven' here, occurring in a context dealing with immortality, refers to the supreme good itself or to its abode, as supported by scriptural texts on attaining all desires and immortality in the heavenly world, and by the earlier reference to 'sovereignty even over the gods' (2.8), showing that Arjuna's disregard for such lesser heavenly gain is being addressed here. 'Having slain the enemies' means that victory over great warriors like Bhishma, Drona and Karna is not possible without slaying them. What results jointly from 'conquering' and 'enjoying' is an unopposed kingdom, since even an established kingdom cannot be enjoyed while hostile forces remain active. To exclude the idea that Arjuna's aim ends merely in the enjoyment of a kingdom he has disregarded, it is added that for one seeking liberation, the enjoyment of a kingdom and the like is merely incidental. The purport of the address 'O son of Kunti' is that a warrior's son slain in battle is not to be mourned by his mother, just as a lion's offspring should not follow the ways of a deer's young; and that Arjuna, born of a pure Kshatriya lineage, should not allow the great tradition of dharma to be broken by him.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
If it be objected that since there is a possibility of death and suffering in battle, together with the fear of disgrace and the like, what use is there in fighting -- the Lord replies 'if slain...'. The word 'or' shows that death is only a possibility, not a certainty. If by chance you are slain, you will attain heaven; or, having conquered, even if some suffering occurs, you will enjoy the earth, and that suffering will then come to an end -- this is the sense. Therefore, firmly resolved for battle, rise up, that is, stand ready.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
In reply to what was said, 'whether we shall conquer them or they shall conquer us,' the Lord says 'if slain...'. The meaning is that for one who stands firm in battle, either heaven or a kingdom is assured, so that both alternatives are beneficial.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O son of Kuntī, either you will be killed on the battlefield and attain the heavenly planets, or you will conquer and enjoy the earthly kingdom. Therefore, get up with determination and fight.

 Even though there was no certainty of victory for Arjuna’s side, he still had to fight; for, even being killed there, he could be elevated into the heavenly planets.
