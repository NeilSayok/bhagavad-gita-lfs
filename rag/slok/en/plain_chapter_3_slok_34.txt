Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
indriyasyendriyasyārthe rāgadveṣau vyavasthitau .
tayorna vaśamāgacchettau hyasya paripanthinau

Word Meanings:
इन्द्रियस्येन्द्रियस्यार्थे - in the object of each sense
रागद्वेषौ - attachment and aversion
व्यवस्थितौ - are established
तयोर्न - of them not
वशमागच्छेत्तौ - should come under sway, they
ह्यस्य - indeed of him
परिपन्थिनौ - two obstacles / foes

Life Application:
Watch for sudden cravings or intense dislikes today, refusing to let impulse dictate your decisions and conduct.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Attraction and aversion for the objects of each sense are seated in the senses; let none come under their sway, for they are his foes.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Attraction and aversion (Ragadveshau) for the objects of each sense are seated inherently in the senses; let no man come under their sway (Tayor vasham na agacchet), for they are verily his highway robbers or foes (Tau asya paripanthinau).

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Sense attraction and repulsion are the main robbers on the soul"s path to Mukti; devotion to Lord Vishnu protects against them.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Sense-attractions and aversions act as highway robbers (Paripanthinau) on the spiritual path; one must overcome them through discrimination.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Attraction and aversion for the objects of each sense are seated in the senses; let none come under their sway, for they are his foes.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Though attraction and aversion naturally reside in senses, a seeker must never succumb to them, but act dedicated to the Divine.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Sadrsam etc. and Indriyasya etc. Attraction and aversion reside in senses toward their objects; a wise man, not succumbing to them, remains free in action.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Attraction and aversion for the objects of each sense are seated in the senses; let none come under their sway, for they are his foes"—identifies Raga and Dvesha as the real obstacles (*Paripanthin*) that must be transcended through wisdom.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Renunciation of attraction and aversion constitutes the authentic practice and discipline of Karma-yoga.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A devotee with unswerving attachment to Krishna"s lotus feet easily overcomes the enemies of sensory attraction and aversion.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Raga and Dvesha are the highway robbers that steal the seeker"s intellect; hence they must be subdued.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing why attraction and aversion inherent in the senses act as foes on the spiritual journey.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A seeker surrendered to Vasudeva utterly defeats sense attraction and aversion via God-dedication.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When a seeker dissolves sense-attractions and aversions in Krishna"s lotus feet, his life becomes holy.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Attachment and aversion are the direct enemies diverting the seeker from the path of liberation.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
There are principles to regulate attachment and aversion pertaining to the senses and their objects. One should not come under the control of such attachment and aversion, because they are stumbling blocks on the path of self-realization.
