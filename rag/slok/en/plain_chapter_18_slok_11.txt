Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
No embodied living being, even if it is a single-celled organism, can abandon all actions. Action indeed is the symbol or symptom of life. Action itself is the fragrance of the flower of life; where there is no action, life is considered to be at an end. Remaining without performing some action is also in itself an action. Physical and mental activities continue to occur until death. Therefore, for us embodied beings, the alternative of whether we should perform actions or not is not even possible. But concerning which actions we should perform and how we should perform them, choices are certainly possible. According to the teaching of the Gita, we should perform our obligatory duties with an attitude of dedication to God. Ignorant people identify with the body and other non-Self limitations (upadhis) as their own nature, become attached to them, and consider themselves to be the doer of their actions. Therefore, such people cannot perform Sattvik abandonment. Lord Krishna's instruction to such people is that they should at least abandon attachment to the fruits of actions, and perform actions with labor, enthusiasm, and efficiency. The person who renounces the fruits of actions is indeed a man of renunciation, and not the person who abandons actions. Listen to what the purpose of this abandonment is:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Having stated the possibility of the renunciation of all actions in the case of one who has Self-knowledge, he introduces the subsequent verse as stating the reason for its impossibility in the case of one devoid of it: "He, however...". The word "dehabhrit" (embodied) means one whose conception of agency in the self is not negated, i.e., an ignorant person; "as such" (tathā) refers to his state, meaning "because of that". To show this meaning, the construction is that he states the reason for the impossibility of the renunciation of all actions by an ignorant person. The word "since" (yasmāt) is connected with the subsequent word "therefore" (tasmāt). Raising the doubt: "Since a man of discrimination also has a body and is thus not different in being a body-wearer, he too might have eligibility for action", he says: "for not...". That is to say: from eligibility for agency and the performance of action preceded by that. The meaning of the word "hence" (ataḥ) is that even though the wise man has a body, he lacks identification with it. Taking the impossibility of the renunciation of all actions by the ignorant person as the reason, he states the result: "Therefore...". To the objection, "It is improper to call one who performs actions a renouncer", he says: "even though he is active...". The meaning is: calling even an active person a renouncer on account of the renunciation of fruits is for the purpose of praising the renunciation of fruits. Raising the question, "For whom then is the renunciation of all actions possible?", he concludes what was said, namely: "Therefore, in the case of one who possesses discrimination, dispassion, etc., and is free from body-identification..."

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Because it is not possible for an embodied being to abandon actions entirely. Therefore, he who renounces the fruits of actions is indeed a man of renunciation — so it is said.

Commentary — na hi dehabhṛtā śakyaṃ tyaktuṃ karmāṇyaśeṣataḥ — It is not possible for embodied beings, i.e., those who identify with the body, to abandon actions entirely, because the body is an effect of Nature (prakriti) and Nature is active by itself. Therefore, how can one who has identification (oneness) with the body remain devoid of action? Yes, it is possible for a person to leave actions like sacrifice, charity, austerity, pilgrimage, etc., but how can he leave essential bodily activities like eating, drinking, walking, coming, going, sitting, standing, sleeping, waking, etc.? Secondly, abandoning the relationship with actions from within is indeed the real abandonment. The relationship cannot be abandoned from the outside. Even if the relationship is abandoned externally, how long will it remain abandoned? For instance, if someone goes into Samadhi, at that time the connection with external actions is severed. But Samadhi is also an activity, an action, because the connection with the causal body born of Nature remains in it. Therefore, there is also awakening (vyutthāna) from Samadhi. No embodied person can sever his relationship with actions physically (Gita 3.5). Actionlessness (Yoganistha) is not attained without initiating actions, and perfection (Sankhyanistha) is not attained merely by abandoning actions (Gita 3.4).
Crucial point — The Purusha (conscious Self) is eternally changeless and uniform, whereas Nature (prakriti) is subject to modifications and is ever-changing. That which is highly active is called Prakriti — "prakarṣeṇa karaṇaṃ iti prakṛtiḥ". As long as the Purusha considers itself associated (identified) with the body, which is the effect of Nature, it can never abandon actions entirely. The reason is: due to the presence of egoism and possessiveness ("I" and "mine") in the body, a person considers every activity performed by the body as his own activity; therefore, he can never be actionless under any circumstances. Secondly, only the Purusha has established a relationship with Nature. Nature has not established a relationship with the Purusha. Where discrimination exists, the Purusha, ignoring that discrimination, assumed a relationship with Nature, i.e., accepted the relationship as real. Bondage has arisen only from accepting the relationship as real. That relationship is of two kinds: considering oneself to be the body and considering the body to be one's own. Considering oneself to be the body leads to egoism (ahanta), and considering the body to be one's own leads to possessiveness (mamata). The deepening of this relationship in the form of egoism and possessiveness is the very nature of an embodied being (dehabhrit). Such an embodied person cannot abandon actions entirely.
yastu karmaphalatyāgī sa tyāgītyabhidhīyate — He who does not keep any relationship with any action or fruit is indeed a man of renunciation. As long as a person keeps a relationship with the auspicious and inauspicious, the good and bad, he is not a man of renunciation. Whatever natural action and object this person considers to be his own, he develops fondness for it. That fondness is called attachment. This attachment is called attachment to action (karmasakti) with respect to present actions, and attachment to fruits (phalasakti) with respect to the future fruits. When a person makes the renunciation of fruits his goal, then all his actions start happening for the welfare of the world, not for himself. The reason is that he understands very well that all the materials for performing actions are obtained from the world and belong to the world, not to himself. These actions also have a beginning and an end, and their fruit also is born and destroyed, but he himself is eternally changeless; he is neither born, nor destroyed, nor ever modified. When such discrimination arises, the abandonment of the desire for fruits is accomplished easily. In abandoning the fruit, pride regarding that discrimination never arises in the person, because both the action and its fruit are separating from us every moment; therefore, where is our relationship with them in reality? Therefore, the Lord says that he who is a renouncer of the fruits of actions is called a man of renunciation. The changeless never had, does not have, cannot have, and has no possibility of having a relationship with the changing fruit of action. The relationship was merely assumed due to non-discrimination. With the removal of that non-discrimination, the person's designation, i.e., his name, becomes a renouncer — "sa tyāgītyabhidhīyate". In reference to an assumed relationship, a story is told as an illustration. A person leaves his home and family and becomes a monk (sadhu-sannyasi) with a sincere heart; after that, no matter how much progress or decline his family members experience, or if all of them die and no trace of them remains, still it has no effect on him. Consider here that the relationship which that person had with the family was accepted from both sides, i.e., he considered the family as his own and the family considered him as their own. But the relationship between the Purusha and Nature is accepted only from the side of the Purusha, not from the side of Nature. When even a relationship accepted from both sides (between the individual and the family) is severed when left from one side, what to say of the relationship accepted only from one side (between the Purusha and Nature) being severed when left?
Relationship — In the previous verse it was said that only the renouncer of the fruits of actions is in reality a man of renunciation. What happens if a person does not renounce the fruits of actions? This is explained in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Niscayam etc.  upto abhidhiyate.  The conclusion here is  this :  Due to the manifoldness of the nature of the Strands, that have been defined earlier, the act of relinishment itself is performed with a certain mental disposition which is a modification of the Sattva, the Rajas and the Tamas (the Strands).  Because it reflects  (is contaminated by)  the nature of the person having the same (the said mental dispositon),  what is called the real (unalloyed)  relinishment is the performance of the actions  by the knowers of the Supreme Brahman by giving up desire to achieve fruits and by avoiding the craving and hatred on account of their eanimity to [the pairs of opposites like]  success and failure etc.  That is why [the Bhagavat] says :  'By the act of relinishment born of the Rajas or of the Tamas  (Strands), no connection with the fruit  [of relinishment] is attained'.  However, for an act of relinishing, born of the Sattva (Strand), there is the fruit in the form of honouring the purport of the scriptures.  The application of the term  'relinishment'  stands to reason, in fact, only in the case of a sage who has relinished his holding on the multitude of the Strands.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He states the characteristics of such a person in: "na dveṣṭi...". The abandoner filled with Sattva, who is wise, does not hate inauspicious action which has undesirable fruit, and does not cling to auspicious action which has desirable fruits like heaven, etc., because he has abandoned all fruits other than the happiness of the Self and has abandoned agency. Here, inauspicious action is spoken of with reference to a negligent person; for it is heard that one who is negligent and has not turned away from bad conduct does not attain the happiness of the Self even through knowledge: "But one who has not ceased from bad conduct, who is not tranquil, who is not composed, or whose mind is not at peace, cannot attain Him even by knowledge" (Katha Upanishad 1.2.24). Therefore, the abandonment of agency, attachment, and fruits in action is the scriptural abandonment, not the abandonment of actions in their physical form. He explains this in: "na hi...". In the sense that actions cannot be abandoned entirely by one who bears a body, the word "śakyam" is an indeclinable. Because actions meant for the maintenance of the body, such as eating, drinking, and their associations, are unavoidable; and for that purpose, actions like the great sacrifices are also unavoidable. Among those, he who is a renouncer of the fruits of sacrifices and other actions — where "fruit" is an implication for agency and possessiveness as well — is called a "tyagi" in scriptural passages like "Through abandonment alone some attained immortality" (Mahanarayana Upanishad 10.5, Kaivalya Upanishad 2).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Thus, it has been said that the renunciation of all actions is possible in the case of one who possesses Self-knowledge, because of the absence of likes and dislikes which are the causes of engagement in action. Now, the reason for the impossibility of the renunciation of actions in the case of an ignorant person is stated: "na hi...". He who bears (bibharti) the body—which is the aggregate of the gross and subtle bodies and senses, the locus of agency and enjoyment, and is of the form of class and stage of life which is the cause of eligibility for action—due to the unnegated identification "I am a man", "I am a Brahmana", "I am a householder", etc.; who, under the influence of beginningless impressions of ignorance, sees it as real although it is unreal, and as non-different from himself although it is different from himself, and thus holds and nourishes it—such a "body-wearer" is one who has body-identification which is the cause of unnegated eligibility for actions. By such an embodied person who is devoid of discriminative knowledge, and who is constantly engaged in actions due to the abundance of likes and dislikes which are the causes of engagement in action, actions cannot indeed be abandoned entirely, i.e., completely. Because when the collection of causes is present, the abandonment of the effect is impossible. Therefore, that ignorant competent person who, even while performing actions for the purification of Sattva, by the grace of the Lord, renounces the fruits of those actions; where the word "tu" is for the purpose of indicating the rarity of such a person; he is called a man of renunciation. This is by figurative designation (gauni vritti) for the purpose of praise, even though he is not a true renouncer. But the complete renunciation of all actions can be performed by an embodied being only by virtue of being a knower of the supreme Truth; hence, he alone is a renouncer in the primary sense. Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Objection: "But instead of such renunciation of the fruits of actions, the renunciation of all actions is better; for in that case, due to the absence of the distraction of actions, steadfastness in Knowledge is easily accomplished." To this He says: "na...". It is indeed not possible for an embodied being, i.e., one who has body-identification, to abandon all actions completely. That was stated in: "For no one can remain even for a moment without performing action..." (Gita 3.5), etc. Therefore, he who, while performing actions, is a renouncer of the fruits of actions, he alone is called a renouncer in the primary sense.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Thus, having shown that one who possesses Sattvik abandonment and a purified mind has eligibility for the renunciation of all actions, i.e., primary sannyasa; and because the complete abandonment of actions is impossible for a competent person who has body-identification, i.e., an ignorant body-wearer who has the firm conviction "I am the doer" because of his unnegated conception of agency in the self; to show that he has eligibility only for performing ordained actions by renouncing the fruits of actions and not for abandoning them, He says: "na hi...". Indeed (hi), because by a "body-wearer"—one who bears, i.e., holds the body as one's own self, i.e., an ignorant person possessing body-identification—actions cannot be abandoned, i.e., renounced, entirely (aśeṣataḥ), i.e., completely. Therefore, that ignorant competent body-wearer who performs ordained actions and renounces the fruits of actions—being a renouncer only of the desire for fruits of actions—he is called a man of renunciation. This is said by way of praise: "even though he is active, he is a renouncer". And thus, the idea is that the complete renunciation of all actions can be performed only by a knower of the supreme Truth who is free from body-identification.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
With the explanation of the reasoning presented in "of the obligatory..." (18.7) etc., the threefold Sattvik abandonment described earlier by the indication of the abandonment of fruits is concluded in the verse "na hi...". He (Sri Ramanuja) states this: "He says this...". Here, "embodied" (dehabhritā) is not a reference to living beings in general, because that would be useless. Therefore, the etymological meaning is intended as the cause of the impossibility of the physical abandonment of actions; with this idea he (Sri Ramanuja) says: "by one who maintains a body". The word "śakyam" (possible) is in the neuter singular because it refers to the act of abandoning, or because it refers to the general sense. He explains the impossibility caused by being embodied: "for those actions meant for body-maintenance...". Objection: "Let activities like earning money etc., which are associated with that, be worldly actions; what have they to do with scriptural actions?" To this he says: "and for that purpose...". To remove the doubt of the physical abandonment of actions which arises from the literal sense of the Shruti, the words starting with "he who, on the other hand..." are spoken; with this idea he says: "he who...". To satisfy the expectation of "by whom is he called?", he shows the scriptural passages like "Through abandonment alone...". To confirm that this is illustrative for the purpose of concluding the topic begun, he says: "threefold..."

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Objection: "But what is the purpose of performing action if the fruits of action are renounced?" To this He says: "na hi...". It is not possible for an embodied being, i.e., one who has body-superimposition, to abandon actions entirely. The word "hi" (indeed) shows that this meaning is proper. Since there is expectation of fruits and expectation of the world when body-superimposition is present, how can abandonment be performed? Therefore, he who is a renouncer of the fruits of actions, i.e., who has no desire for the fruits of the actions performed, is called a man of renunciation. Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Recalling secondary Sattvik abandonment itself, He states its purpose in two verses: "na hi...". Indeed (hi), because by an embodied being, i.e., one who has body-identification, actions cannot be abandoned entirely, i.e., it is impossible. Because it would lead to the disruption of the maintenance of life. Therefore, he who is qualified and is of the nature of renouncing the fruits of actions — where the word "tu" is in the sense of "only" — he alone is called a man of renunciation. But he who is a knower of the supreme Truth and is capable of abandoning actions entirely is the primary renouncer. Such is the meaning.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
It is indeed impossible for an embodied being to give up all activities. But he who renounces the fruits of action is called one who has truly renounced.

 It is said in Bhagavad-gītā that one can never give up work at any time. Therefore he who works for Kṛṣṇa and does not enjoy the fruitive results, who offers everything to Kṛṣṇa, is actually a renouncer. There are many members of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness who work very hard in their office or in the factory or some other place, and whatever they earn they give to the Society. Such highly elevated souls are actually sannyāsīs and are situated in the renounced order of life. It is clearly outlined here how to renounce the fruits of work and for what purpose fruits should be renounced.
