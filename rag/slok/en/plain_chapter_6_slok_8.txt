Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
jñānavijñānatṛptātmā kūṭastho vijitendriyaḥ .
yukta ityucyate yogī samaloṣṭāśmakāñcanaḥ

Word Meanings:
ज्ञानविज्ञानतृप्तात्मा - satisfied with knowledge and wisdom
कूटस्थो - unshakeable
विजितेन्द्रियः - conqueror of senses
युक्त - united in yoga
इत्युच्यते - is said to be
योगी - yogi
समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः - regarding clay, stone and gold as equal

Life Application:
Seek inner contentment through wisdom, viewing a clod of earth, a stone, and gold with equal detachment.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with knowledge and wisdom, who is unwavering, who has conquered his senses, and to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be harmonized.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with spiritual knowledge and realization, who is unwavering, who has conquered his senses, and to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
A Yogi whose mind is satisfied with sacred knowledge and direct realization, who remains unwavering like a rock, who has mastered his senses, and to whom a clod of earth, a stone, and gold are equal, is declared to be fully self-realized.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Satisfied with Knowledge and Wisdom"—a devotee fulfilled through scriptural truth and direct experience of Lord Vishnu views all worldly wealth (gold or clay) with equal vision.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The yogi enriched with scriptural learning (Jnana) and direct Self-realization (Vijnana) remains unwavering like an anvil, perceiving gold and stone with equal vision.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with knowledge and wisdom, who is unwavering, who has conquered his senses, and to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be harmonized.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: One satisfied with essential knowledge of the Self and its direct experience, unwavering and equal-minded toward gold and clay, is declared Yogarudha.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Supreme Self of him who has conquered himself and is deeply tranquil remains perfectly steadfast amid heat and cold, pleasure and pain, honor and dishonor. The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with spiritual knowledge and realization, who is unwavering and master of his senses, to whom a clod of earth, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be fully integrated.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with knowledge and wisdom, who is unwavering, who has conquered his senses, and to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be harmonized"—outlines four cardinal attributes of the liberated yogi: Jnana-Vijnana satisfaction, unwavering poise (Kutastha), sense conquest, and equal vision.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Equal vision toward gold, stone, and earth is the mature fruit of divine Knowledge; one possessing such vision is the true conqueror of senses.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: For the devotee satisfied in Krishna-nectar, gold and dirt are equally material; he delights solely in the Lord"s divine sports.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Equal toward clay, stone, and gold"—equal vision arises only when all phenomenal objects are ascertained to be unreal (Mithya).

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: The Yogi whose mind is satisfied with spiritual knowledge and realization, who is unwavering, who has conquered his senses, and to whom a clod, a stone, and gold are equal, is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Authoritative scriptural analysis of being fulfilled in knowledge-wisdom and possessing equal vision toward clay, stone, and gold.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The yogi experiencing the supernatural bliss of the Self remains completely unattached to worldly treasures like gold.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the soul experiences Lord Krishna"s all-pervading eternal form, all distinctions among material objects dissolve.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Satisfied with Knowledge and Realization"—the mind fulfilled through scriptural truth and Self-realization becomes established in the Immutable State.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A person is said to be established in self-realization, and is called a yogi [or mystic], when he is fully satisfied by virtue of acquired knowledge and realization. Such a person is situated in transcendence and is self-controlled. He sees everything—whether it be pebbles, stones or gold—as the same.
