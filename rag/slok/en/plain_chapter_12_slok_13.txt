Bear no ill will toward any creature, be friendly, compassionate, free from possessiveness and ego, equal in pain and pleasure, forgiving.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Now, to introduce the verse starting with 'adveṣṭā' etc., he recalls the past context: 'Here also...', etc. If there were absolute identity, there would be no Yoga of concentrating the mind on God, because in absolute identity the relation of the meditator and the object of meditation does not exist. Neither is the performance of action or the abandonment of its fruits possible in absolute identity, due to their mutual contradiction. He says 'Atha' (now) to show that by the strength of the Lord's statement, one attains the vision of non-difference through Karma Yoga etc. Just as the worshipper of the Imperishable cannot practice Karma Yoga, the impossibility of the practice of the worship of the Imperishable for a Karma Yogi is also shown: 'Tathā' (similarly). The worshippers of the Imperishable, who are established in right knowledge, reach the Lord Himself according to their knowledge; but not so the performers of actions, who are not fit for direct realization. Hence, the accomplishment of the worship of the Imperishable is not for the performers of action. Moreover, the worship of the Imperishable and the performance of action cannot coexist in one place. He says: 'Akṣara' (the Imperishable). If it is asked: since all others also are not different from Ishvara just like the worshipper of the Imperishable, why is it dependent on that? To this he replies: 'Yadi' (if). He gives another reason why Karma Yoga and the worship of the Imperishable cannot coexist: 'Yasmācca' (and because), referring to the text 'kuru karmaiva' (perform action indeed) etc. Furthermore, the worshipper of the Imperishable knows himself to be Ishvara from the scriptural sentences, and he does not experience agency as a subordinate element in action, because being subordinate and being Ishvara are contradictory in the same subject. Therefore, the worship of the Imperishable and the performance of action cannot coexist. He says: 'Naca' (and not). Having shown the impossibility of the coexistence or sequential occurrence of the worship of the Imperishable and Karma Yoga in one place, he states the conclusion: 'Tasmāt' (therefore). Since the group of virtues to be described cannot be fully practiced by ignorant performers of action, this is stated only for those established in the Imperishable. However, the non-contradictory portion is accepted as applicable to all. Lest one suspect that even a wise man would hate someone who causes him pain, he says: 'Ātmanaḥ' (of the self). The reason is: 'Sarvāṇi' (all). The phrase 'of all beings' is connected with both. 'Free from the idea of mine' applies to the body as well. He states the freedom from egoism born of vows, self-study, etc. by the word: 'Nirgata' (departed).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Free from malice towards all beings, friendly (loving) and compassionate to all, free from mineness, free from egoism, balanced in the attainment of pleasure and pain, forgiving, ever content, yogi, self-controlled, possessed of firm conviction, with mind and intellect dedicated to Me, he who is such a devotee of mine is dear to Me.

Commentary:
'Adveṣṭā sarvabhūtānām'—
There are two kinds of doers of harm: (1) those who create obstacles in obtaining desired things, i.e., wealth, honor, respect, etc., and (2) those who bring contact with undesired objects, actions, persons, events, etc. No matter how much or what kind of behavior anyone does against the devotee's body, mind, intellect, senses, and principles—be it creating obstacles in obtaining the desired, or causing any kind of financial or physical harm—there is never even the slightest malice in the devotee's heart towards them. The reason is that he sees his Lord alone pervading every creature. In such a state, if he opposes, whom should he oppose?—'Seeing the world as full of my Lord, with whom should I oppose?' (Ramacharitamanas 7.112 kh). Not only this, he considers all the actions of those who harm him as the grace-filled, auspicious dispensation of the Lord!

Every creature in its true nature is a part of God. Therefore, having even a little malice towards any creature is malice towards God Himself. Hence, while having malice towards any creature, one cannot have non-difference and exclusive love for God. Only by being free from malice towards every creature can one have complete love for God. Therefore, in a devotee, there is a total absence of malice towards every creature.

'Maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca'—
Not only is there an absolute absence of malice towards all creatures in the devotee's heart, but also because of the presence of God in all beings, his behavior towards all is filled with friendship and compassion. God is the friend of all creatures—'Suhṛdaṁ sarvabhūtānām' (Gita 5.29). Because the nature of God descends into the devotee, the devotee also becomes the friend of all living beings—'Suhṛdaḥ sarvadehinām' (Srimad Bhagavatam 3.25.21). Therefore, the devotee also has a natural feeling of friendship and compassion towards all creatures without any self-interest.
'He who does good to the world without any reason, O enemy of demons, you and your servants are like that.' (Ramacharitamanas 7.47.3)
The devotee behaves friendly even towards those who harm him, because his feeling is that the one who harms has only presented the Lord's dispensation in the form of harm. Therefore, whatever he has done, he has done it right for me. Because the Lord's dispensation is always auspicious. Not only this, the devotee believes that the one who harms me (by becoming an instrument in the harm) is destroying my past sins; therefore, he is especially worthy of respect.
Every seeker has and should have this feeling in his mind that the one who harms him is purifying him by making him experience the fruit of his past sins. When even an ordinary seeker has feelings of friendship and compassion towards the one who harms him, then what to say of a perfected devotee! A perfected devotee has an extraordinary feeling of friendship and compassion not only towards him but towards all creatures.
In the Patanjali Yoga Sutras, four causes of purification of the mind are mentioned:
'maitrī-karuṇā-mudito-pekṣāṇāṁ sukha-duḥkha-puṇyāpuṇya-viṣayāṇāṁ bhāvanātaś-citta-prasādanam' (1.33)
'Friendliness toward the happy, compassion toward the miserable, joy toward the virtuous, and indifference toward the wicked clarify the mind.' But the Lord has divided these four causes into two: 'Maitraḥ ca karuṇaḥ.' The implication is that the perfected devotee has a feeling of 'friendliness' towards the happy and virtuous, and a feeling of 'compassion' towards the miserable and wicked.
Rather than indifference toward the one who inflicts pain, there should be compassion towards him, because the one who suffers is getting freed from sins (by enjoying the fruit of old sins), but the one who inflicts pain is committing new sins. Therefore, the one who inflicts pain is a special object of compassion.

'Nirmamaḥ'—
Although the devotee has a natural feeling of friendship and compassion towards all creatures, yet he does not have even the slightest attachment (sense of 'mine') towards anyone. Attachment (the feeling of 'mine') to creatures and objects is what binds man to this world. The devotee is completely free from this attachment. He does not have any attachment even to his own body, senses, mind, and intellect. The mistake that a seeker makes is that he tries to remove attachment from creatures and objects, but does not pay special attention to removing attachment from his own body, mind, intellect, and senses. This is why he cannot be completely free from attachment.

'Nirahaṅkāraḥ'—
Egoism arises from considering material objects like the body and senses as one's own nature. Since the devotee does not have the slightest identification of 'I' with his body etc., and because of his experience of his eternal relationship with the Lord alone, sublime, divine, and extraordinary virtues naturally manifest in his heart. Even these virtues he does not consider as his own, but rather (being divine wealth) considers them as the Lord's. Because of being of the 'Sat' (Supreme Self), these qualities are called 'Sadguṇa' (virtues). In such a state, how can a devotee consider them as his own! Therefore, he is completely free from egoism.

'Samaduḥkhasukhaḥ'—
The devotee remains equal in the attainment of pleasure and pain, i.e., favorable and unfavorable situations cannot produce modifications like attachment-aversion, joy-grief, etc. in his heart.
In the Gita, the term 'sukha-duḥkha' has come for the favorable and unfavorable circumstances (which are the causes of producing pleasure and pain) as well as for the modifications of joy and grief occurring in the heart.
Favorable and unfavorable circumstances bind man only by making him happy or miserable. Therefore, being equal in pleasure and pain means—not having modifications of joy and grief in oneself upon the arrival of favorable or unfavorable circumstances.
When a devotee is associated with or dissociated from persons, objects, circumstances, events, etc., which are favorable or unfavorable to his body, senses, mind, principles, etc., he does have the 'knowledge' of favorableness and unfavorableness, but no 'modification' of joy and grief arises in his heart. Here it should be understood that having knowledge of a situation is not a defect in itself, rather, the arising of a modification in the heart from it is the defect. The devotee is completely free from modifications like attachment-aversion, joy-grief, etc. For example, when there is some disease in the devotee's body according to his destiny, he will have the knowledge (experience) of physical pain, but there will be no modification of any kind in his heart.

'Kṣamī'—
He who does not desire to give any kind of punishment to the one who commits an offense against him, and forgives him, is called 'kṣamī'.
By giving the word 'adveṣṭā' first among the characteristics of a devotee, the Lord showed the absence of malice in the devotee towards the offender. Now, here by the word 'kṣamī', He shows that the devotee has such a feeling towards the offender that he should not receive punishment from God or anyone else. Such forgiveness is a special characteristic of a devotee.

'Santuṣṭaḥ satatam'—
A living being has a satisfaction in the contact with favorable persons, objects, events, circumstances, etc., and in the separation from unfavorable persons, objects, events, circumstances, etc. Being derived from material and transient objects, this satisfaction cannot remain permanent. Because he himself is eternal, the living being gets real and permanent satisfaction only from the realization of the eternal God.
Upon attaining God, the devotee remains ever satisfied because he is never separated from God, nor does he have any need for the transient world. Therefore, there remains no reason for his dissatisfaction. Because of this satisfaction, he does not hold even the slightest value for any material object of the world.
By placing the word 'satatam' (always) with 'santuṣṭaḥ', the Lord has pointed towards that eternal satisfaction of the devotee, in which there is neither any change nor any possibility of change. A great soul who has achieved perfection through any path of Yoga—Karma Yoga, Jnana Yoga, or Bhakti Yoga—always has this satisfaction (which is real).

'Yogī'—
Here, the one who has attained God through Bhakti Yoga (who is eternally united with God) is called a Yogi.
In reality, no creature is ever separated from God, was never separated, can never be, and it is not even possible. He who has experienced this reality is a Yogi.

'Yatātmā'—
He who has complete control over his body including the mind, intellect, and senses, is a 'yatātmā'. A perfected devotee does not have to control his mind, intellect, etc.; rather, they naturally remain under his control. Therefore, there is no possibility of any sense-born vice or misconduct entering him.
In reality, the mind, intellect, and senses are naturally meant for walking on the right path, but due to attachment with the world, they go astray. The devotee does not have even the slightest attachment with the world, therefore his mind, intellect, and senses are entirely under his control. Hence, his every action is an ideal for others.
It is observed that the senses of virtuous people walking on the path of justice never go towards the wrong path. For example, when King Dushyanta's mind goes towards Shakuntala, he becomes firmly convinced that she is a Kshatriya maiden, not a Brahmana maiden. According to the poet Kalidasa, where there is doubt, the inclination of the inner self of virtuous people is the proof—'satāṁ hi sandeha-padeṣu vastuṣu pramāṇam antaḥkaraṇa-pravṛttayaḥ' (Abhijnanashakuntalam 1.21).
When even the inclination of the senses of a just and virtuous man does not naturally go towards the wrong path, then how can the mind, intellect, and senses of a perfected devotee (who never deviates from the law of righteousness in any condition) go towards the wrong path?

'Dṛḍhaniścayaḥ'—
In the vision of a perfected great soul, there is a complete absence of the independent existence of the world. In his intellect, there is only the unwavering existence of the one God. Therefore, there is no defect of misconception (seeing the transient world as permanent) in his intellect. He experiences his eternally established relationship with God alone. Therefore, he has a firm conviction in God. This conviction of his is not in the intellect, but in his own self, whose reflection is felt in the intellect.
Only by accepting the independent existence of the world or by assuming one's relationship with it does the defect of misconception and doubt arise in the intellect. An intellect filled with misconception and doubt is never stable. The difference between a wise man and an ignorant man lies only in the conviction of the intellect; in essence, both are the same. In the intellect of the ignorant, the existence and importance of the world remain, but in the intellect of the perfected devotee, except for the one God, neither does the independent existence of any object of the world remain nor does it have any importance. Therefore, his intellect is completely free from the defects of misconception and doubt, and he has a firm conviction in God alone.

'Mayyarpitamanobuddhiḥ'—
When a seeker makes the attainment of God alone his sole purpose and becomes God's own (which he is in reality), then his mind and intellect also naturally get fixed in God. Then what to say of the mind and intellect of a perfected devotee being dedicated to God!
Where there is love, man's mind naturally goes there, and in what man considers superior by principle, his intellect naturally gets fixed. For a devotee, there is nothing more beloved and superior than God. The devotee does not even consider his control over his mind and intellect. He considers them entirely as the Lord's. Therefore, his mind and intellect naturally remain fixed in God.

'Yaḥ madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ'—
To God, everyone is dear, but the devotee's love is nowhere else except in God. In such a situation, according to the promise: 'ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāns-tathaiva bhajāmyaham' (Gita 4.11), the devotee is also dear to God.

Relationship—
The second section of the characteristics of a perfected devotee, which describes six characteristics, comes in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
In these and succeeding verses the Lord mentions the nature of the Karma Yogi who adores Him through his works. In other words the Bhakti element in Karma Yoga is emphasised. He never hates any being even though they hate him and do him wrong. For he thinks that the Lord impels these beings to hate him and do him wrong for atoning for his transgressions. He is 'friendly', evincing a friendly disposition towards all beings whether they hate him or do him wrong. He is 'compassionate', evincing compassion towards their sufferings. He is free from the 'feeling of mine,' i.e., he is not possessive with regard to his body, senses and all things associated with them. He is free from the feeling of 'I', i.e., is free from the delusion that his body is the self. Therefore, 'pain and pleasure are the same to him,' i.e., he is free from distress and delight resulting from pain and pleasure arising from his deeds. He is 'enduring', unaffected even by those two (i.e., pleasure and pain) due to the inevitable contact of sense-objects. He is 'content', namely, satisfied with whatever chance may bring him for the sustenance of his body. He 'ever meditates,' i.e., is constantly intent on contemplating on the self as separate from the body. He is 'self-restrained', namely, he controls the activities of his mind. He is of 'firm conviction' regarding the meanings taught in the science of the self. His 'mind and reason are dedicated to Me' i.e., his mind and reason are dedicated to Me in the form 'Bhagavan Vasudeva alone is propitiated by disinterested activities, and when duly propitiated, He wil bring about for me the direct vision of the self.' Such a devotee of mine, i.e., who works in this manner as a Karma Yogin, is dear to Me.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Thus, in six verses, the Lord speaks of the thirty-two characteristics of a devotee possessing such virtues, which indicate spiritual nourishment (Pushti), as being dear to Him, due to the grace and affection of the Lord endowed with six divine attributes. Although in the path of the Lord, association with saints (Satsanga) etc. is the cause of devotion to the Lord, yet the causeless grace of the Lord alone is the real cause for that devotee. As stated in the Bhagavatam (10.51.54) in the words of the servant of the Lord: 'When the wandering of a soul in the cycle of birth and death comes to an end, O Achyuta, then association with saints occurs...' Here, the word 'bhava' is interpreted as 'averting worldly existence', showing that grace alone is the cause, because the devotee is fundamentally blessed by that grace. He begins with 'Adveṣṭā' (non-hating).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Thus, having shown that the worship of the sagoṇa form of the Lord is easier for the less-qualified seekers by depicting the worship of the Imperishable as extremely difficult for them, Bhagavan Vasudeva prescribed other means according to their capacity. The purpose of prescribing these means is to enable them to enter the path of the worship of the Imperishable, which yields the final result, when all obstacles are removed. As has been said: 'For those dull-witted seekers who are incapable of directly realizing the attributeless supreme Brahman, they are shown compassion through descriptions of the saguna form. Once their minds are controlled through devotion to the saguna Brahman, the same attributeless Brahman manifests itself, free from all limiting adjuncts.' Bhagavan Patanjali also has said: 'Samadhi is attained by devotion to God' (Yoga Sutra 1.23) and 'From that comes the realization of the individual self and the removal of obstacles' (Yoga Sutra 1.29). Here, 'from that' means from devotion to God. Therefore, the criticism of the worship of the Imperishable is only for praising the worship of the saguna form, and not for discarding it, just like the criticism of the home in the morning to praise the home in the evening. A criticism does not intend to merely condemn the condemnable, but rather to praise what is to be enjoined. Therefore, the worshippers of the Imperishable are indeed, in the truest sense, the best among yogis. Bhagavan, who is the supreme well-wisher, wishing to make Arjuna understand that their knowledge and virtues should be followed after acquiring fitness, praises the worshippers of the Imperishable, who see non-difference and are fulfilled, in seven verses starting with 'Adveṣṭā': Seeing all beings as his own Self, he has no feeling of opposition even to the cause of his own sorrow, hence he is 'not a hater of any being'. Rather, he is 'friendly' (loving). As he is 'compassionate' (full of mercy towards the suffering), he is the giver of fearlessness to all beings. This means he is a Paramahamsa Parivrajaka (sannyasi). He is 'free from the sense of mine' (nirmama) even with respect to his body, and 'free from egoism' (nirahankara), having risen above the ego born of pedigree, study, etc. He is one to whom pleasure and pain are equal because they do not excite aversion and attachment. Therefore, he is 'forgiving' (ksami), not undergoing any change of state even when abused or assaulted.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
In eight verses starting with 'Adveṣṭā', the Lord speaks of the virtues of such a devotee which quickly lead to the grace of the Supreme Lord. He is free from hatred towards all beings in a fitting manner. He is 'friendly' (maitra) and 'compassionate' (karuṇa); meaning, he is free from hatred towards superiors, behaves friendly with equals, and is compassionate towards subordinates (those in distress). He is free from mineness (nirmama) and free from egoism (nirahankara). Because of his compassion, his pleasure and pain are equal (in harmony) with those of others. He is forgiving (ksami).

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having stated the independence of the worshippers of the Imperishable in attaining salvation through 'They indeed reach Me, who are intent on the welfare of all beings', and having shown the greater difficulty for those who are unqualified due to their dull intellect through 'The trouble is greater for those whose minds are attached to the unmanifested' etc., the merciful Lord, for the upliftment of the unqualified, established the devotion to the personal Lord and concentration of the mind through the Yoga of distinguishing between the Self and the Ruler. As commented in the Kalpataru: 'Those dull-witted who are unable to realize the attributeless supreme Brahman are favored by the descriptions of the saguna form. Once their minds are controlled by devotion to the saguna Brahman, that attributeless Brahman itself manifests, free from all limiting adjuncts.' Thus, keeping in mind the qualified ones, He praises the worship of the saguna form to help them acquire the fitness for the worship of the Imperishable, and not to discard the worship of the Imperishable which is established by scriptures, Smritis, logic, history, and Puranas as the ultimate fruit of other means and the unique path to liberation. Therefore, wishing to describe the collection of virtues like being free from hatred, which are the direct and independent means to liberation for the sannyasis who are established in right knowledge, who have practiced the sequence of means in this or previous births, and who have abandoned the desires for children, wife, and wealth, the Lord says: 'Adveṣṭā' (not a hater). He is free from hatred towards all beings in a fitting manner, both towards those who are superior and those who hate him. He behaves friendly with equals, hence 'maitra' (friendly). He is compassionate (karuṇa) towards the ignorant and suffering, because he beholds all beings as his own Self. Or, seeing all beings as his own Self, he is not a hater because of the absence of any unfavorable attitude even towards the cause of his own suffering; not only is he free from hatred, but he is also 'friendly' (affectionate). And because he is friendly, he is 'compassionate'. Or, is he merely indifferent because of being free from hatred? No, he is 'friendly'. Then, does he do good in expectation of a return or is he bound by attachment? No, he is 'compassionate' (full of mercy), meaning he is a sannyasi who gives fearlessness to all beings. His friendliness is out of compassion for the suffering, and not from attachment or expectation of a return. A worldly person shows hatred towards those who oppose and attachment towards those who favor the house etc. accepted with the idea of 'mine', and the body etc. conceived as 'I'. But for the sannyasi who is a knower of truth, this does not exist. Hence, he is 'nirmama' (free from the idea of mine) due to abandoning the house etc. which are the objects of attachment, and 'nirahankara' (free from egoism), having risen above the ego born of pedigree, study, etc. Consequently, pleasure and pain, which do not excite aversion and attachment, are equal to him. Therefore, he is 'forgiving' (ksami), meaning that even when abused or beaten, he remains without any change of state.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
So far, the superiority of devotion, the statement of means, and the path of the self-established (Atmanishtha) for the unable have been described. In the verse 'These attributes are described as dear...' (Gita Sangraha 16), the two remaining parts are mentioned: the extreme dearhood described in the final verse 'Those indeed who follow this nectar of Dharma...' (Gita 12.20), and prior to that, the path of Atmanishtha described in seven verses starting with 'Adveṣṭā' etc. He says 'Anabhisanhita' (disinterested). The word 'tat-prakārāḥ' (its modes) signifies the specific modes of performance, to indicate which he says 'He describes the virtues to be acquired'. If it is asked: how does it fit to say 'He describes the virtues to be acquired by one established in action (Karmanishtha)', since in those verses, devotion is repeatedly mentioned in terms of dearhood: 'he whose mind and intellect are dedicated to Me is dear to Me' (12.14), 'he who abandons all undertakings is dear to Me' (12.16), 'he who abandons good and evil and is full of devotion is dear to Me' (12.17), and 'he who is homeless, of stable mind, and full of devotion is dear to Me' (12.19)? Surely, the wise man mentioned in the seventh chapter: 'The wise man is exceedingly dear to Me, and he is dear to Me' (7.17) is recognized here by the word 'dear', and it is appropriate that 'Adveṣṭā' etc., which share the same grammatical case, are auxiliary to devotion which is the means. To this, it is replied: because these verses are read immediately after the discussion of action (Karma) in 'Therefore, abandonment of the fruit of action is superior to meditation' (12.12), they are understood to be auxiliary to that. And the benefit of these auxiliary parts has been stated as: 'peace immediately follows abandonment' (12.12). Even earlier, these have been described as auxiliary to Karma Yoga. The devotion understood in the verses through words like 'madbhaktaḥ' (my devotee) etc. is indeed the devotion contained within Karma Yoga, as he will explain under those respective verses. Indeed, Karma Yoga and Jnana Yoga are not devoid of a touch of devotion, as has been said: 'The three Yogas have mutual connection with each other' (Gita Sangraha 24). The ones who are directly established in devotion will be described in the verse starting with 'Ye tu' (those who indeed). For there, by the word 'tu' (but), by the adjective 'matparamā bhaktāḥ' (devotees who hold Me supreme), and by the description of the excess of dearhood 'are exceedingly dear to Me' (12.20), a distinct set of devotees is firmly understood. Therefore, the ones described prior to that with mere dearhood are indeed the preliminary devotees—such is the view of the compilers of the summaries. To prevent a false denial, the Lord gives the qualified expression 'of all beings' (sarvabhūtānām), showing: 'even of those who hate and do harm'. To show that this qualification is intended, Vyasa uses 'all'. 'Those who hate' refers to mental opposition, while 'those who do harm' refers to vocal and physical actions. Not only is there merely the absence of hatred towards them, but there is also friendship, hence he says 'maitra' (friendly). He shows the cause of friendship: 'because of my transgressions...'. This is also the cause of the absence of hatred. The phrase 'friendly disposition' is the explanation of the word 'maitra' applied to the general subject. 'Friendly' means wishing their welfare. For compassion to be without conditions, he says: 'towards those who are in distress'. 'Showing compassion' is the derivation of the word 'karuṇa'. The word 'karuṇa' is derived by adding the suffix 'ach' to the root or as a possessive suffix. Similarly for the word 'maitra'. The word 'eva' (indeed) is used in the sense of 'towards those who hate and do harm'. Egoism is the cause of mineness. Since the context of mineness is where it is to be forbidden, he says: 'with regard to the body, senses, and things associated with them'. Since the false identification of the body as the self is what is to be forbidden here under egoism, he says: 'free from the delusion that the body is the self'. By this, the other interpretation: 'departure of the idea of I' is refuted, because the 'I' itself is established as the self. 'Consequently' means due to being free from mineness and egoism. 'Kṣamī' (forgiving): here forgiveness towards offenders is not what is meant, because that is already covered by 'adveṣṭā' etc. Therefore, the endurance of unavoidable pairs of opposites (like heat and cold) born of sense contact, as mentioned earlier in 'endure them' (Gita 2.14), is recalled here. To avoid redundancy, the term 'samaduḥkhasukhaḥ' (equal in pain and pleasure) refers to intellectual or conceptual subjects. In that case, its mention immediately after being free from mineness and egoism is appropriate, which is why he says: 'in those conceptually created'. It means those arising from egoism and mineness. Since he will say 'santuṣṭo yenakenacit' (content with anything) (12.19) and has said earlier 'content with whatever chance brings' (4.22), it is so.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He describes the nature of such a devotee with 'Adveṣṭā' etc. Since all living beings (sarvabhūtānām) are the forms of My divine play (Lila), he never hates any being, and is free from hatred upon seeing another's superiority etc. He is 'friendly' (maitra) in behaving with friendship towards devotees. He is 'compassionate' (karuṇa) towards non-devotees because of the certainty of their worldly suffering, having compassion to give them instructions etc. By the word 'eva' (indeed), it is indicated that he is never harsh. He is 'free from the idea of mine' (nirmama) as he has no sense of ownership towards them or anywhere else after giving them instructions. He is 'free from egoism' (nirahankara), being free from pride due to his own superiority. He is one to whom pleasure and pain, consisting of separation and union, are equal (samaduḥkhasukhaḥ). He is forgiving (ksami), possessing the nature of enduring insults etc. committed by wicked people.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He praises the worshipper of the Imperishable, who is the main subject, since describing their virtues will create respect for those virtues in the minds of the seekers. He says 'Adveṣṭā'. If he is free from hatred, is he indifferent? No, he says 'maitra' (friendly). He is a friend (maitra) indeed, and never indifferent at any time. But how can there be friendliness when there is an enemy? To this he replies: 'karuṇaḥ' (compassionate). He does not wish to harm even the one who inflicts pain, but rather desires to protect him out of compassion. By this, the sannyasi who gives fearlessness to all beings is described. Therefore, the adjective 'nirmama' (free from mineness) is appropriate for him. The main characteristic of the knower of the Imperishable is 'nirahankara' (free from egoism). Egoism is the root cause of all evils, and he in whom it has departed is 'nirahankara'. Therefore, pleasure and pain are equal to him. As the Sruti says: 'What delusion, what sorrow can there be for him who sees oneness everywhere?' (Isha Upanishad 7). He is 'forgiving' (ksami), remaining healthy-minded even when insulted. It means that any other seeker should also practice these virtues.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
But those who completely dedicate all their activities to Me, regarding Me as the supreme goal, and meditate on Me with single-minded devotion, worshiping Me through the path of unalloyed devotion.

 Coming again to the point of pure devotional service, the Lord is describing the transcendental qualifications of a pure devotee in these two verses. A pure devotee is never disturbed in any circumstances. Nor is he envious of anyone. Nor does a devotee become his enemy’s enemy; he thinks, “This person is acting as my enemy due to my own past misdeeds. So it is better to suffer than to protest.” In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (10.14.8) it is stated: tat te ’nukampāṁ su-samīkṣamāṇo bhuñjāna evātma-kṛtaṁ vipākam. Whenever a devotee is in distress or has fallen into difficulty, he thinks that it is the Lord’s mercy upon him. He thinks, “Thanks to my past misdeeds I should suffer far, far greater than I am suffering now. So it is by the mercy of the Supreme Lord that I am not getting all the punishment I am due. I am just getting a little, by the mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead.” Therefore he is always calm, quiet and patient, despite many distressful conditions. A devotee is also always kind to everyone, even to his enemy. Nirmama means that a devotee does not attach much importance to the pains and trouble pertaining to the body because he knows perfectly well that he is not the material body. He does not identify with the body; therefore he is freed from the conception of false ego and is equipoised in happiness and distress. He is tolerant, and he is satisfied with whatever comes by the grace of the Supreme Lord. He does not endeavor much to achieve something with great difficulty; therefore he is always joyful. He is a completely perfect mystic because he is fixed in the instructions received from the spiritual master, and because his senses are controlled he is determined. He is not swayed by false arguments, because no one can lead him from the fixed determination of devotional service. He is fully conscious that Kṛṣṇa is the eternal Lord, so no one can disturb him. All these qualifications enable him to fix his mind and intelligence entirely on the Supreme Lord. Such a standard of devotional service is undoubtedly very rare, but a devotee becomes situated in that stage by following the regulative principles of devotional service. Furthermore, the Lord says that such a devotee is very dear to Him, for the Lord is always pleased with all his activities in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness.
