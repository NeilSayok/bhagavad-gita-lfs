Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
apāne juhvati prāṇaṃ prāṇe.apānaṃ tathāpare .
prāṇāpānagatī ruddhvā prāṇāyāmaparāyaṇāḥ

Word Meanings:
अपाने - in incoming breath
जुह्वति - sacrifice
प्राणं - outgoing breath
प्राणेऽपानं - in prana apana
तथापरे - so others
प्राणापानगती - movements of prana and apana
रुद्ध्वा - having restrained
प्राणायामपरायणाः - devoted to breath-control

Life Application:
Use conscious breathing exercises during moments of stress to restore physical calm and mental clarity.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Others offer inhalation into exhalation and exhalation into inhalation, restraining the passages of breath, intent on Pranayama.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Others offer inhalation into exhalation and exhalation into inhalation, restraining the passages of breath, intent on Pranayama.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Still others, devoted to the practice of Pranayama (breath-control), offer the inward breath (Prana) into the outward breath (Apana—Puraka), or the outward breath into the inward breath (Rechaka), and by checking the motion of both inhalation and exhalation, remain absorbed in breath-restraint (Kumbhaka).

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Intent on breath-control"—regulating Prana and Apana to stabilize consciousness in Lord Vishnu"s meditation is the supreme reward of Pranayama-yajna.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Regulating inhalation, exhalation, and breath-retention (Puraka, Rechaka, Kumbhaka), Yogis achieve steadiness of mind.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Others offer inhalation into exhalation and exhalation into inhalation, intent on Pranayama, while others restricting food offer vital breaths into vital breaths; all these are knowers of sacrifice, cleansed of sins.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Controlling the mind through Puraka (inhalation), Rechaka (exhalation), and Kumbhaka (breath-retention) constitutes the Yajna of Pranayama.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 4.29–4.30 jointly): Through breath-control (Pranayama) and regulated diet, all these practitioners are knowers of sacrifice whose sins have been erased by sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Others offer inhalation into exhalation and exhalation into inhalation, restraining the passages of breath, intent on Pranayama"—explains the threefold discipline of breath-control (*Puraka, Rechaka, Kumbhaka*) as a divine Yajna.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Through Puraka, Rechaka, and Kumbhaka, mental restlessness is stilled, preparing the mind for Dhyana-yoga.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Engaging vital breaths in Krishna-remembrance through Pranayama is the devotee"s supernatural breath-sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Through Puraka, Rechaka, and Kumbhaka, nerve-purification and mastery over vital breath are achieved for meditation.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Pranayama practiced with Vasudeva-remembrance renders the seeker"s mind supremely pure and steady.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When every breath of the seeker pulses with Krishna"s love, Pranayama turns into a supreme sacrifice of love.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Intent on breath-control"—mastery of vital breath is the key to mental absorption, empowering Self-realization.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Still others, who are inclined to the process of breath restraint to remain in trance, practice by offering the movement of the outgoing breath into the incoming, and the incoming breath into the outgoing, and thus at last remain in trance, stopping all breathing.
