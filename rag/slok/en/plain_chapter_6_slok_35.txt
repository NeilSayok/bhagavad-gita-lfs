Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śrībhagavānuvāca .
asaṃśayaṃ mahābāho mano durnigrahaṃ calam .
abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate

Word Meanings:
श्रीभगवानुवाच - the Supreme Lord said
असंशयं - without doubt
महाबाहो - O mightyarmed
मनो - the mind
दुर्निग्रहं - hard to curb
चलम् - unstable
अभ्यासेन - by practice
तु - indeed
कौन्तेय - O son of Kunti
वैराग्येण - by detachment
च - and
गृह्यते - is restrained

Life Application:
Master your restless mind through steady practice and mental detachment, refusing to give up when progress feels slow.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The Blessed Lord said: Without doubt, O mighty-armed Arjuna, the mind is difficult to control and restless; but by practice and dispassion it is restrained.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Blessed Lord said: Undoubtedly, O mighty-armed, the mind is restless and hard to restrain; but, O son of Kunti, by practice and dispassion it is controlled.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "By practice and dispassion"—continuous remembrance of Lord Vishnu"s name/form combined with worldly detachment is the sole gateway to controlling the mind.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Resolving Arjuna"s doubt, the Lord reveals the two infallible remedies for mind control: continuous spiritual practice and unswerving dispassion.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Restraining the mind is indeed difficult, but through "Abhyasa" (meditating on Self-qualities) and "Vairagya" (perceiving flaws in non-Self objects), it is mastered.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Blessed Lord said: Without doubt, O mighty-armed, the restless mind is difficult to restrain; but by constant practice and dispassion it can be brought under control. Yoga is difficult of attainment by one whose mind is uncurbed; but by the self-controlled man who strives, it can be attained by proper means.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Without doubt, O mighty-armed Arjuna, the mind is difficult to control and restless; but by practice and dispassion it is restrained"—expounds Patanjali"s classical twofold formula (*Abhyasa-Vairagyabhyam tan-nirodhah*): repeated absorption in Self-awareness and total desirelessness.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Practice (repetition of meditation) and dispassion (abandonment of craving) complement each other to achieve mind mastery.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Constant practice of Krishna"s service/discourses and dispassion toward worldliness anchors the mind in Govinda.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "By practice and dispassion"—in accordance with Patanjali"s Yoga Sutra, mental restraint is accomplished through self-similar awareness streams and detached mind.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the mind"s stubbornness and how Abhyasa and Vairagya systematically subdue it.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Unbroken practice of meditating on Lord Vasudeva"s auspicious qualities and dispassion for non-Self objects is the infallible weapon.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker practices drinking Krishna"s nectar-like sports and turns away from worldliness, the mind naturally settles into peace.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "By practice and dispassion it is restrained"—repetition of Self-awareness is Abhyasa, and seeing defects in sense objects is Vairagya.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Lord Sri Krishna said: O mighty-armed son of Kunti, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment.
