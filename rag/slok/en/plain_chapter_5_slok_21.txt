Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
bāhyasparśeṣvasaktātmā vindatyātmani yatsukham .
sa brahmayogayuktātmā sukhamakṣayamaśnute

Word Meanings:
बाह्यस्पर्शेष्वसक्तात्मा - unattached to external contacts
विन्दत्यात्मनि - finds in the Self
यत्सुखम् - that happiness which
स - he
ब्रह्मयोगयुक्तात्मा - self united in Brahman
सुखमक्षयमश्नुते - enjoys imperishable happiness

Life Application:
Look inward for true happiness rather than relying on external sensory thrills that quickly fade away.

With self unattached to external contacts he finds happiness in the Self; with self engaged in the meditation of Brahman he enjoys undecaying happiness.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Enjoys undecaying happiness"—detached from external objects, a practitioner absorbed in contemplation of Lord Vishnu receives everlasting Vaikuntha bliss.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Detached from external sense-contacts, the seeker discovers inner happiness in his own Self; absorbed in Brahman, he enjoys imperishable bliss.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
With self unattached to external contacts he finds happiness in the Self; with self engaged in the meditation of Brahman he enjoys undecaying happiness.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Turned away from external contacts and united in Self-contemplation, the seeker enjoys imperishable bliss of the Self.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He in whom there is no desire for external sense-contacts finds happiness in the Self; with his mind united in Brahman-meditation, he enjoys undecaying bliss.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "With self unattached to external contacts he finds happiness in the Self; with self engaged in the meditation of Brahman he enjoys undecaying happiness"—explains that withdrawal from external sense-objects (*Bahya-sparsha*) is the prerequisite for discovering the imperishable bliss (*Sukham akshayam*) of Brahman-absorption.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Inner Self-bliss is eternal and imperishable, whereas sensory pleasure is transitory and pregnant with grief; hence the wise man delights only in the Self.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The nectar of Krishna"s devotional service is infinitely more imperishable and supremely blissful than all external material pleasures.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Enjoys undecaying happiness"—non-dual Self-knowledge emerging from mental quietude grants the seeker imperishable Mukti-bliss.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
With self unattached to external contacts he finds happiness in the Self; with self engaged in the meditation of Brahman he enjoys undecaying happiness.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing that detachment from outer sense-contacts unveils the imperishable bliss of Brahman-absorption.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A seeker immersed in Vasudeva"s divine nectar finds perishable material pleasures completely tasteless, delighting in eternal bliss.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Unswerving love for Lord Krishna is that imperishable bliss, attaining which the seeker becomes freed from all worldly desires.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Enjoys imperishable bliss"—sensory pleasure is limited and ending, whereas Brahman-bliss is unsurpassed, eternal, and undecaying.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Such a liberated person is not attracted to material sense pleasure but is always in trance, enjoying the pleasure within. In this way the self-realized person enjoys unlimited happiness, for he concentrates on the Supreme.
