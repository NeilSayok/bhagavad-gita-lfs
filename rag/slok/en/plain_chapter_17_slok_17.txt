Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śraddhayā parayā taptaṃ tapastattrividhaṃ naraiḥ .
aphalākāṅkṣibhiryuktaiḥ sāttvikaṃ paricakṣate

Word Meanings:
श्रद्धया - with faith
परया - highest
तप्तम् - practised
तपः - austerity
तत् - that
त्रिविधम् - threefold
नरैः - by men
अफलाकाङ्क्षिभिः - desiring no fruit

Life Application:
This threefold austerity practiced with supreme faith by unattached men seeking no fruit—called Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
This threefold austerity, performed with supreme faith by men who desire no reward and are self-controlled, is called Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
This threefold austerity, practised by steadfast men, with the utmost faith, desiring no reward, they call Sattvic.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
These threefold austerities performed with faith, and without thought of reward, may truly be accounted Pure.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When the threefold austerity—bodily, verbal, and mental—is practiced with supreme faith (Shraddha) by individuals who are integrated (Yukta) and have no desire for fruits (Phala), it is declared as Sattvic. True austerity requires faith in the path and consistency in execution.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
This three-fold austerity, undertaken (observed) with best faith, by  men who are maters of Yoga and have no desire for its fruits-they call it to be of the Sattva.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The threefold austerity, practised with supreme faith by men who desire no fruit and are devoted - they call it austerity of Sattva.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
When that threefold austerity is undertaken with supreme faith by people who do not hanker after results and are self-controlled, they speak of it as born of sattva.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that when the threefold austerity (bodily, verbal, and mental) is practiced with absolute faith free from any doubt or skepticism, by self-controlled performers who are indifferent to success or failure and seek no rewards, it is declared as Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
This threefold austerity, performed with supreme faith by men who desire no reward and are self-controlled, is called Sattvic.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'Sraddhaya paraya taptam' means performing austerity with supreme faith and confidence, without caring for difficulties, as one's highest duty. The term 'naraih' is used specifically for Sattvic performers, indicating they truly live up to the human potential by casting away desires and egotism. 'Trividham' highlights that bodily, verbal, and mental austerities are fully and perfectly integrated only in a Sattvic person. This austerity yields spiritual liberation.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The threefold austerity practised with supreme faith through the body, speech and mind by men who have no thoughts of any reward and who are devoted, viz., are imbued with the thought that it is the worship of the Supreme Person, they call such austerity as Sattvika.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.17–17.19 jointly): Sraddhaya etc. upto tamasam udahrtam.  There is faith in all the three-fold austerity.  the faith of a man of the Sattva is full of austerity itself.  The faith of a man of the Rajas is in the Rajas i.e, showing  (or hyprocricy) etc.  But, the faith (or desire) of a man well established in the Tamas  is merely in ruining others.  Thus the sage speaks of all the three-fold austerity practised with faith.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
When tat, that; trividham, threefold-based on three factors; tapah, austerity, which is being discussed; is taptam, undertaken, practised; paraya, with supreme, with the highest; sraddhaya, faith, belief in God and the other world; naraih, by people, by its performers; aphala-akanksibhih, who do not hanker after results,who are devoid of desire for results; and yuktaih, who are self-controlled;-that austerity which is of this kind, the noble people paricaksate, speak of it; as sattvikam, born of sattva.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that in three verses starting with this one, the Lord describes the threefold division of austerities based on the three Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that when the previously mentioned bodily, verbal, and mental austerities are performed with firm faith, free from any doubt or skepticism, by self-controlled individuals who are indifferent to success or failure and seek no worldly or heavenly rewards, noble people call it Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the three kinds of austerities (bodily, verbal, and mental) performed with supreme faith by men who are free from desire for fruits and have an attentive, single-pointed mind are declared as Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that when the bodily, verbal, and mental austerities are performed with supreme faith by individuals who are free from seeking any rewards, are self-controlled, and remain undisturbed by success or failure, wise men declare it to be Sattvic.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that 'Yuktaih' means performing austerity with the understanding that it is the worship of the Supreme Lord. Performing the bodily, verbal, and mental austerities with supreme faith, without desire for rewards, is called Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that when the three kinds of austerities are performed with supreme faith and without any expectation of fruits, following the scriptural injunctions, it is declared as Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that 'trividham' refers to the bodily, verbal, and mental categories. Performing this with attention and without desire for results is Sattvic austerity.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
This threefold austerity, performed with transcendental faith by men not expecting material benefits but engaged only for the sake of the Supreme, is called austerity in goodness.
