Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
orsaṃśayama abhyāsayogayuktena cetasā nānyagāminā .
paramaṃ puruṣaṃ divyaṃ yāti pārthānucintayan

Word Meanings:
अभ्यासयोगयुक्तेन - engaged in practice of yoga
चेतसा - with the mind
नान्यगामिना - not wandering elsewhere
परमं - the Supreme
पुरुषं - Divine Person
दिव्यं - effulgent
याति - attains
पार्थानुचिन्तयन् - O Partha meditating

Life Application:
Train your mind through daily practice not to wander, meditating constantly on the supreme divine person.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
With the mind made steadfast by the practice of Yoga, not straying after anything else, meditating on the Supreme Resplendent Person, O Partha, one goes to Him.

O Partha, by practicing constant contemplation with an undivided mind disciplined in Yoga, one undoubtedly reaches the Supreme Divine Person.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Supreme Divine Person"—steadfastly meditating on Lord Sri Hari through the Yoga of practice leads the devotee to the supreme realm of Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: "With mind steadfast in Yoga practice"—constant practice of divine contemplation prevents the mind from wandering to sense objects and bestows supreme realization.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
With the mind made steadfast by the practice of Yoga, not straying after anything else, meditating on the Supreme Resplendent Person, O Partha, one goes to Him.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "Yoked with Yoga of practice"—a practitioner whose mind is trained through continuous meditation on the divine form of Lord Hari directly reaches the Supreme Person.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: One who withdraws the mind from other objects and constantly meditates through yoga practice on the Supreme Person (inconceivable, omniscient, sun-brilliant), fixing the life-force between the eyebrows at death with steady devotion, attains the Supreme Divine Person.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "With mind attuned to practice of Yoga, straying nowhere else"—O Partha, disciplined by the practice of repeatedly fixing the mind upon God without wandering to other objects, a seeker constantly meditating on the Supreme Divine Person (Lord Vasudeva) attains Him directly.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: A mind straying nowhere else marks the pinnacle of meditation; a seeker devoted to Lord Vishnu reaches the Supreme Person by the power of practice.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Through Yoga of practice, Krishna-consciousness becomes established in the heart, leading the soul to Purushottama of eternal play.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "With mind wandering nowhere else"—restraining the mind from all other objects into the Supreme Self constitutes Yoga of practice, granting direct Brahman-realization.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: With the mind made steadfast by the practice of Yoga, not straying after anything else, meditating on the Supreme Resplendent Person, O Partha, one goes to Him.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Authoritative explanation of the process of Yoga of practice and attaining the Supreme Person through focused consciousness.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Continuous practice perfects mental focus, enabling the seeker to directly reach the Supreme Divine Person Lord Vasudeva.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Unswerving practice of divine contemplation carries the seeker directly to the Supreme Abode.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Yoked with Yoga of practice"—restraining non-self thoughts and steadily fixing mind in Supreme Brahman constitutes practice of Yoga.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
He who meditates on Me as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, his mind constantly engaged in remembering Me, undeviated from the path, he, O Partha, is sure to reach Me.
