Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
niyataṃ saṅgarahitamarāgadveṣataḥ kṛtam .
aphalaprepsunā karma yattatsāttvikamucyate

Word Meanings:
नियतम् - ordained
सङ्गरहितम् - free from attachment
अरागद्वेषतः - without love or hatred
कृतम् - done
अफलप्रेप्सुना - by one not desirous
कर्म - action
यत् - which
तत् - that
सात्त्विकम् - Sattvic
ुच्यते - is declared.

Life Application:
Obligatory action done without attachment, love or hatred, by one seeking no fruit—is Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That action which is ordained (obligatory), free from attachment, and performed without love or hatred by one who is not desirous of any reward, is declared to be Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
An action which is ordained, which is free from attachment, and which is done without love or hatred by one who is not desirous of any reward  that action is declared to be Sattvic.

नियतम् ordained? सङ्गरहितम् free from attachment? अरागद्वेषतः without love or hatred? कृतम् done? अफलप्रेप्सुना by one not desirous of the fruit? कर्म action? यत् which? तत् that? सात्त्विकम् Sattvic (pure)? ुच्यते is declared.Commentary Niyatam Ordained Obligatory. One is not excited to perform an obligatory action through love or hatred.This is a pure act. The performer of such pure action experiences great joy. He does his duty or any other work wholeheartedly not caring for the reward but offering it willingly at the feet of the Lord. He works in accordancw with the dictates of the scriptures. Now I will explain to thee? O Arjuna? the nature of action which is Rajasic or passionate. Do thou listen to Me with rapt attention.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
An obligatory action done by one who is disinterested, who neither likes nor dislikes it, and gives no thought to the consequences that follow, such an action is Pure.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Among the three kinds of action, Sattvic action is the best, bringing peace to the doer and harmony to the field of action. Generally, people act out of attachment to fruit and personal likes and dislikes. But here it is said that performing ordained duty without attachment, likes, or dislikes is Sattvic action. A Sattvic person performs action simply because it is a duty and a form of worship of Lord. With this attitude, the question of likes and dislikes does not arise, just as when our foot is injured, all body parts immediately serve it because the self resides in the whole body and all limbs are equal. Similarly, a wise person sees all beings (prince or beggar, healthy or sick) as limbs of their own spiritual body, serving the world with joy and fulfillment. Thus, the perfection of Sattvic actions lies in their performance itself, without any thought of results.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The  object that has been acired with determination, without attachment and without desire or hatred, by one who does not crave to reap the fruit  [of his action] - that is declared to be of the Sattva (Strand).

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That obligatory act is said to be Sattvika which is done without attachment, without desire or aversion, by one who seeks no fruit.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
The daily obligatory action which is performed without attachment and without likes or dislikes by one who does not hanker for rewards, that is said to be born of sattva.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The Lord begins the three-fold classification of action. Obligatory actions performed without egoism, attachment, or desire for fruits are Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
That action which is ordained by scriptural injunctions, performed without the sense of agency (egoism), and done without love or hatred by one who seeks no reward, is called Sattvic.

Commentary: 'Niyatam' means duty appropriate to one's caste and stage of life. 'Sanga-rahitam' means done without the pride of being the doer. Just as nature acts automatically through the cosmic energy in trees (shedding leaves, producing fruit), all actions in the body (eating, walking, etc.) are happening through the cosmic energy. Once the seeker realizes this, egoism disappears. 'Araga-dveshatah' means neither accepting action through attachment nor rejecting it through aversion.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'Obligatory act' is that which is appropriate to one's own station and stage of life. Doing it 'without attachment' means devoid of attachment to agency etc., and 'without desire or aversion' means that it is not done through desire to win fame and aversion to win notoriety, i.e., is performed without ostentation - when obligatory works are performed in the above-mentioned way by one who is not after their fruits, they are said to be Sattvika.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.23–18.25 jointly): Explains the threefold nature of action. Action ordained by scripture, performed without attachment or desire for fruit, is Sattvika. Action undertaken with great strain seeking selfish desires or pride is Rajasa. Action initiated through delusion without regard to capacity, loss, or injury is Tamasa.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Niyatam, the daily obligatory; karma, action; yat, which; is krtam, performed; sanga-rahitam, without attachment; araga-dvesatah, without likes or dislikes; aphala-prepsuna, by one who does not hanker for rewards, by an agent who is the opposite of one who is desirous of the fruits of action; tat, that (action); ucyate, is said to be; sattvikam, born of sattva.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
The action appropriate to one's own varna and ashrama, performed without egoistic attachment, without seeking fame or fearing infamy, and without desire for fruits, is Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Obligatory duties (nitya-karma), performed without the pride of being the doer, without seeking reward, and free from love or hatred (such as performing sacrifices, charity, etc.), are Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Obligatory action performed by a desireless agent without attachment, and without attachment to children or hatred for enemies, is Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Obligatory duty performed without attachment, without likes (desire for enjoyment) or dislikes (aversion to pain), by an agent seeking no fruits, is Sattvic action.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Actions performed appropriate to one's varna and ashrama, without egoism and vanity, and without pride or show, by a desireless agent, are Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Obligatory actions performed to please the Supreme Lord, without attachment to worldly objects and free from love or hatred, are Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The daily obligatory duties performed without vanity, without desire for enjoyment, and free from love or hatred, are Sattvic.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
That action which is regulated and which is performed without attachment, without love or hatred, and without desire for fruitive results is said to be in the mode of goodness.

 Regulated occupational duties, as prescribed in the scriptures in terms of the different orders and divisions of society, performed without attachment or proprietary rights and therefore without any love or hatred, and performed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness for the satisfaction of the Supreme, without self-satisfaction or self-gratification, are called actions in the mode of goodness.
