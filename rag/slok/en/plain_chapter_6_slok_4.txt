Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yadā hi nendriyārtheṣu na karmasvanuṣajjate .
sarvasaṅkalpasaṃnyāsī yogārūḍhastadocyate

Word Meanings:
यदा - when
हि - certainly
नेन्द्रियार्थेषु - not in sense objects
न - not
कर्मस्वनुषज्जते - nor attached to actions
सर्वसङ्कल्पसंन्यासी - renouncer of all desires
योगारूढस्तदोच्यते - is then called yogarudha

Life Application:
Free your mind from attachment to sensory enjoyment and selfish projects to become truly established in meditation.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
When a man is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all thoughts, he is then said to have attained Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
When a person is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all mental resolves, he is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
When a practitioner is attached neither to sense pleasures nor to fruitive activities, having completely renounced all mental resolves and desires, he is said to be firmly established in Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When a man is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all thoughts, he is then said to have attained Yoga.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
When a man is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all thoughts, he is then said to have attained Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
When a person is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all mental resolves, he is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Renouncer of all resolves"—a devotee completely detached from sense pleasures and fruitive work, absorbed solely in Lord Vishnu, is Yogarudha.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Explaining the characteristics of a Yogarudha (one established in Yoga); when complete detachment from sense objects, actions, and mental resolves occurs, the sage reaches supreme meditation.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When a man is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all thoughts, he is then said to have attained Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: When the practitioner is no longer attached to sense objects or fruitive work, free from all non-Self thoughts, he is recognized as Yogarudha.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: When the practitioner is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions and becomes a renouncer of all mental resolves, he is called Yogarudha.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "When a person is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all mental resolves, he is said to be established in Yoga"—defines Yogarudha as one who has detached from sense-enjoyment, fruitive action, and desire-driven thoughts.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Detachment from sense objects and renunciation of all mental resolves are true marks of steady mind, by which the yogi abides in Samadhi.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Offering all worldly desires at Lord Krishna"s lotus feet and attaining a serene mind constitutes the state of "Sarvasankalpasannyasi".

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Renouncer of all resolves"—absence of all resolves regarding worldly and heavenly rewards is the unique attribute of Yogarudha.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: When a person is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, having renounced all mental resolves, he is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the marks of a Yogarudha and the complete renunciation of mental resolves.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Complete freedom from sense attraction and desire for fruits is the true outcome of Yogarudha Self-realization.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker"s mind becomes submerged in remembering Lord Krishna alone, all worldly resolves naturally subside.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Renouncer of all resolves"—only upon total sublation of all worldly desires is the state of unshakeable Samadhi attained.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A person is said to be elevated in yoga when, having renounced all material desires, he neither acts for sense gratification nor engages in fruitive activities.
