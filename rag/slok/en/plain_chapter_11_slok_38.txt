Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Primal God: The Self is the primal creator. From the Self of the nature of Consciousness, Brahma the creator is born. The Self unconditioned (or conditioned by the total mind and intellect) is called Brahma. Supreme Abode of this universe: The entire universe rests in the Supreme Self, hence He is called the supreme abode or refuge here. As mentioned earlier, the word 'universe' should not be understood as only the gross world, but the combined form of 'Sat' and 'Asat' (manifest and unmanifest) described in the previous verse is called the universe. If we understand the word 'universe' in this way, students of Vedanta will have no difficulty in understanding the entire meaning of this life. We experience the world through the inert upadhis (vehicles) of body, mind, and intellect. Being inert by themselves, they do not have their own consciousness. It is only in association with the Self-Consciousness that they are able to function as if they were conscious. Actually, these inert objects or upadhis cannot originate from the Self, because the Self is changeless (immutable). We cannot even say that the inert world has originated from some other independent cause, because the Self is the all-pervading, one without a second Reality. Therefore, in Vedanta, it is said that this universe is superimposed (adhyasta) on the supreme Truth, Brahman, just as in a state of delusion, a ghost is superimposed on a post. In such a delusion, the post alone is called the support of the ghost, its movement, and the reactions produced by it. In reality, the ghost has no existence or truth apart from the post. Similarly, here Arjuna beautifully refers to the Lord as the supreme refuge (abode) of the universe. You are the Knower and the Knowable: Consciousness is the principle that gives reality to our experiences. Without being illumined by Consciousness, knowledge of this inert world is not possible; therefore, here Lord Sri Krishna, of the nature of Consciousness, is called the Knower. The process of all spiritual practices taught for Self-realization is to withdraw the mind, which is agitated by the senses, from external objects and fix it on the Self. When this mind becomes free of thought-waves, the direct experiential knowledge of the pure Consciousness-Self is attained. Therefore, the Self is called here 'Vedya', i.e., the object worthy of being known. This entire universe is pervaded by You: Just as sweetness pervades all sweets, or water pervades the waves, similarly the Supreme Self pervades the universe. It was just mentioned that the superimposed object has no existence apart from the substratum. The Self is that substratum on which this manifold universe is perceived. Therefore, it is rightly said here that this universe is pervaded by You. This is only a restatement of the truth propounded in the Upanishads that the infinite Brahman pervades all, but nothing can pervade It.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Why is it 'appropriate' (sthāne) is explained by 'kasmāt' (why) etc. He is 'Mahātmā' (Great Soul) because He is the complete (pūrṇa) Self (ātman). The word 'ātman' is defined in the Mahabharata: 'Because He obtains (āpnoti), because He accepts (ādatte), because He experiences (atti) the objects in this world, and because His existence is constant (santato bhāvaḥ), therefore He is called Ātman.' 'Tat param' means beyond Sat (manifest/effect) and Asat (unmanifest/cause). It is stated in the Mahabharata: 'The universe is both Sat and Asat, and that which is beyond both Sat and Asat is the Supreme (Brahman).'

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Now, by showing that the Lord is worthy of praise also because of being the creator of the world, etc., Arjuna praises Him again. To counter the view that the creator of the universe is the Purusha Hiranyagarbha, he says 'purāṇaḥ' (ancient). To those external-theists (tatastha-īśvara-vādins) who argue that the Lord's creatorhood is merely an efficient or instrumental cause, Arjuna says 'tvam eva' (You Yourself are the supreme resort/refuge). The word 'final dissolution (mahāpralaya) etc.' includes intermediate dissolution (avāntara-pralaya). Furthermore, Arjuna establishes the Lord's twofold causality (both material and instrumental) by virtue of His omniscience. Raising the objection that non-duality is contradicted by the relationship between the knower and the known, he says, 'and that which is knowable (yacca vedyam) is You'. To dismiss the doubt that Brahman, the support of liberation, is a different entity, he says 'param ca dhāma' (and the supreme Abode), meaning that the Supreme Abode is also You alone. He states the Lord's fullness through 'tvayā' (by You). Lest there be a doubt of difference between the pervader and the pervaded, he shows that such difference is merely superimposition (imaginary) by addressing Him as 'anantarūpa' (O infinite form).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
You alone are the Primal Deity and the Ancient Person, and You alone are the supreme refuge of this world. You alone are the knower of all, the worthy of being known, and the Supreme Abode. O Being of infinite forms! The entire universe is pervaded by You.

Commentary—'tvamādidevaḥ puruṣaḥ purāṇasa': You are the Primal Deity of all deities, because You manifest before everyone else. You are the Ancient Person, because You exist from eternity and will exist forever.

'tvamasya viśvasya paraṃ nidhānam': Whatever world comes within the range of sight, hearing, understanding, and knowing; and whatever creation, preservation, and dissolution of the world occurs, You are the supreme support of all that.

'vettāsi': You are the knower of the entire world, i.e., past, future, and present, as well as space, objects, individuals, etc.—You are the omniscient knower of everything.

'vedyam': You alone are the only object worthy of being known through the Vedas, scriptures, saints, and sages.

'paraṃ dhāma': That which is called by the names of liberation, supreme state, etc., entering which one does not have to return, and having attained which nothing remains to be done, known, or gained—such a Supreme Abode is You.

'anantarūpa': There is no limit to Your forms manifested in the cosmic form (virāṭ-rūpa). From all sides, Your forms are infinite.

'tvayā tataṃ viśvam': The entire universe is pervaded by You, which means You are pervading every atom of the world.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Having himself said that 'all bow down to You' (11.36) is appropriate, why does Arjuna ask the seemingly contradictory question, 'And why should they not bow down to You?' (11.37). Indicating that this is indeed a rhetorical question (objection) to remove any doubt, he introduces the verse with 'katham' (how). The phrase 'on such a doubt' is to be supplied, and this verse is the answer to it. To prevent the interpretation of 'Mahātman' in a narrow sense, he explains it as 'One who is complete (pūrṇa) and is the Self (ātman)'. To prevent the understanding of 'ātman' as the ordinary individual soul (jīva), he gives its etymology (from Bharata). 'Yat' means 'since'. (The word Ātman is derived from) 'āp' (to pervade/obtain) with the suffix 'man', with the letter 'p' changing to 't'. Or from the root 'dā' preceded by 'ā' (to accept), with the same suffix and elision of the vowel 'ā'. Or from the root 'ad' preceded by 'ā' (to consume/experience) with the suffix 'man'. Or it refers to 'constant existence' (santato bhāvaḥ) in this body, from the root 'tan' preceded by 'ā' (to extend/pervade) with the suffix 'man'. The statement 'You are the universe consisting of Sat (existence) and Asat (non-existence)' is said only because He is the giver of existence and reality, and not in the sense of literal identity, otherwise it would contradict subsequent verses. With this view, citing that, he explains with proof 'tat param' etc.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
With the words 'tvam...' (You...), He again reveals His being the repository of mutually contradictory attributes (viruddha-dharma-āśrayatva) such as knowledge, the knower, and the knowable, as well as His imperishable nature (akṣaratva) through the expression 'paraṃ dhāma' (the supreme Abode). This has the same meaning as 'That is My supreme abode' (8.21, 15.6). 'By You is the universe pervaded' serves as an explanation of passages such as 'By Me, in My unmanifest form, all this world is pervaded' (9.4).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Out of an abundance of devotion, Arjuna praises the Lord again with the words 'tvam' (You...). You are the Primal Deity (ādidevaḥ) because You are the cause of the creation of the universe; You are the Person (puruṣaḥ) because You fill all; You are the Ancient (purāṇaḥ) because You are beginningless. You are the supreme refuge (paraṃ nidhānam) of this universe because You are its place of dissolution, as everything is deposited in You. Having thus declared His material causality as the source of creation and dissolution, and ruling out the inert Pradhāna (of Sāṅkhya philosophy) by virtue of His omniscience, he asserts His efficient causality with the word 'vettā' (knower). 'Vettā' means the knower of all. He wards off any possibility of duality by saying, 'and the knowable (vedyam) also is You alone.' Since there is no real relationship between the knower of the nature of pure awareness and the object known, all knowables are merely superimposed (imagined). For this very reason, 'and the supreme Abode' (paraṃ ca dhāma), which is the essence of Existence-Consciousness-Bliss, free from ignorance and its effects, the supreme state of Viṣṇu—that also is You alone. By You, who are the cause in the form of Existence (Sat) and Consciousness (Sphurana), this universe, which is the effect and is by itself devoid of existence and consciousness, is pervaded (tatam) during its state of existence, through a illusory (māyika) relationship. O Being of infinite form (anantarūpa), i.e., of limitless, unconditioned nature.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Furthermore, 'tvam...' (You...). You are the Primal Deity (ādidevaḥ), the origin of the gods, because You are the ancient, beginningless Person. Therefore, You are the supreme refuge (paraṃ nidhānam) of this universe, its place of dissolution. Likewise, You are the knower (vettā), the cognizer of the universe. And that which is knowable (vedyam), the entire collection of objects to be known, as well as the supreme Abode (paraṃ ca dhāma), the state of Viṣṇu—that also is You alone. Therefore, O Being of infinite form (anantarūpa), by You alone is this universe pervaded (tatam). The idea is that due to these seven reasons, You alone are worthy of being bowed down to.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Arjuna praises Him again. You are the Primal Deity (ādidevaḥ) because You are the creator of Brahmā and other gods. To ward off the notion of the Lord's detachment, he calls Him the Person (puruṣaḥ) because He resides in the city of the body (purī-śayanāt). To ward off the fear of destruction of the Puruṣa upon the destruction of the body, he says 'purāṇaḥ' (ancient, eternal). You are the supreme refuge (paraṃ nidhānam) of this universe, in which the entire world is deposited at the time of final dissolution (mahāpralaya)—thus You are the supreme dissolution-ground; the sense is that You are not just the agent (efficient cause) but also the material cause (prakṛti). Furthermore, You are the knower (vettāsi), the cognizer of all knowable objects. He refutes any difference (duality) by saying: 'and the knowable (vedyam)', meaning You are also that object which is fit to be known. He says You alone are the supreme goal to be attained: 'and the supreme Abode' (paraṃ ca dhāma), which is the supreme state of Viṣṇu called liberation (mokṣa). And You, who are of the nature of liberation, do not reside in some specific place like the summit of Mount Meru, but rather the entire universe is pervaded (tatam) by You, the Supreme Abode. Since there is no limit or division of Your forms (such as Kaivalya), He is addressed as 'anantarūpa' (O Being of infinite forms).

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Venkatanatha connects the words 'kasmāt' (why) etc. with the preceding verse to show its 'appropriateness' (yuktātā). To prevent the confusion that 'te' is a nominative plural pronoun, he interprets it as 'tubhyam' (to You). The performers of obeisance are understood, though not explicitly mentioned. Since the word 'Brahman' is used in many senses, it is interpreted here as 'Hiranyagarbha' (the creator) to show that He is worthy of everyone's salutation. To remove the misconception that the creator might be some new/ordinary Hiranyagarbha, the adjective 'ādikartre' is interpreted as 'the primal cause' (ādibhūtāya). Since the word 'kartṛ' (creator) indicates efficient causality, the word 'ādi' refers to material causality, or it negates any other cause for the Lord. He says 'namaskuryuḥ' (they should bow down) to show that the dative case (as in 'tubhyam') is used not only with the word 'namas' but also with the act of bowing down, as in the statement 'we bow down to Pañcaśikha and Iśvarakṛṣṇa'.

Since 'tvam akṣaram' (You are the imperishable) was already mentioned in 11.18, 'tvam akṣaram' here must refer to a different meaning. On the strength of the expression 'tat param' (beyond that), the words 'akṣara', 'sat', and 'asat' here appropriately refer to lower principles. Among them, 'sat' and 'asat' refer to the non-conscient matter (acit) which undergoes changes denoted by existence and non-existence, while the word 'akṣara' (imperishable) refers to the individual soul (jīvātman) because of its changeless nature. To show the changeless nature of the jīva, he quotes the Upanishadic text: 'He is not born...'. The designation of cause and effect by the word 'asat' is established in the scriptures: 'In the beginning this was non-existent (asat)...'. The reason for using both 'sat' and 'asat' for the same substance is the difference of name and form. The word 'tat' refers to both 'akṣara' and 'sat-asat'. Since the liberated soul (muktātman) is known to be superior to both of them mixed together (like oil in sesame, fire in wood, etc.), he explains 'that which is beyond Sat and Asat' as 'the liberated soul (muktātman)'. The fact that the liberated soul has the Lord as its body (prakṛti-puruṣa-śarīrakatva) and is dependent on Him makes the Lord the ultimate cause. Therefore: 'Because He is the self of all'. 'Jagannivāsa' is analyzed as 'He in whom the world resides'. Thus, by the word 'nidhāna' (refuge/abode), only the aspect of support (ādhāratva) is intended, which was not explicitly stated before; to show this, he gives the locative derivation: 'nidhīyate tvayi' (that in which things are deposited). By this, he says that the reason for the co-referentiality (sāmānādhikaraṇya) in 'tvam akṣaram' etc. is that the universe is His body: 'You are the supreme support of the universe which constitutes Your body, as You are its Self.' By this, the view of some who explain 'nidhāna' as Avyakta (the unmanifest) is refuted.

Since there is no great excellence in merely declaring the Supreme Lord as the knower, etc., in 'vettāsi' (You are the knower) etc., and since His indwelling in things in the causal state has already been declared, here His indwelling in the knower and the known in the effect state is intended. Hence he says: 'You alone are all the knowers and all that must be known in the world.'

Although the word 'dhāma' has multiple meanings, the meaning 'place/abode' is most appropriate here due to its popularity. A place is known as the goal to be attained (prāpya). Therefore, the goal to be attained, qualified as supreme, is intended here: 'the supreme goal/abode'. Or it could mean the unique, non-material abode of the Lord (aprakṛta-sthāna), as described in the Upanishads: 'Ara and Nya are the two oceans in the world of Brahman, in the third heaven from here... there is the city of Brahman called Aparājita...' and 'where the supreme Deity of the gods resides in the well-built, firm, mighty abode with a thousand pillars'.

He shows that the co-referentiality (sāmānādhikaraṇya) in both general and specific statements, in the middle, is based on the relationship of body and soul (schirara-atma-bhava), as clearly stated in 'by You is the universe pervaded': 'by You, as the Self, is the universe composed of conscient and non-conscient entities pervaded.'

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Furthermore, 'tvam ādidevaḥ' (You are the Primal Deity). You are the origin of the gods. Since You are also the origin of Brahmā, he says 'puruṣaḥ' (the Person). Since You exist prior to and within the Akṣara (the Imperishable), You are the Ancient (purāṇaḥ), meaning You are Puruṣottama (the Supreme Person). Arjuna then describes the characteristics of Puruṣottama: You are the supreme, most excellent refuge (nidhānam), the place of dissolution within Your own Self in the form of Your own independent will (svarati-icchā) in Your divine (ādhidaivika) aspect, of this visible universe. You are the supreme one who sports (ramaṇa-kartā) throughout the universe or within the universe, through the knowledge of Your own nature revealed in play (krīḍā). 'And the knowable (vedyam)' means You alone are the object of knowledge. And further, 'the supreme Abode' (paraṃ dhāma) is the effulgent state called Vaikuṇṭha, or the dwelling place of Puruṣottama. O Being of infinite form (anantarūpa), this universe is pervaded (tatam) by You, meaning it is indeed Your own form.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
