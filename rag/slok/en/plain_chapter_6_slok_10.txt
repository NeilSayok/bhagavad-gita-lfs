Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yogī yuñjīta satatamātmānaṃ rahasi sthitaḥ .
ekākī yatacittātmā nirāśīraparigrahaḥ

Word Meanings:
योगी - yogi
युञ्जीत - should practice meditation
सततमात्मानं - constantly the mind
रहसि - in solitude
स्थितः - established
एकाकी - alone
यतचित्तात्मा - with controlled mind and body
निराशीरपरिग्रहः - free from desire and possessions

Life Application:
Set aside regular time in a quiet space to center your mind, free from cravings and possessiveness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Let the Yogi constantly concentrate his mind on the Supreme Self, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from hope and greed.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Let the Yogi constantly concentrate his mind, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from expectation and greed.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
A practitioner of Dhyana-Yoga should constantly engage his mind in meditation, staying alone in a secluded place, controlling his body and mind, free from worldly desires and possessions.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Let the Yogi constantly engage"—staying in solitude and relinquishing all material possessions, the seeker should absorb his mind exclusively in meditation on Lord Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Seclusion, mental control, and non-possession are indispensable vows for a practitioner of meditation to absorb the mind in God without distraction.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Let the Yogi constantly concentrate his mind on the Supreme Self, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from hope and greed.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Sitting daily in solitude, relinquishing possessions and worldly hopes, let the seeker constantly absorb his mind in contemplation of the Self.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Remaining in solitude, alone, with body and mind controlled, free from desire and possessions, let the Yogi constantly engage his mind in meditation. In a clean spot, establishing a firm seat neither too high nor too low, covered with Kusa grass, a deer skin, and a cloth, making the mind one-pointed and mastering thought and sense, let him practice Yoga for self-purification.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Let the Yogi constantly concentrate his mind, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from hope and greed"—begins the detailed practical manual of Meditation (Dhyana-Yoga), prescribing seclusion, mind-mastery, desirelessness, and non-accumulation.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Seclusion and renunciation of possessions are primary external aids for meditation, calming the restless mind.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Sitting in solitude, contemplating Krishna"s sports alone, and dropping all worldly hopes constitutes divine Yoga sadhana.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Free from hope and possessions"—absence of desires and possessions is the ultimate aid for one-pointed mind concentration.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Let the Yogi constantly concentrate his mind, remaining in solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled, free from expectation and greed.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of solitude, secluded abode, desirelessness, and non-accumulation in meditation practice.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Systematically engaging the mind in constant contemplation of Vasudeva in solitude is the cornerstone of meditation.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker detaches from worldly affairs to contemplate Lord Krishna in solitude, the mind becomes absorbed in divine love.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "With body and mind controlled"—without restraint over body and mind, perfection in Meditation is impossible.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A yogi should always engage his body, mind and self in relationship with the Supreme; he should live alone in a secluded place and should always carefully control his mind. He should be free from desires and feelings of possessiveness.
