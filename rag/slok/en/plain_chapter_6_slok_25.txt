Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śanaiḥ śanairuparamed buddhyā dhṛtigṛhītayā .
ātmasaṃsthaṃ manaḥ kṛtvā na kiñcidapi cintayet

Word Meanings:
शनैः - gradually
शनैरुपरमेद् - gradually should withdraw
बुद्ध्या - by intellect
धृतिगृहीतया - held by resolve
आत्मसंस्थं - fixed in the Self
मनः - mind
कृत्वा - having done
न - not
किञ्चिदपि - anything at all
चिन्तयेत् - should think

Life Application:
Gain inner quiet step by step using a patient intellect, fixing your mind on the Self without thinking of anything else.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Little by little let him attain quietude by intellect held in firmness; having made the mind established in the Self, let him not think of anything at all.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Little by little let him attain quietude by intellect held in fortitude; having made the mind established in the Self, let him not think of anything at all.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Gradually, step by step, let him attain absolute quietude through intellect held with unshakeable fortitude; and having firmly anchored the mind in the Supreme Self alone, let him think of nothing whatsoever.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Making the mind established in the Self"—using resolute intellect, establish the mind in Lord Vishnu"s supreme form and completely renounce all non-Self thoughts.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Gradually, step by step, using steadfast discriminating intellect, turn the mind away from worldly phenomena to merge it into the Self.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Little by little let him attain quietude by intellect held in firmness; having made the mind established in the Self, let him not think of anything at all.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Gradually quiet the mind from all non-Self objects using resolute intellect, establish it in the Self, and think of nothing else.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Completely renouncing all desires born of mental resolves without reserve, and thoroughly restraining the entire group of senses from all sides by the mind; let him attain quietude little by little with intellect held in steadfast fortitude, and making the mind firmly established in the Self, let him not think of anything whatsoever.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Little by little let him attain quietude by intellect held in firmness; having made the mind established in the Self, let him not think of anything at all"—teaches progressive step-by-step quietude (Shanaih Shanair Uparamet) leading to absolute mental silence (*Na kinchid api chintayet*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: "Think of nothing whatsoever" means refraining from contemplating non-Self material objects, maintaining continuous meditation on Vishnu alone.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Steadily fixing the mind on Krishna"s lotus feet and stilling all worldly thoughts is the path to ultimate bliss.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Fixing the mind in the Self"—through fortitude born of continuous practice, the mind becomes void of waves, dissolving into pure Sat-Chit-Ananda as the ultimate quietude.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Little by little let him attain quietude by intellect held in fortitude; having made the mind established in the Self, let him not think of anything at all.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of gradual mind quietude, steadfast intellect, and fixing the mind in the Self.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Establishing the mind immovably in Lord Vasudeva through resolute intellect, remain detached from all other thoughts.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the mind finds complete rest at Lord Krishna"s lotus feet, all worldly anxieties dissolve automatically.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Let him not think of anything"—entertaining no thought of dualistic phenomena other than the inner Self is the summit of Samadhi.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Gradually, step by step, one should become situated in trance by means of intelligence sustained by full conviction, and thus the mind should be fixed on the Self alone and should think of nothing else.
