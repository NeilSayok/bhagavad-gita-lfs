Life Application:
Inquire by what marks one who has transcended the three modes is known, his conduct, and how he rises above them.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
To remove the inevitable difficulty and exhaustion at the initial stages of studying philosophy and to make the study more engaging, the Gita is composed in a dialogue style. It is clear that in this dialogue between the fully enlightened Lord Krishna and the deluded Arjuna, the sage Vyasa did not forget human nature even during philosophical exposition. In any assembly of wise men, Arjuna's questions might seem like childish curiosity or mere intellectual cleverness. Nevertheless, the patience with which Lord Krishna answers the questions of a disciple of average intellect shows that it is the duty of a realized sage to answer in detail even the questions asked by restrained individuals or skeptics.

Although we have been fortunate to inherit such a healthy tradition of imparting knowledge, due to some reason, a malicious tendency to keep it secretive is undermining this healthy tradition of our glorious culture. When philosophical principles are not brought to light through intellectual churning, they begin to decay. Every inquisitive seeker has the freedom to first ask questions to thoroughly understand philosophical principles. Only by understanding them can their importance be recognized. As long as there is no such understanding and recognition, we cannot live those principles in our daily lives. Hindu philosophy is a way of life, not merely a perspective on life. Therefore, it is necessary that we live this knowledge in our lives.

Here Arjuna asks three questions: (1) What are the marks of a man who has transcended the three Gunas, by which he can be recognized? (2) What is his conduct? and (3) How does that wise man transcend the three Gunas and attain the glory of the Self? In reply to these, Lord Krishna first explains the marks of a person who has transcended the three Gunas.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Hearing that the fruit of right knowledge is immortality preceded by transcending the Gunas, Arjuna raises a question intending to ask about the characteristics of a liberated soul—'even while living'. Having transcended the superimpositions which are the transformations of Sattva and other Gunas that were described, by what marks is he known? Those marks—which are to be cultivated earlier for attaining perfection and which become effortless after perfection—should be stated; asking what they are, he says 'by what marks'. To exclude arbitrary behavior, he asks the second question—'what is his conduct?'. Since knowledge was stated as the means for transcending the Gunas, desiring to know the manner of that means, he asks the third question—'how?'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna said — O Lord! By what marks is a man who has transcended these three Gunas endowed? What are his conducts? And how can these three Gunas be transcended?

Commentary — "Kair lingais trin gunan etan atito bhavati prabho" — O Lord, I wish to know what the symptoms/marks of a man who has transcended the Gunas are. The idea is, what unique distinction arises in a Gunatita man compared to a worldly man, by which an ordinary person can recognize that he is a Gunatita person? "Kim acarah" — How are the conducts of that Gunatita man? That is, does a Gunatita man have a daily and nightly routine similar to an ordinary man, or is it distinct? Are his conduct, eating habits, living style, sleeping and waking similar to an ordinary man's or are they distinct? "Katham caitan trin gunan ativartate" — What is the means to transcend these three Gunas? That is, by practicing what discipline can a man become Gunatita?

Connection — In response to the first of Arjuna's questions, the Lord describes the marks of a Gunatita person in the next two verses.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Hearing that "by transcending these Gunas one enjoys immortality even while living", desiring to thoroughly know the marks, conduct, and means of transcending the Gunas of a person who has gone beyond them, Arjuna said — With what marks is he endowed who has transcended these Gunas, by which marks he can be known, tell me those — this is the first question. Indicating that the sorrow of a servant should be removed by the Lord Himself due to His being the Master, he addresses Him as 'Prabho' (O Lord). 'What is his conduct' (kimacarah) — is his conduct arbitrary or regulated? This is the second question. And 'how' (katham) — in what manner does he transcend these three Gunas, i.e., what is the means to transcend the Gunas? This is the third question.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Hearing that "by transcending these Gunas one enjoys immortality", desiring to thoroughly know the characteristics, conduct, and means of transcending the Gunas of a person who has transcended them, Arjuna said — 'By what marks' etc. O Lord, by what marks, i.e., by what signs produced in oneself, does an embodied soul become one who has transcended the Gunas — this is the question regarding characteristics. 'What is his conduct' (kimacarah) — how does he behave/exist? And 'how' — by what means does he exist having transcended all these three Gunas, please tell me that.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Hearing the fruit of right knowledge declared by the Lord, which is the attainment of immortality preceded by the cessation of all evils through transcending the Gunas, having obtained the seed for the question and desiring to thoroughly know the marks, conduct, and means of transcending the Gunas of a person who has transcended them, Arjuna said — 'By what' etc. By what marks (signs) is one considered to have transcended these aforesaid three Gunas? Indicating by what signs one becomes capable/master in transcending the Gunas, he addresses Him as 'Prabho' (O Lord). Or the intention of the address is that 'You are extremely capable of answering this initial question'. 'What is his conduct' (kimacarah) — what is his behavior? And 'how' (katham) — in what manner does he transcend these three Gunas? That is to say, tell me the characteristics of a Gunatita through signs and conduct, as well as the means to transcend the Gunas.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Here he introduces Arjuna's question for the sake of determining one's own state, etc. — "Now" etc. With the intention that "By what marks" and "What is his conduct" belong to one group as they refer to internal and external characteristics, and that "and how" is a separate question about the means, he says: indicating the nature, manner of conduct, and cause of transcending the Gunas. Since the word "linga" is used also for dress/appearance, and by the rule "dress is not the cause of righteousness" it cannot serve as a characteristic of being transcendental to Gunas, keeping in mind the intended reference to extraordinary internal qualities like equanimity, he says — "by what marks". Taking "kimacarah" as a Bahuvrihi compound meaning "what is his conduct", he says — "with what conduct is he associated". Here, stating that the word "acara" (conduct) refers to an unfailing external sign, he says — "serving as a sign for knowing his true nature". Although transcending the Gunas within oneself is known through self-experienced internal signs, in others it is inferred through external conduct; hence the separate question for that — this is the idea. That the word "katham" (how) here means the mode of means is known from the nature of the answer given; thus rejecting other interpretations, he says — "by what means".

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
"Thus these three Gunas" having been stated by the Lord, considering therefrom that there are other attributes/powers by which transcending these (modes of nature) occurs, Arjuna submits accordingly — 'By what signs' etc. O Lord, who are capable of accomplishing everything! By what marks (signs) does one become transcendental to these three binding Gunas, that is to say, having transcended them becomes endowed with a divine/transcendental body? Then, after attaining that body, 'what is his conduct' — with what kind of conduct is he endowed; and again, having transcended these three Gunas, 'how' — by what means does he exist, please tell me that — this is the meaning.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Arjuna inquired: O my dear Lord, by which symptoms is one known who is transcendental to these three modes? What is his behavior? And how does he transcend the modes of nature?

 In this verse, Arjuna’s questions are very appropriate. He wants to know the symptoms of a person who has already transcended the material modes. He first inquires of the symptoms of such a transcendental person. How can one understand that he has already transcended the influence of the modes of material nature? The second question asks how he lives and what his activities are. Are they regulated or nonregulated? Then Arjuna inquires of the means by which he can attain the transcendental nature. That is very important. Unless one knows the direct means by which one can be situated always transcendentally, there is no possibility of showing the symptoms. So all these questions put by Arjuna are very important, and the Lord answers them.
