Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Yourself (Dronacharya) and the grandsire Bhishma, and Karna, and the battle-victorious Kripacharya, and likewise Ashvatthama, Vikarna, and Bhurishrava the son of Somadatta.

Commentary -- 'bhavan bhishmashcha' -- You and the grandsire Bhishma -- both are most exceptional men. In the world there is no third equal to you two. If even one of you two were to fight with full might, there is none among gods, yakshas, rakshasas, men and the rest who could stand before you. The prowess of you both is famed in the world. The grandsire Bhishma is a lifelong celibate (brahmachari from boyhood) and one who dies at will -- that is, none can slay him without his own wish. 'karnashcha' -- Karna is exceedingly heroic. I firmly believe he alone can win victory over the Pandava army; before him even Arjuna can do nothing. Such a Karna too is on our side. 'kripashcha samitimjayah' -- What to speak of Kripacharya -- he is immortal (chiranjivi), our supreme well-wisher, and can win victory over the entire Pandava army. Although here, right after Drona and Bhishma, Duryodhana should have named Kripacharya, yet he did not trust Kripacharya as much as he trusted Karna; so Karna's name slipped out from within, in the middle. Lest Drona and Bhishma take it as a slight to Kripacharya, Duryodhana wishes to please Kripacharya by giving him the epithet 'battle-victorious.' 'ashvatthama' -- He too is immortal and is your very son; he is exceedingly heroic; he learned the science of weapons from you yourself and is most skilled in the art of arms. 'vikarnashcha saumadattistathaiva ca' -- Do not think that only the Pandavas are righteous; on our side too my brother Vikarna is greatly righteous and heroic. Likewise Bhurishrava, the grandson of Bahlika (the brother of our great-grandsire Shantanu) and son of Somadatta, is also greatly righteous; he has performed many sacrifices with large gifts and is a great hero and mighty warrior. In naming these heroes, Duryodhana's intent appears to be: O Acharya! In our army there are such greatly valorous heroes as you, Bhishma, Karna, Kripacharya and others, whose like is not seen in the Pandava army. In our army Kripacharya and Ashvatthama -- these two are immortal, whereas in the Pandava army there is not even one such. Nor is there any lack of righteous men in our army. Therefore we have no cause for fear.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within. Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory. Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra: Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
If, seeing the exceedingly vast strength of the enemy, you are afraid, then let peace itself be sought with the foes — why this obstinacy of war? Suspecting such to be the teacher's intent, Duryodhana speaks. By the word "tu" (but), concealing the fear arisen within, he displays his boldness. Those among us who are distinguished, superior to all, whom I now name — know them; grasp my words with certainty. And the leaders, the chief captains of my army, I tell you by name for identification — taking a few among countless ones to indicate the rest — not that I make known anything unknown. By the epithet "dvijottama" (best of the twice-born) he praises the teacher and wins him over to his cause. In the fault-finding sense "dvijottama" means: being a brahmin you are unskilled in war, so even if you turn away, with Bhishma and other foremost kshatriyas present our loss will not be great. The sense of "for identification" (samjnartham): lest you, your mind agitated with joy on seeing the army of your dear pupils the Pandavas, forget your own heroes — therefore I say this. There he counts the distinguished ones — your honoured self (Drona), Bhishma, Karna, and Kripa. "Samitimjaya" (victor in battle) is an epithet of Kripa; since he is counted after Karna, it is used to ward off Karna's possible anger. These four are supreme in every way. He counts the leaders — Ashvatthama (Drona's son). As Drona was named first before Bhishma, so his son is named first before Vikarna and others, to please the teacher. Vikarna is his younger brother. Saumadatti is the son of Somadatta, being eminent, Bhurishrava. Jayadratha is the king of Sindhu. "Are there only these leaders?" No — "and others" (anye ca): Shalya, Kritavarma and the rest who are resolved to give up even life for my sake — hence "who have renounced life" (tyaktajivita); by this their extreme devotion to Duryodhana is told. Thus by the epithets "heroic" and so forth, the vastness of his army, their devotion, valour, readiness for war, and skill in battle are shown.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He speaks of those very (chief warriors) in two verses beginning with "bhavan." "Bhavan" means Drona. "Samitimjaya" is derived from "he conquers samiti (battle)." "Saumadatti" means the son of Somadatta, Bhurishrava.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
He enumerates those very distinguished leaders of his own army with "bhavan" and so on. "Samitimjaya" is from "he conquers samiti (battle)." The word "samitimjaya" connects on both sides (to Kripa and the others) by the maxim of the central gem. Since Kripa is counted after Karna, lest Kripa and Ashvatthama feel anger, the word "samitimjaya" is used as an epithet of them both. "Vikarna" is his (Duryodhana's) brother. "Saumadatti" means Bhurishrava.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Thus, Sanjaya — who is about to state the direct answer "Wherever is Krishna, the Lord of yoga..." (18.78) to the question (asked by Dhritarashtra) out of a desire to know "Will Suyodhana (Duryodhana) be victorious or not?" — narrates, in order to make that answer credible, the entire intervening course of events as well, with "having seen" (drishtva) and so on. "Having seen the marshalled army of the Pandavas" is the cause of Duryodhana's loss of nerve; and the particular state consisting in that loss of nerve is indicated by the word "tu" (but). The unexpressed portion from "drishtva" up to "anunadayan" (in Ramanuja's Bhashya) he makes explicit with "Duryodhana..." up to "he spoke" (Ra.Bha. 1.19). "Samjnartham" means for the sake of full knowledge, or for the sake of enumeration (counting). There, the passage up to "he became inwardly dejected" and up to "Let all of you, stationed in your respective divisions, guard Bhishma alone" (1.11) is explained.

The meaning of the verse "aparyaptam" (1.10) is this: "tat" (therefore) — our force, guarded by Bhishma, is "aparyapta," not adequate for victory over the enemy's force; but this force of the Pandavas, guarded by Bhima, is "paryapta," adequate for victory over our force. — But this (interpretation) is untenable, for the grammatical concord (samanadhikaranya) implied in "tad balam" (that force) ought not to be broken, and earlier there is no statement of any cause for the adequacy of the enemy's force or the inadequacy of one's own. Nor does he (Duryodhana) consider his own force, guarded by Bhishma, Drona and others, to be incapable; for the slaying of the mighty Bhishma, Drona and the rest is itself conditional. In "Fear not, O great king" (Mbh. Udyoga 55.1) and elsewhere, Duryodhana has many times asserted the capability of his own force; and now there is no cause for the contrary impression. At the start of the second day too Duryodhana will say the same: "Inadequate is that force of ours guarded by Partha (Bhima); but adequate is this force of theirs, O best of kings" (Mbh. Udyoga) — there the construction is "ours is inadequate," not "our force." Hence here too a like verbal construction is fitting.

Therefore (to remove this contradiction) the construction should be made by a variant reading, or by a distant (transposed) construction, or by splitting the sentence, or by a difference in word-meaning. (1) By variant reading — through the transposition of the words "Bhima" and "Bhishma"; then, in the construction "that (Pandava) force guarded by Bhima is inadequate for us," the grammatical concord, the naturalness of the remote pointing "tat," and non-contradiction with Duryodhana's intent are all established. (2) In the distant construction too the same meaning; and it is possible in two ways — one by transposing "guarded by Bhima" and "guarded by Bhishma," the second by transposing "aparyaptam tat" and "paryaptam tu idam." For fittingness of sense, such mere separation is tolerable. (3) In the sentence-split — "aparyaptam tat" is one assertion, "paryaptam tu idam" a second; to the doubt "what is the reason here?" there are two reason-giving clauses: "our force is guarded by Bhishma, their force is guarded by Bhima," i.e. our force rests on a mighty base and the enemy's force on a weak base. (4) In the difference of word-meaning — "paryapta" means completing, finishing off; "paryaptam" here is a krit suffix in the active sense, i.e. capable of destroying; "aparyaptam" means incapable of destroying. Our force guarded by Bhishma is "aparyaptam" — not incapable of destroying (the enemy) (i.e. it is capable); "tat" points to the Pandava force as the agent, "idam" to one's own force as the agent that finishes off the enemy force. By the addition of the participial suffix, the genitive in the objective sense does not arise. Or — "aparyapta" means unlimited (immeasurable), "paryapta" means limited; for one's own force is furnished with eleven akshauhinis and the enemy's with seven.

In no way, at the very start of battle, would Duryodhana broach the weakness of his own force and the strength of the enemy's — that would be like leaving the highway to enter a thief's hovel (an incongruity). Likewise — here, with what intent is "guarded by Bhima" stated as the counterpart of "guarded by Bhishma"? Not that Bhima, like Bhishma, is a commander, for Dhrishtadyumna is stated to be their commander; nor that he is of prowess equal to Bhishma, for the extreme disparity (between them) is well known — as Bhishma himself said: "I am able with a single bow to slay all the Pandavas, were not Vishnu, the Primal Person, their protector" (Mbh.). Nor as the overlord of the opposing force, for that is the son of Dharma (Yudhishthira); nor as the foremost warrior of the enemy force, for such is Arjuna alone who is renowned. Therefore — because Bhima is consecrated to the slaying of all the Dhartarashtras (Kauravas) and is endowed with the daring, strength, allies and the like fit for it, he is specially mentioned. And this being so, the mention of Bhishma as his counterpart is also fitting, since he is devoted to protecting all the sons of Pandu. Hence the two words "Bhima" and "Bhishma" are indicative of fear of the foe and of great anxiety about one's helpers — this is what is stated. And as for what was said earlier, that the cause of the adequacy of the enemy's force and inadequacy of one's own (is absent)...

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus intimated, he states their names in two verses beginning with "bhavan." "Bhavan" means Drona, the teacher of all, our chief; "I regard you as the dearest for the task." By the saying "the prowess of Drona among the two," being supremely mighty he is counted first. Bhishma too is likewise chief. By the word "ca" (and) — apprehending that, being a kshatriya, he may lack the power of a curse, it is intimated that being the grandsire he does possess such power. Since Karna will later be reckoned among the ardha-rathas (half-warriors) and will be pained, he too is counted as chief; this very thing is signified by "ca." Kripacharya likewise (is chief). All these are "samitimjaya" (victors in battle) — this is severally the epithet of each. "Ashvatthama" is your son, and "Vikarna" and "Saumadatti" is Bhurishrava. "Tatha" (thus) means: just as your good self and the rest, though equal (to the Pandavas' peers), are partial to us, so too is Saumadatti — this is the sense; or "yatha... tathaiva" (as... so too) is to be construed with what follows.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
There are personalities like you, Bhīṣma, Karṇa, Kṛpa, Aśvatthāmā, Vikarṇa and the son of Somadatta called Bhūriśravā, who are always victorious in battle.

 Duryodhana mentions the exceptional heroes in  the battle, all of whom are ever victorious. Vikarṇa is the brother of Duryodhana, Aśvatthāmā is the son of Droṇācārya, and Saumadatti, or Bhūriśravā, is the son of the King of the Bāhlīkas. Karṇa is the half brother of Arjuna, as he was born of Kuntī before her marriage with King Pāṇḍu. Kṛpācārya’s twin sister married Droṇācārya.
