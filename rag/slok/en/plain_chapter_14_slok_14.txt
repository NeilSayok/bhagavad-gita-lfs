Life Application:
If the embodied soul meets death when goodness prevails, it reaches the pure worlds of knowers of the highest.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
When this embodied soul (dehabhrit) meets with death during the predominance of Sattvaguna, then it attains the pure, spotless realms (such as heaven etc.) of those who perform noble deeds.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
If the embodied one meets with death when Sattva is predominant, then he attains to the spotless worlds of the knowers of the Highest.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The subject of the existence and destiny of the soul after death is beyond the reach of the senses. Therefore, the truth of the theory presented in this context cannot be verified through the senses while living in this very body; but upon a subtle study of human mental behavior in current life, no doubt remains regarding the logical validity of this theory. A physician does not suddenly start pondering a subtle problem of architecture one fine day, nor does an engineer overnight get inspired to treat cancer. At any given time, both individuals think only in accordance with their education and training.

Thus, in the present body itself, we experience the continuity of thought life. This continuity is observed across years, months, weeks, days, and even every single moment of thoughts. Each moment's thought is merely an extension of the previous moment's thought. Thus, if we clearly experience a continuity and orderliness in thought life—the integrity of which is maintained across past, present, and future—then at the time of death, there is no logical reason for this chain to suddenly break. Death is merely a specific experience that can influence subsequent experiences. But this is nothing novel or special, because every experience continuously influences the experience following it with its own merits and defects. Therefore, the cumulative result of thoughts throughout life determines the soul's destination after leaving the body. Although its connection with this physical world ends, its mental/thought life continues.

The Lord says that if Sattvaguna is predominant and the soul departs the body at that time, it attains the stainless realms of those who know the Highest (Uttamavit). These are the realms ranging from heaven to Brahmaloka. In Brahmaloka, because the influence of Rajas and Tamas is negligible, that realm is supremely happy.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He illustrates the distribution of results in the next world of states such as sattvika, etc.—with the words starting with 'marana' (death). 'Sanga' means attachment, 'raga' means craving; the result obtained through performance by the force of those—this is the meaning. 'Gaunam' means produced by the gunas such as Sattva, etc. There, he states the specific result caused by the increase of Sattvaguna—with 'yada' etc. 'Mala-rahitan' means free from impurity (mala), impurity being the emergence of either Rajas or Tamas; thus it refers to the realms established by the scriptures such as Brahmaloka, etc.—this is the meaning.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When Sattvaguna is predominant, if the embodied person dies at that time, he goes to the taintless realms of the knowers of the Highest (or those of noble deeds).

Commentary -- Yada sattve pravriddhe... pratipadyate -- At whichever time in any embodied person, whether he be satvik, rajasik, or tamasik, due to whatever reason Sattvaguna temporarily increases—that is, the functions of Sattvaguna like clarity, purity, etc., temporarily increase—if at that moment that person dies, he goes to the spotless worlds of those who perform noble (auspicious) deeds. The purport of saying 'Uttamavidam' is that those people who perform only noble (auspicious) deeds and never commit inauspicious deeds—meaning their attitudes are noble, their actions are noble, and their knowledge is noble—whatever realms such virtuous people gain right over, the person whose body departs during the rise of Sattvaguna goes to those very same stainless realms. The meaning is that the high realms attained by those who perform good deeds throughout their lives are attained by the one whose life leaves during the temporarily elevated state of Sattvaguna.

The fact that individuals leaving the body during the rise of Sattvaguna reach the elevated realms meant for virtuous souls proves that the mental states born of the gunas are no weaker than actions (karmas). Therefore, a satvik state of mind is as sublime as virtuous actions. From this perspective, even in scripturally ordained virtuous deeds, it is the attitude (bhava) that matters most, not the specific action itself. Hence, the position of satvik attitude is very high. Object, action, attitude, and goal—these four are progressively higher than one another.

Compared to Rajoguna and Tamoguna, the state of Sattvaguna is subtle and pervasive. In the world too, a subtle entity requires less nourishment than a gross one; for instance, gods being subtle are satisfied merely by fragrance. Yes, the subtle definitely possesses more power than the gross. This is why, due to the predominance of a subtle attitude, the increase of Sattvaguna at the final moment leads a person to higher realms.

The meaning of 'Amalan' is that the nature of Sattvaguna is stainless; hence one who dies when Sattvaguna increases attains only stainless realms.

Here a doubt arises: how can someone who dies during a temporary rise of Sattvaguna attain the same realms as those who perform good deeds throughout their lives? This is a special allowance of the Lord that whatever a person's state of mind is at the final moment, such is his destination (Gita 8.6). Therefore, there is no room for doubt that a person relinquishing his body during the rise of Sattvaguna goes to noble realms.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
When the 'Sattva prevails' i.e., while the Sattva continues to be prevalent, if the embodies self meets with death, It reaches the pure worlds, i.e., regions conducive to the knowledge of the self. The purport is this:  If Satva preponderates in a person at the time of death, he will be rorn in the families of those who have the knowledge of the self, and thus be alified to perform auspicious acts which are the means of attaining the true knowledge of the self.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 14.14–14.15 jointly): Yada etc. Rajasi etc. When the Sattva is predominantly on the increase on account of increase on account on account of incessantly practising actions of the Sattva throughout the entire life-at that [time] having met dissolution  [of body], one attains the auspicious worlds.  Likewise whosoever has practised throughout his life the activities of the Rajas, he, by his [last] journey attains manhood for mixed  enjoyment.  Likewise : i.e. in the same order, if one practises action of the Tamas alone by one's entire life, then [on his death] he is rorn in the bodies of the hell, of the animals, of the trees and so on.
 
Those, who explain  [the passage under study to the effect] :  'These results [are for him in whom] the Sattva etc., have predominantly increased only at the time of death' - these commentators have not correctly entered into (grasped) the behaviour of the embodied.  For, nothing but delusion arises, by all means at the last moment, without exception in the case of one and all.  However, with regard to our explanation  [given above] these passages and other verses  (Ch. VIII, 5ff) speak in one voice.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He states the variety of results and destinations of Sattva etc., which become predominant at the time of death, in two verses beginning with 'yada'. When Sattva is predominant, the embodied soul meeting death reaches the bodies (i.e. families) of yogis and knowers of noble means, meaning he is born in their family. Taking birth there, he becomes qualified for auspicious deeds which are the means of true knowledge of the self, or he attains noble realms.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Now he states the specific result of Sattva etc., when increased at the time of death, in two verses—When Sattvaguna is predominant, if the embodied soul (the jiva attached to the body) undergoes dissolution i.e. death, then he attains the stainless (free from the impurities of rajas and tamas) worlds (special places of divine enjoyment) of those who know the highest beings such as Hiranyagarbha etc., i.e. of their worshipers.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He states the specific result of Sattva etc., increased precisely at the time of death, in two verses starting with 'yada'. When Sattva is predominant, when the jiva meets with death, then those who know i.e. worship the highest entities like Hiranyagarbha are 'uttamavid'; he attains their spotless, luminous worlds (special realms of happiness and enjoyment).

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Showing that whatever result in the next world is attained even through death is all caused by and prompted by the gunas such as Sattva etc., he speaks in two verses starting with 'yada'. Therein he states the specific result produced by the increase of Sattva. When Sattva is fully manifest/increased and the embodied jiva undergoes dissolution i.e. death, then he attains the spotless (free from the impurity of the emergence of either Rajas or Tamas) worlds established by scripture (such as Brahmaloka, etc.) of those who know the highest principles (Mahat-tattva, Hiranyagarbha, etc.) and their worshipers—this is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Thus when Sattva increases, until death one moves by that state, hence attaining a body related to that state—he states this through the verse 'yada'. When Sattva is predominant—the word 'tu' excludes the other two gunas—when the embodied jiva undergoes dissolution i.e. meets with death, then he attains the spotless worlds (such as Vaishnavas, Brahmanas, etc.) that are worthy of being known by noble wise ones—this is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'Pralayam' means death. 'Uttamavidam' means the worlds of the deities or the worshipers of Hiranyagarbha etc. 'Amalan' means free from sorrow.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When one dies in the mode of goodness, he attains to the pure higher planets of the great sages.

 One in goodness attains higher planetary systems, like Brahmaloka or Janaloka, and there enjoys godly happiness. The word amalān is significant; it means “free from the modes of passion and ignorance.” There are impurities in the material world, but the mode of goodness is the purest form of existence in the material world. There are different kinds of planets for different kinds of living entities. Those who die in the mode of goodness are elevated to the planets where great sages and great devotees live.
