Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
karmaṇyakarma yaḥ paśyedakarmaṇi ca karma yaḥ .
sa buddhimānmanuṣyeṣu sa yuktaḥ kṛtsnakarmakṛt

Word Meanings:
कर्मण्यकर्म - inaction in action
यः - who
पश्येदकर्मणि - sees, in inaction
च - and
कर्म - action
यः - who
स - he
बुद्धिमान्मनुष्येषु - wise among men
स - he
युक्तः - yogi
कृत्स्नकर्मकृत् - performer of all actions

Life Application:
Maintain inner stillness while busy with tasks, and stay mentally attentive even during moments of physical rest.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He who sees in-action in action, and action in in-action, he is wise among men; he is a Yogi and a performer of all action.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
He who sees in-action in action, and action in in-action, he is wise among men; he is a Yogi and a performer of all action.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who beholds non-action in action (recognizing the immutable Self as the non-doer amidst bodily activity), and action in non-action (recognizing egoic agency in mere physical passivity), he is truly wise among human beings; he is a true Yogi and a accomplished performer of all actions.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Seeing non-bondage (akarma) in God-dedicated work, and seeing karmic bondage (karma) in godless passivity, marks the truly enlightened soul.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Seeing the non-agent Self (akarma) amidst body activities (karma), and seeing ego-driven mental effort in physical quietude (akarma), is the highest wisdom.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He who sees in-action in action, and action in in-action, he is wise among men; he is a Yogi and a performer of all action.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Seeing Self-knowledge (non-action) while executing actions, and recognizing subtle activity in passive non-action, is the true mark of a Karma-yogi.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He who finds the actionless Self amidst activities and recognizes agency in inaction is wise among men; he is a Yogi and performer of all action.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He who sees in-action in action, and action in in-action, he is wise among men; he is a Yogi and a performer of all action"—expounds the core Vedantic paradox of non-agency (*Akartritva*) amidst empirical activity.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The vision of the non-agent Self consumes the fruits of karma; this is the pinnacle of correct knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Engaging in Krishna"s service while realizing oneself as a non-doer is the true mark of a loving devotee and wise sage.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "He is wise among men"—beholding ultimate non-agency in empirical work and subtle agency in non-work is the fruit of non-dual realization.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He who sees in-action in action, and action in in-action, he is wise among men; he is a Yogi and a performer of all action.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis substantiating why seeing non-action in action and action in non-action constitutes supreme wisdom.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A practitioner devoted to Vasudeva, while acting, realizes the non-agent Self; he alone is truly wise.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the soul surrenders all pride of agency unto Krishna, every action turns into non-action (untainted).

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "He is wise among men"—realizing the non-agent Self is seeing non-action in action, rendering the soul a perfect Yogi.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, is intelligent among men, and he is in the transcendental position, although engaged in all sorts of activities.
