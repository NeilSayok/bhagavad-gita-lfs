Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yayā tu dharmakāmārthāndhṛtyā dhārayate.arjuna .
prasaṅgena phalākāṅkṣī dhṛtiḥ sā pārtha rājasī

Word Meanings:
यया - which
तु - but
धर्मकामार्थान् - Dharma
धृत्या - by firmness
धारयते - holds
अर्जुन - O Arjuna
प्रसङ्गेन - on account of attachment
फलाकाङ्क्षी - desirous of the fruit
धृतिः - firmness
सा - that

राजसी - Rajasic .

Life Application:
Fortitude by which one holds fast to duty, pleasure, wealth through attachment, desiring fruits—is Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
O son of Pritha, Arjuna! That determination by which a person desiring the fruit of action, out of intense attachment, holds fast to Dharma, Artha and Kama, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
But that, O Arjuna, by which, on account of attachment and desire for reward, one holds fast to Dharma (duty), enjoyment of pleasures and earning of wealth  that firmness, O Arjuna, is Rajasic (passionate).

He is desirous of getting the rewards of his actions. He endeavours to attain Dharma? wealth and pleasure. The firmness of such a person is Rajasic or passionate.Now listen? O Arjuna? to the third kind of firmness -- the Tamasic type.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The conviction which always holds fast to rituals, to self-interest and wealth, for the sake of what they may bring forth - that comes from Passion.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Human life has four goals (purusharthas): Dharma (merit), Artha (wealth/power), Kama (pleasure), and Moksha (liberation). The determination with which a person holds fast to Dharma, Artha, and Kama is Rajasic. Notably, Moksha is absent here, as a Rajasic person has no desire for liberation from worldly bonds. Their religious practices are meant for heavenly pleasures, their economic activities are for wealth and authority, and their desires are for sensory gratification. They believe happiness lies solely in contact with sense objects.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
O Arjuna !  The content by which one restrains one's bounden duty,  pleasure and wealth, and conseently desiring the fruits [of action]-that content is of the Rajas (Strand),  O son of  Prtha !

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That Dhrti, O Arjuna, by which one, who is desirous of fruits, longs for them with intense attachment, and holds fast to duty, desire and wealth - that Dhrti is Rajasika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
But, O Partha, the firmness with which one holds on to righteousness, covetable things and wealth, being desirous of their fruits as the occasion for each arises, that firmness is born of rajas.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The Rajasic determination holds fast to the threefold goals of life: righteousness (Dharma), pleasure (Kama), and wealth (Artha). Such a person is driven by attachment and expects worldly rewards for their actions.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Partha, that determination by which a person, desiring rewards, holds fast to Dharma (duty), Kama (pleasure), and Artha (wealth) with intense attachment, is Rajasic.

Commentary: Under Rajasic determination, a person performs righteous duties (like charity at auspicious times) solely to gain worldly prestige or heavenly rewards. They pursue Kama (pleasure) believing sensory enjoyment is the true purpose of life, and accumulate Artha (wealth) believing money brings power and respect. They are deeply attached to the world and expect recognition and rewards for all their good deeds.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
That Dhrti by which a person who, desirous of fruits, i.e., through intense attachment holds fast to duty, desires, and wealth, is of the nature of Rajas. By the terms 'Dharma-kam'artha,' the activities of the mind, vital force and senses as a means for the attainment of Dharma (duty) Kama (pleasure) and Artha (wealth) are signified. Even in the expression, 'One desirous of fruits,' that term indicates duty, desire and wealth, on account of the Rajasika nature of the aspirant. Therefore, what is said amounts to this:  the Dhrti by which one maintains activities of the mind etc., with the purpose of attaining duty; desire and wealth, is of the nature of Rajas.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.33–18.35 jointly): The Tamasic determination is that by which a person fixes his pleasure solely in sleep, disputes, and laziness.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Tu, but, O Partha; the dhrtya, firmness; yaya, with which; a person dharayate, holds on to; dharma-kama-arthan, righteousness, covetable things and wealth-entertains the conviction in the mind that these ought to be pursued always; and becomes phala-akanksi, desirous of their fruits; prasangena, as the occasion for each arises, according as the situation arises for holding on to any one of dharma etc.; sa, that; dhrtih, firmness; is rajasi, born of rajas.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
The determination which holds fast to the threefold goals (Dharma, Kama, Artha) out of attachment and with a strong desire for rewards, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The fortitude by which one holds fast to Dharma, Kama, and Artha as duties, with intense desire for fruits and attachment, never aiming for liberation, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The fortitude by which one chiefly holds fast to Dharma, Artha, and Kama, and is desirous of fruits through attachment, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
The determination by which a person, expecting rewards, holds fast to Dharma, Artha, and Kama as constant duties, without ever contemplating the Supreme Brahman or release, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The Rajasic determination coordinates the mind and senses solely for the pursuit of material duty, wealth, and pleasure, driven by self-interest and attachment.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
The determination by which one nurtures Dharma, Artha, and Kama for personal enjoyment and material rewards, rather than for the service of the Lord, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The determination by which a person holds fast to Dharma, Artha, and Kama for the sake of their fruits, is Rajasic.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
But that determination by which one holds fast to fruitive results in religion, economic development and sense gratification is of the nature of passion, O Arjuna.

 Any person who is always desirous of fruitive results in religious or economic activities, whose only desire is sense gratification, and whose mind, life and senses are thus engaged is in the mode of passion.
