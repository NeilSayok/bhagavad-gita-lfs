Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
udārāḥ sarva evaite jñānī tvātmaiva me matam .
āsthitaḥ sa hi yuktātmā māmevānuttamāṃ gatim

Word Meanings:
उदाराः - noble
सर्व - all
एवैते - surely all these
ज्ञानी - the man of wisdom
त्वात्मैव - but My very self indeed
मे - my
मतम् - My opinion
आस्थितः - established
स - he
हि - certainly
युक्तात्मा - with steadfast mind
मामेवानुत्तमां - Me alone as the highest
गतिम् - supreme goal

Life Application:
Respect all sincere seekers, while striving to become established in steady devotion to the supreme goal.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "The wise man I consider My very Self"—Lord Vishnu regards the wise devotee as His own Self due to his unswerving single-minded devotion.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Though Arta and others are noble devotees, the Jnani is non-different from God Himself—he is the very Self of Lord Vasudeva.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Noble indeed are all these; but the wise man I deem as My very Self; for, steadfast in mind, he resorts to Me alone as the supreme goal.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "All these are noble, but the wise man I consider My very Self"—all four are noble recipients of grace, but the Jnani is My very Soul, without whom I do not wish to exist.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Four types of virtuous men worship Me: the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of wealth, and the wise. Of these, the wise one who is ever steadfast in exclusive devotion excels; for I am exceedingly dear to the wise, and he is dear to Me. Noble indeed are all these, but the wise man I consider My very Self. At the end of many births, the wise man surrenders unto Me, realizing "Vasudeva is all"; such a great soul is exceedingly rare.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Noble indeed are all these; but the wise man I deem as My very Self"—declares that while all devotees are noble, the Jnani is non-different from Lord Vasudeva Himself, established in the Supreme Goal.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The wise devotee realizes Lord Vishnu as the ultimate fruit of all human endevors; hence to Vishnu, the Jnani is dear as His very Soul.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The wise devotee abides fully absorbed in Lord Krishna"s supreme inner form; the Lord holds him as the jewel of His heart.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The wise one is My very Self"—when the distinction between knower and known dissolves, the wise devotee is realized as Brahman Vasudeva Himself.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Noble indeed are all these; but the wise man I deem as My very Self; for, steadfast in mind, he resorts to Me alone as the supreme goal.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Authoritative scriptural analysis of the wise devotee"s oneness with God and his refuge in the Supreme Goal.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The wise devotee becomes the very sustenance of Lord Vasudeva; without the Jnani, the Lord feels Himself incomplete.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Since Lord Krishna is everything to the wise devotee, Lord Krishna likewise considers that devotee as His everything.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "The wise man is My very Self"—through non-dual wisdom of Knower and Known, the wise devotee becomes Sat-Chit-Ananda Brahman.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
All these devotees are undoubtedly magnanimous souls, but he who is situated in knowledge of Me I consider to be just like My own self. Being engaged in My transcendental service, he is sure to attain Me, the highest and most perfect goal.
