Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yaṃ labdhvā cāparaṃ lābhaṃ manyate nādhikaṃ tataḥ .
yasminsthito na duḥkhena guruṇāpi vicālyate

Word Meanings:
यं - which
लब्ध्वा - having attained
चापरं - and other
लाभं - gain
मन्यते - thinks
नाधिकं - not greater
ततः - then
यस्मिन्स्थितो - in which established
न - not
दुःखेन - by sorrow
गुरुणापि - even by heavy
विचाल्यते - is moved

Life Application:
Grasp that spiritual fulfillment is the greatest treasure, which no heavy sorrow or trial can ever disturb.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Obtaining which no other gain superior"—attaining eternal communion with Lord Vishnu is the supreme gain, compared to which all three worlds" riches are nothing.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Self-realization is the ultimate unexcelled gain; obtaining which the yogi desires no other mundane gain and remains unshakeable in grief.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Which having obtained, he thinks no other gain superior to it; wherein established, he is not shaken even by heavy sorrow—

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Obtaining which Self-realization one considers no other gain higher, and established in which one is not shaken even by intense physical agony.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: When the mind, restrained by the practice of Yoga, attains absolute quietude, and when beholding the Self by the self, one rests satisfied within the Self alone; when one experiences that boundless joy which transcends the senses and is grasped solely by purified intellect, and established in which one never swerves from the Truth; obtaining which one regards no other gain as superior, and anchored in which one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow—know that severance from union with pain to be called Yoga. That Yoga should be practiced with unshakeable resolve and an undaunted heart.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Which having obtained, he thinks no other gain superior to it; wherein established, he is not shaken even by heavy sorrow"—emphasizes that Self-realization is the supreme non-excelled gain (Anuttama Labha) rendering the sage immune to all grief.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Attaining Lord Vishnu"s supreme realm is the unexcelled Purushartha, reaching which the soul completely transcends all pain.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The gain of Krishna"s eternal service is the highest reward, obtaining which the devotee forgets all material afflictions.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Not shaken even by heavy sorrow"—the sage established in unbroken non-dual realization remains unagitated even by severe Karmic physical suffering.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Having obtained which, he considers no other gain superior to it; wherein established, he is not shaken even by heavy sorrow—

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the supreme unexcelled gain of Self-realization and absolute immunity to intense grief.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Attaining Lord Vasudeva is the unexcelled supreme gain, before which no worldly sorrow can stand.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Upon attaining Lord Krishna"s lotus feet, the soul retains no longing for any other object in existence.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Obtaining which no other gain superior"—realizing Supreme Brahman is the ultimate cessation of all suffering and the supreme goal.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Upon gaining this position, one thinks there is no greater gain. Being situated in such a position, one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest difficulty.
