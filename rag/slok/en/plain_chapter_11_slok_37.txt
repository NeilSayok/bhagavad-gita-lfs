Life Application:
Worship God as the primal creator, greater than all, infinite Lord of celestial beings, abode of the universe.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Why should they not bow down to You, O Great Soul, when You are the primal creator of even the creator Brahma? The primal cause is that which pervades the entire world of effects and supports all names and forms, just as clay is the cause of pots, and gold is the cause of ornaments. The Infinite Lord is not only this universe, but also the Lord of all gods, because it is from that very Almighty Lord that all demigods and natural forces derive their power. This moving and unmoving world can be divided into two parts: Sat (existence) and Asat (non-existence). Here, the word Sat refers to those things which can be known through the senses, mind, and intellect, that is, which are manifest in gross and subtle forms. Gross objects, emotions, and thoughts are called manifest (Sat). The cause of this manifest is called Asat, i.e., unmanifest. This unmanifest cause, which controls a person's way of life, is nothing but that person's impressions (Samskaras) or desires (Vasanas). Here, according to the definition of God, He is both Sat and Asat, and He is also beyond both of them. You are also beyond them—a play performed on a theater stage can be happy or tragic, but the light that illuminates it is beyond both. A ring and a necklace are undoubtedly made of gold, but gold cannot be defined merely as a ring or a necklace. It is indeed both of these ornaments, yet it is also beyond them. In this light, being the essence of all names and forms, God is both manifest and unmanifest, and from the standpoint of His own nature, He is the imperishable Truth (Akshara) beyond both. That imperishable Reality is of the nature of Consciousness, which illuminates both the gross and the subtle. It is this very Imperishable that has assumed this cosmic form (Virat-rupa), which Arjuna is praising. This section represents the universal prayers available in all religions of the world. No person of any religion or caste can have any objection to this, because it is the very essence of whatever established principles there are regarding the Eternal Truth. It can raise the heart of a devotee almost to the limit of the immeasurable. The devotee can experience Him directly. Arjuna praises the Lord, saying:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Why it is appropriate is explained by 'Why' (kasmad) etc. He who is complete (Purna) and is the Self (Atma) is 'Mahatma'. The word 'Atma' is defined in the Mahabharata: 'Because He pervades, accepts, experiences the objects here, and because of His continuous existence, He is called Atma.' 'That which is supreme' (tat param) means that which is beyond existence (Sat) and non-existence (Asat). As stated in the Mahabharata (1.1.23): 'The universe is indeed both Asat (effect) and Sat (cause), and He is beyond both Sat and Asat.'

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
To explain the reason for the statement made, the next verse is introduced with 'of the Lord' etc. 'Mahatmatvam' (exalted nature) means not being narrow-minded. He is worthy of salutations because He is greater, which is expressed by 'to the greater one' (gariyase). Another reason is given there by 'since' (yatah). The result of these reasons like being Mahatma, etc., for the liberated ones is stated by 'therefore' (atah). In the same verse, other reasons are indicated by 'O Infinite' (he ananta) etc. The meaning is that Your being unlimited, being the controller of all gods, and being the refuge of the entire universe are the reasons for Your being worthy of salutation. Another reason is given there by 'You' (tvam). The proof for that is indicated by 'that which' (yat). To the question how one and the same entity can be of the form of both existence (Sat) and non-existence (Asat), He says 'those two'. To the question how Sat and Asat serve as limiting adjuncts (upadhis) to the Imperishable (Akshara), He says 'through which' (yad-dvarena). He explains 'that which is beyond' (tatparam yat) by 'in reality, however' (paramarthatas tu). By qualities like infinity, etc., the Lord's worthiness of salutation is established.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O great Soul! Why should they (the hosts of Siddhas) not bow down to You, who are the Guru of Gurus and the primal creator of even Brahma? Because, O Infinite Lord! O God of gods! O Abode of the universe! You are the imperishable Reality (Akshara); You are existence (Sat), You are non-existence (Asat), and You are also that which is beyond both Sat and Asat.

Commentary: 'kasmācca te na nameranmahātman garīyase brahmaṇo'pyādikartre'—Why should they (the hosts of Siddhas) not bow down to You, who are the Great Being revealed as the source of all? Salutations are offered to two types: (1) those from whom a person receives education and light—teachers, elders, etc., are saluted, and (2) those from whom we are born—parents, and those who are elder to us in age, knowledge, etc. Arjuna says that You are the Guru of Gurus—'gariyase', and You are the creator of even Lord Brahma, the creator of the universe—'brahmaṇo'pyādikartre'. Therefore, it is only proper that the Siddha sages bow down to You.

'Ananta'—From whichever perspective we look at You—space, time, object, person, etc.—You have no end. The meaning is that if we look from the perspective of space, where did You begin and where will You end—there is no such thing. If viewed from the perspective of time, since when have You been and till when will You remain—there is no end to it. If viewed from the perspective of objects or persons, in how many forms do You exist—there is no beginning and no end. From all perspectives, You are infinite alone. Whichever eye of intellect goes to see You, that eye itself gets exhausted, but Your end is never reached. Therefore, from all sides, You are limitless, boundless, and bottomless.

'Devesa'—There are many deities like Indra, Varuna, etc., described in the scriptures. You are the master, controller, and ruler of all those deities. Therefore, You are 'Devesa'.

'Jagannivasa'—Infinite universes reside in a tiny fraction of Yours, yet that fraction of Yours is not filled up; on the contrary, it remains empty. Such are You, the limitless Abode of the universe.

'tvamakṣaraṃ sadasattatparaṃ yat'—You are of the form of the Imperishable (Akshara). You are 'Sat' (existence) which has self-established independent existence; and You are also 'Asat' (non-existence/inert) which has no independent existence but whose existence is perceived only as dependent on Sat. That which is distinct from both Sat and Asat, which cannot be described in any way, which cannot be imagined by the mind, intellect, or senses, i.e., which is completely beyond all imaginations, is also You.

The conclusion is that there is no one greater than You, there cannot be, and it is not even possible—therefore, it is only proper to bow down to You.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
O Mahatman, for what reason should Brahma and others not bow down to You, who are great and are the First Being and the Creator even of Brahma, namely, Hiranyagarbha?

O Infinite, O Lord of the gods, O You who have the universe for Your abode! You are the 'Aksara'. What does not perish, is the Aksara, here, the 'principle of individual self'; for the individual self does not perish as established in Sruti passages:  'The intelligent self is not born, nor dies' (Ka. U., 1.2.18). You alone are the 'existent and the non-existent,' the principle of Prakrti, in its condition as effect and in its condition as cause. This is denoted by the terms 'Sat' (existent) and 'Asat' (non-existent). You alone are the state of effect denoted by the term 'Sat', which is the state of diversification by names and forms, and also the state of cause, denoted by the tetm 'Asat', which is the state incapable of such divisions and diversities. 'What is beyond both' - what is beyond Prakrti and the individual self associated with the Prakrti, as also from the principle of liberated selves who are different from those associated with Prakrti, i.e., bound souls. You alone are that also.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Having himself said that 'all bow to You' (11.36) is appropriate, how does he ask the seemingly contradictory question, 'And why should they not bow to You?' To show that this is a rhetorical question (objection), and to clear this doubt beforehand, he introduces the verse with 'How...'. The response to this doubt is given in the text. To remove the misconception of 'Mahatma' as merely having a non-narrow mind, he says 'complete' (Purna). To prevent the understanding of the word 'Atma' as the individual soul (Jiva), he defines 'Atma'. The word Atma is derived from 'ap' (to pervade), 'da' (to take/accept), 'ad' (to eat/experience), and 'tan' (to stretch/remain continuous). He who has a continuous existence in this body is Atma. The statement 'You are the universe consisting of Sat and Asat' is made because He is the giver of existence, etc., to them, and not otherwise; for if it were otherwise, it would contradict the subsequent statement. Keeping this in mind, he explains it with proof: 'tat param' (beyond that).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
By 'And why...' (kasmad ca). Due to being the primal creator of Brahma, who is the collective cosmic soul (samasti-purusha-jiva), He is untouched (by creation). Hence, He is the supreme imperishable (Akshara) beyond Sat (manifest) and Asat (unmanifest). Being eternal, there is no decay (kshara), and He possesses supreme lordship, being the repository of all diverse and distinct attributes. Thus He says: You Yourself are Brahman. 'Param' means the ultimate cause.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
He gives the reason for the Lord being the object of joy, etc. with 'Why' (kasmad) etc. For what reason should they not bow down (na nameran) to You, all the hosts of Siddhas? O Great Soul of extremely noble mind! O Infinite, free from all limitations of time and space! O Lord of gods, the controller of even deities like Hiranyagarbha! O Abode of the universe, the refuge of all! To You, who are of what nature? To Him who is greater (gariyase) than even Brahma, the primal creator, the generator of even Brahma. Each of these reasons, such as being the controller, the instructor, or the father, is individually sufficient to make one worthy of salutation. What to say when there is a combination of multiple auspicious attributes like magnanimity, infinity, and being the refuge of the universe! This shows that their salutation is not at all surprising. The word 'ca' (and) in 'kasmad ca' is used in the sense of 'or' (va). Furthermore, You are 'Sat' (existence) which is perceived positively as 'it exists', and 'Asat' (non-existence) which is perceived negatively as 'it does not exist'; or, 'Sat' is the manifest and 'Asat' is the unmanifest. And You are also that which is beyond both Sat and Asat (tat param), the ultimate cause, the imperishable Brahman. The meaning is that there is nothing other than You. In 'tat param yat', some read a 'ca' (and) before 'yat'. It means it is not surprising that all bow down to You due to these reasons.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
With the intention that the Lord's being the object of joy etc. is indeed appropriate, he says 'And why should they not bow down to You?'. The meaning is that there is no reason not to bow down, whereas there are indeed reasons to bow down. With this in mind, he says 'O Mahatma!' meaning the Supreme Self, and defines 'Mahatmatva'. 'To the greater one' (gariyase), because He is the first creator (adi-kartre), the primal cause of Brahma (Hiranyagarbha); the word 'adi' is used to indicate that He is the non-different material and efficient cause (abhinna-nimitta-upadana). Establishing the Lord's greatness and His being the first creator of Brahma, he says 'O Infinite!'. He who has no limitation of space, time, or objects, for Him alone is greatness appropriate; this is the idea. 'O Lord of gods' (devesa), the lord of gods like Brahma, etc. 'O Abode of the universe' (jagannivasa), the substratum of the universe. Thus, You, the controller of all and the substratum of all, are the first creator. Having thus defined the literal meaning of the word 'tat' (that), he explains the indicated meaning (lakshya) by 'You are the Imperishable' (tvam aksharam). What is that which is propounded by Vedanta? 'Sat and Asat'. 'Sat' is that which exists, perceived positively, meaning the manifest effect. 'Asat' is that about which there is the notion 'it is not', perceived negatively, meaning the unmanifest cause. Because of having Sat and Asat as limiting adjuncts, the Imperishable also is referred to as Sat and Asat. In reality, however, You are the Supreme Reality beyond both Sat and Asat; therefore, why should they not bow down to You?

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
He connects 'kasmad' etc. with the previous verse by explaining its appropriateness (yuktata). In order to remove the confusion that 'te' might be a nominative plural, he explains it as 'tubhyam' (to You). The performers of the bowing are understood from the context, even though they are not explicitly mentioned. Since the word 'Brahma' has many meanings, it is explained here as Hiranyagarbha to show that He is worthy of salutations by all. To prevent the misconception of some new creator of Hiranyagarbha due to the qualified word 'adi-kartre', it is split and explained as 'adibhuta' (being the first). Since the word 'kartri' (creator) indicates efficient cause, the word 'adi' refers to the material cause, or it denies any other cause for Himself. To show that the dative case is used here in connection with the act of bowing, just like with the word 'namas', he uses 'namaskuryuh'. As in 'We bow to Panchashikha and Ishvarakrishna' etc.
Since 'You are the Imperishable' was already stated in 11.18, it is appropriate here for 'You are the Imperishable' to have a different meaning. On the strength of 'tat param' (beyond that), it is logical that the words 'aksara', 'sat', and 'asat' here refer to the subordinate principles. Among these, 'sat' and 'asat' refer to the insentient matter (acit) which undergoes changes described by the words existence and non-existence, while 'aksara' refers to the individual self (jivatma) due to its changeless nature. This is why he says 'it does not perish'. He cites the Upanisad (Katha Upanisad 1.2.18) to show the changeless nature of the individual self: 'The intelligent self is not born, nor dies'. The representation of cause and effect by the words 'sat' and 'asat' is established in Upanisads like 'In the beginning, this was non-existent. From that, the existent was born' (Taittiriya Upanisad 2.7.1); keeping this in mind, he explains the cause-and-effect relationship. He explains the reason for using 'sat' and 'asat' for the same substance as 'due to name and form'. The word 'tat' in 'tat param' refers to both of these. In the absence of a specific qualifier, like oil in sesame, wood in fire, etc., the state of being beyond both of these combined is well known in the case of the liberated self (muktatma); hence he says 'the principle of the liberated self' (muktatmatattvam). He shows that being the soul of both Prakriti and Purusha and their dependence on Him is the proof for His causality by saying 'therefore' (atah), which means 'because He is the self of all principles'. The compound 'jagannivasa' is dissolved as 'He in whom the universe resides'. Since the word 'nidhana' (refuge) indicates support, which has not been mentioned, he shows the locative derivation as 'that in which all is deposited' (nidhiyate tvayi). By this, he shows that the reason for the co-referentiality (samanadhikaranya) in 'tvam aksaram' etc. is His having the universe as His body (visvasya sarirabhutasya). This refutes the view of some who say 'nidhana' means the unmanifest (avyakta) here. In 'You are the knower...' etc., since merely stating the Lord's knowership does not show any excellence, and since His being the inner controller of the cause-state has been mentioned, here His being the inner controller of the effect-state of knower and knowable is intended; hence he says 'the knower and knowable in the world'. Though the word 'dhama' has many meanings, its meaning as 'place' is most appropriate here due to its popularity. A place is known as the goal to be reached (prapya). Therefore, the goal to be reached qualified as supreme is intended here; hence he says 'the goal to be attained' (prapyasthanam). Or, the Lord's unique, non-material abode (aprakrita-sthana) is intended, which also explains the co-referential usage as before. The scriptures declare that non-material abode: 'Ara and Nya are the two oceans in the world of Brahma...' (Chandogya Upanisad 8.5.3) and 'where the supreme God of gods resides' etc. The co-referentiality in the middle sentence, 'By You the universe is pervaded', clearly shows that the basis for the previous and subsequent co-referentialities is the body-soul relationship (sarira-atma-bhava); hence he says 'by You as the Self'.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
If it is asked: 'Why do the hosts of Siddhas bow to Me, since they too, like Me, are capable of creating hundreds of universes?' In reply, he says: 'Why' (kasmad) etc. O Great Soul! For what reason should they not bow to You? Indeed, they must bow. The reason is 'to the greater one' (gariyase). Even though they are gurus and You are also a guru, still You are the most exalted Guru; this is the meaning. If it is asked: 'Why is there such excellence in Me alone, when their truth-resolve (satya-sankalpatva) etc. are equal to Mine?' In reply, he says: He who is the primal creator (adi-kartre) of even Brahma (Hiranyagarbha), the progenitor. It means You create Brahma through the creation of the five great elements. According to the rule (Brahmasutra 4.4.17) 'Except for the activity of managing the universe, due to context and non-proximity', it is by the command of You, the eternally perfect Lord, that they all enjoy lordship, but they are not equal to You. Therefore, O Infinite! O Lord of gods! O Abode of the universe, the dwelling place of all worlds! You are the Imperishable (Akshara), the pure Brahman. Of what nature is this Imperishable? 'That which is beyond Sat and Asat'—meaning Sat (effect), Asat (cause), and that which is beyond both; thus it is of three kinds: effect, cause, and that which transcends both.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O great one, greater even than Brahmā, You are the original creator. Why then should they not offer their respectful obeisances unto You? O limitless one, God of gods, refuge of the universe! You are the invincible source, the cause of all causes, transcendental to this material manifestation.

 By this offering of obeisances, Arjuna indicates that Kṛṣṇa is worshipable by everyone. He is all-pervading, and He is the Soul of every soul. Arjuna is addressing Kṛṣṇa as mahātmā, which means that He is most magnanimous and unlimited. Ananta indicates that there is nothing which is not covered by the influence and energy of the Supreme Lord, and deveśa means that He is the controller of all demigods and is above them all. He is the shelter of the whole universe. Arjuna also thought that it was fitting that all the perfect living entities and powerful demigods offer their respectful obeisances unto Him, because no one is greater than Him. Arjuna especially mentions that Kṛṣṇa is greater than Brahmā because Brahmā is created by Him. Brahmā is born out of the lotus stem grown from the navel abdomen of Garbhodaka-śāyī Viṣṇu, who is Kṛṣṇa’s plenary expansion; therefore Brahmā and Lord Śiva, who is born of Brahmā, and all other demigods must offer their respectful obeisances. It is stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that the Lord is respected by Lord Śiva and Brahmā and similar other demigods. The word akṣaram is very significant because this material creation is subject to destruction but the Lord is above this material creation. He is the cause of all causes, and being so, He is superior to all the conditioned souls within this material nature as well as the material cosmic manifestation itself. He is therefore the all-great Supreme.
