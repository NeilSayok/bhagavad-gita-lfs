Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yaṃ yaṃ vāpi smaranbhāvaṃ tyajatyante kalevaram .
taṃ tamevaiti kaunteya sadā tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ

Word Meanings:
यं - whatever
यं - whichever
वापि - or also
स्मरन्भावं - remembering the state
त्यजत्यन्ते - leaves at the end
कलेवरम् - the physical body
तं - that
तमेवैति - to that alone attains
कौन्तेय - O son of Kunti
सदा - always
तद्भावभावितः - absorbed in that state

Life Application:
Be mindful of your daily thoughts, for whatever state of being you remember at death determines your next state.

Whosoever at the end leaves the body, thinking of any entity, to that alone does he go, O son of Kunti, being ever absorbed in its thought.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Whatever entity one remembers"—the final thought regulates the soul"s future rebirth; therefore one must contemplate Lord Hari at all times to secure His remembrance at death.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: "Whatever object one remembers at the end"—the thought at the moment of death is not accidental, but arises according to the lifelong habitual meditation of the soul.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Whosoever at the end leaves the body, thinking of any entity, to that alone does he go, O Kaunteya, being ever absorbed in its thought.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "Ever absorbed in its thought"—whatever demigod or object a person constantly contemplates during life naturally arises in mind at death, determining his next body.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: One who withdraws the mind from other objects and constantly meditates through yoga practice on the Supreme Person (inconceivable, omniscient, sun-brilliant), fixing the life-force between the eyebrows at death with steady devotion, attains the Supreme Divine Person.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Whatever state of being one remembers at the end"—O son of Kunti, whatever deity or object one contemplates while relinquishing the body at death, owing to lifelong habitual absorption in that thought, one inevitably attains that very state of being in the next rebirth.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Constant spiritual practice alone guarantees the desired remembrance at death; one practicing worldly desires attains worldly realms, while a devotee of Hari reaches Lord Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The soul"s final mental state becomes its destiny; for an exclusive devotee of God, Krishna-remembrance at death occurs spontaneously.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Ever absorbed in its thought"—continuous meditation governs the final remembrance; hence, lifelong practice of divine contemplation is essential for God-realization.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Whosoever at the end leaves the body, thinking of any entity, to that alone does he go, O Kaunteya, being ever absorbed in its thought.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Explaining how post-mortem destination is shaped by impressions at death; scriptural proof of "as the mind at the end, so the destiny".

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Whatever subject is constantly contemplated during lifetime becomes prominent at death, carrying the soul to that form.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: The final remembrance paints the picture of the soul"s next birth; a lover of God naturally departs with Krishna on his mind.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Absorbed in its thought"—the final thought is the culmination of lifelong mental habit; hence a seeker of Mukti must contemplate Brahman constantly.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Whatever state of being one remembers when he quits his body, O son of Kunti, that state he will attain without fail.
