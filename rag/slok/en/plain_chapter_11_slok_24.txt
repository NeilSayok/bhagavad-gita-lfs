Life Application:
When overwhelmed by cosmic terrifying visions, seek inner calm and ask for divine reassurance and peace.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
This extraordinary, wondrous, and fierce vision experienced by Arjuna could not be focused on any one place. Indeed, it extended to the limit of all-pervasiveness. However, Arjuna in his inner vision saw it in a limited form and a definite shape. Whenever we intellectually comprehend formless virtues (such as freedom, love, nationalism, etc.), we give them a definite shape, which is only for our own understanding but is never perceptible to the senses. Similarly, although the Cosmic Form is universal, Arjuna feels as if it has some specific shape. But again, when he tries to describe this experienced vision, his words fail to express his own feelings, and his own purpose is not served. Arjuna sees that all the worlds are terrified on seeing that Cosmic Person, in whom there are forms with many mouths, eyes, many arms, thighs, and feet, many bellies, etc., and he says, 'I too am terrified.' Everyone must have experienced that if we are in the midst of an excited crowd or in a spiritual gathering (satsang), the atmosphere there has a similar effect on our mind. All the worlds are terrified, and Arjuna admits that, 'I too am troubled.' After his own confession, feeling this fear seemed humiliating and a sign of cowardice for a Kshatriya warrior. Therefore, to justify his fear, he describes that terrifying form as infinite, i.e., formless, saying that the Cosmic Form contains everyone within itself. This Cosmic Form is touching the sky. It is shining with countless colours. Its huge fiery eyes are glowing. Its mouth is devouring everyone. All this combined is enough to shake even the courage of the gods. Arjuna also admits that by the vision of this form, 'I am terrified; I find neither courage nor peace.' It is worth noting here that in this state of senseless fear, he addresses the Cosmic Form as 'O Vishnu'. As I said in the beginning, the Cosmic Form experienced very clearly in Arjuna's inner vision is, in fact, a description of the infinite Supreme Self from the perspective of the limitless extension of the names and forms of this universe. Students of the Gita should not forget these subtle lines of thoughts which Vyasaji has kept hidden for the benefit of industrious and diligent seekers. Stating the cause of his fear, Arjuna says:

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Vishnu! You have many blazing colours, You are touching the sky, Your mouth is wide open, and Your eyes are glowing and large. Seeing You like this, I, whose heart is terrified, am not obtaining courage and peace.

Commentary—[In the twentieth verse, Arjuna described the length and breadth of the Cosmic Form; now here he describes only its height.]
'Visno'—You are the direct all-pervading Vishnu, who has incarnated as Krishna to remove the burden of the earth.
'Diptamanekavarnam'—You have many colours like black, yellow, dark, fair, etc., which are highly radiant.
'Nabhah sprsam'—Your form is so tall that it is touching the sky. Since touch is a quality of air, it belongs to air, not to space (sky). Then what is the meaning of touching the sky here? As far as human sight goes, it sees space, but beyond that, darkness is visible. The reason is that when the sight cannot go further and gets tired, it returns from there, showing darkness beyond. This is the sight touching the sky. Similarly, as far as Arjuna's sight goes, he sees the Cosmic Form of the Lord. The implication is that the Lord's Cosmic Form is infinite, before which even the divine vision is limited.
'Vyattananam diptavisalanetram'—Just as a terrifying beast opens its mouth to devour some creature, similarly, Your mouth appears wide open just to gulp down the universe. Your eyes appear very blazing and large.
'Drstva hi tvam pravyathitantaratma dhrtim na vindami samam ca visno'—Seeing You like this, I am deeply troubled from within. I am not finding courage from anywhere, nor am I finding peace.
Here a doubt arises: Arjuna has his own capability on one hand, and the God-given capability (divine vision) on the other. Even then, Arjuna got scared seeing the Cosmic Form, but Sanjaya did not get scared. What is the reason for this? It is heard from saints that Bhishma, Vidura, Sanjaya, and Kunti—these four knew the essence of Lord Krishna particularly well. Therefore, Sanjaya knew the Lord's essence and His power beforehand, whereas Arjuna did not know the Lord's essence to that extent. Arjuna's delusion (vimuha-bhava) was not yet completely removed (Gita 11.49). Due to this delusion, Arjuna became terrified. But Sanjaya knew the Lord's essence, meaning there was no delusion in him; hence, he did not get terrified.
From the above discussion, one thing is proven: the grace of the Lord and great souls is especially upon undeserving people, but that grace is especially recognized by deserving people. Just as a mother has more affection for a small child, but the elder boy knows the mother more than the small child does. Similarly, the Lord showers more affection on the simple, innocent residents of Vraja, cowherd boys, gopis, and cows than He does on liberated souls (jivanmukta mahapurushas). But liberated souls know the Lord particularly well compared to the cowherd boys, etc. Sanjaya did not even pray for the Cosmic Form, yet he saw it. But to see the Cosmic Form, the Lord Himself made Arjuna eager and also showed His Cosmic Form; because compared to Sanjaya, Arjuna was younger in knowing the Lord's essence and had a friendly relation (sakhabhava) with the Lord. Therefore, the Lord's grace on Arjuna was greater. Due to this grace, in the end, Arjuna's delusion was destroyed—'nasto mohah... tvatprasadat' (Gita 18.73). This proves that the delusion of the recipient of grace is ultimately destroyed.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The term 'Nabhas' denotes the Supreme Heaven (Parama-Vyoman), which is beyond the Prakrti composed of the three Gunas as established by the Sruti passages such as:  'That is in the Imperishable Supreme Heaven' (Ma. Na. U., 1.2), 'Him, sun-coloured and beyond Tamas' (Sve., 3.8) 'The dweller beyond the Rajas' (Rg. S., 2.6.25.5) and 'He who is the president in the Supreme Heaven' (Rg. S., 8.9.17.7). This can be understood as implied in the statement that 'the form touches the Supreme Heaven.' It expresses the idea that it is the foundation of all - of the principle of the Prakrti with its conditions, and of the individual selves in all states. It has also been initially declared:  'For by You alone are pervaded the interspace of heaven and earth ৷৷.' (11.20). 'Beholding Your form shining, multicoloured, and with yawning mouths and large and resplendent eyes, my inner being trembles in fear. I am unable to find support, namely, I am unable to find support for the body. I am unable to get peace of mind and of the senses. O Visnu, namely, O Pervader, beholding You pervading everything, incomparable in magnitude, extremely wonderful and terrible, I find my limbs ivering and my senses agitated.' Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
He (the Lord's form) is described in detail as being terrifying starting with the words 'nabhah-sprsam' (touching the sky). (Arjuna says): 'Not only am I terrified upon seeing You, but I, whose inner being (mind) is perturbed, do not find dhriti, i.e., courage or the capacity to sustain the body and senses, and shama, i.e., peace or tranquility of mind.' He addresses: 'O Vishnu!' What kind of form? 'Seeing You who are nabhah-sprsam (pervading the mid-region), diptam (blazing), aneka-varnam (having many terrifying forms), vyattananam (with mouths wide open), and dipta-visala-netram (having blazing and large eyes)—indeed, seeing You, I, whose inner being is terrified, do not find courage and peace.' This is the grammatical connection (anvaya).

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Not only am I frightened, but also: 'nabhah-sprsam' means touching the sky, that is, pervading the mid-region. 'diptam' means full of splendour (blazing). 'aneka-varnam' means having many colours. 'vyattananam' means one whose mouths are wide open. 'dipta-visala-netram' means one whose eyes are blazing and large. Seeing You of such a nature, I, whose mind (inner being) is perturbed, do not obtain dhriti, i.e. courage, and peace.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
He states the cause of his distress starting with 'nabhah' (sky) etc. 'nabhah-sprsam' means touching heaven. 'diptam' means blazing. 'aneka-varnam' means one in whom there are many different terrifying colours. 'vyattananam' means one in whom there are open, terrifying mouths. 'dipta-visala-netram' means one in whom there are blazing and wide eyes. Seeing You, I, whose inner self, i.e., mind, is shaken (perturbed), do not obtain courage, and for that very reason, do not obtain peace, i.e., contentment of mind. Addressing Him as 'O Vishnu', he indicates: 'Your all-pervading nature has been seen by me, and now I am unable to look at it.' Or, the import of the address is: 'You, who are all-pervading, know even what is in my mind.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
With reference to the usage of many synonyms of space (Akasa) to denote the Supreme State (Paramam Padam/Heaven), he (Ramanuja) says: 'The word Nabhas...' By qualifying it as 'beyond the three Gunas', it is indicated that the Supreme Heaven (Parama-Vyoman) is composed of pure Sattva (Suddha-Sattva). To answer the objection, 'What would be the defect if it refers to the physical space (prakrta-akasa) here, or if it refers to the primordial nature (mula-prakriti) as in the Gargi Vidya?', he says: 'of all, with its modifications...' The idea is that it has been stated: 'See here today the entire universe... and whatever else you desire to see' (11.7) and 'Behold many wonders never seen before' (11.6). He states another reason: 'dyavaprthivyoh...' (between heaven and earth). The pervasiveness of all well-known worlds like heaven and earth was already stated there (11.20); otherwise, there would be repetition. Therefore, the statement of touching the sky (nabhas-sprs), as the support of everything including Prakriti and Purusha, is not merely touching the physical sky. The quality of 'having many colours' (aneka-varnatva) here means variegatedness with colours like white, red, etc., residing in infinite body parts; for, in other contexts also, the body of the Cosmic Form is described as having many colours. Since the mind (manomaya) is interior compared to the physical sheath (annamaya), and if it referred to the essential nature of the soul, the word 'pravyathita' (perturbed) would have no purpose. Therefore, here 'antara-tman' refers to the mind. With this intent, it is said: 'extremely terrified in mind'. Attributing fear to the insentient inner organ (mind) is by superimposing consciousness on it, to highlight the intensity of the fear. Since 'sharma' (comfort) will be mentioned later in 'na labhe ca sharma', the word 'dhriti' here does not refer to pleasure. It is well known in the sense of sustaining (dharana). Therefore, although the object of sustenance is not specified, by context and the nature of the matter, this sustenance refers to the body. With this intent, he says: 'sustaining the body'. The phrase 'of the mind and of the senses' is understood by implication and the well-known meaning of the word 'shama' (quietude); otherwise, there would be repetition. Since the word 'Vishnu' here is not merely a proper noun but denotes its etymological definition (one who pervades), with this intent he says: 'O Pervader!' He states the consolidated meaning: 'O all-pervading one.' 'Atimatram' means of supreme magnitude.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Moreover, he says 'nabhas-sprsam' etc., indicating that the fear is not only for the individual soul (jiva) due to its identification with the body it resides in, but fear has also arisen in the inner controller (antar-atman), who is Your portion. 'nabhas' refers to space, 'sprsati' means touching it, i.e., pervading the sky, which is impossible to comprehend. 'diptam' means a blazing mass of splendour, fit only for meditation. 'aneka-varnam' means one who has many colours like white, red, etc., fit for comprehension. 'vyattananam' means one whose mouths are wide open, fit for prayer. 'dipta-visala-netram' means one whose eyes are blazing and large, unfit for viewing (too terrifying to look at). Indeed, having seen You of such a nature, I, whose inner self is distressed, do not find, i.e., do not obtain, dhriti (courage) and shama (peace). The address 'O Vishnu' is for the sake of self-protection.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He describes his own distress by elaborating upon the terrifying nature of the form, starting with 'nabhas' etc. 'nabhah-sprsam' means pervading the sky. 'diptam' means blazing like fire. 'vyattananam' means having extended (wide-open) mouths. 'dipta-visala-netram' means having red (fiery) eyes. Indeed, seeing You directly, I, whose mind is intensely perturbed (distressed), do not find, i.e., do not obtain, dhriti (courage) and shama (peace and composure). The address 'O Vishnu' means 'O all-pervading one!'; the import is: 'It is impossible to leave this terrifying region occupied by You and go elsewhere, due to Your all-pervasiveness.'
