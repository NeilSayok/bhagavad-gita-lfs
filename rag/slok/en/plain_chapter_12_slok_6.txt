Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Through the verses beginning with 'ye tu' (but those), the Lord shows that there is no difficulty or affliction for His devotees who worship Him. It is also stated in the Saukarayana Sruti: 'For those who worship the Supreme Person Vasudeva, who is desired even by the unmanifest (avyakta), what indeed is unattainable for them?' And in the Mokshadharma (Mahabharata 12.348.4): 'Among them, the single-minded devotees are the best, and they do not worship any other deity. I alone am the goal for those desireless workers of action.'

Commentary—[In the 55th verse of the eleventh chapter, the Lord gave three positive traits ('matkarmakrt, matparamah, and madbhaktah') and two negative traits ('sangavarjitah' and 'nirvairah') among the characteristics of an exclusive devotee. Those very terms are indicated in this verse as follows:
(1) The words 'sarvani karmani mayi sannyasya' point toward 'matkarmakrt'.
(2) The word 'matparah' indicates 'matparamah'.
(3) The words 'ananyenaiva yogena' target 'madbhaktah'.
(4) Due to being exclusively absorbed in the Lord, they have no attachment anywhere, hence they are 'sangavarjitah'.
(5) Having no attachment anywhere, there is no feeling of enmity, hatred, or anger in their mind toward anyone; therefore, the sense of the term 'nirvairah' also falls under this. However, to emphasize this, the Lord has used the word 'advesta' (free from malice/hatred) first among the traits of perfected devotees in the thirteenth verse. Thus, a seeker should not harbor even the slightest hatred toward anyone].

'ye tu sarvani karmani mayi sannyasya'—Now, from here, to show the ease of Saguna worship compared to Nirguna worship, the Lord changes the topic using the word 'tu' (but).
Although the word 'karmani' itself, being plural, denotes all actions, yet by adding the adjective 'sarvani' (all), all worldly actions (related to maintenance of the body and livelihood) and spiritual actions (related to chanting and meditation) prescribed by the scriptures, performed through mind, speech, and body, have been included (Gita 9.27).

Here, by the words 'mayi sannyasya', the Lord's intention is not the physical abandonment of actions. This is because, firstly, the physical abandonment of actions is not possible (Gita 3.5, 18.11). Secondly, if a worshipper of Saguna abandons scripturally ordained actions physically out of delusion, then that abandonment will be Tamasika (Gita 18.7); and if he abandons them out of fear of bodily suffering, considering them painful, then that abandonment will be Rajasika (Gita 18.8). Therefore, abandoning actions in this manner will not sever one's connection with actions. To be liberated from the bondage of actions, it is essential that the seeker relinquishes attachment, possessiveness, and desire for the fruits of actions, because only actions performed with attachment, possessiveness, and desire for fruits are binding.
If the seeker's sole goal is God-realization, he does not desire material objects, and by understanding himself to belong to the Lord, his sense of 'mine' (possessiveness) shifts from the body and other things to the Lord alone. By dedicating himself to the Lord, all his actions also become dedicated to the Lord.
There are several ways of performing actions for the Lord, which in the Gita are called 'Madarpana karma' (offering actions to Me), 'Madartha karma' (actions for Me), and 'Matkarma' (My work).
1 — 'Madarpana karma' refers to those actions whose initial purpose might be something else, but while performing them or after performing them, they are offered to the Lord.
2 — 'Madartha karma' are those actions which are performed for the Lord from the very beginning, or which are in the form of service to the Lord. Performing actions for God-realization, performing actions obeying the Lord's command, and performing actions for the pleasure of the Lord—all these are 'Bhagavadartha karma' (actions for the sake of the Lord).
3 — 'Matkarma' means performing all worldly actions (business, job, etc.) and spiritual actions (chanting, meditation, etc.) understanding them to be the Lord's work.
In reality, no matter how actions are performed, their sole purpose must be God-realization alone. A seeker who attains perfection through all the three aforementioned ways (Madarpana-karma, Madartha-karma, Matkarma) has not even the slightest connection with actions, because he has neither the desire for fruits nor the ego of being the doer, nor does he have any attachment to objects, body, mind, intellect, and senses. When the instruments of action—body, mind, intellect, etc.—do not belong to oneself, then how can there be attachment to actions? Thus, becoming entirely free from actions is true surrender. Perfected souls naturally surrender their actions, and a seeker, keeping complete surrender as his goal, endeavors to act in the same manner.
Just as a Bhakti-yogi becomes free from the bondage of actions by offering his activities to the Lord, in the same way, a Jnana-yogi, realizing that all actions are performed by nature (prakrti) and experiencing himself as completely unattached and untainted by them, becomes free from the bondage of actions.

'matparah'—Being devoted (parayana) means living with a feeling of surrender to the Lord, regarding the Lord as the most worshipful and supreme. Being completely devoted to the Lord, the Saguna worshipper considers himself an instrument of the Lord. Therefore, he believes all good actions are performed by the Lord through him, and since he has no worldly purpose, he has no desire for enjoyment, and in the absence of desire, he does not perform any bad actions at all.

'ananyenaiva yogena mam dhyayanta upasate'—In these words, both one-pointedness regarding the object of worship (Ista) and the means (Upaya) are indicated. That is, the sole object of worship for these devotees is the Lord; they see no other goal besides Him, and for attaining Him, their shelter is also Him alone. They believe the success of their spiritual practice is due to the Lord's grace, and not by the strength of their own self-effort or practice. They consider the Lord as both the means (Upaya) and the end (Upeya). Keeping only the Lord as their goal and object of meditation, they practice worship, i.e., chanting, meditation, singing His glories, etc.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Objection: In the Gita, a great deal of difficulty is observed for the worshippers of the unmanifest (avyakta), but the absence of such difficulty is not explicitly stated for the worshippers of the Lord. Therefore, how can it be said that 'all this is thus'? In response to this, the commentator says, 'of My worshippers' (mad-upasakanam). The term 'devotees' (bhaktanam) indicates that they have no supreme regard or interest in anything else. Here, by the phrase 'by single-minded Yoga alone' (ananyenaiva yogena), it is understood as 'excluding the worship of the unmanifest'. By using only the word 'worship' (upasana), the absence of excessive hardship in worship is implied. By 'ere long' (na cirat), the same is indicated. The statement 'I become their deliverer' (12.7) is made to establish this understanding in the mind beforehand, in anticipation of the subsequent Gita verses. The commentator also shows the agreement of the Sruti and other scriptures by stating, 'And it is said...' (uktam ca). Among the various types of devotees (such as the distressed ones, etc.), the Lord as the 'goal' (gati) is the provider of the means of attainment.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Having thus described the destination of the worshippers of the Imperishable (Akshara), the Lord describes the path of His own devotees in two verses starting with 'ye tu' (but those). The word 'tu' (but) is used to show the distinction from the previous class of seekers. Renouncing and offering all their actions, both worldly and Vedic, unto Me, their Lord who is worthy of service, and meditating on Me with the exclusive Bhakti-yoga of the described characteristics—meaning, they perform meditation, worship, service, prostrations, hearing and singing of My glories which are extremely dear to them and identical to the goal itself—they worship Me. I quickly deliver them from the ocean of material existence (samsara), which is death-like and opposed to attaining Me. Or, since I, the Supreme Self, am their eternally beloved Lord, they abandon all actions, both worldly and Vedic, outwardly and inwardly for My sake. Such devotees, who are as dear as the residents of Vraja, regarding Me as their supreme goal and meditating on Me, their beloved Supreme Self, with exclusive Yoga which is of the nature of constant loving devotion, reside in My close proximity.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Objection: If the result is the same, superiority or inferiority would be determined by the lesser or greater amount of difficulty. But that is not even the case. For, the result for the knowers of the attribute-less Brahman (nirguna-brahma) is Brahmanhood itself in the form of supreme, non-dual bliss-consciousness, through the cessation of ignorance (avidya) and its effects. But for the worshippers of the qualified Brahman (saguna-brahma), since there is no realization of the substratum, ignorance does not cease, and their result is special divine powers (aisvarya) obtained upon reaching the world of the creator God (Brahma-loka). Therefore, if there is greater hardship for a greater result, it does not imply inferiority.
Answer: No, this is not correct. For, those who, by worshipping the Saguna Brahman, have all their obstacles removed, attain the realization of Truth without the trouble of repeatedly hearing, reflecting, and meditating, and without the instructions of a Guru, solely by the Upanishadic statements appearing on their own, aided by the grace of God. Consequently, by the cessation of ignorance and its effects, they attain supreme liberation (kaivalya)—which is the result of Nirguna-vidya—at the end of their enjoyment of divine powers in Brahma-loka. This is supported by the Sruti: 'He beholds the Supreme Person, who is higher than the highest collective life (jivaghana) and who resides in the city [of the heart]' (Prasna Upanishad 5.5). The meaning is: having attained the lordly powers of Hiranyagarbha, at the end of that enjoyment, one directly realizes the supreme, secondless Self, which is distinct and superior to Hiranyagarbha (the collective soul of all living beings, Jivaghana), and which resides in the cave of one's own heart, through the self-revealed Vedic wisdom, and thereby becomes liberated. Thus, even without the aforementioned hardships, the worshippers of the Saguna Brahman obtain the result of the Nirguna Brahman realization through the grace of God. Explaining this truth, the Lord speaks these two verses starting with 'ye tu' (but those).
The word 'tu' (but) is used to dispel the stated objection. Those who dedicate all actions to Me, the Saguna Vasudeva, and accept Me, Lord Vasudeva, as their supreme object of intense love; and worship Me, Lord Vasudeva—who is the reservoir of all beauty, the embodiment of bliss-consciousness, two-armed or four-armed, filling the flute with highly enchanting seven notes that charm the minds of all people, or whose hands are adorned with the conch, lotus, mace, and discus, or in the form of Narasimha, or the most compassionate and beautiful Sri Raghunandana (Rama), or in the form of Varaha, or in the Cosmic Form (Visvarupa) as shown earlier—meditating on Me, i.e., continuously maintaining an uninterrupted flow of mental states focused on Me, with single-minded Yoga, i.e., with concentration which has no other support than Me, also known as exclusive Bhakti-yoga; they remain established in My proximity. For those whose minds are thus absorbed in Me, i.e., whose minds are single-pointedly entered into Me, I, the constantly worshipped Lord, quickly—in that very birth—become the deliverer from the ocean of material existence (samsara-sagara) which is fraught with death. Samsara is the world of false knowledge and its effects, which is like an ocean extremely difficult to cross. I become the deliverer who, without any effort, lifts them up and establishes them in the pure Brahman, the ultimate limit of all negation, by granting them the support of knowledge. The address 'O Partha' is used for reassurance.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
In these two verses starting with 'ye tu' (but those), the Lord says that His devotees attain perfection effortlessly by His grace. Renouncing and dedicating all their actions unto Me, the Supreme Lord, and becoming fully devoted to Me, they meditate on Me and worship Me with exclusive (single-minded) Bhakti-yoga in which there is no other object of worship.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
By saying that the worshippers of the Imperishable (Akshara) attain Me alone, their eligibility to attain Me directly is declared. However, the Saguna worshippers, without the excessive hardship of listening to scriptures, etc., are liberated from the material world by the knowledge bestowed by Me. With this in mind, the Lord speaks these two verses beginning with 'ye tu' (but those). Those Saguna worshippers who dedicate all actions to Me, the Supreme Lord, and who are devoted to Me—meaning, they hold Me as their supreme goal, and not heaven, etc.—worship Me by meditating on Me with exclusive Yoga (concentration). This exclusive Yoga has no other object of support than Me, the Infinite Ocean of virtues, the Cosmic Self, who descends on this earth in various incarnations. For those whose minds are thus fully absorbed in Me, the Supreme Lord, I quickly deliver them from the ocean of material existence (samsara) which is filled with death. The implication is that, being pleased with their exclusive devotion, I bestow upon them the Yoga of wisdom (Buddhi-yoga) and deliver them from the world of primal ignorance (Mula-ajnana) and its effects. By addressing him as 'O Partha', the Lord intimates that just as He is won over by the devotion of the sons of Pritha (Pandavas) and delivers them from all difficulties, in the same way, He delivers all His devotees.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
'But those who...' (ye tu sarvāṇi, etc.). The actions include worldly activities such as agriculture, etc., necessary for body sustenance, along with their causes, as well as scriptural duties like Sandhyavandana, which are prescribed with specific motives like attaining heaven. Offering these with 'adhyātma-cetas' means with a mind dedicated to the Supreme Self. 'Matparāḥ' means those for whom I am the supreme goal of attainment; keeping this in mind, the commentator uses the term 'madekaprāpyāḥ' (attaining Me alone as the goal). The phrase 'by exclusive Yoga' is illustrative of qualities like 'glorifying Me always' (Gita 9.14). The understanding that the means itself is the goal is the rule of this devotion. 'Svayam eva' (spontaneously) means not expecting any other fruit. In the phrase 'from the death-like ocean of material existence' (mṛtyubhūtāt-saṁsārākhyāt), an appositional compound is appropriate. The adjective 'death-like' is used to show the lowliness/misery of samsara. Metaphorically, samsara is called 'death' because it is antagonistic to the attainment of Me. Indeed, attaining Me leads to immortality. To avoid any misinterpretation of linking 'na' (not) separately with the verb, the connection is explained as 'samuddhartā acireṇaiva kālena' (deliverer in a very short time).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Although the worshippers of the Imperishable (Akshara) experience difficulty in their realization, I deliver My devotees through their meditation on My form and even through their service to My sacred deity. To explain this, the Lord speaks these two verses beginning with 'ye tu' (but those). Those who renounce and dedicate all their worldly and Vedic actions for My sake, and who are devoted to Me—meaning, they for whom I alone am the supreme and highest goal of attainment—worship Me, meditating on Me with exclusive Yoga (Bhakti-yoga) in which there is no other object of worship. Here, 'worship' implies serving Me in My deity forms (murtis), etc.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Objection: Even if there is greater difficulty for those whose minds are attached to the unmanifest (avyakta), yet at the end of that difficulty, they immediately attain absolute liberation (kaivalya). What is the use of meditating on the manifest (vyakta) which is accomplished with delay?
To remove this doubt, the Lord speaks these two verses starting with 'ye tu' (but those). Dedicating all actions—whether daily, occasional, or natural—unto Me, and being devoted to Me—meaning, those for whom I alone am the goal to be attained by all actions, or those who are intent on meditating on Me—they worship Me, meditating on Me with exclusive Yoga. This exclusive Yoga is characterized by the meditation of identity with the Supreme Lord, i.e., 'I am indeed Lord Vasudeva,' which is free from any sense of difference (duality), and they attain stability in that meditation.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
But those who worship Me, giving up all their activities unto Me and being devoted to Me without deviation, engaged in devotional service and always meditating upon Me, having fixed their minds upon Me, O son of Pṛthā—for them I am the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death.

 It is explicitly stated here that the devotees are very fortunate to be delivered very soon from material existence by the Lord. In pure devotional service one comes to the realization that God is great and that the individual soul is subordinate to Him. His duty is to render service to the Lord – and if he does not, then he will render service to māyā. As stated before, the Supreme Lord can be appreciated only by devotional service. Therefore, one should be fully devoted. One should fix his mind fully on Kṛṣṇa in order to achieve Him. One should work only for Kṛṣṇa. It does not matter in what kind of work one engages, but that work should be done only for Kṛṣṇa. That is the standard of devotional service. The devotee does not desire any achievement other than pleasing the Supreme Personality of Godhead. His life’s mission is to please Kṛṣṇa, and he can sacrifice everything for Kṛṣṇa’s satisfaction, just as Arjuna did in the Battle of Kurukṣetra. The process is very simple: one can devote himself in his occupation and engage at the same time in chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. Such transcendental chanting attracts the devotee to the Personality of Godhead. The Supreme Lord herein promises that without delay He will deliver a pure devotee thus engaged from the ocean of material existence. Those who are advanced in yoga practice can willfully transfer the soul to whatever planet they like by the yoga process, and others take the opportunity in various ways, but as far as the devotee is concerned, it is clearly stated here that the Lord Himself takes him. The devotee does not need to wait to become very experienced in order to transfer himself to the spiritual sky. In the Varāha Purāṇa this verse appears: nayāmi paramaṁ sthānam arcir-ādi-gatiṁ vinā garuḍa-skandham āropya yatheccham anivāritaḥ The purport of this verse is that a devotee does not need to practice aṣṭāṅga-yoga in order to transfer his soul to the spiritual planets. The responsibility is taken by the Supreme Lord Himself. He clearly states here that He Himself becomes the deliverer. A child is completely cared for by his parents, and thus his position is secure. Similarly, a devotee does not need to endeavor to transfer himself by yoga practice to other planets. Rather, the Supreme Lord, by His great mercy, comes at once, riding on His bird carrier Garuḍa, and at once delivers the devotee from material existence. Although a man who has fallen in the ocean may struggle very hard and may be very expert in swimming, he cannot save himself. But if someone comes and picks him up from the water, then he is easily rescued. Similarly, the Lord picks up the devotee from this material existence. One simply has to practice the easy process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness and fully engage himself in devotional service. Any intelligent man should always prefer the process of devotional service to all other paths. In the Nārāyaṇīya this is confirmed as follows: yā vai sādhana-sampattiḥ puruṣārtha-catuṣṭaye tayā vinā tad āpnoti naro nārāyaṇāśrayaḥ The purport of this verse is that one should not engage in the different processes of fruitive activity or cultivate knowledge by the mental speculative process. One who is devoted to the Supreme Personality can attain all the benefits derived from other yogic processes, speculation, rituals, sacrifices, charities, etc. That is the specific benediction of devotional service. Simply by chanting the holy name of Kṛṣṇa – Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare – a devotee of the Lord can approach the supreme destination easily and happily, but this destination cannot be approached by any other process of religion. The conclusion of Bhagavad-gītā is stated in the Eighteenth Chapter: sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ One should give up all other processes of self-realization and simply execute devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. That will enable one to reach the highest perfection of life. There is no need for one to consider the sinful actions of his past life, because the Supreme Lord fully takes charge of him. Therefore one should not futilely try to deliver himself in spiritual realization. Let everyone take shelter of the supreme omnipotent Godhead, Kṛṣṇa. That is the highest perfection of life.
