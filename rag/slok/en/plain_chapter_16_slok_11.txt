Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
cintāmaparimeyāṃ ca pralayāntāmupāśritāḥ .
kāmopabhogaparamā etāvaditi niścitāḥ

Word Meanings:
चिन्ताम् - cares
अपरिमेयाम् - immeasurable
च - and
प्रलयान्तम् - ending only with death
उपाश्रिताः - refuged in
कामोपभोगपरमाः - regarding gratification of lust
एतावत् - that is all
इति - thus
निश्चिताः - feeling sure.

Life Application:
Bound by endless cares ending only at death, regarding gratification of lust as supreme goal, convinced 'this is all'.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Beset with immeasurable cares ending only with death, and regarding the gratification of lust as their highest goal, they are convinced that this is all.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Giving themselves over to immeasurable cares ending only with death, regarding gratification of lust as their highest aim, and feeling sure that that is all.

चिन्ताम् cares? अपरिमेयाम् immeasurable? च and? प्रलयान्तम् ending only with death? उपाश्रिताः refuged in? कामोपभोगपरमाः regarding gratification of lust as their highest aim? एतावत् that is all? इति thus? निश्चिताः feeling sure.Commentary They are beset with immense cares? worries and anxieties and their minds are engrossed in aciring and preserving the countless sensual objects. They have got the strong conviction that the sensual enjoyment is the highest end of a man. They are steeped in enjoying the objects of the senses. They firmly believe that that is everything. They believe that sensual enjoyment is the supreme source of happiness and there is no such thing as eternal bliss of the soul or transcendental bliss of the Self. They have no belief in the happiness in another world (or plane) or in the perennial bliss which is independent of sensual objects? which is beyond the reach of the senses. They have a dull and gross intellect? and so they cannot grasp the subtle higher truth. Sensual enjoyment is the greatest object of attainment for them.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Poring anxiously over evil resolutions, which only end in death; seeking only the gratification of desire as the highest goal; seeing nothing beyond;

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Giving themselves over to immeasurable cares ending only with death, regarding gratification of lust as their highest aim, and feeling sure that that is all.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda describes the tragedy of a life spent in endless anxiety over acquiring (yoga) and preserving (kshema) material possessions. The demoniac follow the Charvaka philosophy, which holds that physical pleasure (kama) is the ultimate purpose of life. Lacking any vision of a higher spiritual reality or life after death, they believe that this physical existence is all (etavaditi niscitah). This narrow focus leads to pessimism, moral decay, and ultimately a desperate, exhausted death.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Adhering to  their anxiety that is ultimited and may end only at the time of dissolution; viewing the  gratification of their desires alone as their highest goal; ascertaining that this much alone exists;

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Obsessed by unlimited cares which end with dissolution, looking upon enjoyment of desires as their highest aim, and convinced that this is all;

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Beset with innumerable cares which end (only) with death, holding that the enjoyment of desirable objects is the highest goal, feeling sure that this is all.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that Asuri individuals are plagued by endless anxieties concerning the acquisition and preservation of worldly objects. They are firmly convinced that the gratification of physical senses is the sole purpose of human life, with no afterlife or higher reality.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Setting their minds on countless anxieties that end only with death, engrossed only in acquiring and enjoying things, and holding the firm conviction that 'this is all that exists,' they remain.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that Asuri individuals live in endless anxiety about worldly matters (money, health, children, social status, preservation of property) until death. They completely ignore spiritual welfare. They believe that enjoying materials and senses is the only purpose of human birth, and they deny life after death or moral accountability, asserting that the body is destroyed at death and nothing remains.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Those who are sure to die today or tomorrow 'obsess themselves with cares' in regard to objects the attainment of which is not possible even by the time of death. Likewise, they look upon 'enjoyment of desires' as their highest aim, viz., they regard the satisfaction of sensual enjoyments as the highest aim of human life. They are convinced that this is all, viz., they are assured that there is no value in human life greater than this.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 16.9–16.12 jointly): Etam etc. upto arthasancayam :  Their anxiety ends only at the time of dissolution i.e. never ceases, becaue the rise and dissolution never end.  This much alone :  For them the highest goal to be achieved is but the gratification of desires, and when this (aim) is just ruined, there arises anger.  Hence the Lord says 'Devoted to their craving and anger'.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Upasritah, beset with; aparimeyam, innumerable; cintam, cares-worries that defy estimation of their limits!, i.e., constantly burdened with cares; pralayantam, which end (only) with death; kama-upabhoga-paramah, holding that the enjoyment of desirable objects is the highest goal-kama is derived in the sense of 'that which is desired for', viz sound etc.; considered their enjoyment to be the highest; having their minds convinced thus that this alone, viz the enjoyment of desirable objects, is the highest human goal; niscitah, feeling sure; iti, that; etavat, this is all-

Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that Asuri people are continuously weighed down by infinite worries regarding the gain and protection of sensory objects until their death. They deny the existence of any happiness after the dissolution of this body, adhering to Charvaka rules.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the demoniac are always anxious about worldly affairs, which end only with death. They have a firm conviction that sensual pleasure is the highest human goal and nothing else exists.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that Asuri people spend their entire lives in endless anxiety about worldly gain and protection. They believe that physical enjoyment is the only goal, denying the presence of a soul that experiences afterlife.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that their worries concern impossible goals that cannot be achieved even by the time of death. They view sensual gratification as the sole purpose of life, rejecting any higher spiritual values.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that Asuri people spend day and night in endless anxiety. They are convinced that physical enjoyment is the only success, completely ignoring spiritual devotion.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that their anxiety is centered on acquiring and preserving wealth. They believe that the body is the self, and sensual enjoyment is the only purpose of existence.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Taking shelter of insatiable lust and absorbed in the conceit of pride and false prestige, the demoniac, thus illusioned, are always sworn to unclean work, attracted by impermanent things.

 The demoniac accept that the enjoyment of the senses is the ultimate goal of life, and this concept they maintain until death. They do not believe in life after death, and they do not believe that one takes on different types of bodies according to one’s karma, or activities in this world. Their plans for life are never finished, and they go on preparing plan after plan, all of which are never finished. We have personal experience of a person of such demoniac mentality who, even at the point of death, was requesting the physician to prolong his life for four years more because his plans were not yet complete. Such foolish people do not know that a physician cannot prolong life even for a moment. When the notice is there, there is no consideration of the man’s desire. The laws of nature do not allow a second beyond what one is destined to enjoy. The demoniac person, who has no faith in God or the Supersoul within himself, performs all kinds of sinful activities simply for sense gratification. He does not know that there is a witness sitting within his heart. The Supersoul is observing the activities of the individual soul. As it is stated in the Upaniṣads, there are two birds sitting in one tree; one is acting and enjoying or suffering the fruits of the branches, and the other is witnessing. But one who is demoniac has no knowledge of Vedic scripture, nor has he any faith; therefore he feels free to do anything for sense enjoyment, regardless of the consequences.
