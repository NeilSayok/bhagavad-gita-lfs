Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Addressing Dronacharya as 'best of the twice-born,' Duryodhana recites the names of the chief heroic warriors of his own army. A coward, to dispel the fear he feels in the dark, whistles or begins to hum something; Duryodhana's condition too was somewhat like this. The mind of the tyrant Duryodhana, tormented by guilt, was breaking down. Although his army was equipped with capable heroes, on seeing the heroes of the enemy side he was gripped by fear. So he wished to hear words of encouragement from Dronacharya's own mouth; but when he reached the teacher, he remained calm and silent. Therefore, to gather up his broken enthusiasm again, he begins to enumerate the names of the chief warriors of his army. It is natural that a person weighed down by the burden of guilt, lacking moral strength, forgets the propriety of speech and begins to talk excessively. At such a time of mental tension a person's real disposition (samskaras) comes to light. Here Duryodhana addresses his teacher as 'best of the twice-born' (dvijottama). On account of the development of inner knowledge, a brahmana is called 'dvija' (twice-born). Born from the mother's womb, a person, being without refinement, is virtually like an animal; through refinement and study he becomes an educated and cultured person, and this is considered his second birth. This is the meaning of the word 'dvija.' Dronacharya was born in a brahmana family and by nature possessed noble qualities such as tenderness of heart. In the Pandava army were present his own dear disciples. Knowing all this, the clever but shameless Duryodhana had begun to doubt even his teacher's impartiality. When our aims are sinful and full of crookedness, we begin to imagine those very faults even in those close to us and subordinate to us.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
If, taking the enemy's army to be exceedingly vast, you speak thus in fear, then let peace itself be sought with the foes — what use is this obstinate insistence on war? Suspecting this to be the teacher's (Drona's) intent, Duryodhana says "asmakam" (ours). By the word "tu" (but), concealing the fear that has arisen within, he displays his own boldness. Those stationed on our side, superior to all, whom I now name — know them; grasp my words with certainty, is the sense. Although you, being foremost among the knowers of the three Vedas, are yourself able to discern them, still I tell you by name the chief warriors of my army — taking a few among countless ones so as to indicate the rest — for identification, not that I inform you of anything unknown. Thinking thus he addresses him as "dvijottama" (O best of the twice-born).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O best of the twice-born! Take note also of those who are the principal ones on our side. To remind you, I speak of the leaders of my army.

Commentary -- 'asmakam tu vishishta ye tan nibodha dvijottama': Duryodhana says to Dronacharya: 'O best of the twice-born! Just as there are excellent maharathas in the Pandava army, so too in our army there are maharathas of no less distinction -- rather, of even greater distinction than the maharathas of their army. Understand them as well.' The purport of using 'pashya' (behold) in the third verse and 'nibodha' (take note) here is this: the Pandava army stands in front, so to see it Duryodhana uses the verb 'pashya' (behold); but his own army is not in front -- that is, Dronacharya's back is toward his own army -- so, not speaking of seeing it, Duryodhana uses the verb 'nibodha' (take note) to draw his attention to it.

'nayaka mama sainyasya samjnartham tan bravimi te': The distinguished commanders, army-leaders and maharathas in my army too -- I mention their names only to remind you, only to draw your attention there. The word 'samjnartham' (for information) means: we have very many army-leaders; how far shall I recount their names? So I merely give an indication of them, because you know them all already.

In this verse Duryodhana's intent appears to be that our side is by no means weak. But according to statecraft, however weak the enemy side may be and however strong one's own, even so one must not regard the enemy as weak, nor allow the least feeling of neglect or indifference to arise in oneself. Therefore, for the sake of vigilance, I spoke of their army, and now I speak of my own.

The second intent is this: on seeing the Pandava army, a great impression was made upon Duryodhana, and some fear too arose in his mind. The reason is that, though fewer in number, on the Pandava side were many righteous men, and the Lord Himself. The side on which dharma and the Lord abide makes a great impression on all. Even upon the most sinful and wicked person its influence falls; not only that, its influence falls even upon animals, birds, trees, creepers and the like -- because dharma and the Lord are eternal, while material powers, however lofty, are all impermanent. Therefore the Pandava army made a great impression upon Duryodhana. But since faith in material strength was uppermost within him, in order to reassure Dronacharya he says that our side has as much distinction as the Pandava army does not have; therefore we can easily conquer them.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within. Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory. Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra: Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
If, seeing the exceedingly vast strength of the enemy, you are afraid, then let peace itself be sought with the foes — why this obstinacy of war? Suspecting such to be the teacher's intent, Duryodhana speaks. By the word "tu" (but), concealing the fear arisen within, he displays his boldness. Those among us who are distinguished, superior to all, whom I now name — know them; grasp my words with certainty. And the leaders, the chief captains of my army, I tell you by name for identification — taking a few among countless ones to indicate the rest — not that I make known anything unknown. By the epithet "dvijottama" (best of the twice-born) he praises the teacher and wins him over to his cause. In the fault-finding sense "dvijottama" means: being a brahmin you are unskilled in war, so even if you turn away, with Bhishma and other foremost kshatriyas present our loss will not be great. The sense of "for identification" (samjnartham): lest you, your mind agitated with joy on seeing the army of your dear pupils the Pandavas, forget your own heroes — therefore I say this. There he counts the distinguished ones — your honoured self (Drona), Bhishma, Karna, and Kripa. "Samitimjaya" (victor in battle) is an epithet of Kripa; since he is counted after Karna, it is used to ward off Karna's possible anger. These four are supreme in every way. He counts the leaders — Ashvatthama (Drona's son). As Drona was named first before Bhishma, so his son is named first before Vikarna and others, to please the teacher. Vikarna is his younger brother. Saumadatti is the son of Somadatta, being eminent, Bhurishrava. Jayadratha is the king of Sindhu. "Are there only these leaders?" No — "and others" (anye ca): Shalya, Kritavarma and the rest who are resolved to give up even life for my sake — hence "who have renounced life" (tyaktajivita); by this their extreme devotion to Duryodhana is told. Thus by the epithets "heroic" and so forth, the vastness of his army, their devotion, valour, readiness for war, and skill in battle are shown.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
"Those many mighty warriors — how can they be held back by me, a single atiratha?" Dispelling this apprehension, Duryodhana says that in our army there are yet other great heroes to aid you — "asmakam" (ours). Some raise the doubt: "If, seeing the enemy's exceedingly vast strength, you are afraid, then let peace be sought with the foes — why this obstinacy of war?" — and to this he responds. Know those who among us all are distinguished (eminent) and are the chief of my army. Taking a few among countless ones by name so as to indicate the rest, I tell you, I inform you — since you are most excellent I make known nothing unknown; with this intent he says "dvijottama." "Dvijottama" means: being a brahmin you are unskilled in war, so even if you turn away, with Bhishma and other foremost kshatriyas present our loss will not be great — this is Duryodhana's wickedness, so some say.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Thus, intending to give directly the answer 'yatra yogeśvaraḥ' (18.78) to the question that was asked out of a desire to know Suyodhana's (Duryodhana's) victory, Sanjaya — in order to make that answer credible — narrated all the intervening events as well: 'dṛṣṭvā.' 'Having seen the arrayed army of the Pandavas' is the cause of Suyodhana's loss of courage. The particular state consisting in that loss of courage, dependent upon it, is indicated by the word 'tu.' The unexpressed portion from 'dṛṣṭvā' up to 'anunādayan' is made explicit by the passage from 'duryodhana' up to 'akathayat' (Ramanuja-bhashya 1.19). 'saṃjñārtham' means 'for the sake of right knowledge,' or 'for the sake of enumeration by name or number.' There, the portion up to 'antarviṣaṇṇo'bhavat' — that is, ending with 'bhīṣmam evābhirakṣantu bhavantaḥ sarva eva hi' (1.11) — has been explained. The meaning of the verse 'aparyāptam' (1.10) is this: 'tat' — therefore — 'our army guarded by Bhishma is aparyāptam (insufficient), not adequate to conquer the enemy's army; but this army of these Pandavas, guarded by Bhima, is paryāptam (sufficient), adequate to conquer our army.'
But this is untenable, since it would break the apprehended co-referentiality (sāmānādhikaraṇya) in 'tat balam,' and since earlier no cause was adduced for the capability or incapability of the enemy's army and one's own. Nor does he regard his own army, guarded by Bhishma, Drona and others, as incapable; for the slaying of the very powerful Bhishma, Drona and the rest is conditional. And in passages such as 'na bhetavyaṁ mahārāja' (Mahabharata, Udyoga 55.1), the capability of his own army is set forth by Duryodhana many times over. Nor is there now any ground for the contrary notion. Moreover, at the beginning of the second day Duryodhana will say: 'aparyāptaṁ tad asmākaṁ balaṁ pārthābhirakṣitam, paryāptaṁ tv idam eteṣāṁ balaṁ pārthivasattamāḥ' (MBh, Udyoga). There the construction is simply 'asmākam aparyāptam,' not again 'asmākaṁ balam.' Therefore here too the same mode of expression is fitting. Hence the construal should be effected by a variant reading (pāṭhabheda), by remote construction (vyavahita-anvaya), by splitting the sentence (vākyabheda), or by a difference in word-meaning (padārthabheda).
Among these: the variant reading arises by transposing the words 'bhīma' and 'bhīṣma.' Thus, in the construction 'that army of ours, guarded by Bhima, is aparyāptam,' co-referentiality, the naturalness of the remote reference by 'tat,' and non-contradiction with Duryodhana's intent are all established. In remote construction too this same meaning holds; and here remote construction is possible in two ways — one by transposing 'bhīmābhirakṣita' and 'bhīṣmābhirakṣita,' the second by transposing 'aparyāptaṁ tat' and 'paryāptaṁ tv idam.' Merely the interval of separation is tolerated for the sake of appropriateness of meaning.
In splitting the sentence, the construal is thus: 'aparyāptaṁ tat' is one assertion, and 'paryāptaṁ tv idam' is a second. To the doubt 'what is the reason here?', the two sentences serve to supply the reason: 'our army is guarded by Bhishma,' 'their army is guarded by Bhima' — meaning, because our army is presided over by the strong and the enemy's by the weak.
In difference of word-meaning, the construal is thus: 'paryāptam' means 'paryāpana,' completion; 'paryāpta' is a kṛt formation in the agentive sense, meaning 'capable of destroying'; 'aparyāptam' means 'incapable of destroying.' 'This army of ours, guarded by Bhishma — tat — is aparyāptam, unable to destroy the enemy.' Here 'tat' designates the Pandava army as agent, and 'idam' designates one's own army as the agent that finishes off the enemy army. And because of the connection with the past-participle (niṣṭhā), the genitive in the objective sense is not obtained. Or else, 'aparyāptam' means 'unlimited (aparimita)' and 'paryāptam' means 'limited (parimita),' since one's own army is furnished with eleven akshauhinis and the enemy's with seven.
In any case, at the very outset of the war Duryodhana would surely not adduce the weakness of his own army and the strength of the enemy's — that would be like leaving the highway to enter a thief's hut. To explain: here, over against 'guarded by Bhishma,' with what intent is 'guarded by Bhima' pointed out? Not by way of Bhima's generalship like Bhishma's, since Dhrishtadyumna has been declared the commander of that (Pandava army). Nor by way of valour equal to Bhishma's, since Bhima is well known to be greatly unequal to him — as Bhishma himself said: 'Were not Vishnu, the primal Person, their protector, I am able with a single bow to slay all the Pandavas' (MBh). Nor by way of being lord of the opposing army, since the son of Dharma (Yudhishthira) is that. Nor by way of being the foremost warrior of the enemy army, since Arjuna alone is so renowned. Therefore, because Bhima is consecrated to the slaying of all the sons of Dhritarashtra and is endowed with the daring, strength, allies and the like suited to that end, he is specially mentioned. This being so, the mention of Bhishma as set over against him is likewise because Bhishma is understood to be devoted to protecting all the sons of Pandu. Hence it is said that by these two words (Bhima and Bhishma) the excessive apprehension of fear — regarding the enemy and regarding the ally — is indicated. As for what was stated earlier concerning the cause of the capability and incapability of the enemy's army and one's own...

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus spoken of the soldiers of the enemy side, Duryodhana, to encourage his own men, speaks with "asmakam" and so on. Those of ours who are distinguished (great) — know them, hold them in your mind. The address "dvijottama" is used in view of the possibility that (through joy) forgetfulness may overcome you. The leaders (captains) of my army I mention particularly for identification, wondering whether you know their form or not — with this thought I tell you.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
"Vishishtah" means the eminent, the best. "Nibodha" means know; it is the form of the root "budh" (parasmaipada, first conjugation). "Samjnartham" means: to make known that on our side too there are heroes. The sense is — lest your enthusiasm be broken on seeing the strength of the enemy.
