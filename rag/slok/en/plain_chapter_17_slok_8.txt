Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
āyuḥsattvabalārogyasukhaprītivivardhanāḥ .
rasyāḥ snigdhāḥ sthirā hṛdyā āhārāḥ sāttvikapriyāḥ

Word Meanings:
आयुःसत्त्वबलारोग्यसुखप्रीतिविवर्धनाः - those which increase Ayus
रस्याः - savoury
स्निग्धाः - oleaginous
स्थिराः - substantial
हृद्याः - agreeable
आहाराः - the foods
सात्त्विकप्रियाः - are dear to Sattvic
जैसा अन्न - as the food, so the mind

Life Application:
Foods increasing life, purity, strength, health, joy, cheerfulness, succulent, fatty, wholesome, pleasing—liked by Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Foods that promote longevity, intellectual alertness, strength, health, pleasure and happiness, and those that are sweet, oleaginous, substantial and agreeable, are dear to Sattvik men.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The foods which increase life, purity, strength, health, joy and cheerfulness (good appetite), which are savoury and oleaginous, substantial and agreeable, are dear to the Sattvic (pure) people.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The foods that prolong life and increase purity, vigour, health, cheerfulness and happiness are those that are delicious, soothing, substantial and agreeable. These are loved by the Pure.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
To a spiritually inclined Sattvic person, food is naturally appealing if it promotes longevity rather than just fattening the body. It should provide the vitality required for meditation and build strength to resist sensory temptations. Healthy and joy-promoting food is Sattvic. Here, foods are classified into four Gunas: succulent (rasyah), oleaginous (snigdhah), substantial (sthirah), and agreeable (hridyah). Food has an direct influence on the consumer's mind, and conversely, a person's inner nature controls their dietary preferences.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The foods that increase life, energy, strength, good health, happiness and satisfaction; and which are delicious, soft, substantial and pleasant to heart (stomach) - they are dear to the men of the Sattva (Strand).

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Foods which promote longevity, intellectual alertness, strength, health, pleasure and happiness and those that are sweet, oil, substantial and agreeable, are dear to Sattvika men.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Foods that augment life, firmless of mind, strength, health, happiness and delight, and which are succulent, oleaginous, substantial and agreeable, are dear to one endowed with sattva.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that 'Sattva' here refers to mental strength or clarity. 'Priti' means immediate delight. 'Hridya' refers to food that is pleasing to the sight. 'Sthira' means substantial foods that are not digested instantly but sustain the body's energy over time (such as clarified butter/ghee).

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that 'Sattva' means mental steadiness and 'Bala' means the capacity of the body and organs to perform their functions. 'Priti' refers to the joy felt upon seeing such nourishing food. Succulent (Rasyah) means juicy and sweet, and substantial (Sthirah) means having long-lasting benefits for the body.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Foods that increase life, Sattva (purity of mind), strength, health, happiness, and cheerfulness, which are succulent, oleaginous, substantial, and agreeable, are dear to a Sattvic person.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'Ayus' refers to increasing longevity. 'Sattva' increases purity, and 'Bala' brings strength and enthusiasm to the body, mind, and intellect. 'Arogya' promotes health, while 'Sukha' brings inner peace and joy. 'Priti' means that which is pleasant to behold. Substantial (Sthirah) foods are easily digestible yet sustain the body's energy for a long time. Savoury (Rasyah) refers to juicy items like fruits and milk, and oleaginous (Snigdhah) refers to foods containing healthy fats like ghee, butter, and nuts. These are preferred by Sattvic people, indicating their nature.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
To a man endowed with Sattva, foods preponderating in Sattva become dear. The foods preponderating in Sattva promote longevity. Again they promote intellectual alertness. 'Sattva', means internal organ, viz., knowledge which is the effect of the internal organ is here meant by the term Sattva. For the Sattva is the cause of growth of knowledge, as declared in:  'From Sattva arises knowledge' (14.17). Even as the food preponderant in Sattva is the cause of the growth of knowledge, likewise, they promote strength and health; they also promote pleasure and happiness. As the time of assimilation they, by themselves, promote happiness, viz, by the performance of actions which cause happiness. They are sweet, viz., abundant in sweet juices. They are mixed with oil, viz., wholly oily. They are substantial, viz., they originate substantial effects. They are agreeable viz., they appear to the eye in beautiful forms. Food of this kind, full of Sattva-guna, is dear to the person characterised by Sattva.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.7–17.10 jointly): Sri Abhinavagupta explains that food is of three kinds according to Gunas, just like faith, sacrifice, penance, and charity. 'Yatayamam' refers to food for which three hours (one yama) have elapsed after cooking.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Aharah, foods; ayuh-sattva-bala-arogya-sukha-priti-vivardhanah, that augment life, firmneess of mind, strength, health delight; [Life-a brilliant life; firmness of mind or vigour; strength-ability of body and organs; happiness-pleasure of mind; delight-great joy even at seeing other persons prosperous.] and which are rasyah, succulent; snigdhah, oleaginous; sthirah, substantial, lasing in the body for long; [Beneficial to the body for long.] and hrdyah, agreeable, to one's liking; are sattvika-priyah, dear to one endowed with sattva.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains the terms 'Priti' and 'Hridya'. 'Priti' refers to the immediate pleasure felt at the time of eating, whereas 'Hridya' refers to the lasting satisfaction and agreeableness afterwards. 'Sthira' indicates foods that are prepared fresh and provide long-lasting energy to the body, such as ghee.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that a person dominated by Sattva Guna naturally prefers Sattvic food. Such food increases longevity, knowledge (represented by Sattva), strength, health, happiness, and delight. These foods are sweet, oily, substantial in assimilation, and visually pleasing, which promote overall well-being.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati defines the terms: 'Ayus' is longevity; 'Sattva' is mental courage and poise under distress; 'Bala' is bodily strength to perform one's duties without fatigue; 'Arogya' is freedom from disease; 'Sukha' is satisfaction after eating; and 'Priti' is eagerness and absence of aversion during meals. Sattvic foods are sweet, oleaginous, lasting in the body, and pure (free from bad odor or dirt). Seekers of Sattva must consume these.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that foods dear to Sattvic people are juice-filled (Rasyah), fatty/oleaginous (Snigdhah), substantial (Sthirah - sustaining the body for long), and pleasing to look at (Hridyah). These promote life, enthusiasm (Sattva), strength, health, mental peace (Sukha), and appetite.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that Sattvic foods are sweet (like sugar), oleaginous (like milk and ghee), long-lasting in the body, and pure from all visible and invisible defects. They augment life, mental fortitude (Sattva), strength, health, inner joy, and satisfaction. Aspiring seekers must adopt these.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that 'Ayus' is placed first because it is necessary for all human goals. 'Sattva' means knowledge or mental clarity. 'Bala' means bodily energy, and 'Arogya' is the balance of bodily humors (Dhatus). 'Sukha' is the happiness experienced during assimilation, and 'Priti' is the joy that motivates righteous actions. The qualities Rasyah (sweet), Snigdhah (oily), Sthirah (substantial), and Hridyah (beautiful/pleasing) characterize Sattvic food.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that 'Sattva' means a pure heart. 'Priti' is love. Foods are described as juicy, oily, sustaining, and delightful. Such foods are dear to Sattvic people, and those who consume them are recognized as Sattvic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that 'Sattva' is enthusiasm, and 'Bala' is energy. Rasyah means juicy, Snigdhah means oily/fatty, Sthirah means staying long in the body, and Hridyah means pleasant to look at. These include milk, ghee, and sugar, which are dear to the Sattvic.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Foods dear to those in the mode of goodness increase the duration of life, purify one’s existence and give strength, health, happiness and satisfaction. Such foods are juicy, fatty, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart.
