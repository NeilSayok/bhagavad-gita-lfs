Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
brahmaṇyādhāya karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ .
lipyate na sa pāpena padmapatramivāmbhasā

Word Meanings:
ब्रह्मण्याधाय - offering to Brahman
कर्माणि - actions
सङ्गं - attachment
त्यक्त्वा - having abandoned
करोति - performs
यः - who
लिप्यते - is tainted
न - not
स - he
पापेन - by sin
पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा - like lotus leaf by water

Life Application:
Offer your work to the divine presence, remaining untouched by worldly troubles just as a lotus leaf repels water.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who performs all duties as an offering to Supreme Brahman, completely relinquishing attachment and desire for rewards, remains unsullied by sin, just as a lotus leaf floating on water remains untouched by moisture.

He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Offering actions to Brahman"—dedicating all actions to Supreme Brahman Lord Vishnu frees the soul from all sins to attain fearlessness.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Offering all works to Supreme Brahman and relinquishing attachment, the practitioner remains untouched by worldly sins, just like a lotus leaf on water.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Offering actions to the Supreme Lord (Brahman) and recognizing bodily traits as belonging to nature, the seeker remains undefiled by sin.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He who is devoted to Yoga, pure in self, master of his body and senses, who realizes his own Self as the Self of all beings, is not tainted though acting. The knower of Truth, centered in Yoga, while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing eyes, thinks: "I do nothing at all; the senses merely move among their objects." He who performs actions offering them to Brahman, abandoning attachment, is not stained by sin, even as a lotus leaf is untouched by water. Yogis perform actions without attachment, with body, mind, intellect, and senses alone, solely for the purification of the self.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water"—uses the famous simile of the lotus leaf (*Padma-patram ivambhasa*) to illustrate complete detachment in action.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The attitude of offering all work to God neutralizes the toxic effects of karma, granting the seeker the Supreme Abode.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Work performed with dedication to Lord Krishna turns into selfless devotion, leaving the soul completely untainted.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Like a lotus leaf on water"—a seeker doing God-dedicated work, though staying in the world, remains entirely untainted by karmic impurities.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He who performs actions, offering them to Brahman and abandoning attachment, is not tainted by sin, as a lotus leaf by water.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis demonstrating how offering work to Brahman and surrendering fruits keeps the practitioner untainted by sin.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Surrendering all actions to Lord Vasudeva and remaining selfless, the practitioner stays forever untouched by worldly bonds.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When every action of the practitioner becomes an offering to Lord Krishna, it ceases to be work and turns into unswerving divine service.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Like a lotus leaf on water"—one acting solely for inner purification, though staying in the world, remains transcendent of worldly bondage.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who performs his duty without attachment, surrendering the results unto the Supreme Lord, is unaffected by sinful action, as the lotus leaf is untouched by water.
