Life Application:
Acts of sacrifice, gift, and austerity should not be abandoned but performed; they are purifiers of the wise.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity are not to be abandoned, but indeed they must be performed; sacrifice, charity and austerity are the purifiers of the wise (seekers).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Acts of sacrifice, gift and austerity should not be abandoned, but should be performed; sacrifice, gift and also austerity are the purifiers of the wise.

Seekers who desire liberation from the bondage born of ignorance must practice sacrifice, charity and austerity with faith and devotion. Through this, they can attain inner peace and balance. Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
To determine that very decision of the Lord specifically, [the commentator] shows the introduction to the subsequent verse by prefixing a question: "What then [is that decision]?" He states the reason for the obligatoriness of sacrifice etc. with the words: "Sacrifice" etc. He says: "[It is] to be performed," meaning it is not only not to be abandoned, but it must indeed be performed. Having thus analyzed what was promised, he analyzes the reason by asking: "Why?"

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be abandoned; rather, they must indeed be performed, because sacrifice, charity and austerity—all three of these actions—are the purifiers of the wise.

Commentary — yajñadānatapaḥkarma na tyājyaṃ kāryameva tat — Here the Lord has accepted the opinion of others (18.3) as correct. The Lord does not refute anyone's opinion with harsh words. To show respect, the Lord takes the true essence of others' opinions and includes His own opinion in it. Here, in accordance with the opinion of others, the Lord said that the acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be abandoned. Along with this, the Lord stated His own opinion that not only this, but if they are not being done, they must certainly be performed — kāryameva tat. The reason is that sacrifice, charity and austerity — all three actions — are the purifiers of the wise.
yajño dānaṃ tapaścaiva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām — Here, the term caiva implies that all obligatory duties, whether daily (nitya), occasional (naimittika), related to livelihood, or related to the body, etc., must also be performed because they too are the purifiers of the wise. Those who, endowed with evenness of mind, renounce the fruits of action, are the wise (manisi) — karmajaṃ buddhiyuktā hi phalaṃ tyaktvā manīṣiṇaḥ (Gita 2.51). These actions of sacrifice etc. purify such wise people. But those who are not truly wise and whose senses are not under control, i.e., who perform acts of sacrifice, charity, etc., only for their own pleasure and enjoyment, are not purified by those actions; instead, those actions become a cause of bondage.
In the first half of this verse, the compound word yajñadānatapaḥkarma is used, and in the second half, the separate words yajño dānaṃ tapaḥ are used. This implies that the Lord has shown through the compound word that sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be abandoned, but rather must be performed; and through the separate words, He has shown that even each one of these actions individually is a purifier of the wise.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Acts such as sacrifices, gifts, austerities etc., enjoined in the Vedas should not be relinished by the aspirant for release, but should be performed day after day until his death.

Why?

Acts like sacrifices, gifts and austerities associated with the different stations of life, are the means of purification for the wise., i.e., for those given to contemplation. Contemplation is worship. For the aspirants who perform such worship (Upasana) throughout their lives, they (sacrifices etc.) are a help to erase the previous Karmas which stand in the way of the fulfilment of such worship.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.4–18.11 jointly): Niscayam etc.  upto abhidhiyate.  The conclusion here is  this :  Due to the manifoldness of the nature of the Strands, that have been defined earlier, the act of relinishment itself is performed with a certain mental disposition which is a modification of the Sattva, the Rajas and the Tamas (the Strands).  Because it reflects  (is contaminated by)  the nature of the person having the same (the said mental dispositon),  what is called the real (unalloyed)  relinishment is the performance of the actions  by the knowers of the Supreme Brahman by giving up desire to achieve fruits and by avoiding the craving and hatred on account of their eanimity to [the pairs of opposites like]  success and failure etc.  That is why [the Bhagavat] says :  'By the act of relinishment born of the Rajas or of the Tamas  (Strands), no connection with the fruit  [of relinishment] is attained'.  However, for an act of relinishing, born of the Sattva (Strand), there is the fruit in the form of honouring the purport of the scriptures.  The application of the term  'relinishment'  stands to reason, in fact, only in the case of a sage who has relinished his holding on the multitude of the Strands.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Yajna-dana-tapah-karma, the practice of sacrifice, charity and austerity-this threefold practice; na tyajyam, is not to be abandoned; tat, it; is eva, surely; karyam, to be undertaken. Why? Yajnah, sacrifice; danam, charity; and tapah, austerity; are eva, verily; pavanani, the purifiers, the causes of sanctification; manisinam, of the wise, i.e. of those who do not seek results for themselves.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He accepts the Mimamsaka view in one part with the words: "yajña..." Sacrifice is indeed of the form of the Lord, in accordance with the Shruti passage: "Sacrifice is indeed Vishnu" (Taittiriya Samhita 1.7.4, Shatapatha Brahmana 1.3.1); charity is of the nature of offering wealth dedicated to a deity; and austerity is the performance of one's own duty; this Vedic action, which is of the nature of the steps of the Lord's dharma, should not be abandoned even by an aspirant for liberation (mumukshu); rather, it must indeed be performed daily until death. It is to be performed because it is spoken in the Vedas, and because of the rule that no part of the Vedas should be abandoned. Now, why is there this instruction of the injunction to perform them? To this he says: "pāvanāni" (purifiers). Sacrifice etc. are purifiers of the wise (manīṣī). Since they bring about virtues for those very wise men who had said "they are full of defects" (in 18.3), the view that "they are full of defects" is refuted. For this very reason, the devotee Yudhishthira, accepting the command, "I shall perform sacrifice to your manifestations" (Bhagavata Purana 10.7.3), performed it indeed for the sake of purification. And this topic — whether action should be performed by the devotees of the Lord or not — is detailed in the Bhashya (commentary) by presenting the debates and discussions, and is therefore not elaborated here.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
What is that decision, which is the second of the two opposing views? He states this in two verses beginning with "yajña...". The word "ca" (and) is in the sense of cause. Because sacrifice, charity and austerity are the purifiers of the wise who do not seek the fruits of actions, cleansing the impurity of sin which obstructs knowledge and bringing about the virtuous quality of fitness for the arising of knowledge, it is only for those who do not seek the fruits of actions that sacrifice, charity and austerity are indeed purifiers. Here, the purification of the consciousness associated with the adjunct (upahita) through the purification of the limiting adjunct (upadhi) is intended. Therefore, by those qualified for action who desire purification of the inner instrument (antahkarana), action in the form of sacrifice, charity and austerity, performed without seeking fruits, should not be abandoned, but indeed it must be performed. Even though obligatoriness is implied by "should not be abandoned", it is stated again as "must indeed be performed" (kāryam eva) for the sake of extreme emphasis. Or, because it is prescribed by scripture as a duty to be performed, therefore it is indeed not to be abandoned.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
First, He states His decision in two verses beginning with "yajñadāna...". They are the purifiers of the wise, i.e., the discriminating ones, causing purification of the mind.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Showing the promised decision, He states the reason for it: the threefold action consisting of sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be abandoned, i.e., must not be given up. He strengthens the meaning stated by negation through the positive statement: that threefold action is to be performed indeed, meaning it must certainly be done. The word "ca" is in the sense of cause. Because sacrifice, charity and austerity alone are the purifiers, i.e., the cause of purification. Or indeed, they are purifiers of the wise, i.e., the competent ones who do not seek the fruits of actions.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Thus, the repetition of the threefold abandonment is stated for the purpose of excluding the abandonment of the essential nature of action itself, in the words starting with "yajñadāna...". Some say that sacrifice etc. should be performed prior to the arising of the desire to know etc., but should be abandoned afterwards. To refute their view, he (Sri Ramanuja) says: "never indeed". With the intention of the Upanishadic text, "He indeed spending his life thus..." (Chandogya Upanishad 8.15.1), he says: "but until death". And so says the Brahmasutra: "Until death; for there also it is seen" (Brahma Sutra 4.1.12). "Day after day" is indicative of the prescribed time. Now, why should one who is established in knowledge perform action? First, it cannot be as an indirect helper (aradu-pakara), because the view of combination of knowledge and action is not accepted. Nor is it for the sake of the knowledge of truth, since that is established from the scriptures along with their auxiliaries. Nor is it for the sake of worship (upasana) which is in the form of constant remembrance of that, since that too is accomplished by the clarity of mental impressions etc. And we do not see any other purpose to be achieved by action which is desired by the seeker of liberation. And even a dull-witted person does not act without a purpose. With this intention, the expectation of "Why?" is shown. The word "prabhriti" (etc.) is used with the intention that the word "ca" (and) is for accumulating what is not explicitly stated. By "[actions] associated with the stations of life (varnashrama)", the idea is that the view that even daily and occasional actions should be abandoned in their essential form is rejected here. To indicate the direct help of sacrifice etc., the word "manisi" here refers to the nature of knowledge that is to be helped; with this intention, he (Sri Ramanuja) says: "for those given to contemplation". This is the resulting statement from the etymology: "those who control the mind (manasah ishinah) are manishis". To exclude logical reflection which occurs after hearing (shravana), he says: "contemplation is worship". He explains the mode of help established by the combination "purifiers of the wise" with the words beginning with "for the aspirants...". Beginning with "One should meditate until death" (Prashna Upanishad 5.1), it is understood that worship, as in "He who meditates on the Supreme Person with this very syllable Om..." (Prashna Upanishad 5.5), is to be performed throughout life. Therefore, its auxiliary must also be performed throughout life. And this is described by passages like "He indeed spending his life thus...", which is the idea. The word "previous" (pracina) is used because, unlike worship, it does not destroy subsequent sins.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He will describe the threefold nature later (18.7-9); first, He states His decision in the two verses starting with "yajñadāna...". Action such as sacrifice should not be abandoned, because it is a duty that must certainly be performed for the sake of avoiding sin of omission. Sacrifice is offering, charity and austerity of the wise, i.e., the knowers of truth, who know their true nature; when these are performed for the sake of knowledge of the Self, they are indeed purifiers, i.e., cleansers of the mind. Therefore, this threefold action must be performed. By the word "eva" (indeed), it is implied that they should not be performed with the desire for any other fruit.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Before describing the nature of abandonment according to the maxim of the needle and the cauldron (suchi-kataha-nyaya), He presents the opponent's view of non-abandonment in the words "yajña...". Action such as sacrifice is not to be abandoned, but must indeed be performed; it must certainly be carried out by a person just like one who is pressed into forced labor. Because scripture declares sin of omission for not doing it. The word "ca" has the meaning of cause. Because sacrifice, charity and austerity are indeed purifiers, i.e., cleansers of the mind, of the wise who are desireless and free from hypocrisy. And so says the Shruti: "There are three branches of dharma: sacrifice, study and charity are the first; austerity is indeed the second; and living in the house of the teacher as a celibate student is the third. All these lead to worlds of merit" (Chandogya Upanishad 2.23.1). This shows the purifying nature of sacrifice etc. which are the duties of the householder, of austerity which is the duty of the forest-dweller, and of living in the teacher's house which is the duty of the celibate student. Here also, by the words "sacrifice" and "charity", the duties of a householder are to be understood; by "austerity", the duties of a forest-dweller; and by remainder, by "action", the duties of a celibate student are to be understood.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Acts of sacrifice, charity and penance are not to be given up; they must be performed. Indeed, sacrifice, charity and penance purify even the great souls.

 The yogīs should perform acts for the advancement of human society. There are many purificatory processes for advancing a human being to spiritual life. The marriage ceremony, for example, is considered to be one of these sacrifices. It is called vivāha-yajña. Should a sannyāsī, who is in the renounced order of life and who has given up his family relations, encourage the marriage ceremony? The Lord says here that any sacrifice which is meant for human welfare should never be given up. Vivāha-yajña, the marriage ceremony, is meant to regulate the human mind so that it may become peaceful for spiritual advancement. For most men, this vivāha-yajña should be encouraged even by persons in the renounced order of life. Sannyāsīs should never associate with women, but that does not mean that one who is in the lower stages of life, a young man, should not accept a wife in the marriage ceremony. All prescribed sacrifices are meant for achieving the Supreme Lord. Therefore, in the lower stages, they should not be given up. Similarly, charity is for the purification of the heart. If charity is given to suitable persons, as described previously, it leads one to advanced spiritual life.
