Life Application:
Build healthy mental boundaries so that sensory impulses no longer dictate your daily actions and mood.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When explaining a principle, one does not reveal the final conclusion without first offering sufficient reasoning, however acceptable that conclusion may be. Keeping this in mind, after giving Arjuna the necessary arguments, Sri Krishna arrives at the conclusion in this verse: if, in life, we seek something higher than mere tears and grief, then a life of self-restraint alone is worth living. He whose senses are completely under control with respect to their objects is truly a man of steady wisdom.

Controlling the senses should not be understood to mean that the wise man's senses become useless, incapable of perceiving objects at all - weakness of the senses is not a mark of wisdom. It only means that contact with sense-objects can no longer disturb the peace of his mind; nothing can shake him. The ignorant man is a slave to his senses, while the man of steady wisdom is their master.

Elaborating further on the marks of the wise, the Lord continues.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
The Lord now concludes that only one whose senses are completely restrained in every way is truly possessed of knowledge. Hence the word 'therefore' (tasmat) is used.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
An objection may be raised that the present verses merely restate, and thereby needlessly repeat, the meaning already conveyed in the verses beginning 'yatato hy api.' This is answered by explaining the intended sense of the word 'that' (tat) referred to in the verse, which is to be understood from the earlier statement beginning 'dhyayato vishayan' (dwelling on sense-objects). The connection is this: because the senses, when followed by an uncollected mind, forcibly carry away one's wisdom, the word 'therefore' (tasmat) is used to introduce the conclusion that follows.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Therefore, O mighty-armed one, the intellect of the person whose senses are completely restrained from the objects of the senses is steady.

Explanation: Concluding the subject of controlling the mind and senses that has been under discussion since verse 60, the word 'therefore' declares that one whose mind and senses no longer feel the pull of the world has a firmly established intellect.

The word 'in every way' here means that whether engaged in worldly dealings or absorbed in solitary contemplation, in no state do his senses become engaged with enjoyments or objects. However many objects come into contact with him during worldly activity, they cannot shake him; his mind, even when joined with the senses, cannot unsettle his intellect. Just as a mountain cannot be moved, his intellect becomes so firm that the mind can never shake it under any circumstance, because objects have ceased to hold any importance for his mind.

The word 'restrained' means that the senses are completely brought under control with respect to objects - that is, not the slightest attachment or fascination toward objects remains in them. Just as a snake whose fangs have been removed loses its venom, so that even if it bites someone it has no effect, so too freeing the senses from attachment and aversion is like removing their venomous fangs; then the senses no longer have the power to lead the seeker down the path of downfall.

The purport of this verse is that the seeker should firmly resolve that the goal is attainment of God, not enjoyment of pleasures or accumulation of possessions. If such vigilance remains constant in the seeker, the intellect will become steady.

Connection: The next verse explains the difference between one whose senses are thus completely controlled and ordinary people.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Here the highest subject of inquiry - the mark of the man of knowledge - is under discussion. To remove any doubt about the very possibility of such knowledge, it is shown that knowledge is attainable only through control of the senses, itself achieved by great effort. Since it was not clear to whom this concluding statement applies, the verse beginning with 'therefore' explains that it is the man of knowledge who is thus produced. Since it is only one whose senses are restrained who attains inner clarity, and only one possessing that clarity attains right reasoning, and only one possessing right reasoning attains knowledge of truth through hearing and reflection, and only one possessing knowledge of truth attains the meditation that is the means to direct realization - and not otherwise - the word 'therefore' is used here.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Summing up what has been said, the verse begins with 'therefore.' O Arjuna, endowed with the power of action, one whose senses are completely restrained from their objects should be understood to have a firmly established wisdom - such a person is recognized by this mark. By addressing him as 'mighty-armed,' it is also suggested that Arjuna himself should exercise this restraint of the senses with his own two arms.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Since it is thus, 'in every way' means all the senses together with the mind. By addressing Arjuna as 'mighty-armed,' the Lord suggests that just as Arjuna is capable of subduing all enemies, he is equally capable of subduing the enemies that are the senses. The rest is clear. The word 'his' refers both to the perfected sage and to the seeker still striving, since the control of the senses is to be concluded here both as the defining mark of the man of steady wisdom and as the means to wisdom for the seeker of liberation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Summarizing what has been said - that control of the senses is both the means to and the defining mark of steady wisdom - the verse begins with 'since.' It means that his wisdom becomes established. In concluding this defining mark, one should understand that his wisdom is established. By addressing him as 'mighty-armed,' it is suggested that just as Arjuna has the capacity to subdue enemies, he would also have the capacity for this control of the senses.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Summing up, the verse begins with 'therefore.' Just as brave warriors secure a kingdom by conquering enemies with their mighty arms, so too the discerning secure the establishment of wisdom by conquering the senses, which are their enemies. This is suggested by addressing Arjuna as 'O mighty-armed one.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
It is stated, beginning with the word 'therefore,' that wisdom becomes established only for one who completely restrains the senses. Since, in the absence of control over the senses, wisdom is destroyed, therefore one whose senses are restrained from their objects has a firmly established wisdom. By addressing him as 'mighty-armed,' his capacity to accomplish this is indicated.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Having established at length the point begun with 'even of the striving' (yatato hy api), the verse now concludes with 'therefore.' Since the mind is dependent on the senses, and wisdom follows the mind, therefore, O mighty-armed one, know that the wisdom of the ascetic whose senses - together with the mind, their root cause - are completely restrained in every way from their objects such as sound and so on, becomes firmly established.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Therefore, O mighty-armed, one whose senses are restrained from their objects is certainly of steady intelligence.

 One can curb the forces of sense gratification only by means of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, or engaging all the senses in the transcendental loving service of the Lord. As enemies are curbed by superior force, the senses can similarly be curbed, not by any human endeavor, but only by keeping them engaged in the service of the Lord. One who has understood this – that only by Kṛṣṇa consciousness is one really established in intelligence and that one should practice this art under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master – is called a sādhaka, or a suitable candidate for liberation.
