Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
In spiritual literature, this illustration given by Lord Parthasarathy is highly attention-arresting. This illustration makes the relationship between the Field (Kshetra) and the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna) easily comprehensible. Just as the single sun situated in the far-off sky always illuminates this world, so also the one Self merely illumines the objects, body, mind, and intellect.

Although, in colloquial language, we say 'the sun illumines the world,' and thereby attribute the agency of the action of illumining to the sun, yet upon reflection it will be found that this attribution of ours is completely baseless. Action is that which begins at a particular moment and ends at another, and is generally performed for the fulfillment of some strong desire or silent purpose. From this perspective, the sun does not 'act' to illumine the world. Illumination is its very nature, and every object is illuminated in its presence. Similarly, Consciousness is the very nature of the Self, and in Its presence, all things are known.

The good and the bad, the virtuous and the vicious, the beautiful and the ugly of the world—all these are illumined by the single sun, but it is not tainted by the virtues or defects of any of them. In the same way, the Self, which is Existence-Consciousness-Bliss (Sat-Chit-Ananda), though manifested in the adjuncts (upadhis), remains untouched by the sins of the mind, the modifications of the intellect, and the transgressions of the body.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He concludes the meaning of the entire chapter along with its fruit by the word 'samasta' (entire) etc. The distinction between the Field (Kshetra) and the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna) is characterized by immutability (kutasthatva) and mutability (parinama), respectively. Thus, for one who possesses the true knowledge of the reality of the Field and the Knower of the Field by being established in virtues like humility (amanitva) etc., the attainment of the human goal (purushartha), characterized by the manifestation of complete, supreme bliss through the cessation of all evils/miseries, is established.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, descendant of Bharata! Just as the single sun illumines the entire universe, so does the Lord of the Field (the Knower of the Field, the Self) illumine the entire Field.

Commentary: 'yathā prakāśayatyekaḥ kṛtsnaṁ lokamimaṁ raviḥ' — The single sun alone illumines this entire world visible to the eyes, all the objects of the world, and all the activities of the world take place under the light of the sun; yet there is no sense of agency in the sun, such as 'I illumine all.' For example, in the sun's light itself, a Brahmin recites the Vedas and a hunter kills animals; but the light of the sun does not become the cause of performing or causing to perform the actions of Vedic chanting or hunting. Here, the word 'loka' denotes the universe (the fourteen worlds). The reason is that whatever light exists in the universe (in the moon, stars, fire, gems, herbs, etc.) is all from the sun alone.

'kṣetraṁ kṣetrī tathā kṛtsnaṁ prakāśayati bhārata' — Like the sun, a single Lord of the Field (Kshetrajna, Self) illumines all the fields, meaning that all activities of doing and causing to do in all fields take place in the light of the Lord of the Field, but the Lord of the Field does not become the cause in performing or causing to perform those activities.

The sun illumines only the physical world, and only the activities of the physical world take place in its light; but the Lord of the Field does not illumine only the physical field (world), rather He illumines the physical, subtle, and causal — all three fields, and in His light, all the activities of the physical, subtle, and causal bodies take place.

Just as the sun, despite illumining the entire world, does not get the egoism (of illumining all), and despite illumining various kinds of activities, does not undergo diverse divisions; similarly, the Lord of the Field, despite illumining all fields and giving them existence and consciousness, does not acquire egoism or agency, and despite illumining various kinds of actions, does not undergo diverse divisions. That Lord of the Field always remains as He is, unattached and untouched.

No action or object exists without a locus (support), and no perception occurs without light (knowledge). The Lord of the Field is the locus and the illuminer of all actions, objects, and perceptions.

Connection: Now, the Lord concludes the section by describing the fruit of knowing the division between the Field (Kshetra) and the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna).

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
As the 'one sun' illumines 'all this world' by his radiance, so the 'knower of the body' illumines the entire Ksetra, i.e., the body, by Its own knowledge, within and without and from head to toe, by conceiving 'This my body is of this nature.' This self of the said nature is totally different from the body, because It is the knower of the body. The body is the object of Its knowledge and is therefore different from It, even as the illuminating sun is totally different from the illumined world.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He clarifies the aspect of being the illuminer through another illustration, by 'yathā' (as) etc. Consciousness, which is of the nature of knowledge, is the attribute of the Self, not of Prakriti (nature). Just as the sun, possessing the nature of light, illumines everything, so does this Lord of the Field (Ksetrin). By this, it is taught that the Purusha's inherent state is indeed Ksetritva (being the owner of the field), but now, through the knowledge of reality (tattvajnana), due to the essence of the Lord's attributes, it is designated as Ksetrajnatva (being the knower of the field).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The Self is not contaminated not only because of Its unattached nature, but also because, being the illuminer, It is not affected by the attributes of the illumined. To show this, He gives an illustration with 'yathā' (as) etc. Just as the single sun illumines this entire world, i.e., the aggregate of body and senses, or rather all physical objects, and is neither contaminated by the properties of the illumined nor divided by the differences among the illumined; similarly, the Lord of the Field (Ksetrin), the Knower of the Field, being one, illumines the entire Field. O descendant of Bharata! Therefore, He is not contaminated by the attributes of the illumined, nor is He divided by the differences of the illumined. This is the meaning. As is declared by the Sruti: 'Just as the sun, which is the eye of the entire world, is not contaminated by the external ocular defects, so also the one Self within all beings is not contaminated by the misery of the world, being external (unattached).'

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
It was stated through the illustration of ether that there is no contamination due to unattachment. Now, through the illustration of the sun, He says that being the illuminer, It is not associated with the attributes of the illumined, with 'yathā' (as) etc. The meaning is clear.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
He shows by an illustration that being of the nature of light, It is not contaminated by the attributes of the illumined, with 'yathā' (as) etc. Just as the single sun illumines this entire world experienced by perception etc., and while illumining it, is not contaminated by the attributes of the illumined; similarly, the entire Field from the great elements down to fortitude is illumined by the single Lord of the Field (Ksetrin), who is the Supreme Self, identical with the inner Self; and while illumining it, that one Supreme Self is not contaminated by the attributes of the illumined. This is the meaning. As is also declared by the Sruti: 'Just as the sun, which is the eye of the entire world, is not contaminated by the external ocular defects, so also the one Self within all beings is not contaminated by the misery of the world, being external (unattached).' He addresses him as 'Bhārata' (descendant of Bharata) to indicate that just as a single Bharata illumined (made famous) all of you by his name, similarly does the Self.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Now, He establishes with an illustration the difference between the object of knowledge (vedyatva) and the knower (veditrtva) as expounded at the beginning of the chapter in 'He who knows this is called the Knower of the Field' (13.2), and the difference between multiplicity and oneness arising respectively from being an aggregate and being non-aggregate, by this verse 'yathā prakāśayati' (as he illumines). The example of radiance (prabhā) is given to demonstrate the distinction between the essential nature of the Self and Its attribute of knowledge, even though they are of the same class. By 'krtsnaṁ kṣetram' (the entire field), the word 'krtsna' implies the inclusion of all bodies. Others (like Shankaracharya) have commented that the illustration of the sun is to show that like the sun, the Self is one in all fields and is uncontaminated. But that is inappropriate, because the selves (atmans) are different in each field, and there is no illumination of all fields by any single self. If it is interpreted in terms of class (jati), then the illustration of the single sun would be inconsistent. Keeping this in mind, (Ramanuja) says, 'within and without, and from head to toe'. 'Without' means the skin etc., and 'within' means the flesh etc. The word 'krtsna' is used to show the distinction of the Self from the body which is characterized by multiplicity due to the differences of its organs, and to dispel the doubt that any of these organs could be the knower. The meaning is that one Self, who is cognized as the knower of the whole body, is distinct from the body which is an aggregate of many organs, and this is established by the strength of direct experience. This illustration is not meant to show mere illumination, but rather the difference based on it, as indicated by 'from the illumined'. The distinction is obvious here due to the relationship of the object of action and the agent (karma-kartr-bhava).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
The doubt is: if the Self is uncontaminated, how can It be the illuminer of the attributes of the body etc.? Raising this doubt, He says 'yathā' (as) etc. Just as the one sun, being of the nature of My portion, illumines this entire world, so also the Lord of the Field (Ksetrin), solely due to being My portion, illumines the entire Field. Since the sun is of the nature of the eye, by that illustration it is indicated that for the purpose of being fit for play (leela) through My grace, the Self also illumines the Field. By addressing him as 'Bhārata', it is indicated that just as you, being My portion, are not contaminated by the defects despite being situated in the midst of the army, so also is the Self.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He expounds both points, 'He neither acts nor is contaminated', by another illustration with 'yathā' (as) etc. Just as the sun illumines the universe by its mere presence and not by being engaged in activity like a weaver weaving a cloth; and just as it is not contaminated by the defects such as foul odors of the illumined objects; similarly, this Lord of the Field (Ksetrin), the Knower of the Field, being one like the sun, illumines by His mere presence the entire Field, which is manifested in diverse ways, consists of twenty-four principles starting from the great elements, and is associated with modifications like desire and hatred. O descendant of Bharata! He does not produce it by being engaged in activity. Nor is He contaminated by its attributes like virtue and sin. Through the illustration of the sun, the oneness of the Self and Its uncontaminated nature resulting from non-agency (akartrtva) are shown. As is declared by the Srutis: 'Just as the luminous sun, entering into different bodies of water, appears in multiple forms, so also this unborn Deity appears in different forms in various fields (bodies) due to the adjuncts (upadhis).' And 'Just as the sun, which is the eye of the entire world, is not contaminated by external ocular defects, so also the one Self within all beings is not contaminated by the misery of the world, being external (unattached).'

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O son of Bharata, as the sun alone illuminates all this universe, so does the living entity, one within the body, illuminate the entire body by consciousness.

 There are various theories regarding consciousness. Here in Bhagavad-gītā the example of the sun and the sunshine is given. As the sun is situated in one place but is illuminating the whole universe, so a small particle of spirit soul, although situated in the heart of this body, is illuminating the whole body by consciousness. Thus consciousness is the proof of the presence of the soul, as sunshine or light is the proof of the presence of the sun. When the soul is present in the body, there is consciousness all over the body, and as soon as the soul has passed from the body there is no more consciousness. This can be easily understood by any intelligent man. Therefore consciousness is not a product of the combinations of matter. It is the symptom of the living entity. The consciousness of the living entity, although qualitatively one with the supreme consciousness, is not supreme, because the consciousness of one particular body does not share that of another body. But the Supersoul, which is situated in all bodies as the friend of the individual soul, is conscious of all bodies. That is the difference between supreme consciousness and individual consciousness.
