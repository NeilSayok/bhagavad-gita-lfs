Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
adhyeṣyate ca ya imaṃ dharmyaṃ saṃvādamāvayoḥ .
jñānayajñena tenāhamiṣṭaḥ syāmiti me matiḥ

Life Application:
Study sacred spiritual teachings regularly today as a form of intellectual worship of the Divine.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
And he who will study this sacred dialogue of ours, by him I shall be worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge; such is My view.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
And he who will study this sacred dialogue of ours, by him I shall have been worshipped by the sacrifice of wisdom,  such is My conviction.

अध्येष्यते shall study? च and? यः who? इमम् this? धर्म्यम् sacred? संवादम् dialogue? आवयोः of ours? ज्ञानयज्ञेन by the sacrifice of wisdom? तेन by him? अहम् I? इष्टः worshipped? स्याम् (I) shall have been? इति thus? मे My? मतिः conviction. Commentary There are four kinds of sacrifice -- Vidhi? Japa? Upamsu and Manasa. Vidhi is ritual. Japa is recitation of a Mantra. Upamsu is Japa done in a whisper. Of the four kinds? JnanaYajna or the wisdomsacrifice comes under Manasa and is? therefore? the highest. The Gita is eulogised as a JnanaYajna. He who studies this scripture with faith and devotion will attain the fruit that is eal to that of performing JnanaYajna or meditation on a deity and the like.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who will study this spiritual discourse of ours, I assure thee, he shall thereby worship Me at the altar of Wisdom.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
After honoring all teachers of the Gita, Lord Krishna now praises those students who study this holy Bhagavad Gita. The philosophy of life contained in this dialogue between the infinite Lord Krishna and the finite Jiva Arjuna has its own powerful attraction. Even those who study it superficially are gradually drawn into its sacred depths. Such a reader unknowingly offers his ego, selfishness, and other desires as oblations in the fire of knowledge lit through hearing, contemplation, and meditation. This is the significance of the term 'Jnana-yajna' (sacrifice of knowledge) used metaphorically. Therefore, those seekers who purify themselves by offering their ego and desires into the fire of wisdom are indeed the great worshippers of God and are worthy of praise. Now, the Lord praises the listener of this knowledge and describes the fruit obtained.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
And he who will study this sacred dialogue of ours, by him I shall be worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge; such is My view. Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains the significance of studying the Gita: 1. Studying this sacred dialogue (adhyeṣyate ca ya imaṃ dharmyaṃ saṃvādam): Reading or reciting the dialogue of Krishna and Arjuna. Even if the reader does not fully understand the meaning, the mere recitation is beneficial. 2. Worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge (jñānayajñena tenāhamiṣṭaḥ): Studying the Gita is equivalent to performing Jnana-yajna, the highest form of sacrifice. The reader contemplates the words of the Lord, which is a direct connection with Him. 3. Such is My view (iti me matiḥ): The Lord confirms that this is His absolute conviction, assuring the devotee of His grace through the study of the Gita.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:

The meaning is that whateve sacrifice of knowledge is taught herein, by that I shall become worshipped by the mere fact of its study.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.68–18.72 jointly): Ya idam etc. upto Dhananjaya. The very act of explaining this among My devotees is itself an act of cultivating devotion towards Me.  Whosoever shall declare etc :  Whosoever directs the mind [of the devotees] by face to face method, following the best procedure prescribed in the scriptures; i.e., he who shall impart  [this knowledge] - he becomes one with Me.  This is an injunction  [concerning the result]  and not a sentence of praise.  Likewise is  [the case] in other places also.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Ca, and; even he yah, who; adhyesyate, will study; imam, this; samvadam, conversation, the text in the form of a dialogue; between avayoh, us two; which is dharmyam, conducive to virtue, not divorced from virtue; tena, by him; this will be accomplished through that study; aham, I; syam, shall be; istah, adored; jnana-yajnena, through the Sacrifice in the form of Knowledge. Iti, this is me, My; matih, judgement. As compared with the various sacrifices, viz rituals, loud prayer, prayer uttered in a low voice and mental prayer, the Sacrifice in the from of Knowledge is the best [See 4.33.] because it is mental. Hence, the study of the scripture Gita is praised as that Sacrifice in the form of Knowledge. Or, this (verse) may merely be a judgement about the result. The idea is that the result of the study is comparable to the result of the Sacrifice in the form of the knowledge of gods and others.
Now, this is the reward for the hearer:

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Having stated the fruit of the teacher, the Lord describes the fruit of the student: 'adhyesyate ca...'. He who recites this sacred dialogue as a repetition (japa) performs the sacrifice of knowledge (Jnana-yajna) which is superior to material sacrifices. Even if he does not fully understand the meaning, he obtains the fruit of Jnana-yajna. For those who study it with reflection on the meaning, it leads directly to liberation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The Lord states the fruit of the reader: 'adhyesyate...'. He who recites this sacred dialogue of Krishna and Arjuna as a japa, by him I am worshipped through the sacrifice of knowledge. Even if he does not know the meaning, My view is that by reciting it, he reveals Me. Just as someone hears his name called even in passing and draws near, so do I draw near to the reader.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
