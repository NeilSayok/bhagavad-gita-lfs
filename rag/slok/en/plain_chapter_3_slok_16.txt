Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
evaṃ pravartitaṃ cakraṃ nānuvartayatīha yaḥ .
aghāyurindriyārāmo moghaṃ pārtha sa jīvati

Word Meanings:
एवं - thus
प्रवर्तितं - set in motion
चक्रं - wheel
नानुवर्तयतीह - does not follow here
यः - who
अघायुरिन्द्रियारामो - sinful life of sense pleasures
मोघं - in vain
पार्थ - he
स - lives
जीवति - lives

Life Application:
Live responsibly as an active participant in life's communal cycle rather than wasting your time in self-indulgence.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who does not follow here the cosmic wheel thus set in motion (Chakram), living a sinful life (Aghayuh) and indulging only in sensory pleasures (Indriyaramah), O Partha, lives in vain (Mogham sa jivati).

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: A lustful person disregarding Lord Vishnu"s cosmic law and Yajna-cycle lives a completely wasted life.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: A worldly person indulging in sense pleasures who fails to follow the divine cosmic wheel lives a completely useless life.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: One who neglects the sacrificial wheel instituted by God and Veda, indulging only in sense gratification, lives in vain.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Evam etc. He who does not follow the cosmic wheel thus set in motion, indulging his senses, lives in vain.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain"—strongly admonishes those who shirk their cosmic duty for self-indulgence.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Disobeying divine law is the origin and mother of all sins.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Turning away from Krishna"s service-cycle to indulge bodily desire renders life totally meaningless.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: The life of a sensualist who disregards the cosmic order is blameworthy and meaningless.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He who does not follow here the wheel thus set in motion, living in sin and indulging his senses, O Partha, lives in vain.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis proving why failing to follow the cosmic wheel leads to a sinful and futile existence.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A man turning away from Vasudeva"s scriptural cycle leads a sinful and futile life.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: As long as a soul does not engage in Krishna"s service-cycle, his human birth is spent in vain.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Abandoning the cycle of duty for mere bodily pleasure renders one"s life completely fruitless.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
My dear Arjuna, one who does not follow in human life the cycle of sacrifice thus established by the Vedas certainly leads a life full of sin. Living only for the satisfaction of the senses, such a person lives in vain.
