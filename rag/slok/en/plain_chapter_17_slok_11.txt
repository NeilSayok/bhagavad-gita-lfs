Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
aphalāṅkṣibhiryajño vidhidṛṣṭo ya ijyate .
yaṣṭavyameveti manaḥ samādhāya sa sāttvikaḥ

Word Meanings:
अफलाकाङ्क्षिभिः - by men desiring no
यज्ञः - sacrifice
विधिदृष्टः - enjoined by scriptural ordinance
यः - which
इज्यते - is offered
यष्टव्यम् - ought to be offered
एव - only
इति - thus
मनः - the mind
समाधाय - having fixed
सः - that
सात्त्विकः - Sattvic or pure.

Life Application:
Sacrifice offered according to scriptural rules by those desiring no fruit, believing 'it ought to be done'—is Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That sacrifice which is offered in accordance with scriptural injunctions, by people desiring no fruits, with the resolve in mind that 'to sacrifice is a duty', is Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
That sacrifice which is offered by men without desire for reward as enjoined by the ordinance (scripture), with a firm faith that to do so is a duty, is Sattvic or pure.

अफलाकाङ्क्षिभिः by men desiring no fruit? यज्ञः sacrifice? विधिदृष्टः as enjoined by the ordinance? यः which? इज्यते is offered? यष्टव्यम् ought to be offered? एव only? इति thus? मनः the mind? समाधाय having fixed? सः that? सात्त्विकः Sattvic or pure.Commentary When a sacrifice is done with all due Sattvic rites? faith and devotion? without the least taint of desire for reward? with the mind fixed on the sacrifice only? for its own sake (for the,sake of discharging the duty only)? then it is said to be pure in its nature. Here the sacrifice is done in a disinterested spirit or with an attitude of desirelessness (Nishkamya Bhava) as an auxiliary to the attainment of the knowledge of the Self. Such selfless and desireless actions purify the mind and prepare the aspirant for the reception of divine light or knowledge of the Self. The Sattvic nature of a man forces him to do such selfless and desireless sacrifices. He does not care even for his own emancipation. He performs them with the firm belief that they ought to be done. He does them with the firm resolve that sacrifice is a duty.Yajna here is not limited to the ceremonial sacrifice. It is used in a broad sense. Any unselfish action done without attachment? without agency or egoism and without expectation of reward? as an offering unto the Lord? is a Yajna or sacrifice.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Sacrifice is Pure when it is offered by one who does not covet the fruit thereof, when it is done according to the commands of scripture, and with implicit faith that the sacrifice is a duty.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The sacrifice performed by Sattvic individuals is always selfless and free from attachment to fruits. Performing action for the sake of duty alone, in accordance with scriptural ordinances (Nitya and Naimittika Karma), without wasting energy on future anxieties, is what characterises a Sattvic Yajna. The performer is happy simply because they are serving the welfare of society.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
That sacrifice is of the Sattva (Strand), which is offered, as found in the injunction, by men craving for no fruit, by stabilizing their mind with the thought that it is just a thing to be offered.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The sacrifice (worship) marked by Sattva is what is offered by those desiring no fruit, and having the conviction that it should be performed as enjoined in the Sastras.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
That sacrifice which is in accordance with the injunctions, (and is) performed by persons who do not hanker after results, and with the mental conviction that it is surely obligatory, is done through sattva.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that the Lord divides sacrifice into three categories after explaining food. A Sattvic sacrifice is one performed without any desire for reward, with the conviction that the execution of the sacrifice itself is the duty to be accomplished, as prescribed by the scriptures.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
To perform sacrifice is a duty—with this resolve in mind, the sacrifice performed by those who do not desire fruit, in accordance with scriptural injunctions, is Sattvic.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'yastavyam eva' means performing sacrifice simply because it is a duty, without thinking about what benefit it will bring in this world or the next. When there is no desire for fruit, the performer is freed from binding karma and their ego becomes pure. Ramsukhdas emphasizes that actions performed merely out of duty free the soul from attachment.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
That sacrifice is marked by Sattva which is offered by those desiring no fruits, with the 'conviction', i.e., with the idea that it ought to be performed or that it should be offered for its own sake as worship of the Lord as enjoined in the injunctions of the Sastras, i.e., with hymns, right materials and proper rituals.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.11–17.13 jointly): Sri Abhinavagupta explains that a sacrifice performed contrary to scriptural injunctions, devoid of proper rituals, where no food is distributed, and which is done without stabilizing the mind in faith, is declared Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sah, that; yajnah, sacrifice; vidhi-drstah, which is in accordance with the injunctions, which is known through scriptural unjunctions; (and) yah, which; is ijyate, performed; a-phala-akanksibhih, by persons who do not hanker after results; manah samadhaya, with the mental conviction; iti, that; yastavyam, eva, it is surely obligatory, their duty is to accomplish the sacrifice just as it should be-with the firm idea, 'I have no human goal to achieve through this'-; is said to be a sacrifice which is sattvikah, done through sattva.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that the three verses describe the threefold nature of scriptural sacrifice. A Sattvic sacrifice is done by one who is free from any desire for reward.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that sacrifice is of two types: Kamya (for a specific result) and Nitya (obligatory). A Sattvic sacrifice is of the Nitya type, performed by those who avoid Kamya rituals and focus on purifying the mind, with the conviction that it must be done as a duty under scriptural injunctions.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that a Sattvic sacrifice is one performed by persons free of desire for fruits, knowing it to be an obligatory duty (Nitya-karma) ordained by the scriptures, with the mind firmly resolved on the act of sacrifice itself rather than any secondary reward.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that a Sattvic sacrifice is performed according to scriptures by those seeking purification of the mind rather than worldly or heavenly fruits. They focus entirely on the duty of sacrifice, which leads to spiritual welfare.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that a Sattvic sacrifice is performed without hankering for any fruits other than the pleasure of the Supreme Lord. It is performed in accordance with scriptural injunctions using proper materials, mantras, and rituals, treating the sacrifice itself as its own purpose.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that a Sattvic sacrifice is performed by those who do not desire any fruit other than the grace of the Supreme Lord, which is its own reward. The mind is focused on the sacrifice as a divine command.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that a Sattvic sacrifice is performed under scriptural rules by stabilizing the mind with the realization that the sacrifice is to be performed as a duty, without thinking about any visible or invisible fruits.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Of sacrifices, the sacrifice performed according to the directions of scripture, as a matter of duty, by those who desire no reward, is of the nature of goodness.

 The general tendency is to offer sacrifice with some purpose in mind, but here it is stated that sacrifice should be performed without any such desire. It should be done as a matter of duty. Take, for example, the performance of rituals in temples or in churches. Generally they are performed with the purpose of material benefit, but that is not in the mode of goodness. One should go to a temple or church as a matter of duty, offer respect to the Supreme Personality of Godhead and offer flowers and eatables without any purpose of obtaining material benefit. Everyone thinks that there is no use in going to the temple just to worship God. But worship for economic benefit is not recommended in the scriptural injunctions. One should go simply to offer respect to the Deity. That will place one in the mode of goodness. It is the duty of every civilized man to obey the injunctions of the scriptures and offer respect to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
