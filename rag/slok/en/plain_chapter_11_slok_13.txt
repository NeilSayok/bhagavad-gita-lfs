Speaker: Sanjaya

Slok:
tatraikasthaṃ jagatkṛtsnaṃ pravibhaktamanekadhā .
apaśyaddevadevasya śarīre pāṇḍavastadā

Life Application:
Perceive the entire universe divided into its many aspects, resting unified within the body of God.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
At that time, Arjuna, the son of Pandu, saw the entire universe, divided into many groups, resting in one place in the body of Sri Krishna, the God of gods.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Arjuna saw in that divine form of the Lord how the entire universe, with all its diversity, was brought and established in one place. We have already seen that the concept of the Cosmic Person (Virat Purusha) is a vision seen by a mind that is not functioning through the medium of space and time, meaning that the concept of space and time has vanished. The vision of seeing the many in the one is not so much objectified by the senses as it is graspable by the intellect. It is not that the entire universe shrank to the physical size of Sri Krishna's body. If Arjuna had the necessary understanding of the oneness of the universe, and if he could see the world from the perspective of that knowledge, that would be sufficient. A similar illustration can be drawn from modern science. In chemistry, substances are classified and studied. By studying the chemical elements of the world, we know that there are about one hundred and three elements in the universe. With more subtle study, scientists reached the atom, and now by dividing it, it is found that the atom is also made of electrons, protons, and neutrons. A scientist well-acquainted with this nature of the atom, when looking at the manifold world, easily understands that all these substances are made of atoms. Similarly, here when Arjuna, by the causeless grace of Sri Krishna, obtained this special knowledge, he became capable of seeing the entire universe within the body of the Lord itself. Observing the reactions on Arjuna's body and mind upon seeing this vision, Sanjaya watched attentively and, narrating their description, he says:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Not only did Arjuna see what was described, but also in that Cosmic Form, he experienced the entire universe resting in one place; this is what is stated by 'moreover' (kincha). 'Tada' means at the time of witnessing the Cosmic Form, the divine form of the Lord.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
At that time, Arjuna saw the entire universe, divided into various categories, resting in one place within the body of the Lord, the God of gods.

Commentary: 'tatraikasthaṃ jagatkṛtsnaṃ pravibhaktamanekadhā' — Divided into various categories, i.e., these are gods, these are humans, these are animals and birds, this is the earth, these are the oceans, this is the sky, these are the stars, etc. Arjuna saw the entire animate and inanimate universe, with all its divisions (not contracted but in expanded detail), directly in one part of the Lord's body with the divine eyes given by the Lord. The implication is that even in a single fraction of Lord Krishna's small body, the entire universe, including all moving and non-moving entities, exists. That universe is also divided and expanded in the form of multiple cosmoses, the worlds of various deities, and diverse individuals and objects — Arjuna saw this clearly (Footnote p. 582). 'apaśyaddevadevasya śarīre pāṇḍavastadā' — 'Tadā' (then) signifies that Arjuna saw it at the very moment when the Lord granted him divine vision and showed His Cosmic Form. 'Apaśyat' means that Arjuna saw the form exactly as the Lord displayed it. The form Sanjaya described earlier was the very form Arjuna saw. Just as the celestial world is far more extraordinary than the human world, the Lord is infinitely more extraordinary than the celestial world, because all worlds, including the celestial worlds, are material (prākṛta), whereas the Lord is beyond material nature (prakṛti-ātīta). Therefore, the Lord is 'Devadeva,' meaning the God of gods (their Master).

Relationship: Sanjaya describes what happened to Arjuna after seeing the Lord's extraordinary Cosmic Form in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'There', in that unie and divine body of the God of gods - infinite in length and breadth, with innumerable hands, stomachs, faces and eyes, of immeasurable splendour, eipped with innumerable divine weapons, adorned with innumerable divine ornaments appropriate to itself and with divine garlands and raiments, fragrant with celestial perfumes and full of wonders , there Arjuna beheld with the appropriate divine eyes granted by the grace of the Lord, the 'entire universe' consisting of Prakrti (material Nature) and the selves, all remaining in 'one single spot,' namely, at one single point. He beheld 'the whole universe' with all its sub-divisions, differentiated into varied and wonderful classes of experiencing beings like Brahma, gods, animals, men, immovables etc., and the places, objects and means of experiences such as earth, ether, Rasatala, Atala, Vitala, Sutala etc. He beheld thus the entire universe as depicted in such texts as those starting with 'I am the origin of all; from Me proceed everything' (10.8), 'Indeed I shall tell you, O Arjuna, My glorious self-manifestations' (10.9), 'I am the Self, O Arjuna, dwelling in the hearts of all beings' (10.20), and 'Of Adityas, I am Visnu' (10.21), and ending with 'Nothing that moves or does not move exists without Me' (10.39), and 'I remain, with a single fraction of Myself sustaining this whole universe' (10.42).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Thereafter, what happened? In response to this expectation, Sanjaya says — 'tatra' (there). Arjuna (Pandava) saw the entire universe, including the fourteen worlds, consisting of conscious and unconscious entities, divided in many ways by differences of womb, seed, disposition, senses, and form, in the body-form of the Supreme Person (Purushottama), who is of imperishable lordship, just like Sri Yashoda during the clay-eating incident, resting in one place, i.e., in one part of His limb. The vision is established by the Upanishad text (Shruti) — 'Some wise man saw the inner Self' (Katha Upanishad 4.1). Indeed, without seeing the majesty of the Lord, the firmness of taking refuge in the Lord through devotion does not occur; Hari showed this, and thereafter, by His grace alone, Partha (Arjuna) saw it — this represents the aspect of Pushti (grace/divine nourishment). In the case of Sri Yashoda, however, the knowledge of majesty arising from the displayed lordship was veiled by the Lord's Vaishnavi power to keep the love (prema-bhava) firm, since it was not useful for liberation there; this is pure Pushti majesty. Here, it is not so; therefore, the statement that the sons of Pritha (Pandavas) are eligible for Maryada-Pushti is indeed appropriate. More details are to be seen in the Bhashya.

Arjuna (Pandava) saw the entire universe resting in one place, divided in many ways into various categories such as gods, manes, humans, and others, in the body of the Lord, the God of gods, in that Cosmic Form, at the time of witnessing the wonders of the Cosmic Form.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Arjuna also saw that which was previously said by the Lord, 'Behold the entire universe established in one place' (11.7) — Sanjaya states this with 'tatra' (there). 'Anekadha pravibhaktam' (divided in many ways) is explained: to show that it was not like a tree being present in a subtle form within a tamarind seed sprouting in the rainy season, he saw it with distinct and spacious divisions. 'Ekastham' means located in a single limb of the body. The meaning is this: when the four-armed form of the Lord is contemplated upon and the mind attains stability in it, then gradually, leaving other limbs of His, the mind is fixed on the smiling face or the toenail. Once stability is achieved there, leaving even that, the mind rises to the Cosmic Form. The divine eye is indeed the mind itself which has attained such subtlety, as stated in the Upanishad (Shruti): 'The mind is his divine eye; he, with this divine eye, the mind, seeing these desires, rejoices.' Here, 'desires' mean objects, and 'these' mean those located in the qualified Brahman (Saguna Brahman) called the space of the heart — this is the meaning of the words of the Shruti. As is stated in the Srimad Bhagavatam: 'Having stabilized the mind there, one should draw it and hold it in one place. One should not think of anything else, but repeatedly contemplate on the smiling face. Having stabilized the mind there, one should draw it and hold it in the sky (the causal space). Leaving even that, ascending to Me, one should not think of anything at all.' In that context, 'there' means in one limb of the form; 'in the sky' means in the cause; and 'ascending to Me' means established in the attributeless Brahman (Nirvikalpa Brahman). This is what is meant by saying that Arjuna (Pandava) saw the entire universe resting in one place within the body of the God of gods.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
At that time Arjuna could see in the universal form of the Lord the unlimited expansions of the universe situated in one place although divided into many, many thousands.

 The word tatra (“there”) is very significant. It indicates that both Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa were sitting on the chariot when Arjuna saw the universal form. Others on the battlefield could not see this form, because Kṛṣṇa gave the vision only to Arjuna. Arjuna could see in the body of Kṛṣṇa many thousands of planets. As we learn from Vedic scriptures, there are many universes and many planets. Some of them are made of earth, some are made of gold, some are made of jewels, some are very great, some are not so great, etc. Sitting on his chariot, Arjuna could see all these. But no one could understand what was going on between Arjuna and Kṛṣṇa.
