Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yadā viniyataṃ cittamātmanyevāvatiṣṭhate .
niḥspṛhaḥ sarvakāmebhyo yukta ityucyate tadā

Word Meanings:
यदा - when
विनियतं - thoroughly controlled
चित्तमात्मन्येवावतिष्ठते - mind rests in Self alone
निःस्पृहः - free from longing
सर्वकामेभ्यो - from all desires
युक्त - united in yoga
इत्युच्यते - is said to be
तदा - then

Life Application:
Rest established in self-awareness when your disciplined mind becomes free from craving for all sensory desires.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
When the completely controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be integrated.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
When the completely restrained mind rests steadily in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
When the perfectly controlled mind rests steadily in the Self alone, free from longing for all objects of desire, then one is declared to be fully integrated in Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When the completely controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be integrated.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
When the completely controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be integrated.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
When the completely restrained mind rests steadily in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Rests in the Self alone"—when the seeker"s mind abandons all material desires to merge solely in Lord Vishnu"s feet, he attains Samprajnata Samadhi.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: When the mind, completely restrained from all distractions, rests immovably in the Supreme Self alone, free from all desires, the seeker is called Yukta.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When the completely controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be integrated.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: When the thoroughly disciplined mind abides steadily in the Self alone, free from attachment to non-Self objects, one is declared Yukta.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: When the thoroughly regulated mind rests in the Self alone, free from desire for all objects, then one is said to be integrated (Yukta).

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "When the completely controlled mind rests in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be integrated"—defines the climax of Samadhi as unwavering absorption in the inner Self accompanied by total desirelessness.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Freedom from all desires is the touchstone of mental stability; as long as a trace of craving lingers, Samadhi remains unaccomplished.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Becoming thoroughly absorbed in Krishna-bliss, entirely free from worldly longings, is the supreme Yogarudha condition.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Abides in the Self alone"—forgetting all phenomenal dualities and merging the mind into undivided Sat-Chit-Ananda is the summit of Samadhi.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
When the completely restrained mind rests steadily in the Self alone, free from longing for all desires, then one is said to be established in Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the self-governed mind abiding solely in the Self, devoid of fruitive desires.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Renouncing all non-Self cravings and abiding solely in Lord Vasudeva is the true mark of perfected Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When no desire other than Lord Krishna remains in the mind, the soul attains supreme Samadhi.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Free from longing for all desires"—only upon total eradication of worldly and heavenly cravings is unbroken Samadhi accomplished.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When the yogi, by practice of yoga, disciplines his mental activities and becomes situated in transcendence—devoid of all material desires—he is said to be well established in yoga.
