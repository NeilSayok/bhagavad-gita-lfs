Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
evametadyathāttha tvamātmānaṃ parameśvara .
draṣṭumicchāmi te rūpamaiśvaraṃ puruṣottama

Life Application:
Desire to experience divine truth directly, longing to behold the sovereign majesty of the supreme spirit.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Through the Sanskrit idiom 'evametat' (so it is, exactly as You say), Arjuna accepts the theoretical aspect of the philosophical truth. The proof of the immanence of God in all names and forms was intellectually satisfying. However, the intellect was still waiting for direct perception. Therefore, Arjuna says, 'I wish to see Your divine form.' In our scriptures, God is described as being endowed with these six attributes: knowledge (jnana), sovereignty (aishwarya), power (shakti), strength (bala), valour (virya), and splendour (tejas). On this occasion, the Lord decided to show Arjuna that He is not only immanent in all individual (vyashti) forms, but He is also the cosmic (samashti) vessel in which alone all names and forms exist. The Lord is both all-pervading and all-transcending. Although in the enthusiasm of rigid intellectualism Arjuna placed his demand for the vision of the cosmic form before the Lord, he immediately realized that this was audacious of him and that he had transgressed the limits of proper conduct. In the next verse, he speaks with greater humility:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
It is not out of a lack of faith in Your words that Arjuna desires to see Your form, but rather with the wish to feel blessed (fulfilled); hence he says, 'evametat' (so it is). 'In the manner (described)', i.e., in both the conditioned (sopadhika) and unconditioned (nirupadhika) aspects, is the meaning. (The Lord might ask:) 'If, having established My reliability, My word is authority for you, then why do you wish to see what I have described?' Anticipating this, and wishing to be fulfilled, he says, 'tathapi' (yet). To exclude the ordinary four-armed form etc., he says, 'aishvaram' (divine/lordly form). That is explained (by the Commentator) starting with 'jnana' (knowledge) etc.

Commentary:
'Purushottama'—The purpose of addressing the Lord with this name is: 'O Lord! In my view, there is no one superior or greater than You in this world; that is, You alone are the highest and the greatest.' The Lord Himself also says this later in the fifteenth chapter: 'I am beyond the perishable (Kshara) and superior to the imperishable (Akshara); therefore, I am renowned in the scriptures and the Vedas as Purushottama' (15.18).

'Evam-etad-yathāttha tvam ātmānam'—O Supreme Person! Whatever description You gave me (from the seventh chapter to the tenth chapter) of Your extraordinary influence and power, it is indeed exactly like that.

This universe arises from Me alone and merges into Me alone (7.6), there is no other cause of it besides Me (7.7), everything is Vasudeva (7.19); in the form of Brahman, Adhyatma, Karma, Adhibhuta, Adhidaiva, and Adhiyajna, it is I who exist (7.29-30); I alone am the Supreme Reality attainable through unswerving devotion (8.22); this entire universe is pervaded by Me, but I am not in the universe and the universe is not in Me (9.4-5); I alone am everything in the form of Sat and Asat (9.19); I alone am the source of the universe and the entire universe receives its existence and animation from Me (10.8); this whole universe is situated in but a fraction of My being (10.42), etc. Whatever You have said about Yourself is completely true.

'Parameshvara'—Arjuna has previously heard from the Lord's mouth: 'I am the Great Lord of all beings and all worlds—"bhūtānām īśvaro'pi" (4.6); "sarvaloka-maheśvaram" (5.29).' Therefore, Arjuna, being awed by the Lord's extraordinary influence, addresses Him as 'Parameshvara', which means: 'O Lord, in truth, You alone are the Supreme Lord, You alone are the master of all sovereignty.'

'Draṣṭum icchāmi te rūpam aiśvaram'—Arjuna says: 'I have heard Your glory along with Your influence, and I have also gained firm faith in this matter in my heart. Hearing that "the entire universe is in one part of My body," a intense longing has arisen in my mind to see that form of Yours.'
Another feeling is: 'Although You are so extraordinary and great, how much affection You have for me, how much intimacy You show, that You do exactly what I say and answer whatever I ask. For this reason, having not even the slightest hesitation in speaking or asking You, a strong desire has arisen in my mind to see that form of Yours, in one part of which the entire universe is pervaded.'

In the sixteenth verse of the tenth chapter, Arjuna had said: 'You should describe Your divine glories in full, leaving nothing out—"vaktum arhasy aśeṣeṇa."' So the Lord, while describing His glories, said in the beginning and in the conclusion that there is no end to His glories (10.19, 40). Therefore, the Lord described His glories only in brief. But here, when Arjuna says, 'I wish to see Your form (in the singular)—"draṣṭum icchāmi te rūpam,"' the Lord will say in the next verses: 'See My hundreds and thousands of forms' (11.5). Just as in the world, if someone greedily asks for more, the giver's desire to give decreases and he gives less. On the contrary, if someone hesitantly asks for less, the giver generously gives more. Similarly, there Arjuna had explicitly said, 'Please tell me all Your glories,' so the Lord said, 'I will tell My glories in brief.' Taking note of this, Arjuna becomes cautious so that no improper word comes out of his mouth. Therefore, Arjuna hesitantly says here, 'If Your cosmic form can be seen by me, then please show it.' Seeing this hesitation of Arjuna, the Lord says with great generosity, 'Behold My hundreds and thousands of forms.'
Another aspect is that while sitting in Arjuna's chariot, the Lord said: 'What you are seeing of this body of Mine, in one part of it the entire universe (which contains infinite millions of universes) is pervaded.' The implication is that the Lord has a small body, and in one part of that small body is the entire universe. Therefore, 'I wish to see the form situated in that one part'—this seems to be Arjuna's intention in saying 'rūpam' (a single form).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
'Evam-etat' (so it is). O Supreme Lord! In whatever manner You describe Yourself—whether conditioned (sopadhika) or unconditioned (nirupadhika) and possessed of unsurpassed sovereignty—it is exactly so and not otherwise. The meaning is that there is absolutely no doubt or disbelief in my mind regarding Your words. Even though this is so, still, with a desire to feel fulfilled, I wish to see Your wonderful, divine form endowed with knowledge, sovereignty, power, strength, valour, and splendour. By addressing Him as 'Purushottama' (Supreme Person), Arjuna implies: 'I have no disbelief in Your words, but I have a great longing to see Your form, which You know because You are omniscient and the inner indweller of all.'

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Approving that everything spoken by You is true, Arjuna expresses his desire: 'evam' (thus). The manner in which You speak, it is exactly so. There is no doubt (asambhāvanā) or contrary understanding (viparīta-bhāvanā) in my mind, because when You, the Supreme Lord, are the speaker, any doubt is impossible. To indicate this, he addresses Him as: 'O Parameshvara' (Supreme Lord). Even though this is so, still, with a desire to feel fulfilled, I wish to see Your form. Bearing in mind that the perception of the unconditioned (nirupadhika) form is impossible, he specifies the form as 'aishvaram'—that which is endowed with sovereignty, power, etc. By addressing Him as 'Purushottama', he implies: 'Since You are beyond the perishable and the imperishable, Your lordly form, which comprises both the perishable and the imperishable, is extremely wonderful, and therefore my eagerness to see it is very great.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
With the intention that the word 'Parameshvara' signifies the Lord being the supreme trustworthy authority, which is the cause of faith in the meaning of the statement, the commentator says: 'O Parameshvara'. To reveal this and to establish what is heard by rejecting any idea that it is merely for agreement, he says: 'evam etad iti avadhṛtam' (it is ascertained to be even so). It is said that only low men find faults in those who take refuge in them. Therefore, with the view that the word 'Purushottama' signifies the supreme heroism in the form of disregarding any faults in the one who wishes to see Him, the commentator says: 'O ocean of compassion for Your dependents'. Or, the etymological derivation 'puru sanoti' (he who gives abundantly) is intended, or that which is going to be declared in 'entering, He supports' (15.17) is indicated. Thus, the Lord's supremacy (Paratva) and accessibility (Saulabhya) are stated by these two names. Even though the word 'Te' (Thy) is mentioned, the expression 'aishvaram' (lordly) is used to bring out the nature of His Lordship, which is why it is called 'peculiar to You (tvad-asādhāraṇam)'. Its detail is explained in the manner previously described as 'of all...', etc. The meaning of the word 'Ishvara' is characterized by this nature. The existence of the form as the ruler, protector, etc., means its manifestation through qualities and activities corresponding to them. Or, its existence as the ruler is because, as stated in the Mahabharata, 'men helplessly took to active service in Krishna in a fitting manner,' it turns away false understanding and becomes the cause of right activity by its mere sight. Its existence as the mine of auspicious attributes means that like the incarnational body, it is not fit for showing ignorance, etc. Its existence as the protector is through the promotion of Sattva, etc. Its existence as the creator is from the production of Brahma, Rudra, and the four varnas from His limbs. Its existence as the destroyer is through the swallowing, etc., to be described later. Its existence as the supporter will become clear from verses like 'standing there in one' (11.13). Its existence as the supreme entity is appropriate, as the dwelling of Brahma, Rudra, and others (who are doubted to be supreme) in a single part of His body will be described in 'Brahma, the Lord...' (11.15). The phrase 'distinct from all other entities (sakaletara-visajātīyatve)' is the conclusion of all that has been said, or its result, or the explanation of the intention of the address 'Parameshvara' and 'Purushottama'. In accordance with the prayer to see the apparent meaning of what the Lord Himself said in the beginning of the most secret section, 'Behold My lordly yoga,' the phrase 'rupam aishvaram' is used. In interpreting this as referring to His essential nature, the word 'aishvaram' is figurative in one sense, and everything else is primary. But in connection with the essential nature, the desire to see the divine form is implied. Or, the word 'rupa' refers to all extraordinary forms, including His essential form. Meaning 'rupa' as 'type' or 'manner'. Since the word 'to see' (draṣṭum) is used even for knowledge that is similar to perception, to exclude that and to rule out mere sensory perception, it is said 'to realize directly (sākṣātkartum)'.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Anticipating the question, 'How is it to be known that the delusion has been destroyed?' the commentator says that those whose delusion is destroyed have an unwavering faith in the Lord's words: 'evam' (thus). O Parameshvara, Supreme Ruler of all! Just as You describe (speak about) Yourself—indicating Your being unknowable to all in 'they do not know Me' (10.2), Your being the Self of all in 'I support' (10.42), and Your being known only by Your own grace in 'I give the yoga of intellect' (10.10) etc.—this is exactly so; meaning, it is completely true. By declaring it to be true, Arjuna's own experience is expressed: 'Previously I did not know Your nature, but now through the description of Your glories and through the contemplation of Your grace-filled words "I support", I have obtained the knowledge of Your being the Self of all.' Now, having known Your nature, he requests Him to show that form: 'draṣṭum' (to see). O Purushottama (Supreme Person)! I wish to see Your divine form, which belongs to You alone and is filled with various divine plays (vilāsas).

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'Evam' (thus). What You have said about Yourself as the support of the universe: 'I support this entire universe with a single fraction of My being'—that also is exactly so; I have no doubt or disbelief about this. O Supreme Lord (Parameshvara)! I wish to see Your 'aishvaram' form—that is, the form of the Ruler, which is cosmic and illusory (mayic) in nature. O Purushottama (Supreme Person), who is beyond the perishable and the imperishable! The terms 'aishvaram' and 'Purushottama' imply that while the cosmic form is illusory (mayic), the real form is beyond illusion (maya). As the Lord will declare later: 'O Narada, this is a Maya created by Me that you see Me endowed with all the qualities of all elements; you must not think of Me as being like this.' And regarding the pure form, it is said: 'This is unmanifested, this is unthinkable' (2.25) etc.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O greatest of all personalities, O supreme form, though I see You here before me in Your actual position, as You have described Yourself, I wish to see how You have entered into this cosmic manifestation. I want to see that form of Yours.

 The Lord said that because He entered into the material universe by His personal representation, the cosmic manifestation has been made possible and is going on. Now as far as Arjuna is concerned, he is inspired by the statements of Kṛṣṇa, but in order to convince others in the future who may think that Kṛṣṇa is an ordinary person, Arjuna desires to see Him actually in His universal form, to see how He is acting from within the universe, although He is apart from it. Arjuna’s addressing the Lord as puruṣottama is also significant. Since the Lord is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, He is present within Arjuna himself; therefore He knows the desire of Arjuna, and He can understand that Arjuna has no special desire to see Him in His universal form, for Arjuna is completely satisfied to see Him in His personal form of Kṛṣṇa. But the Lord can understand also that Arjuna wants to see the universal form to convince others. Arjuna did not have any personal desire for confirmation. Kṛṣṇa also understands that Arjuna wants to see the universal form to set a criterion, for in the future there would be so many imposters who would pose themselves as incarnations of God. The people, therefore, should be careful; one who claims to be Kṛṣṇa should be prepared to show his universal form to confirm his claim to the people.
