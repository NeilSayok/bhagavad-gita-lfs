Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
dravyayajñāstapoyajñā yogayajñāstathāpare .
svādhyāyajñānayajñāśca yatayaḥ saṃśitavratāḥ

Word Meanings:
द्रव्ययज्ञास्तपोयज्ञा - sacrificers of wealth and austerity
योगयज्ञास्तथापरे - sacrificers of yoga and others
स्वाध्यायज्ञानयज्ञाश्च - sacrificers of study and knowledge
यतयः - striving ascetics
संशितव्रताः - of rigid vows

Life Application:
Choose a form of self-discipline that fits your life, whether through charity, quiet study, or personal restraint.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Some perform sacrifice with material possessions, some with austerity, some with Yoga; while striving seekers with rigid vows perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Some perform sacrifice with material possessions, some with austerity, some with Yoga; while striving seekers with rigid vows perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Some seekers offer material possessions (Dravya-Yajna), some offer austerities (Tapo-Yajna), some offer Ashtanga-Yoga practices (Yoga-Yajna); while self-controlled striving ascetics with rigorous vows perform the sacrifice of scriptural study (Svadhyaya-Yajna) and wisdom (Jnana-Yajna).

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Sacrifice of study and wisdom"—all disciplines from charity to Veda-study and Divine Knowledge become true Yajnas only when dedicated to Lord Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Charity of wealth, austerity, Ashtanga-yoga, and scriptural study—all these when performed selflessly turn into mind-purifying Yajnas.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Some perform sacrifice with material possessions, some with austerity, some with Yoga; while striving seekers with rigid vows perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Offering wealth in charity, austerities, Pranayama and Yoga practices, and Veda-study—all these are distinct disciplines leading to Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Offering all sensory and vital breath acts into self-restraint, sacrifice of wealth, penance, Yoga, and study of wisdom—all these are sacrifices of self-controlled ascetics.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Some perform sacrifice with material possessions, some with austerity, some with Yoga; while striving seekers with rigid vows perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge"—categorizes the fivefold practical disciplines (*Pancha-yajna*) leading to spiritual purification.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The ultimate goal of diverse spiritual practices is single-pointed mental concentration and God-realization.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Offering wealth, penance, Yoga, and study into Krishna"s loving service is the most fruitful sacrifice of wisdom.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Seeking seekers with rigid vows achieve mental purification according to their respective qualifications through these five sacrifices.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Some perform sacrifice with material possessions, some with austerity, some with Yoga; while striving seekers with rigid vows perform sacrifice of scriptural study and knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis detailing the five categories of sacrifices performed by self-controlled striving ascetics.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Inspired by Vasudeva-bhakti alone do sacrifices of wealth, austerity, Yoga, and study bear divine fruit.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When a seeker offers wealth, penance, or wisdom to Krishna according to his capacity, his life turns sublime.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Sacrifice of study and knowledge"—study of scriptures and Self-knowledge are paramount among all forms of sacrifice.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Having accepted strict vows, some are enlightened by sacrificing their possessions, and others by performing severe austerities, by practicing the yoga of eightfold mysticism, or by studying the Vedas to advance in transcendental knowledge.
