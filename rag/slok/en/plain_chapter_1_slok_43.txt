Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
doṣairetaiḥ kulaghnānāṃ varṇasaṅkarakārakaiḥ .
utsādyante jātidharmāḥ kuladharmāśca śāśvatāḥ

Word Meanings:
दोशैः - by faults
एतैः - these
कुलघ्नानाम् - of family destroyers
वर्णसङ्करकारकैः - causing lineage confusion
उत्साद्यन्ते - are destroyed
जातिधर्माः - community traditions
कुलधर्माः - family customs
च - and
शाश्वताः - eternal

Life Application:
Guard against actions that weaken social trust or break down essential communal bonds in your daily life.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The meaning of the previous verse's commentary becomes still clearer through this statement of Arjuna's. As we have seen, dharma means the Indian spiritual culture, training in which used to begin, from the very outset, within every household. Arjuna's fear that this civil war will destroy jatidharma and kuladharma is simply a matter of common sense. It is well known how suddenly moral and cultural values decline in society after every war. Our present age, gasping under the weight of dishonesty and deceitful tendencies, is a vivid example of this very fact. After a war, it is not only the number of the crippled and maimed that increases; something even more terrible follows, in the form of grave distortions of the mind.

In these verses we find Arjuna as the world's very first anti-war figure. In these passages he offers, to lovers of peace, a beautiful chain of arguments valid for all time.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
By these faults that give rise to admixture of castes, the eternal, age-old kuladharma and jatidharma of the family-destroyers perish.

Explanation: 'Doshair etaih kulaghnanam... kuladharmash cha shashvatah' -- when the family is destroyed in war, the kuladharmas that had continued alongside the family also perish. With the destruction of the kuladharmas, unrighteousness increases within the family. With the increase of unrighteousness, women become corrupted. With the corruption of women, admixture of castes is produced. Thus, through these faults that give rise to admixture of castes, the jatidharma (varnadharma) of the family-destroyers perishes.

What are kuladharma and jatidharma? Within a single caste (jati), each family (kula) has its own distinct traditions, distinct proprieties, distinct customs -- all of these are called the 'kuladharma' of that family. The dharmas that apply to the entire community of families within a single caste are all called jatidharma, that is, varnadharma -- these are the general dharmas fixed by scriptural rule. Through non-observance of these kuladharmas and jatidharmas, these dharmas perish.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said -- Sanjaya said: The high-souled Partha, supremely compassionate, seeing there, stationed in both armies, his fathers, grandfathers, teachers, maternal uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends -- his own kinsmen arrayed for battle -- was overcome, and spoke thus to Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Nihatya etc. upto anususruma. Sin alone is the agent in the act of slaying these desperadoes. Therefore here the idea is this : These ememies of ours have been slain, i.e., have been take possession of, by sin. Sin would come to us also after slaying them. Sin in this context is the disregard, on account of greed etc., to the injurious conseences like the ruination of the family and the like. That is why Arjuna makes a specific mention of the [ruin of the] family etc., and of its duties in the passage 'How by slaying my own kinsmen etc'.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Moreover, it is not merely that the family-destroyers themselves go to hell -- since through their connection dharma is destroyed everywhere on earth, he says this, in 'Doshair etaih...': by these faults of the family-destroyers, which are the causes of admixture of castes, the eternal caste-dharmas and family-dharmas are destroyed -- that is, lapse, is the sense. Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O Krishna, maintainer of the people, I have heard by disciplic succession that those whose family traditions are destroyed dwell always in hell.

 Arjuna bases his argument not on his own personal experience, but on what he has heard from the authorities. That is the way of receiving real knowledge. One cannot reach the real point of factual knowledge without being helped by the right person who is already established in that knowledge. There is a system in the varnashrama institution by which before death one has to undergo the process of atonement for his sinful activities. One who is always engaged in sinful activities must utilize the process of atonement, called prayashchitta. Without doing so, one surely will be transferred to hellish planets to undergo miserable lives as the result of sinful activities.
