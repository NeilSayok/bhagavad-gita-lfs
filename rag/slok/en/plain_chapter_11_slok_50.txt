Life Application:
Behold the Lord resuming His gentle, beautiful human-like form, bringing comfort and peace to your anxious heart.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
The expression 'His own form' is from the perspective of mistaken perception (that the Cosmic Form is not His own). Otherwise, even that (Cosmic Form) is indeed His own. The proofs for this have already been stated before.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The charioteer (Sanjaya) narrated this event to the king (Dhritarashtra), hence it is said, 'Sanjaya said'. 'Having spoken thus' refers to the statements starting with 'being pleased by Me' etc., (referring to) the four-armed form. As to why it was appropriate for the Lord to console Arjuna by showing His previously familiar form and by becoming serene in body, the reason is stated by the word 'Mahatman' (the great-souled/exalted one).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Sanjaya said — Having spoken thus to Arjuna, Lord Vasudeva again showed His own form (the divine form) in that manner, and the Mahatma Sri Krishna, again becoming of gentle form (two-armed form), reassured this terrified Arjuna.

Commentary — 'ityarjunaṃ vāsudevastathoktvā svakaṃ rūpaṃ darśayāmaśa bhūyaḥ' — When Arjuna prayed to the Lord to assume the four-armed form, the Lord said, 'Do not be distressed or frightened by seeing this terrible form of Mine. With a glad heart, behold this form of Mine again' (11.49). Sanjaya has referred to this very statement of the Lord here with the words 'ityarjunaṃ vāsudevastathoktvā'. The word 'tatha' (in that manner) implies that just as the Lord had shown His Cosmic Form out of sheer grace, similarly, out of sheer grace, He showed His four-armed form to Arjuna. It is not that Arjuna had any spiritual practice or qualification to see this four-armed form; rather, it was solely due to the Lord's absolute grace.

The word 'bhūyaḥ' (again) indicates that the Lord showed the same divine form (four-armed form) which Arjuna had seen within the Cosmic Form (11.15, 17) and for showing which Arjuna had prayed (11.45, 46).

'āśvāsayāmāsa ca bhītamenaṃ bhūtvā punaḥ saumyavapurmahātmā' — Lord Sri Krishna first showed the four-armed form to Arjuna. Then, for Arjuna's satisfaction, the Mahatma Lord Sri Krishna again appeared in His two-armed (human) form and consoled Arjuna, who was terrified by seeing the Cosmic Form. Was Lord Sri Krishna two-armed or four-armed? The answer is that the Lord always remained in His two-armed form, but whenever and wherever He deemed appropriate, He would assume the four-armed form. In the tenth and eleventh chapters, the Lord has shown His greatness, power, and capability in describing His glories, and has also shown His influence in displaying His extremely extraordinary Cosmic Form. If a person knows or believes in such great influence of the Lord, he would lose attraction for the world. He would be freed from the bondage of the world forever. What an extraordinary grace of the Lord upon Arjuna that the Lord first showed the Cosmic Form, then showed the divine form (four-armed form), and then became of human form (two-armed form). Along with this, the Lord has also shown such extraordinary and wonderful grace upon us that wherever and in whichever specialty our mind goes, we can contemplate upon the Lord there, and read, study, and contemplate upon the Cosmic Form of the Lord. In this terrible time, the opportunity we have got to contemplate upon the glories and Cosmic Form of the Lord is not due to our effort or qualification, but rather it is solely due to the grace of the Lord. Seeing this grace of the Lord, we should be pleased. The opportunity to hear these glories and contemplate/remember the Cosmic Form was available only to a very few people like Sanjaya even at that time. Today, we have received that same opportunity. Therefore, we should not let such an opportunity go to waste.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sanjaya said  Having spoken thus to Arjuna, the Lord, the son of Vasudeva, revealed His own four-armed form. And the Mahatman, i.e., one whose resolves are always treu, reassured him who was terror-stricken on seeing ann unfamiliar form, by resuming the familiar pleasant form. Possession of His own four-armed form alone is proper to this Lord of all, the Supreme Person, the Supreme Brahman, when he has assumed the human form for blessing this world as the son of Vasudeva. But in answer to the prayer of Vasudeva, who was terrified by Kamsa, the two extra arms were withdrawn till the destruction of Kamsa. These became manifest again. For He was prayed to thus:  'You are born, O Lord, O Lord of gods, withdraw this form bearing conch, discus and mace out of grace ৷৷. withdraw this form of four arms, O Self of all' (V. P., 5.3.10 and 13). Even to Sisupala, who hated Him, this form of four arms of Sri Krsna was the object of constant thought, as described in:  'Him who is of four long and robust arms, bearing the conch, discus and the mace' (V. P., 4.15.10). Hence Arjuna also exclaimed here; 'Assume again that four-armed shape' (11.46).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
The phrase 'revealed His own form' uses 'own' as an adjective for the form of Krishna. Lest there be an impression that the Cosmic Form is not His own, it is said 'His own'. The meaning is that the view that Krishna's form is His own, and the Cosmic Form is not, is stated in reiteration of a mistaken perception. Why is this so? To this it is said 'otherwise' (anyatha), which refers to the perception of proof. What are those proofs? To this it is said 'proofs' (pramanani), ending in the accusative case.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Thus Sanjaya spoke, starting with 'iti'. He showed His own form, which was the previously displayed four-armed form. Still, seeing Partha (Arjuna) who, deeming his friendly relation and charioteership to be inappropriate, wanted to get down from the chariot and praise Him, the Purushottama, the inconceivable Lord of Yoga, the all-pervading Great Soul, at the prayer of His devotee, again became of a gentle form — which is the two-armed form accepted in the world — and consoled the terrified Arjuna. Here, the word 'saumya' (gentle) itself indicates the two-armed form, which is the ultimate indicator of the absence of fear. Otherwise, there would be redundancy between the words 'bhuyah' (again) and 'punah' (again). And he says later: 'Having seen this human form...' (11.51).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Starting with 'iti'. Vasudeva, having spoken to Arjuna as stated before, showed again His own form as it was before, which is the four-armed form equipped with crown, alligator-shaped earrings, mace, and discus, and adorned with Shrivatsa, Kaustubha, forest garland, and yellow garments. And He consoled this terrified Arjuna by again assuming a gentle form (a benign body) as before; He who is the Mahatma — the supremely compassionate, the Lord of all, the omniscient, and the source of all auspicious attributes.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sanjaya said, starting with 'iti', indicating that having spoken thus, He showed His former form. Sri Vasudeva, having spoken thus to Arjuna, showed again His own form as it was before, which was the four-armed form equipped with crown etc. He consoled this terrified Arjuna by again becoming of a serene body (pleasant form). The word 'Mahatman' means He who has the Cosmic Form, or who is compassionate.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sanjaya narrated this event to Dhritarashtra, hence it is said, 'Sanjaya said'. 'Having spoken thus' means Vasudeva spoke such words to Arjuna. By the word 'Vasudeva', it is indicated that the expression 'His own' form means the form born in the house of Vasudeva. 'Showed' means He revealed, and He comforted him repeatedly, by becoming of a serene body (pleasant form) again. Now, to remove the doubt as to why the Lord did not cause further agitation to Arjuna, whose mind was unstable, he says 'Mahatman' (the Great Soul). The meaning is that it was indeed appropriate for the Lord, who is the source of all virtues like compassion etc., to console Arjuna by displaying His previously familiar gentle form.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
To express natural affection, it is said—"Thus to the son of Pandu" (Arjuna) and "the son of Vasudeva" (Krishna). In the word "svakam" (one's own), the word "sva" refers to the Krishna incarnation—keeping this in mind, it is explained as "one's own four-armed form". In the phrase "bhitamenam" (this frightened person), to dispel the illusion that Arjuna was frightened by seeing the newly shown four-armed form, he says: "by the sight of the unfamiliar (universal) form". To explain how the Lord can assume various forms by His mere will, the word "mahatma" in this context is explained as "satya-sankalpa" (one of unimpeded resolve). The meaning is that everything is possible due to His unimpeded resolve. Since Krishna resided in a two-armed form in Nanda's Vraja (Vrindavan), how is the four-armed form His own? He explains this with proof through the words "asya sarvesvarasya" (of this Lord of all) etc. The adjectives like "of this Lord of all" are used to express His supreme nature and ease of access (soulabhya), which are suitable for taking shelter. Therefore, "one's own form" means the innate (natural) form of the Krishna incarnation.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sanjaya said "iti" etc. to state that having spoken thus, the Lord showed His form. In this way, Vasudeva, the giver of liberation and the supremely merciful, having spoken to Arjuna as before, again showed His Purushottama form. Having shown this and assuming a gentle form, He again reassured Arjuna, who was terrified by the vision of the previous form (universal form). Now, why did He do this repeatedly? To explain this, he says "mahatma". The idea is that He is the great soul, so He did this out of mercy. Or, He is the soul of the great devotees, so He did this due to His affection for His devotees.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sanjaya said, starting with 'iti'. Vasudeva, having spoken to Arjuna in the manner stated before, showed His own human form again as it was before. The meaning is that even the four-armed form, which was prayed for by Arjuna for the sake of contemplation, was withdrawn (to reveal the two-armed human form). And the Mahatma, though all-pervading, assuming a gentle form (a benign body), consoled this terrified Arjuna.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Sañjaya said to Dhṛtarāṣṭra: The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, having spoken thus to Arjuna, displayed His real four-armed form and at last showed His two-armed form, thus encouraging the fearful Arjuna.

 When Kṛṣṇa appeared as the son of Vasudeva and Devakī, He first of all appeared as four-armed Nārāyaṇa, but when He was requested by His parents, He transformed Himself into an ordinary child in appearance. Similarly, Kṛṣṇa knew that Arjuna was not interested in seeing a four-handed form, but since Arjuna asked to see this four-handed form, Kṛṣṇa also showed him this form again and then showed Himself in His two-handed form. The word saumya-vapuḥ is very significant. Saumya-vapuḥ is a very beautiful form; it is known as the most beautiful form. When He was present, everyone was attracted simply by Kṛṣṇa’s form, and because Kṛṣṇa is the director of the universe, He just banished the fear of Arjuna, His devotee, and showed him again His beautiful form of Kṛṣṇa. In the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.38) it is stated, premāñjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena: only a person whose eyes are smeared with the ointment of love can see the beautiful form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa.
