Life Application:
When the soul obtains a body or leaves it, it carries senses and mind along, as wind carries scents from flowers.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
When the lord (of the body, the Jiva) leaves a body and when he obtains another, he takes these (senses and mind) and goes, like the wind taking fragrance from its source (flowers).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
When the Lord (as the individual soul) obtains a body and when He leaves it, He takes these and goes (with them) as the wind takes the scents from their seats (flowers, etc.).

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
When the Supreme Lord enters a body or leaves it, He gathers these senses together and travels on with them, as the wind gathers perfume while passing through the flowers.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When the Lord (the Jiva, the master of the body-mind-intellect equipment) acquires a body and when he leaves it, he takes these (senses and the mind) and goes, as the wind takes scents from their seats (the flowers).

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that the Jiva is called the 'Lord' (Ishvara) of the body-mind equipment because its presence rules and illumines all activities. When the Jiva leaves a physical body (which we call death), it gathers the subtle body consisting of the five senses and the mind, and moves to another body determined by its unexhausted desires (Vasanas), just as the wind carries fragrance from flowers without carrying the flower itself. In the new body, the Jiva restarts its interaction with the world. Thus, death is only the destruction of the gross physical body, while the subtle personality continues its journey.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Whatsoever body he attains to and also from whatsoever He goes up, the Lord proceeds taking them with Him just as the wind takes odours from their receptacle.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Whatever body Its lord acires and from whatever body It departs, It goes on Its way, taking these senses as the wind carrying scents from their places.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
When the master leaves it and even when he assumes a body, he departs taking these, as wind (carries away) odours from their receptacles.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that the Jiva is not independent in drawing and carrying the senses. The Supreme Lord (Ishvara), who is the ultimate regulator and controller, gathers these senses along with the subtle elements and carries them from body to body, according to the Jiva's karma. The Jiva resides wherever the Lord unites it with a new womb, as stated in the scriptures. Just as the wind carries subtle scent particles, the Lord carries the Jiva and its subtle instruments.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that the term 'Ishvara' (lord) in this sloka refers to the Jiva, which is the master of the body, mind, and senses. When this Jiva departs from the previous body or enters a new one, it carries the senses and mind with it. The analogy of the wind carrying scents shows that the senses are carried in a subtle form (Suksma) without being physically visible, leaving the gross physical body behind.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When the Lord (as the individual soul) obtains a body and when He leaves it, He takes these and goes (with them) as the wind takes the scents from their seats (flowers, etc.).

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that the Jiva is an eternal fragment of the Supreme Lord, but due to ignorance it identifies with the body, mind, and senses, assuming them to be its own. When the Jiva leaves a physical body, it gathers the senses and mind (subtle and causal body) and departs, leaving the gross physical body behind. The analogy of the wind carrying scents from flowers highlights that the soul itself is untouched and distinct from the instruments it carries, yet it experiences transmigration due to its self-created attachment to these material limiting adjuncts.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Whatever body It acires, and from whatever body It departs, the lord of the senses, i.e., the self, goes on Its way taking with It the senses with the subtle elements, just like the wind carrying scents from place to place. Just as the wind takes away scents with subtle parts from flower-garlands, sandal, musk and the rest from their places and moves elsewhere - so does the self.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that the Jiva does not have complete independence in leaving or acquiring a body. The Supreme Lord is the ultimate inner controller who directs the Jiva to enter a new womb and function there, according to the Jiva's karma. The word 'Ishvara' here highlights that the Supreme Lord is the regulator of this entire transmigratory process.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that when the Jiva, who is the master of the senses, leaves the physical body or acquires a new one, it takes the senses along with the subtle elements (Bhuta-sukshma) and departs, just like the wind carries subtle scent particles from their sources like musk and flowers.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that at the time of leaving the body, the Jiva, who is the ruler of the body-mind complex, draws the senses and mind from the gross body. When it obtains a new body, it carries these subtle instruments with it, just as the wind carries scents from flowers. This migration is completely dependent on past impressions and karma, and continues until final liberation is achieved.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the Jiva, which is the master of the body, draws the mind and senses from the old body due to its past karma and enters the new body carrying them. The example of the wind carrying fragrance shows that the Jiva takes the subtle essence of the senses without bringing any gross parts of the old body.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that when the Jiva, the master of the body-mind aggregate, departs from the gross body, it draws the mind and senses. It then carries them to the new body it acquires. The wind carrying scents from flowers indicates that the subtle organs are transported invisibly, leaving the gross physical structure behind to disintegrate.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the individual soul, who is the lord of the senses, departs from the old body taking the senses and the subtle elements (Bhuta-sukshma) with it, and enters the new body. This process is determined by karma, and the soul remains untouched by the gross elements while in transit.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the Jiva, as the lord of the senses, draws the senses and mind for the sake of experiencing fruits of actions. When it departs from one body and enters another, it carries these instruments in their subtle, seed-like form (Samskaras), just as the wind carries subtle fragrance particles from flowers.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the Jiva is called 'Ishvara' because it regulates the body-mind system. When it leaves the body at death, or wakes up from deep sleep (Sushupti) to waking state, it draws the mind and senses from their dissolution state and goes towards the field of experience, just as the wind carries scents from flowers to other places.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The living entity in the material world carries his different conceptions of life from one body to another, as the air carries aromas. Thus he takes one kind of body and again quits it to take another.

 Here the living entity is described as īśvara, the controller of his own body. If he likes, he can change his body to a higher grade, and if he likes he can move to a lower class. Minute independence is there. The change his body undergoes depends upon him. At the time of death, the consciousness he has created will carry him on to the next type of body. If he has made his consciousness like that of a cat or dog, he is sure to change to a cat’s or dog’s body. And if he has fixed his consciousness on godly qualities, he will change into the form of a demigod. And if he is in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he will be transferred to Kṛṣṇaloka in the spiritual world and will associate with Kṛṣṇa. It is a false claim that after the annihilation of this body everything is finished. The individual soul is transmigrating from one body to another, and his present body and present activities are the background of his next body. One gets a different body according to karma, and he has to quit this body in due course. It is stated here that the subtle body, which carries the conception of the next body, develops another body in the next life. This process of transmigrating from one body to another and struggling while in the body is called karṣati, or struggle for existence.
