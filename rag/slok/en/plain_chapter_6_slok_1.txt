Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śrībhagavānuvāca .
anāśritaḥ karmaphalaṃ kāryaṃ karma karoti yaḥ .
sa saṃnyāsī ca yogī ca na niragnirna cākriyaḥ

Word Meanings:
श्रीभगवानुवाच - the Supreme Lord said
अनाश्रितः - without depending on
कर्मफलं - fruit of action
कार्यं - exists
कर्म - action
करोति - acts
यः - who
स - he
संन्यासी - a renunciant
च - and
योगी - yogi
च - and
न - not
निरग्निर्न - not without sacred fire
चाक्रियः - nor without action

Life Application:
Perform your required work without depending on the rewards, living as a true practitioner of inner renunciation.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The Blessed Lord said: He who performs his bounden duty without depending on the fruits of actions is a sannyasi and a yogi, not he who is without fire and without action.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Blessed Lord said: He who performs his duty without relying on the fruit of action is a renunciate and a yogi, not he who merely discards the sacrificial fire or actions.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The Blessed Lord said: He who performs his prescribed duty without relying on the fruits of action is a true renunciate (Sannyasi) and a true practitioner of Yoga (Yogi); not he who merely abandons the sacred fires or external activities.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Relinquishing fruits of action"—one who performs duties solely for the pleasure of Lord Vishnu without attachment to rewards is a true Sannyasi and Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: At the beginning of Chapter 6, Karma-Yoga is praised and identified with true Sannyasa and Yoga for spiritual purification.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: One who performs duty without attachment to fruits is a Karma-Yogi; being conducive to knowledge-discipline, he is rightly designated as Sannyasi and Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He who performs his bounden duty without desiring the fruits of action is a true Sannyasi and Yogi, not he who merely relinquishes sacred fire or actions. Know that which is called Sannyasa to be Yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a Yogi without renouncing mental resolve.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He who performs his prescribed duty without relying on the fruit of action is a sannyasi and a yogi, not he who is without fire and without action"—praises Karma-Yoga as an indirect stepping-stone to true Knowledge-Sannyasa by purifying the mind for Dhyana-Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Action performed with surrender to God and renunciation of fruits qualifies the soul for Dhyana-Yoga (meditation).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Performing selfless action in service of Lord Krishna according to His divine command is true Sannyasa of total detachment.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Unattached to action-fruits"—Karma-Yoga devoid of selfish desires directly leads to mind-purification and divine Knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: He who performs his duty without relying on the fruit of action is a renunciate and a yogi, not he who merely discards the sacrificial fire or actions.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of how a selfless Karma-Yogi embodies true renunciation and yoga.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Selfless Karma-Yoga is the primary gateway to the perfection of Jnana-Yoga; one attached to fruits is no Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Duty performed selflessly for Lord Krishna"s joy alone renders the mind fit to experience divine bliss.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Unattached to fruits"—renunciation of desire for results is true Sannyasa, not the mere physical abandonment of work.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: One who is unattached to the fruits of his work and who works as he is obligated is in the renounced order of life, and he is the true mystic, not he who lights no fire and performs no duty.
