Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yadakṣaraṃ vedavido vadanti viśanti yadyatayo vītarāgāḥ .
yadicchanto brahmacaryaṃ caranti tatte padaṃ saṃgraheṇa pravakṣye

Word Meanings:
यदक्षरं - which imperishable goal
वेदविदो - knowers of the Vedas
वदन्ति - declare
विशन्ति - enter
यद्यतयो - which the renunciates
वीतरागाः - freed from passion
यदिच्छन्तो - desiring which
ब्रह्मचर्यं - vow of celibacy
चरन्ति - they practice
तत्ते - that unto you
पदं - the supreme goal
संग्रहेण - in brief summary
प्रवक्ष्ये - I will explain

Life Application:
Seek that imperishable goal which knowers of truth enter, for which ascetics practice self-control and celibacy.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That which Veda-knowers declare as Imperishable, which ascetics freed from passion enter, desiring which they practice celibacy, that goal I shall declare to you in brief.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "That which is Imperishable"—the meditation on the sacred syllable Om is the means to attain the Supreme Imperishable Lord Sri Hari described by Vedic sages.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: "That which Veda-knowers call Imperishable"—the Supreme Goal described in Kathopanishad and denoted by Om is now concisely expounded with its practice by the Lord.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
That which Veda-knowers declare as Imperishable, which ascetics freed from passion enter, desiring which they practice celibacy, that goal I shall declare to you in brief.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Expounding the practice of meditating on the sacred monosyllable Om to attain the Supreme Imperishable Brahman as propounded in the Kathopanishad.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Restraining all sense-gates, locking the mind within the heart, and established in concentration uttering Om while remembering the Lord at departure, that ever-steadfast yogi easily attains the Supreme Goal.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "That which Veda-knowers declare as Imperishable, into which enter dispassionate ascetics, desiring which they practice celibacy—that goal I shall concisely expound to you"—Lord Vasudeva promises to explain the Supreme Brahman described in scriptures like Kathopanishad and the method of attaining It.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The sacred syllable Om is the supreme symbol of the Ultimate Reality declared by all Veda-knowers; dispassionate ascetics attain Mukti through its practice.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The Supreme Abode acknowledged by Veda-knowers is the very form of Lord Krishna, to attain which seekers perform austerities.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "That which Veda-knowers declare"—concisely expounding the sacred practice of Om meditation to attain the Supreme Brahman revealed in Kathopanishad.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: That which Veda-knowers declare as Imperishable, which ascetics freed from passion enter, desiring which they practice celibacy, that goal I shall declare to you in brief.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural explanation of Akshara Brahman as propounded in the Upanishads and the means to attain It.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Concise instruction presenting meditation on the sacred Om for seekers of liberation to attain Akshara Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: The scriptural Akshara Brahman is the supreme abode of Lord Krishna; the brief method to attain It is revealed here.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "That which Veda-knowers declare"—the Supreme Brahman state delineated by Vedic rishis is the ultimate goal of seekers.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Persons who are learned in the Vedas, who utter omkara and who are great sages in the renounced order enter into Brahman. Desiring such perfection, one practices celibacy. I shall now briefly explain to you this process by which one may attain salvation.
