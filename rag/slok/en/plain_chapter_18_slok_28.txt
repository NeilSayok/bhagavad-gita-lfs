Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ayuktaḥ prākṛtaḥ stabdhaḥ śaṭho naiṣkṛtiko.alasaḥ .
viṣādī dīrghasūtrī ca kartā tāmasa ucyate

Life Application:
Overcome tendencies toward stubbornness, laziness, deceit, and procrastination by developing steady personal discipline today.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The agent who is unsteady, vulgar, stubborn, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent, and procrastinating, is called Tamasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Unsteady, vulgar, unbending, cheating, malicious, lazy, desponding and procrastinating  such an agent is called Tamasic.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
While he whose purpose is infirm, who is low-minded, stubborn, dishonest, malicious, indolent, despondent, procrastinating - he may be assumed to be in Darkness.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
This verse provides a detailed description of the Tamasic doer. 'Ayukta' is one whose mind is not aligned with intellect, acting willfully against guidance. 'Prakrita' is uncultured and uncivilized, lacking self-control. 'Stabdha' is stubborn like a stick, never bowing to anyone and refusing noble advice. 'Shatha' is deceitful, hiding true intentions to cheat others. 'Naishkritika' refers to one who destroys others' livelihood, always ready to pick fights and bear malice. 'Alasa' is lazy, avoiding even duties, desiring results without effort. 'Visadi' is always morose and depressed, unable to face life's challenges, seeking escape. 'Dirghasutri' is a procrastinator who delays today's work for a month, unable to make quick decisions or execute them. These profiles are meant for self-analysis and self-improvement, not for criticizing others.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The agent, who does not exert, is vulgar, obstinate and deceitful; who is a man of wickedness and is lazy, sorrowful, and procrastinating - that agent is said to be of the Tamas (Strand).

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That doer is said to be Tamasika who is unalified, unrefined, stubborn, depraved, dishonest, indolent, despondent and dilatory.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
The agent who is unsteady, naive, unbending, deceitful, wicked, [A variant reading is naikrtikah.-Tr.] lazy, morose and procrastinating is said to be possessed of tamas.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
A procrastinator (dirghasutri) is one who points out others' faults, even if committed long ago, out of malice.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The word 'dirghasutri' indicates one who deliberates endlessly and works very slowly, postponing today's and tomorrow's duties for months.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The doer who is careless, uneducated (unrefined), stubborn, wicked (hostile), malicious (injuring benefactors), lazy, despondent, and procrastinating, is declared to be Tamasic.

Commentary: 'Ayuktah' means careless, acting without considering right and wrong. 'Prakritah' means uncultured by scriptures or good company. 'Stabdha' means rigid and unbending, refusing to show respect to parents or teachers. 'Shathah' means obstinate, rejecting good advice. 'Anaishkritikah' means ungrateful, returning evil for good. 'Alasah' means lazy, preferring sleep or useless daydreaming over duties. 'Visadi' is the constant inner grief and restlessness from deviating from righteousness. 'Dirghasutri' means spending excessive time on trivial tasks.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'Ayukta' is the doer who is unalified for acts enjoined by the Sastras; the meaning is that he is engaged in perverse acts; who is 'unrefined', means one uninstructed; who is 'stubborn', means one who is not disposed to act; who is 'depraved' means one who has the taste for black magic etc; who is dishonest is one who is treacherous; who is 'indolent' is one who is not inclined to carry out actions undertaken; who is 'despondent' is one given to excessive despondency; and one who is 'dilatory', is a person who, while engaged in black magic, etc., pays malevolent attention to produce long-standing evil to others - such a doer is declared to be Tamasika.

Thus, has been told the threefold division in terms of the Gunas of the knowledge about the work that ought to be performed, and about the agent of work. Now, Sri Krsna describe s the threefold division of Buddhi and Dhrti (fortitude) on the basis of Gunas. These faculties give the determinate knowledge of all realities in existence and of all ends of human life (Purusarthas).

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.26–18.29 jointly): Explains the threefold nature of the agent. An agent free from attachment and egoism, endowed with fortitude and enthusiasm, is Sattvika. An agent swayed by passion, craving fruit, greedy and impure, is Rajasa. An agent discordant, vulgar, obstinate, deceitful, and indolent is Tamasa.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
The agent who is ayuktah, unsteady; prakrtah, naive, of very unrefined intelligence, like a child; stabdhah, unbending like a staff-he does not bend down to anyone; sathah, deceitful, cunning, hiding his own powers; naiskrtikah, wicked, given to destroying the livelihood of others; alasah, lazy, not inclined even to his own duties; visadi, morose, ever in a mood of dejection; and dirghasutri, procrastinating, postponing duties for long, [Ast. adds here, 'sarvada mandasvabhavah, always slow by nature'.-Tr.] not accomplishing even in a month what is to be done today or tomorrow;-one who is such, he ucyate, is said to be; tamasah, possessed of tamas.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
The agent who acts contrary to scripture, is uneducated, sluggish in beginning, dishonest, lazy, sorrowful, and procrastinating, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The agent whose mind is always distracted by sense objects, unrefined in intellect, stubborn before elders, deceitful to cheat others, malicious in destroying others' livelihood, lazy, unsatisfied and sorrowful, and excessively slow in starting duties, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The agent who is careless, devoid of discrimination, rigid, hiding one's capacity (dishonest), insulting to others, lazy, mournful, and procrastinating, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
The agent whose mind is distracted, infantile in intellect, proud and stiff like a staff, hiding capability to deceive, malicious to others, lazy, morose, and procrastinating due to endless doubts, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The agent who is engaged in prohibited acts, uninstructed in scriptures, stiff in behaviour toward worshipful ones, interested in black magic, treacherous, lazy, excessive despondent, and malevolent, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
The agent who lacks foresightedness, is unrefined in behavior, rigid, cunning, disrespectful, inactive, prone to sorrow over failures, and takes months to do a moment's work, is Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The agent who has abandoned attachment, has no egoistic boasting, is endowed with patience and the firm resolve 'I will accomplish this', and is unaffected by success or failure, is Tamasic.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The worker who is always engaged in work against the injunctions of the scripture, who is materialistic, obstinate, cheating and expert in insulting others, and who is lazy, always morose and procrastinating is said to be a worker in the mode of ignorance.

 In the scriptural injunctions we find what sort of work should be performed and what sort of work should not be performed. Those who do not care for those injunctions engage in work not to be done, and such persons are generally materialistic. They work according to the modes of nature, not according to the injunctions of the scripture. Such workers are not very gentle, and generally they are always cunning and expert in insulting others. They are very lazy; even though they have some duty, they do not do it properly, and they put it aside to be done later on. Therefore they appear to be morose. They procrastinate; anything which can be done in an hour they drag on for years. Such workers are situated in the mode of ignorance.
