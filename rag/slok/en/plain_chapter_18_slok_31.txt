Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yayā dharmamadharmaṃ ca kāryaṃ cākāryameva ca .
ayathāvatprajānāti buddhiḥ sā pārtha rājasī

Life Application:
Refine your intellect so you can distinguish correctly between righteous duty and unrighteous action today.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
O Partha, that intellect by which one wrongly understands Dharma and Adharma, and what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
That, by which one wrongly understands Dharma and Adharma and also what ought to be done and what ought not to be done  that intellect, O Arjuna, is Rajasic (passionate).

यया by which? धर्मम् Dharma? अधर्मम् Adharma? च and? कार्यम् what ought to be done? च and? अकार्यम् what ought not to be done? एव even? च and? अयथावत् wrongly? प्रजानाति understands? बुद्धिः intellect? सा that? पार्थ O Partha? राजसी Rajasic (passionate).Commentary There is no proper eivalent in the English language for the word Dharma. Duty? righteousness? virtue? law are very poor translations of the word. That which elevates you and takes you to the goal? i.e.? Brahman or the Self? is Dharma. That which hurls you down into the dark abyss of ignorance is Adharma. What is ordained in the scriptures is Dharma what is prohibited by them is Adharma. A Rajasic intellect is not able to distinguish between right and wrong or to understand the difference between righteous and unrighteous actions.Ayathavat Wrongly Contrary to what is determined by all authorities or men of wisdom or to the highest knowledge.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The intellect which does not understand what is right and what is wrong, and what should be done and what not, is under the sway of Passion.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
While the Sattvic intellect knows things as they truly are, the Rajasic intellect's understanding is doubtful, vague, or distorted. This distortion is caused by personal prejudices, deep attachment (raga), and aversion (dvesha).

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The intellect, by means of which one decides incorrectly  the righteous and the unrighteous ones and what is a proper action and also an improper onethat intellect is of the Rajas (Strand),  O son of  Prtha !

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The Buddhi which gives an erroneous knowledge of Dharma and Adharma (its opposite) and also of what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, O Arjuna, is Rajasika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
O Partha, that intellect is born of rajas with which one wrongly understands virtue and vice as also what ought to be done and ought not to be done.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
The Rajasic intellect lacks the rule of understanding things as they truly are (doubtful or uncertain).

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The terms Dharma and Adharma relate to scriptural unseen results, whereas Karya and Akarya refer to the actual visible doing and not doing of actions under temporal circumstances. The Rajasic intellect is unable to resolve them properly.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Partha, that intellect by which one wrongly understands Dharma and Adharma, and also what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, is Rajasic.

Commentary: Dharma is that which leads to spiritual elevation and Adharma is that which leads to bondage. Karya (duty) refers to performing actions according to time, place, and class, whereas Akarya is acting against them (e.g., dishonesty in work). A Rajasic intellect, defiled by attachment (raga) and aversion (dvesha), becomes prejudiced and selfish. It cannot make clean, unbiased decisions about righteousness and duties, just as muddy water cannot reflect clearly.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
That Buddhi by which one does not know exactly the twofold Dharma previously mentioned and its opposite, and what ought to be done and what ought not to be done by those intent on them in accordance with place, time and conditions - that Buddhi is Rajasika.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.30–18.32 jointly): Explains the threefold intellect. The intellect that knows action and renunciation, duty and non-duty, fear and fearlessness, bondage and release, is Sattvika. The intellect that misjudges dharma and adharma is Rajasa. The intellect enveloped in darkness that regards adharma as dharma is Tamasa.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
O Partha, sa, that; buddhih, intellect; is rajasi, born of rajas; yaya, with which; prajanati, one understands; ayathavat, wrongly, not truly, not by discerning it from all points of view; dharmam, virtue, as prescribed by the scritpures; and adharmam, vice, what is prohibited by them; [By dharma and adharma are implied the seen and the unseen results of actions as revealed by the scriptures; karya and akarya respectively refer to the actual doing of what ought to be done and the not doing of what ought not to be done.] ca eva, as also; karyam, what ought to be done; and akaryam, what ought not to be done-those very 'duty' and 'what is not duty' as stated earlier.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
The phrase 'ayathavat' means the Rajasic intellect lacks the rule of true determination (remains doubtful), differing from the Tamasic intellect which is completely inverted (understands the opposite of truth).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
The intellect by which one understands the twofold Dharma and its opposite, but not as they truly are, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The intellect by which one incorrectly understands Dharma (scriptural duties), Adharma (scriptural prohibitions), Karya (temporal duties), and Akarya (temporal prohibitions), falling into doubt or uncertainty, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The Rajasic intellect is that which understands things doubtfully or uncertainly.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
The intellect by which one wrongly understands Dharma, Adharma, Karya, and Akarya, failing to arrive at a definite conclusion, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The two-fold Dharma refers to Pravritti and Nivritti. The Rajasic intellect fails to comprehend them clearly relative to place, time, and class.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
The intellect by which one understands Dharma (the Lord's will), Adharma, Karya (worship), and Akarya with doubt or in a distorted way, is Rajasic.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'Ayathavat' means doubtfully or with uncertainty. Other points are clear.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O son of Pṛthā, that understanding which cannot distinguish between religion and irreligion, between action that should be done and action that should not be done, is in the mode of passion.
