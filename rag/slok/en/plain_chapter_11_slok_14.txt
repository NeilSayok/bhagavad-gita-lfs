Speaker: Sanjaya

Slok:
tataḥ sa vismayāviṣṭo hṛṣṭaromā dhanañjayaḥ .
praṇamya śirasā devaṃ kṛtāñjalirabhāṣata

Life Application:
Bowing your head with reverence when struck with awe, offer your heartfelt prayers to the divine presence.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Upon beholding this supreme and grand spectacle, the feeling of wonder in Arjuna's mind and the horripilation on his body were clearly visible. Although Sanjaya is far from the scene of action, yet through his divine vision, he seems capable of knowing not only the physical bodies of all the warriors but also their state of mind. Arjuna's feeling of wonder is as clearly visible to him as his standing hair. Bowing down with his head and with folded hands, Arjuna opens his mouth to say something. Until now Arjuna had not spoken anything, which is a clear indication of his throat being choked. When he saw that spectacle for the first time, which seemed to gently dishearten him, Arjuna's throat had become choked due to intense emotion.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
'Then (tatah)...' etc., is said in anticipation of the question: 'What did Arjuna do after seeing the Lord, the possessor of the Cosmic Form, in whom the entire universe is unified as described by the aforesaid characteristics?' 'Vismaya' is the sense of wonder (amazement). The 'hrstatva' of the hairs means their standing on end (thrilled). 'Prakarsa' (implied in pranamya) denotes the abundance of devotion and faith.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna was highly astonished to see the Cosmic Form of the Lord and out of wonder, his body experienced horripilation. Folding his hands and bowing down his head to the Lord of the Cosmic Form, he spoke.

Commentary: 'tatah sa vismayavisto hrstaroma dhananjayah'—Arjuna had never imagined such a form of the Lord; seeing it, he was filled with great wonder. Believing that the Lord, out of His sheer grace, had revealed extraordinary spiritual truths on His own and was now showing His wonderful Form, Arjuna was thrilled with joy. 'pranamya sirasa devam krtanjalirabhasata'—Beholding the Lord's extraordinary grace, Arjuna was overwhelmed with a feeling of gratitude. He thought, 'What gratitude can I offer in return? I have nothing to offer Him. I can only bow down with my head, which means dedicating myself to Him.' Therefore, folding his hands and bowing his head in reverence, Arjuna began to pray to the Lord of the Cosmic Form.

Relation: In the next three verses, Arjuna praises the Lord while describing the extraordinary features of the Cosmic Form that filled him with amazement.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
'Then (tatah)...' etc. It is said that even upon seeing such a wonderful sight, Arjuna did not become afraid, nor did his eyes wander, nor did he forget his duty due to bewilderment, nor did he retreat from that place; rather, owing to his supreme fortitude, he acted in a manner appropriate to that moment, even in the midst of great mental agitation. 'Tatah' means after that vision. 'Vismaya-avistah' means possessed or permeated by wonder, which is a unique, extraordinary mental astonishment born of seeing that wonderful form. 'Hrsta-roma' means having his hair standing on end (thrilled). 'Sah Dhananjayah' refers to that Dhananjaya who is renowned for his valor, having defeated all heroes to collect wealth during King Yudhishthira's Rajasuya sacrifice and the cattle rescue at Uttaragograha (or one who is like Agni itself due to his great splendor). Bowing down with his head touching the ground to 'devam'—the Lord Narayana who has assumed the Cosmic Form—prostrating with intense devotion and faith, and being 'krta-anjalih', with his hands folded in salutation, he spoke.

Here, it is indicated that the permanent emotion (sthayi bhava) of wonder (vismaya) residing in Arjuna is nourished by the primary stimulus (alambana vibhava) which is the Lord's Cosmic Form, the secondary stimulus (uddipana vibhava) which is the repeated viewing of that form, the physical expressions (anubhavas) such as horripilation, bowing, and joining of palms, and the transitory emotions (vyabhichari bhavas) implied by these expressions, such as fortitude (dhriti), intelligence (mati), joy (harsa), and deliberation (vitarka). Through this nourishment, the mental astonishment is also aroused in the listeners who have similar latent impressions, culminating in the experience of supreme bliss in the form of the aesthetic sentiment of wonder (Adbhuta Rasa).

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Having thus seen (the Cosmic Form), what did Arjuna do? To answer this, it is said: 'Tatah' (then) etc. 'Tatah' means after seeing (that form). Filled or permeated with wonder, Dhananjaya (Arjuna), whose hairs were standing on end due to thrill, bowing down with his head to 'devam'—the Lord Himself, and with folded hands (palms joined in salutation), spoke (addressed Him).

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
In response to the question, 'What did he do then?', it is said: 'Tatah' (then) etc. 'Tatah' means after seeing the entire universe situated in one place. He, Arjuna of renowned glory, Dhananjaya, became filled with wonder (amazement). The indicator of that wonder is mentioned: 'hrsta-roma', i.e., one whose hairs are standing on end (thrilled). Bowing down with intense devotion ('pranamya') with his head ('sirasa') to the Lord ('devam') who has assumed the Cosmic Form, and with folded hands ('krta-anjalih') for salutation, he spoke.

By the words 'sah Dhananjayah' (that Dhananjaya), it is implied: 'Even before seeing the Cosmic Form, he was of renowned power through acts such as the burning of the Khandava forest, and by conquering wealth from kings in the Rajasuya sacrifice and reclaiming cattle wealth from Bhishma and others in the cattle rescue (gograha), he was called Dhananjaya. Now, having also seen the Cosmic Form, you (Dhritarashtra) should not harbor any hope of retaining the kingdom.' This is suggested by these words.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
In response to the question, 'What did he do after seeing?', it is said: 'Tatah' (then) etc. 'Tatah' means after that. He, the aforesaid Arjuna who had received divine vision, became filled with wonder, i.e., immersed in the sentiment of wonder (adbhuta rasa). 'Hrsta-roma' means having his limbs thrilled (hair standing on end) due to inner bliss. Dhananjaya, before whom the form of divine glory had manifested, bowing down with his head to 'devam'—the worshipful Lord, and being 'krta-anjalih', i.e., humble, spoke (made a supplication).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Then, bewildered and astonished, his hair standing on end, Arjuna bowed his head to offer obeisances and with folded hands began to pray to the Supreme Lord.

 Once the divine vision is revealed, the relationship between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna changes immediately. Before, Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna had a relationship based on friendship, but here, after the revelation, Arjuna is offering obeisances with great respect, and with folded hands he is praying to Kṛṣṇa. He is praising the universal form. Thus Arjuna’s relationship becomes one of wonder rather than friendship. Great devotees see Kṛṣṇa as the reservoir of all relationships. In the scriptures there are twelve basic kinds of relationships mentioned, and all of them are present in Kṛṣṇa. It is said that He is the ocean of all the relationships exchanged between two living entities, between the gods, or between the Supreme Lord and His devotees. Here Arjuna was inspired by the relationship of wonder, and in that wonder, although he was by nature very sober, calm and quiet, he became ecstatic, his hair stood up, and he began to offer his obeisances unto the Supreme Lord with folded hands. He was not, of course, afraid. He was affected by the wonders of the Supreme Lord. The immediate context is wonder; his natural loving friendship was overwhelmed by wonder, and thus he reacted in this way.
