Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Describing Bhakti Yoga and its supreme goal, Lord Krishna had said earlier, "Thereafter, you shall dwell in Me alone." Inspired by love for God, the devotee forgets his separate personality and merges with his goal, the Supreme Self. In the previous verse, the Lord stated that a seeker who serves Him through unswerving Bhakti Yoga gradually becomes free from identification with non-Self adjuncts (upadhis). To the extent ego disappears, to that extent the divinity of the Self is expressed. Just as one moves away from the waking state and merges into the peaceful state of sleep as sleep increases, abandoning one level of experience means entering into another experience. 'I am the abode of Brahman' — The Consciousness residing as the innermost Self in the seeker's heart is the same all-pervading, immortal, immutable, eternal, blissful Truth called Brahman. Identification with the Self is the realization of the infinite Brahman, the substratum of the universe. From the perspective of the pot-adjunct, the space enclosed within it (pot-space) appears distinct from the external all-pervading space; but in the absence of the pot-adjunct, that pot-space itself becomes the great space (mahakasha). Similarly, due to the body-adjunct, the Consciousness element is called the individual Self (Atman), but in truth, it is the infinite Brahman. This Brahman is immortal, immutable, eternal, and of the nature of bliss. Sri Shankaracharya in his highly logical and analytical commentary points to four interpretations of this verse. These meanings are not contradictory, but each highlights the philosophical aspect of this verse even further. He says 'pratistha' means that in which a thing abides; since I am the abode of immortal and immutable Brahman, I am the innermost Self (Pratyagatman). That this Pratyagatman is indeed the Paramatman (the Self of all beings) is established through right knowledge. Further, that Power by which Brahman inclines to bestow grace upon devotees is Brahman Itself, which is Me. Here the word Power indicates God, the possessor of power. The implication is that Unconditioned Brahman bestows grace upon devotees in the form of God through Maya-power. Or, by the word Brahman is meant conditioned, Saguna Brahman, whose abode is Me, the Unconditioned Brahman. As stated earlier, there is no mutual conflict among these meanings. Through our limited intellectual capacity, only conditioned Brahman can be comprehended and described in words as distinct from Prakriti. The abode of both Prakriti and conditioned Brahman is Unconditioned Consciousness Brahman, which illumines both. Therefore, in truth, I am the unconditioned, immortal, immutable, indescribably blissful Brahman. Now it becomes clear that an accomplished seeker attains My nature as Lord Krishna described, and since I am Brahman, that seeker becomes Brahman itself. In the next chapter, a more detailed presentation of Brahman is given. Conclusion: Thus in the Upanishads of the glorious Bhagavad Gita, the science of the Eternal, the scripture of Yoga, the dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna, ends the fourteenth discourse entitled 'The Yoga of the Division of the Three Gunas'.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Asking for the reason why the wise person becomes Brahman itself, Sri Anandagiri explains Sri Shankara's commentary: In the absence of any contradiction, the word Brahman should be taken in its primary sense, i.e., the Supreme Self (Paramatman). How the inner Self (Pratyagatman) is the support/abode (pratistha) of the Supreme Self is explained: that Brahman which rests in the inner Self has qualities like 'immortal' (amrtasya) etc. To avoid tautology between 'immortal' and 'immutable' (avyaya), 'avyaya' means changeless (avikarina). Eternity means absence of decay, thus avoiding repetition with the previous terms. Addressing the doubt regarding how the word 'dharma' fits Brahman, it is explained as the virtue attainable through Jnana-yoga. To exclude happiness born of sense-object contacts, the word 'absolute' (aikantikasya) is used. After explaining the word meanings, the sentence meaning is stated: since the inner Self is the abode of the Supreme Self possessing immortality etc., the inner Self is ascertained as the Supreme Self through right knowledge. The connection with the previous verse is shown: this is what was meant by 'he qualifies for becoming Brahman'. Elaborating on the intended meaning: the Divine Power through which Brahman acts is Brahman itself, owing to the non-difference between power and the possessor of power. Alternative explanation: or Brahman means Saguna Brahman, and I (Unconditioned Brahman) am Its abode. Thus, in this chapter, through five questions, it is established that the connection of Kshetra and Kshetrajna causes samsara, and right knowledge alone terminates all samsara; also distinguishing between what a seeker must achieve through effort and what is natural to a liberated soul. Thus ends Anandagiri's commentary on Chapter 14.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
For I am the abode of Brahman, of imperishable immortality, of eternal Dharma and of absolute bliss.

Commentary -- 'Brahmano hi pratisthaham' — 'I am the abode, the support of Brahman' — saying this intends to convey non-difference from Brahman. Just as flaming fire is personal (formful) and fire residing in wood is formless — these are two aspects of fire, but in reality fire is one and the same. Similarly, the Lord exists in personal form and Brahman in formless aspect — these two forms are from the perspective of the seeker's worship, but in truth God and Brahman are one, not two. Just as food has a fragrance and a taste; from the nose's view it is fragrance and from the tongue's view it is taste, but food is one. Likewise, from the standpoint of Jnana it is Brahman and from Bhakti it is God, but in essence God and Brahman are one. It is not that Lord Krishna is different and Brahman is different; rather Lord Krishna Himself is Brahman and Brahman Himself is Lord Krishna. In the Gita, the Lord has used the word Brahman for Himself ('brahmanyadhaya karmani' 5.10) and called Himself of unmanifest form ('maya tatamidam sarvam jagadavyaktamurtina' 9.4). The point is that the personal and formless are one and the same, not two.
'Amrtasyavyayasya ca' — I am the substratum of imperishable immortality, and imperishable immortality is My substratum. The meaning is that imperishable immortality and I are not two principles, but one. The Lord has spoken of attaining this imperishable immortality by the words 'amrtamasnute' (13.12, 14.20).
'Sasvatasya ca dharmasya' — I am the foundation of Eternal Dharma and My foundation is Eternal Dharma. Meaning, Eternal Dharma and I are one and the same. Eternal Dharma is My very nature. In the Gita, Arjuna called the Lord the protector of eternal Dharma (11.18). The Lord also incarnates to protect Eternal Dharma (4.8).
'Sukhasyaikantikasya ca' — I am the foundation of absolute bliss and My foundation is absolute bliss, i.e., My essential nature is absolute bliss. The Lord has called this absolute bliss as imperishable bliss (5.21), ultimate bliss (6.21), and supreme bliss (6.28).
In this verse, in words like 'brahmanah', 'amrtasya', the genitive case is used to denote non-difference, like 'Rahu's head'. That is, in the phrase 'head of Rahu', it does not mean Rahu is different and the head is different; rather Rahu itself is the head. Similarly here, Brahman, imperishable immortality, etc., are Lord Krishna Himself. Call it Brahman or Krishna, call it imperishable immortality or Krishna, call it eternal Dharma or Krishna, call it absolute bliss or Krishna — it is the very same reality. There is no real container-contained relationship here, only one Truth. Therefore, worshipping the Lord naturally leads to the attainment of Brahman.
Thus ends the fourteenth discourse entitled 'The Yoga of the Division of the Three Gunas' in the Upanishad of the Bhagavad Gita.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The term 'hi' (for) denotes cause. I, who am to be served by unswerving Bhakti Yoga, am 'the ground of the individual self, immortal and immutable, and also of eternal Dharma,' namely, surpassing eternal prosperity and also perfect felicity, i.e., of the felicity attained by the Jnanin stated in texts such as 'Realising that Vasudeva is all' (7.19). I, being of such nature, devotion to Me helps the Jiva to transcend the Gunas. Although the expression 'eternal Dharma' is indicative of the conduct to be observed, in the given context, it means the goal to be attained; for, what follows and what precedes it, denote the goal and not conduct. The purport is this:  It has been stated that seeking refuge with the Lord is the only means for transcending the Gunas and the attainment of self-realisation, prosperity and the Supreme Being in the earlier text beginning with, 'For this divine Maya of Mine consisting of the three Gunas is hard to break through, except for those who take refuge in Me alone ৷৷.' (7.14). Thus, seeking surrender to the Lord with one-pointed mind is the only means for transcending the Gunas and for the attainment of the state of brahman through that. [Here Prapatti, surrender to the Lord, is mentioned as a limb of unswerving Bhakti Yoga according to some interpreters. This is however a disputable point, as some maintain that Prapatti is in itself an independent path].

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Hi, for; aham, I, the inmost Self; am the pratistha brahmanah, Abode-that in which something abides is pratistha-of Brahman which is the supreme Self. Of Brahman of what kind? Amrtasya, of that which is indestructible; avyayasya, of that which is immutable; and sasvatasya, of that which is eternal; dharmasya, of that which is the Dharma, realizable through the Yoga of Jnana which is called dharma (virtue); and aikantikasya sukhasya, of that which is the absolute, unfailing Bliss by nature.
Since the inmost Self is the abode of the supreme Self-which by nature is immortal etc.-, therefore, through perfect Knowledge it (the former) is realized with certainty to be the supreme Self. This has been stated in, 'he alifies for becoming Brahman'.
The purport is this: Indeed, that power of God through which Brahman sets out, comes forth, for the purpose of favouring the devotees, etc., that power which is Brahman Itself, am I. For, a power and the possesser of that power are non-different. Or, brahman means the conditioned Brahman, since It (too,) is referred to by that word. 'Of that Brahman, I Myself, the unconditioned Brahman-and none else-am the Abode.'
(The abode of Brahman) of what alities? Of that which is immortal; of that which has the ality of deathlessness; of that which is immutable; so also, of that which is the eternal; which is the dharma having the characteristics of steadfastness in Knowledge; of that which is the absolute, unestionably certain Bliss born of that (steadfastness);-'I am the Abode' is understood.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha's commentary — Some (like Shankaracharya) explain 'Brahmano hi' as 'of Supreme Brahman', but that is incorrect, because the Lord explicitly states 'I am the abode'. If it is argued that this is figurative, taking a figurative meaning when the primary meaning is applicable is improper. With this intent he states — 'Brahmano' refers to Maya (the contextual Mahalakshmi).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Showing His supremacy with the intention that 'I alone am the non-different abode of Brahman, immortality, Dharma, and bliss mentioned in verse 14.26 and elsewhere', the Lord says 'Brahmano hi' etc. I am the root foundation (pratistha) of Akshara Brahman, which is the abode of heaven, earth, etc.; or I am that by which It is established, being the repository of Its divine opulence. I am the abode of imperishable immortality which is Moksha and Brahman-bliss, as well as of eternal Bhagavad-Dharma meant for liberation, and of absolute devotional bliss (bhajananda) which transcends the perishable and imperishable. By those terms, I alone am the object of contemplation and the goal to be attained for respective eligible seekers.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudana Saraswati provides the reason for the previous verse: I, the unconditioned Vasudeva, am the ultimate reality, the unconditioned Sachchidananda target of the word 'Tat' — the foundation (pratistha) of the conditioned Brahman (the creator, preserver, and destroyer of the world). Where something rests is its pratistha, i.e., the real unsuperimposed nature. Therefore, one who serves Me, the unconditioned Brahman, naturally becomes eligible for Brahman-hood. To the question 'of what kind of Brahman am I the foundation?', the attributes are given: immortal (indestructible), immutable (change-less), eternal (undecaying), and of the nature of supreme bliss attainable through Jnana-nistha. To exclude sense-born pleasure, the word 'absolute' (aikantikasya, present at all times and places) is used. Since I am the true nature of such Brahman, My devotee is liberated from samsara. As Lord Brahma prayed to Krishna: 'You alone are the ancient Person, Self-luminous, Eternal, Immutable, and Absolute Bliss free from upadhis.' Alternatively, to the objection 'your devotee may attain your nature, but how can he attain Brahman-hood?', the Lord answers: I Myself am the ultimate culmination (paryapti) of Brahman; there is no Brahman separate from Me. Liberation, eternal Dharma, and supreme bliss all culminate in Me alone. Therefore, it is rightly said that My devotee qualifies for Brahman-hood.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states the reason: 'Brahmano hi' etc. For I am the pratistha (embodiment or condensed form) of Brahman; just as condensed light is the solar orb, so am I condensed Brahman. Also I am the abode of immutable and eternal immortality (Moksha), being eternally liberated. Also I am the support of Eternal Dharma which is the means to it, being of pure sattva nature. And I am the abode of absolute (unbroken) bliss, being of the nature of supreme bliss. Therefore, for My devotee, attaining My nature (Brahman-hood) is inevitable; hence it is rightly said that he qualifies for Brahman-hood.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati Suri (Abhinava-Gita-Bhasya-Utkarsha-Dipika): Explaining the reason why an unswerving devotee transcends the Gunas and qualifies for Brahman-hood ('Brahmano hi' etc.): I, the inner Self (Pratyagatman), am the abode of Supreme Brahman, who is indestructible, immutable, eternal, attainable through Jnana-yoga virtue, and of the nature of absolute bliss. Or, the Divine Power through which Brahman functions is Brahman Itself, which is Me. Or, I, the Unconditioned Brahman, am the support of Conditioned (Saguna) Brahman. According to Sridhara Swami: Just as condensed light is the solar orb, I am condensed Brahman; I am the abode of liberation, pure sattva dharma, and supreme bliss. According to Madhusudana Saraswati: I, Vasudeva, am the unconditioned reality of conditioned Brahman. From the perspective of the seven stages of knowledge (Jnana-bhumis) mentioned in Vasishtha Samhita (Subhechha, Vicharana, Tanumanasa, Sattvapatti, Asansakti, Padarthabhavini, Turyaga), this verse presents the progressive stages of a seeker established in wisdom. Therefore, attaining Brahman-hood through devotional service to the Lord is entirely justified.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Sri Venkatanatha (Tatparya-Chandrika): Commenting on Ramanuja's commentary, he explains that the word 'hi' indicates cause. Here the word Brahman does not refer exclusively to Supreme Brahman, but figuratively denotes the purified individual self (Pratyagatman) attained in liberation. Just as surrender to the Lord was declared in verse 7.14 as the sole means to overcome the Gunas, here too it is established that serving the Lord through unswerving Bhakti Yoga (including Prapatti/surrender as its limb) enables the Jiva to transcend the Gunas and attain the immortal, immutable self-realization (Brahman-hood), supreme eternal prosperity (eternal Dharma), and the absolute bliss of experiencing the Lord (aikantika sukha). The Lord alone is the ultimate ground of all these goals.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji (Vallabha Sampradaya): Clarifying the doubt that if Brahman means Akshara Brahman, attaining Brahman-hood might be secondary rather than supreme, the Lord says 'Brahmano hi' etc. Truly, I alone am the foundation (pratistha) of Akshara Brahman; I am the root/source of immortality (Moksha), imperishable eternal Vaikuntha, eternal Dharma in the form of scriptural devotion, and absolute bliss in the form of protective divine love (Bhagavad-bhava). Therefore, the state produced by these is My very nature.
Thus in the 14th chapter, Lord Hari explained to Arjuna the nature of the Gunas and their dual aspects for His divine play.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth (Bhava-Dipa): Presenting the second stage of knowledge called 'Vicharana' through its subject matter ('Brahmano hi' etc.): I am the final culmination and purport of the Veda (Brahman), which is self-evident, apaurusheya, free from doubt, and the means to immortality through both Karma and Jnana. Analyzing the entire Veda via introduction, conclusion, etc., with the desire to realize Me constitutes the second stage 'Vicharana'. Showing the cause and fruit, the first stage 'Subhechha' is declared by 'Sasvatasya' etc.: Eternal Dharma dedicated to God yields eternal fruit whose supreme goal is Me. I am also the ultimate height of unalloyed Moksha-bliss (aikantika sukha). The desire for absolute bliss arising in a purified mind through selfless duty is the first stage 'Subhechha'.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
And I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman, which is immortal, imperishable and eternal and is the constitutional position of ultimate happiness.

 The constitution of Brahman is immortality, imperishability, eternity and happiness. Brahman is the beginning of transcendental realization. Paramātmā, the Supersoul, is the middle, the second stage in transcendental realization, and the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the ultimate realization of the Absolute Truth. Therefore, both Paramātmā and the impersonal Brahman are within the Supreme Person. It is explained in the Seventh Chapter that material nature is the manifestation of the inferior energy of the Supreme Lord. The Lord impregnates the inferior, material nature with fragments of the superior nature, and that is the spiritual touch in the material nature. When a living entity conditioned by this material nature begins the cultivation of spiritual knowledge, he elevates himself from the position of material existence and gradually rises up to the Brahman conception of the Supreme. This attainment of the Brahman conception of life is the first stage in self-realization. At this stage the Brahman-realized person is transcendental to the material position, but he is not actually perfect in Brahman realization. If he wants, he can continue to stay in the Brahman position and then gradually rise up to Paramātmā realization and then to the realization of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. There are many examples of this in Vedic literature. The four Kumāras were situated first in the impersonal Brahman conception of truth, but then they gradually rose to the platform of devotional service. One who cannot elevate himself beyond the impersonal conception of Brahman runs the risk of falling down. In Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam it is stated that although a person may rise to the stage of impersonal Brahman, without going further, with no information of the Supreme Person, his intelligence is not perfectly clear. Therefore, in spite of being raised to the Brahman platform, there is the chance of falling down if one is not engaged in the devotional service of the Lord. In the Vedic language it is also said, raso vai saḥ, rasaṁ hy evāyaṁ labdhvānandī bhavati: “When one understands the Personality of Godhead, the reservoir of pleasure, Kṛṣṇa, he actually becomes transcendentally blissful.” ( Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.7.1) The Supreme Lord is full in six opulences, and when a devotee approaches Him there is an exchange of these six opulences. The servant of the king enjoys on an almost equal level with the king. And so eternal happiness, imperishable happiness, and eternal life accompany devotional service. Therefore, realization of Brahman, or eternity, or imperishability, is included in devotional service. This is already possessed by a person who is engaged in devotional service. The living entity, although Brahman by nature, has the desire to lord it over the material world, and due to this he falls down. In his constitutional position, a living entity is above the three modes of material nature, but association with material nature entangles him in the different modes of material nature – goodness, passion and ignorance. Due to the association of these three modes, his desire to dominate the material world is there. By engagement in devotional service in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he is immediately situated in the transcendental position, and his unlawful desire to control material nature is removed. Therefore the process of devotional service, beginning with hearing, chanting, remembering – the prescribed nine methods for realizing devotional service – should be practiced in the association of devotees. Gradually, by such association, by the influence of the spiritual master, one’s material desire to dominate is removed, and one becomes firmly situated in the Lord’s transcendental loving service. This method is prescribed from the twenty-second to the last verse of this chapter. Devotional service to the Lord is very simple: one should always engage in the service of the Lord, should eat the remnants of foodstuffs offered to the Deity, smell the flowers offered to the lotus feet of the Lord, see the places where the Lord had His transcendental pastimes, read of the different activities of the Lord, His reciprocation of love with His devotees, chant always the transcendental vibration Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare, and observe the fasting days commemorating the appearances and disappearances of the Lord and His devotees. By following such a process one becomes completely detached from all material activities. One who can thus situate himself in the brahma-jyotir or the different varieties of the Brahman conception is equal to the Supreme Personality of Godhead in quality. Thus end the Bhaktivedanta s to the Fourteenth Chapter of the Śrīmad Bhagavad-gītā in the matter of the Three Modes of Material Nature.
