Life Application:
Understand that neither scriptural study, nor austerity, nor charity, nor ritual sacrifice can reveal God as unalloyed devotion does.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Not by study of the scriptures, or by austerities, not by gifts or sacrifices, is it possible to see Me as thou hast done.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
To behold this Cosmic Form of the Lord is not easy for anyone. This experience of vision cannot be obtained either by the study of the Vedas, nor by penance, nor by charity, nor by sacrifice. Even the gods residing in heaven, despite their vast intellect, long life, and difficult spiritual practices, are unable to see this form and are always longing for it. In spite of this, Lord Krishna, out of mere grace, showed this vast and wonderful form to His friend Arjuna, as He Himself admitted. We might wonder why the Lord showers His grace on one particular person and not on others. Surely, it cannot be an accidental distribution by an almighty ruler who acts whimsically, arbitrarily, and without any rule or reason; for in that case, the Lord would be guilty of partiality and despotism, which is not fitting. In this verse, a rational explanation is given as to why the Lord is compelled to shower His special grace upon a particular person sometimes, and not always upon everyone.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
It is doubted that it is difficult to see due to the absence of the means of perception—for what reason? Even though means like the Vedas exist, the Lord cannot be seen in the aforementioned form, hence he says, 'nāham' (Not I). Then, raising the doubt that it is impossible to see due to the unfitness of being seen, he says, 'dṛṣṭavān' (you have seen).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
In the manner you have seen Me, I in this form (possessing four arms) cannot be seen either by the study of the Vedas, nor by penances, nor by charity, nor by sacrifice.

Commentary—'dṛṣṭavānasi māṃ yathā'—You have seen My four-armed form only by My grace. The meaning is that My vision can be attained only by My grace, not by any personal qualification. 'nāhaṃ vedairna tapasā na dānena na cejyayā śakya evaṃvidho draṣṭum'—It is a principle that whatever is purchased at a price must be of lesser value than that price. For example, if a shopkeeper sells a watch for a hundred rupees, he must have bought it for a lower price, which is why he sells it for a hundred rupees. Similarly, it is not that by studying many Vedas, undergoing great penances, giving massive charity, or performing grand sacrifices, one will attain the Lord. No matter how great the action or how highly qualified one may be, the Lord cannot be bought by these. All of them combined cannot equal the value of attaining the Lord. One cannot claim ownership or control over the Lord through them. Arjuna has clearly said in the forty-third verse of this chapter that there is no one equal to You in the three worlds, so how can there be anyone greater than You? The point is that without being greater than You, one cannot claim authority over You.
In worldly matters, a highly qualified person can dominate a less qualified one, a wiser person can awe less intelligent ones, and a wealthier person can manifest superiority over the poor; but the Lord cannot be purchased by any power, intellect, qualification, person, or object. For how can the Lord, by whose mere resolve infinite universes are instantly created, be bought by any object or person residing in a mere fraction of a single universe? This means that the attainment of the Lord occurs only through the grace of the Lord. That grace is obtained when a person completely surrenders his capacity, time, understanding, and possessions to the Lord, and experiences complete weakness and unworthiness within himself, i.e., when he does not possess the slightest pride in his strength, qualification, etc. Thus, when he becomes completely helpless, surrenders himself entirely to the Lord, and calls upon Him with single-minded devotion, the Lord appears instantly. This is because as long as a person holds onto the importance and support of worldly objects, qualification, power, intellect, etc., in his heart, the Lord appears far away, even though He is extremely near.
The rarity mentioned in this verse is spoken only regarding the four-armed form, not the Cosmic Form. If it were assumed to be for the Cosmic Form, it would lead to tautology (repetition) because the rarity of the Cosmic Form has already been stated in the forty-eighth verse. Secondly, in the next verse, the Lord states that it is possible to see Him through unswerving devotion (ananya-bhakti). Unswerving devotion cannot exist towards the Cosmic Form, because even a heroic warrior like Arjuna, despite obtaining divine vision from the Lord, became terrified upon seeing the Cosmic Form. How then can there be unswerving devotion, unswerving love, or attraction towards that form? It is simply not possible.

Connection—If no one can attain You by any practice, qualification, or resources, then how are You attained? The Lord answers this in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Krsna says  By Vedas, i.e., by mere study, teaching etc., of these sacred texts, it is not possible to know Me truly. It is also not possible through meditation, sacrifices, gifts and austerities, destitute of devotion towards Me. But by single-minded devotion i.e., by devotion characterised by extreme ardour and intensity, it is possible to know Me in reality through scriptures, to behold Me directly in reality, and enter into Me in reality. So describes a Sruti passage:  'This Self cannot be obtained by instruction, nor by intellect nor by much hearing. Whomsoever He chooses, by him alone is He obtained. To such a one He reveals His own form' (Ka. U., 2.2.23) and (Mun. U., 3.2.3).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Having thus described the extreme difficulty of beholding the Cosmic Form, He says the same about Himself who was seen at the beginning—'nāhaṁ vedaiḥ' (not by the Vedas). I am the Self possessed of unsurpassable and imperishable sovereignty. I am known through the Upanishads to be unattainable by means other than His choosing (grace), as stated: 'This Self cannot be attained by instruction, nor by intellect, nor by much hearing. Whomsoever He chooses, by him alone is He attained. To such a one He reveals His own form' (Katha Upanishad 1.2.23 / Mundaka Upanishad 3.2.3). You have seen Me in this form by becoming fit in nature through His choosing (grace) in the proper manner. Thus, it is established that practices such as teaching (instruction) and others, being solely oriented toward self-realization, generate the fitness of nature in the individual soul, but do not directly bind or constrain the Supreme Purusha (Purushottama).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Why have the gods not seen this form, nor will they see it? Because they are devoid of devotion to Me, He says—'nāham' (not I). The meaning of this verse has already been explained by the text beginning with 'not by Vedas, sacrifices, study, etc.' (11.48); it is repeated here in order to emphasize the supreme rarity of this vision.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The reason for that is stated—'nāham' (not I). The meaning of this verse is clear.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
In response to the desire to know the reason for that, He says—'na' (not). In this manner, as you have seen Me in My Cosmic Form, I cannot be seen by others who are devoid of My supreme grace, neither by the Vedas like the Rigveda etc., nor by severe penances like the Candrayana, nor by gifts such as the donation of cows, etc., nor by sacrifice or worship.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
In order to show that there is no tautology, because the next verse explains this one, he (Sri Ramanuja) introduces it by saying: 'Why? On this He says...' etc. Since the Vedas by themselves cannot be considered a direct means (to see God), their prohibition would be improper; therefore, explaining the real intent, he says: 'By Vedas, i.e., by teaching, lecturing, and study.' The mention of gifts and sacrifice also implies homa (fire offerings), and with this intent, he says: 'By sacrifice, gifts, homa, and austerities.' Their status as means is only through devotion, as known from Upanishadic texts like 'Him the Brahmanas seek to know through the study of the Vedas' (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.22) etc.; therefore, he says 'destitute of devotion to Me, by mere means'. The word 'evaṁvidhaḥ' (in this form) refers to the supernatural state free from the delusion of mere humanity; hence he says: 'I, established as I truly am.' Devotion is required not only as a means for realization (direct perception) but also for determining the meaning of scriptures, as in 'Having attained pure state through devotion, I know Janardana from the scriptures' (Mahabharata 5.69.5), and also for entering (attaining) Him which follows realization. With this in mind, both 'to know' (jñātum) and 'to enter' (praveṣṭum)—which were not mentioned in the previous verse—are introduced here. The phrase 'in reality' (tattvataḥ) applies to all three (knowing, seeing, and entering) as it is required without distinction. 'Entering in reality' means complete attainment, which is the experience of Him in His true state and in all His aspects. By this, the mere attainment of the Vyuhas (emanations) or Vibhavas (incarnations) is excluded. The lower stages of attainment achieved before final liberation are mentioned in Smritis: 'Some dwell in the worlds of Vishnu (salokya), others obtain proximity (samipya), others attain a similar form (sarupya), and others attain union (sayujya) which is said to be liberation.' Indeed, in the commentary on 'This birth is very rare in the world...' (BG 6.42), Yadavaprakasha also stated: 'This is the fruit of one who dies having attained a minor yogic power. If one dies having attained the first yogic power, or abandons yoga, he is born in Shvetadvipa or attains Salokya of the Lord. If he dies having attained higher than that, he becomes an attendant of Vishnu. If higher, the lord of attendants. If higher, a gatekeeper. If higher, a personal attendant (who massages the Lord's limbs). If higher, a minister-like attendant endowed with separate glory. If he dies having attained full yogic power, he attains Sayujya (union) with the Lord, liberated and endowed with supreme glory.' This is the rule in Vaishnava Yoga scriptures. All this is indicated here by the Lord, or by verses like 'or of yogis...' (6.42). There is no defect of mutual dependence (anyonyashraya) because devotion has different stages in being the cause of knowledge, vision, and attainment. A basic form of devotion, consisting of a general inclination generated by the association with saintly persons due to the merits of previous births and in accordance with proper hearing, serves as a cooperative cause in the generation of scriptural knowledge. But 'Para-bhakti' (higher devotion), filled with an intense desire to see the Lord, is the cause of direct perception (realization). Once He is perceived, 'Parama-bhakti' (supreme devotion), characterized by an intense absorption in the complete experience of Him, is the cause of entering Him. Here, the word 'ananyayā' (single-minded) should be understood as 'having no other purpose', as explained before. Some say it means 'having no other deity'. The interpretation of it as meditation on identity (oneness with Brahman) was already refuted earlier, and is contrary to perception, etc. Bearing all this in mind, it is summarized in the Gitartha Sangraha (verse 15) by Yamunacharya: 'In the eleventh chapter, the vision of the direct perception of His own greatness is given, and it is stated that devotion is the sole means for knowing and attaining Him.' He refutes the doubt that this is mere praise of devotion (arthavada) by saying 'Thus also...' (referring to the Upanishadic text). In 'This Self cannot...' etc., there is a prohibition of mere means (without devotion), similar to the prohibition in 'the fire should not be piled on the earth...'. It is established by the nature of things and the strength of supporting scriptures (upabrimhana) that the specific quality which causes one to be chosen (varaniyata-hetu), referred to by the word 'whom' (yam), is indeed devotion. Here, if the word 'tanū' (body/form) refers to the physical form, the nature, etc. is implied; if it refers to the nature, the physical form, etc. is obtained.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
If it is asked: 'Why do they not see Me by the means mentioned in the Vedas, etc.?', He says—'nāham' (not I). In this manner as you have seen Me, I, the Supreme Purusha (Purushottama), cannot be seen by the visible Vedas, by the means prescribed in the Vedas, or by the Vedas alone; therefore it is said, 'Whence words return along with the mind, without reaching Him' (Taittiriya Upanishad 2.4.1 / 2.9.1). I cannot be seen by painful austerity, nor by charity involving the donation of all one's wealth, nor by sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'nāham' (not I). The very same meaning stated previously in 'not by Vedas, sacrifices, study, etc.' (11.48) is repeated here to indicate the extreme rarity of beholding the Cosmic Form. The meaning of the verse is clear.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The form you are seeing with your transcendental eyes cannot be understood simply by studying the Vedas, nor by undergoing serious penances, nor by charity, nor by worship. It is not by these means that one can see Me as I am.

 Kṛṣṇa first appeared before His parents Devakī and Vasudeva in a four-handed form, and then He transformed Himself into the two-handed form. This mystery is very difficult to understand for those who are atheists or who are devoid of devotional service. For scholars who have simply studied Vedic literature by way of grammatical knowledge or mere academic qualifications, Kṛṣṇa is not possible to understand. Nor is He to be understood by persons who officially go to the temple to offer worship. They make their visit, but they cannot understand Kṛṣṇa as He is. Kṛṣṇa can be understood only through the path of devotional service, as explained by Kṛṣṇa Himself in the next verse.
