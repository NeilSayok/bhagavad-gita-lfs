Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
tapasvibhyo.adhiko yogī jñānibhyo.api mato.adhikaḥ .
karmibhyaścādhiko yogī tasmādyogī bhavārjuna

Word Meanings:
तपस्विभ्योऽधिको - greater than ascetics
योगी - yogi
ज्ञानिभ्योऽपि - even than men of knowledge
मतोऽधिकः - considered superior
कर्मिभ्यश्चाधिको - greater than ritualists
योगी - yogi
तस्माद्योगी - therefore a yogi
भवार्जुन - be you, O Arjuna

Life Application:
Aspire to become a true practitioner of inner discipline, rising above mere austerity or dry intellectual study.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to scholars, and superior to men of action; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna!

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to scholars, and superior to ritualists; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna!

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The Meditation-Yogi is superior to mere bodily ascetics, superior even to scholars possessing dry scriptural knowledge, and superior to ritualists performing fruitive actions; therefore, O Arjuna, be a selfless Meditation-Yogi!

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna"—mere asceticism or intellectual scholarship cannot yield Lord Vishnu; Dhyana-Yoga (Bhakti) alone secures His grace.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Compared to mere bodily ascetics, dry scholars, and ritualistic performers, the integrated meditator (Yogi) is infinitely superior.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to scholars, and superior to men of action; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna!

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Superior to bodily ascetics, scriptural scholars, and ritual workers, the Yogi absorbed in Self-realization is the highest of all.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to men of scriptural knowledge, and superior to ritualists; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna. And among all Yogis, he who worships Me with faith, with his inmost self absorbed in Me, is regarded by Me as the most devout and highest Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to scholars, and superior to men of action; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna"—establishes Dhyana-Yoga as superior to tapas, paroksha-jnana, and karma-kanda.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Yoga means focusing the mind on Lord Vishnu, which is infinitely superior to all other religious practices.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Meditation consisting of Krishna-remembrance is the supreme fruition of all austerity, knowledge, and work; hence Arjuna is commanded to be a Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna"—compared to austerities, indirect knowledge, and fruitive work, the direct realization-yogi of Asamprajnata Samadhi is supreme.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The Yogi is thought to be superior to ascetics, superior even to scholars, and superior to ritualists; therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna!

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis proving the absolute superiority of the Meditation-Yogi over ascetics, scholars, and ritual workers.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The Yogi worshipping Lord Vasudeva is the highest among all ascetics and scholars, beloved of God.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Lord Krishna imparts the supreme command to Arjuna to firmly ascend the path of selfless Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Therefore be a Yogi, O Arjuna"—the Yogi established in direct intuition of the inner Self is the crown of all spiritual practices.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A yogi is greater than the ascetic, greater than the empiricist and greater than the fruitive worker. Therefore, O Arjuna, in all circumstances, be a yogi.
