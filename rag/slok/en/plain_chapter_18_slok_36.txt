Life Application:
Hear now of threefold happiness, in which one rejoices by practice and reaches end of pain.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
O best of the Bharatas, now hear from Me of the three kinds of happiness, in which one rejoices by practice and attains the end of all sorrow.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Having analyzed the components of action (knowledge, actor, action, intellect, determination), Shri Krishna now explains the psychology of action: why do we act? Every living being acts for the sake of happiness. However, due to differences in Gunas, the paths to happiness differ. A seeker who understands these Gunas can identify the cause of their sorrow and reorganize their life. Through consistent practice (abhyasa), one rejoices in the higher self and attains the end of all pain.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O best of the Bharatas, now hear from Me of the three kinds of happiness: That in which one rejoices by long practice, and in which one comes to the end of all pain.

Commentary: The Lord addresses Arjuna as 'Bharatarṣabha' (best of the Bharatas) to remind him that he has already conquered material pleasures (like rejecting Urvasi) and laziness (conquering sleep, hence called Gudakesha). Happiness is the ultimate goal of all activities. The desire for pleasure can bind even spiritual seekers. Sattvic happiness comes from the purification of the intellect (atmabuddhiprasada) and initially feels like poison (due to the struggle of abandoning worldly habits) but ends up like nectar, resolving all suffering. One must rise even above Sattvic happiness to attain the Supreme.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.36–18.39 jointly): Explains the threefold classification of happiness. Sattvika joy feels like poison initially during arduous mental discipline, but yields the nectar of liberation in the end through the tranquility of Self-knowledge. Rajasa joy stems from sense-object contact, tasting like nectar at first but ending in sorrow like poison. Tamasa joy is delusive from start to finish, born of sleep, indolence, and negligence.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O best of the Bhāratas, now please hear from Me about the three kinds of happiness by which the conditioned soul enjoys, and by which he sometimes comes to the end of all distress.

 A conditioned soul tries to enjoy material happiness again and again. Thus he chews the chewed. But sometimes, in the course of such enjoyment, he becomes relieved from material entanglement by association with a great soul. In other words, a conditioned soul is always engaged in some type of sense gratification, but when he understands by good association that it is only a repetition of the same thing, and he is awakened to his real Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he is sometimes relieved from such repetitive so-called happiness.
