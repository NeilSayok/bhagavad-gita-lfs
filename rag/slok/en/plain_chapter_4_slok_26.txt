Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śrotrādīnīndriyāṇyanye saṃyamāgniṣu juhvati .
śabdādīnviṣayānanya indriyāgniṣu juhvati

Word Meanings:
श्रोत्रादीनीन्द्रियाण्यन्ये - hearing and other senses others
संयमाग्निषु - in fires of restraint
जुह्वति - sacrifice
शब्दादीन्विषयानन्य - sound and other objects others
इन्द्रियाग्निषु - in fires of senses
जुह्वति - sacrifice

Life Application:
Practice sensory discipline today by filtering out noise, gossip, and harmful sights from your daily awareness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Some Yogis offer hearing and all other senses into the fires of self-restraint; while other householder-yogis offer sound and all sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Offer into fires of restraint"—withdrawing the senses from unholy objects to engage them in Lord Vishnu"s worship is the true Yajna of self-control.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Celibates practice sensory withdrawal (Pratyahara), while householder seekers consume sense-objects with detachment in the fire of controlled senses.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Restraining senses from sense-objects is the Yajna of self-control; unattached scriptural enjoyment of sense-objects is also a Yajna.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Srotradini etc. But others offer senses into the fires of restraint, and some offer sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses"—contrasts total sense-restraint (*Pratyahara*) with detached lawful sense-engagement.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Transforming sense-contact into an offering for God forms the inner Yajna of the wise.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Utilizing senses and sense-objects solely for Krishna"s service is the supreme divine sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Celibates restrain sensory modifications, whereas householders view unattached enjoyment of objects as a Yajna.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Others offer hearing and other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and other sense-objects into the fires of the senses.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis substantiating the offering of senses into restraint and sense-objects into sense-fires.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A practitioner devoted to Vasudeva restrains senses and utilizes objects solely as a God-dedicated sacrifice.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Regulating the senses in Krishna"s love is the true sacrifice of self-control, rendering the seeker pure.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Offer into fires of restraint"—both Pratyahara and detached enjoyment of objects serve as spiritual means for mental purification.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Some of them (the unblemished brahmacaris) sacrifice the hearing process and the senses in the fire of mental control, and others (the regulated householders) sacrifice the objects of the senses in the fire of the senses.
