Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ārurukṣormuneryogaṃ karma kāraṇamucyate .
yogārūḍhasya tasyaiva śamaḥ kāraṇamucyate

Word Meanings:
आरुरुक्षोर्मुनेर्योगं - for an aspirant seeking yoga
कर्म - action
कारणमुच्यते - is said to be the means
योगारूढस्य - of one established in yoga
तस्यैव - for him alone
शमः - serenity
कारणमुच्यते - is said to be the means

Life Application:
Use unselfish action to climb the spiritual ladder, and quiet contemplation to maintain inner stability once established.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
For a Muni desirous of attaining Yoga, action is said to be the means; for the same Muni when he has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
For a Muni desirous of ascending Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
For a seeker desirous of ascending the height of Yoga, selfless action is declared to be the means; but for the same seeker when he has attained to Yoga, quietude and serenity are declared to be the means.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
For a Muni desirous of attaining Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
For a Muni desirous of attaining Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
For a Muni desirous of ascending Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
For a Muni desirous of ascending Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Action is the cause"—for a beginner, prescribed duties serve as the means to Vishnu-devotion; for the established yogi, inner quietude is the chief means.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: For a beginner seeking to ascend the ladder of Yoga, Karma-Yoga is the means; for one established in Yoga, complete cessation of action (Shama) is the means.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
For a Muni desirous of attaining Yoga, action is said to be the means; for the same Muni when he has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: For one seeking to ascend to Self-realization, selfless Karma-Yoga is the means; once established in Yoga, quietude of mind (Shama) becomes the means.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: For an aspirant desirous of ascending the peak of Yoga, action is the means; for one who has already attained Yoga, quietude is the means.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "For a Muni desirous of attaining Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means"—demarcates two stages of spiritual practice: Karma-Yoga for the aspirant (Arurukshu) and Actionless Quietude (Shama) for the established sage (Yogarudha).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Mind-purification attained via Karma-Yoga leads to Shama (inner quietude), which is the intimate internal means for Dhyana-Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Performing selfless action for Krishna"s delight is the beginner"s duty, while absorbing in divine bliss with inner quietude marks the established yogi.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "For the established Yogarudha, quietude is the cause"—after mental purification, cessation of all actions (Shama) becomes the direct cause of Knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: For a Muni desirous of ascending Yoga, action is said to be the means; for one who has attained Yoga, quietude is said to be the means.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural distinction between the aspirant and established yogi, delineating action and quietude as respective means.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Beginning with selfless Karma-Yoga, quietude of mind (Shama) culminates in Self-realization.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Steadfastness of mind in Lord Krishna is Shama, the supreme state achieved after the perfection of Karma-Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "For the aspirant desiring Yoga"—Karma-Yoga purifies the mind, while Shama (renunciation) grants perfection in Dhyana-Yoga.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
For one who is a neophyte in the eightfold yoga system, work is said to be the means; and for one who is already elevated in yoga, cessation of all material activities is said to be the means.
