Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
āvṛtaṃ jñānametena jñānino nityavairiṇā .
kāmarūpeṇa kaunteya duṣpūreṇānalena ca

Word Meanings:
आवृतं - covered
ज्ञानमेतेन - knowledge by this
ज्ञानिनो - of the wise
नित्यवैरिणा - by eternal enemy
कामरूपेण - in form of desire
कौन्तेय - O son of Kunti
दुष्पूरेणानलेन - by insatiable fire
च - and

Life Application:
Stop feeding obsessive cravings today, understanding that indulging them fuels an insatiable fire rather than bringing satisfaction.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Wisdom is enveloped (Avritam jnanam), O son of Kunti (Kaunteya), by this constant enemy of the wise (Jnanino nityavairina), in the form of Desire (Kamarupena), which is insatiable as fire (Dushpurena analena ca).

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Just as fire is never satisfied by adding fuel, desire is never extinguished by enjoyments; it is the wise man"s permanent foe.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The ignorant misplace faith in desire as pleasure, whereas the wise recognize desire as a constant source of pain and an unrelenting enemy.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Desire is the perpetual enemy of the wise, destroying awareness of spiritual bliss and plunging the soul into material craving.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Kama esa etc., Dhumena etc., and Avritam etc. Desire born of Rajoguna veils wisdom; it is the constant enemy of the wise, insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire"—explains why desire is called the eternal, unyielding enemy (*Nitya-vairi*) of the enlightened.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The wise alone recognize desire as their eternal enemy and strive to eradicate it completely.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The fire of desire is never extinguished by endless pleasures; it is quieted solely by the nectar of Krishna-surrender.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: The ignorant misinterpret desire as joy, whereas the wise with spiritual vision perceive it as a constant foe from the start.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Enveloped, O Kaunteya, is wisdom by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of desire, which is insatiable as fire.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis establishing why desire, insatiable as fire, is designated as the eternal enemy of the wise.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Continuous remembrance of Vasudeva is the sole remedy to burn away this fire-like perpetual enemy of desire.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: The wise devotee alone recognizes the venomous nature of desire and seeks refuge at Krishna"s feet.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Desire seems pleasant to the ignorant during enjoyment, but to the wise man of discrimination it is ever a bitter foe.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Thus the wise living entity"s pure consciousness becomes covered by his eternal enemy in the form of lust, which is never satisfied and which burns like fire.
