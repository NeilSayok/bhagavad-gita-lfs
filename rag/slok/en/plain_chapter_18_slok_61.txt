Life Application:
Remember that the Lord resides within your heart, guiding all beings along their life path.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Lord Krishna's teaching is clear and free from doubt. The teacher of the Gita says, 'Remember Me as Ishvara, i.e., the ruler of the entire universe.' Ishvara is the regulator and controller. Only in His presence can all events in the universe occur, not otherwise, just as steam is the 'lord' of a steam engine without which the engine cannot move. The Lord, who is omniscient and omnipotent, dwells in the hearts of all living beings. The statement that He 'causes all beings to spin by His Maya' means that He awards fruits to all beings in accordance with their past actions. Without Him, individual souls cannot exist, and they receive their powers of action and knowledge from Him alone. Understanding this Vedanta principle makes the metaphor of the machine clear.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The Supreme Lord dwells in the hearts of all living beings, O Arjuna, causing them to spin by His illusory power, as if mounted on a machine.

Commentary: The Lord is the ultimate controller (antaryami) residing in the heart of every creature. Our physical bodies and minds are like machines (yantras) created by material nature under the Lord's supervision. The souls are mounted on these machines, and the Lord, by His power of Maya, causes them to act (spin) according to their past habits and tendencies (karmas). Therefore, thinking 'I am independent, I will not fight' is a complete illusion. Everything is under the Lord's control. Real wisdom lies in surrendering to Him.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Lord Vasudeva, who is the ruler over all, lives in 'the heart of all beings,' i.e., in the region from which arises all knowledge which is at the root of all secular and spiritual activities. How and doing what does He exist?  He exists enabling, by His Maya (power), 'all beings who are mounted, as it were, on the machine Prakrti' in the form of body and senses created by Himself, to act in accordance with their Gunas of Sattva and others. It was already expressed in 'And I am seated in the hearts of all. From Me are memory, knowledge and their removal also' (15.15) and in 'From Me proceed everything' (10.8). The Srutis also proclaim 'He who, dwelling in the self' (Br. U. Madh., 3.7.22).

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Arjuna, O Arjuna-one whose self is naturally white (pure), i.e. one possessing a pure internal organ. This follows from the Vedic text, 'The day is dark and the day is arjuna (white) (Rg. 6.9.1). Isvarah, the Lord , Narayana the Ruler; tisthati, resides, remains seated; hrd-dese, in the region of the heart; sarva-bhutanam, of all creatures, of all living beings. How does He reside? In answer the Lord says: bhramayan, revolving; mayaya, through Maya, through delusion; sarva-bhutani, all the creatures; as though yantra-arudhani, mounted on a machine-like man' etc., made of wood, mounted on a machine. The word iva (as though) has to be thus understood here. Bhramayan, revolving, is to be connected with tisthati, resides (conveying the idea, 'resides৷৷.while revolving').

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Now the Lord states the Brahma-sutra doctrine that all living beings are regulated in their nature through His divine play in accordance with their past actions: 'īśvaraḥ...'. The Lord Vasudeva, the controller of all, resides in the heart-space of all material beings, from Brahma down to a blade of grass, created by Himself. Although He resides as the indwelling ruler, He remains unattached, which is why He is called 'Ishvara'. Residing in the heart but remaining untouched is His lordship. Thus the scriptures say: 'He is all-pervading like space, the one hidden God in all beings, the witness, the conscious, absolute and free from attributes.' That consciousness is self-luminous and self-manifesting like a lamp. He causes beings to spin, i.e., He inspires them, as if mounted on a machine. The machine is the self-made body, senses, etc., and He directs the conscious souls mounted on them according to their qualities. This verse serves as a commentary on: 'And I am seated in the hearts of all' (15.15).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
If it is asked how nature and past actions can control a person without the command of God, the Lord replies: 'īśvaraḥ...'. The controller, the Supreme Lord Vasudeva, resides in the heart-region of all living beings, causing all beings, mounted on the machine of the body, to spin by His power of illusion (Maya), just as a puppeteer controls puppets mounted on a wooden machine. Thus, under the direction of the Lord, nature and past actions prompt the individual to act. The name 'Arjuna' is used here to indicate one whose heart is pure (white/spotless).

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Since it is so, therefore—'īśvaraḥ': the Supreme Lord Narayana, the controller of all, resides in the heart-region of all living beings. What does He do while residing there? The Lord answers: 'bhrāmayan': He exists, causing all beings to spin (act) by His power of illusion (Maya). The illustration is: 'yantrārūḍhāni': just as a puppeteer, through his skill, causes wooden puppets mounted on a wooden machine to move. The name 'Arjuna' is used to address him because of his pure heart.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
In response to what happens next, the Lord says: 'īśvaraḥ...'. The Lord resides in the heart-region of all beings, from Brahma down to a blade of grass. If it is asked what His function is while residing there, the Lord answers: 'bhrāmayan': He exists, causing all beings to spin, i.e., directing their actions by His power of illusion (Maya) consisting of the three gunas. How does He cause them to spin? 'yantrārūḍhāni': just as a puppeteer causes puppets mounted on a wooden machine to revolve by turning the machine. In the same way, the souls are mounted on the machine of the subtle body and ignorance. The address 'Arjuna' refers to his pure and spotless nature.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Having stated that turning away from the war leads to ruin, the Lord now states the impossibility of avoiding the war. The controller is 'īśvaraḥ', Vasudeva, who dwells in the heart-region of all beings, from which all knowledge and actions originate. By His Maya (consisting of three gunas), He causes all beings to spin, i.e., He directs their actions according to their past impressions (vasanas), as if they were puppets mounted on a wooden machine. Thus, you are not independent and will be compelled by His power.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
If it is asked how nature and past actions can compel a person without the command of God, the Lord replies: 'īśvaraḥ...'. The controller, who regulates everything, resides in the heart-region of all beings. By His Maya, He causes all beings to spin, i.e., He directs them while they are mounted on the machine of the body. Just as a puppeteer controls dolls mounted on a wooden machine, the Lord directs them according to their qualities. Therefore, nature and past actions act as facilitators of the Lord's promptings.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
If you ask who this Supreme Being is under whose control you exist, the Lord replies: 'īśvaraḥ...'. The inner Controller, who has the power to rule over all, resides in the heart-cave (intellect) of all living beings. He causes all beings to spin, i.e., to wander through higher and lower paths, as if they were puppets controlled by a puppeteer, mounted on the machine of the subtle body and life-force by His own power of Maya. The address 'Arjuna' (meaning white/pure) implies one who has a pure mind and is fit for divine knowledge.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The Supreme Lord is situated in everyone’s heart, O Arjuna, and is directing the wanderings of all living entities, who are seated as on a machine, made of the material energy.

 Arjuna was not the supreme knower, and his decision to fight or not to fight was confined to his limited discretion. Lord Kṛṣṇa instructed that the individual is not all in all. The Supreme Personality of Godhead, or He Himself, Kṛṣṇa, as the localized Supersoul, sits in the heart directing the living being. After changing bodies, the living entity forgets his past deeds, but the Supersoul, as the knower of the past, present and future, remains the witness of all his activities. Therefore all the activities of living entities are directed by this Supersoul. The living entity gets what he deserves and is carried by the material body, which is created in the material energy under the direction of the Supersoul. As soon as a living entity is placed in a particular type of body, he has to work under the spell of that bodily situation. A person seated in a high-speed motorcar goes faster than one seated in a slower car, though the living entities, the drivers, may be the same. Similarly, by the order of the Supreme Soul, material nature fashions a particular type of body to a particular type of living entity so that he may work according to his past desires. The living entity is not independent. One should not think himself independent of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The individual is always under the Lord’s control. Therefore one’s duty is to surrender, and that is the injunction of the next verse.
