Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
A philosophy that seeks to understand the Totality without fear or prejudice, remaining firm in its objective of seeking Truth, cannot ignore the destructive aspect of Nature. No new creation is possible without the destruction or modification of the material (raw material). Wherever there is existence in the universe, it is but a repetition of change, and when this change is viewed from the standpoint of the created object, it is called creation, and from the standpoint of the material, it is called destruction. Thus we see that the courageous Aryan seers of Hinduism, in praising the beauty of the Supreme Truth, did not view Him merely as the omniscient Creator or the omnipotent Preserver, but also experienced and extolled Him as the all-powerful Destroyer of all names and forms. To those religious creeds where life has not yet been observed and analysed in its entirety, the above statement may appear terrifying. Arjuna's words are meaningful. He does not see the Cosmic Form rising to consume all names and forms, but rather sees those names and forms rapidly entering the mouth of the Cosmic Person. If we look at the ocean, we find that it does not rise from its place to absorb the waves, but the waves themselves, after a brief play, naturally and quickly merge back into the ocean. Similarly, this creation of diversity manifested from Truth will, after its play on the surface of Truth, inevitably merge with rapid speed into its source, the Perfect Person. Arjuna sees the warriors of the Kaurava side, such as Bhishma and Drona, as well as the main warriors of his own side, entering with speed into the yawning mouth of the destructive element of Nature. This sight not only terrifies Arjuna, breaking his courage, but also gives him the confidence to peer into the future. Although the Kauravas were more powerful in terms of troop strength and weapons supply, Arjuna finds reassurance in seeing their destruction. This was a sign of future events. When the Lord manifests in the Cosmic Form, in that concept of oneness, not only does space shrink, but time also becomes a subject of our observation. Therefore, it is no wonder if Arjuna saw the past merging with the present and moving towards the future in that Cosmic Form. Whether I read the first two pages of the entire Gita or skip them to read the third depends on my will. Similarly, when the entire Cosmic Form was present before Arjuna, he could see here, there, and everywhere in one glance, including the past, present, and future. Modern scientists also accept the fact that space and time are actually one, and they manifest in relation to each other. Men of integrity and seekers of truth are not afraid of where their search for truth will lead them. If that truth is terrifying, they accept it too. This world is a mixture of two opposites. In it, beauty and ugliness, good and evil, soft and hard, sweet and bitter, all exist. The Supreme Soul is manifested in all these forms. Therefore, if we accept only the beautiful, auspicious, soft, and sweet aspects of God according to our taste, then this worship of God or this evaluation of Truth cannot be called complete. An unbiased and detached person must recognize the ugly, inauspicious, harsh, and bitter aspects of God. That philosophy alone is complete which makes us realize that although the Supreme Soul is manifested in all these names, forms, and qualities, yet in His transcendental nature, He is entirely beyond all these qualities. Therefore, in a purely scientific manner, Arjuna has to give a detailed description of the Cosmic Form, no matter how terrifying and blood-curdling that description may be. Undoubtedly, there is a sense of reality in the Gita. The mouth of Time has been realistically depicted here as having terrible and frightful fangs. How are they entering? Arjuna says:

Commentary: 'Bhishmo dronah sutaputrastathasau sahasmadiyairapi yodhamukhyaih'—The leading warriors of our side, like Dhrishtadyumna, Virata, Drupada, etc., are all on the side of righteousness and have come to fight solely out of a sense of duty. Along with these commanders of ours, Grandfather Bhishma, Acharya Drona, and the famous Suta-putra Karna are entering into You.
The purpose of naming Bhishma, Drona, and Karna here is that all three of them had come to the battlefield to perform their respective duties.
'Ami cha tvam dhritarashtrasya putrah sarve sahaivavanipalasanghaih'—All the kings on Duryodhana's side, who want to do what is pleasing to Duryodhana in battle (Gita 1.23), i.e., those who are not giving him beneficial advice, along with all the groups of kings, the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra including Duryodhana, Dushasana, etc., are entering with great speed into Your extremely fearsome mouths due to the terrifying fangs—'vaktrani te tvaramanah vishanti damstrakaralani bhayanakani'.
In the Cosmic Form, whether they enter into the Lord or into His mouths, it is the same divine play (Lila). However, according to the feelings, their movements appear different. Therefore, whether they go into the Lord or into His mouths, they are in the Cosmic Form itself.
'Kechidvilagnah dasanantaresu sandrishyante churnitairuttamangaih'—Just as among food items, some go directly into the stomach when chewed, while others get stuck between the teeth and jaws during chewing; similarly, among those entering Your mouths, some are going directly inside (into the stomach), while some are seen stuck between Your teeth and jaws with their heads crushed.
A doubt arises here: the warriors are still standing on the battlefield right now, so how did they appear to Arjuna as entering the mouths of the Cosmic Form? The explanation is that in the Cosmic Form, the Lord is showing Arjuna what will happen in the near future. While showing the Cosmic Form, the Lord had told Arjuna to see whatever else he wished to see in this Cosmic Form (11.7). Arjuna had a doubt in his mind about whether they would win the war or the Kauravas (2.6). Therefore, to dispel that doubt, the Lord shows Arjuna the scene of the imminent future, as if telling him that they alone will win the war. Later, in response to Arjuna's question, the Lord says the same thing (11.32 - 11.34).
Connection: The description of those who have come to the battle with a sense of duty from a righteous perspective and who desire to attain the Supreme Lord, entering the Cosmic Form using the analogy of rivers, is described by Arjuna in the next verse.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Explaining the reason for the occurrence of the meaning of the five verses beginning with 'ami' and its connection with the preceding, he says: 'evam' (thus) etc. Since it is inevitable, it is called 'svamanishitam' (His own resolve). The revelation of the terrifying form is to indicate the removal of the earth's burden according to His resolve, thereby encouraging the war. Arjuna and others are mere instruments; the task is possible even without their efforts, which is why it is said 'to be done by Me alone.' He dispels the doubt of magic or illusion regarding the entrance of the warriors present on the battlefield into the Lord's mouth, which was not physically happening at that moment, by saying 'sa cha parthah' (and that Arjuna) etc. In the realization of the Lord as the creator of everything, the destruction of a few creatures like the sons of Dhritarashtra is not very wonderful; with this intention, he says 'tasminneva' (in that itself) etc. As expressed in 'Sarvam samapnoshi tato'si sarvah' (11.40), the Lord, being the self of all and of true resolve, is the sole cause of the destruction of Dhritarashtra's sons. The Lord, devouring in His extraordinary, supersensuous form, is the primary cause, whereas Arjuna's arrows etc. are merely incidental causes (like the crow and the palm fruit fall). Even though the event is in the future, it is described as present due to realization. Since 'asmadiyaih' (our own) is mentioned separately, the phrase 'avanipalasanghaih' refers to the enemy's side; to express this, he says 'tatpaksiyaih' (belonging to their side). Since all of their side like Duryodhana are to be slain, the word 'sarvaih' (all) is used. By saying 'asmadiyaih', the destruction of warriors of one's own side and the survival of oneself is intended. By using the adjective 'all' for the enemies and 'chief warriors' for one's own side, the non-destruction of all of the Pandava side is indicated by the word 'kaischid' (some). The haste in destruction is due to the haste of their offences; this is intended by the word 'tvaramanah' (hastening). Or rather, all their actions in the battle are for their own destruction. Since 'bhayanakani' is mentioned separately, the word 'karala' here refers to being wide-gapped or deformed, or having projecting teeth. Although no action is specified in 'ami cha tva' (11.21), the connection of action is made through ellipsis. Or the word 'vishanti' should be read here as well. Or the sentence ends with the connection of 'drstva pravyathitantaratmano dhrtim na vindanti', rather than the two verses being intended as a single sentence; because in the previous verse 'tva' (You) is used as the object, and in the next 'vaktrani te' (Your mouths) is said. The reading 'ami sarve dhritarashtrasya putrah' is approved by the Bhashya. That is why it is explained as a single sentence with 'vishanti'. To exclude entry into the Lord for protection, 'vinashaya' (for destruction) is stated. 'Tatra' means among the sons of Dhritarashtra, or rather in those mouths. Some enter for destruction, and some are seen already destroyed. (The commentary further discusses additional verses read by Yadava Prakasha, which are not accepted by other commentators or the Bhashyakara).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'Te' means those Bhishma and others. 'Uttamangaih' means with their heads. The idea here is: the sinful sons of Dhritarashtra are entering into You, who are the body of the three worlds. Corresponding to their sins, they are going to the hells located in the region of Your lower organs, which is why it is said 'they enter You'. On the other hand, Bhishma and others are devotees; hence they enter the Lord's mouth from which fire, Brahmanas, and the Vedas are born. In order to indicate this difference in their destinations, it is appropriate to make the division: the sons of Dhritarashtra enter 'You', whereas Bhishma and others enter 'Your mouths'.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
I see all people rushing full speed into Your mouths, as moths dash to destruction in a blazing fire.

 In a previous verse the Lord promised to show Arjuna things he would be very interested in seeing. Now Arjuna sees that the leaders of the opposite party (Bhīṣma, Droṇa, Karṇa and all the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra) and their soldiers and Arjuna’s own soldiers are all being annihilated. This is an indication that after the death of nearly all the persons assembled at Kurukṣetra, Arjuna will emerge victorious. It is also mentioned here that Bhīṣma, who is supposed to be unconquerable, will also be smashed. So also Karṇa. Not only will the great warriors of the other party like Bhīṣma be smashed, but some of the great warriors of Arjuna’s side also.
