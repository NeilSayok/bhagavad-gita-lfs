Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ātmaupamyena sarvatra samaṃ paśyati yo.arjuna .
sukhaṃ vā yadi vā duḥkhaṃ sa yogī paramo mataḥ

Word Meanings:
आत्मौपम्येन - by comparison with oneself
सर्वत्र - everywhere
समं - equal
पश्यति - sees
योऽर्जुन - who, O Arjuna
सुखं - happily
वा - or
यदि - if
वा - or
दुःखं - sorrow
स - he
योगी - yogi
परमो - supreme
मतः - is regarded.

Life Application:
Treat the joy and sorrow of others as your own, reaching the highest standard of spiritual empathy.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He who sees equality everywhere by analogy with himself, O Arjuna, whether in pleasure or in pain, he is considered the highest Yogi.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
O Arjuna, he who by analogy with himself beholds absolute equality in all beings—feeling others" joy as his own joy and others" pain as his own pain, working for the welfare of all without causing harm—he is considered the supreme and highest Yogi among all.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "By analogy with oneself"—perceiving Lord Vishnu indwelling all beings and maintaining equal empathy toward their joy and sorrow is the supreme Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Just as pleasure is desirable and pain undesirable for oneself, so the yogi who holds universal compassion and non-injury toward all beings is supreme.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: As one cares for one"s own joy and sorrow, so he who considers the joy and sorrow of all beings as his own, abiding in non-violence, is highest.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He whose heart is harmonized by Yoga sees the Self abiding in all beings and all beings abiding in the Self, beholding the same everywhere. He who sees Me everywhere and sees all in Me, to him I am never lost, nor is he ever lost to Me. The Yogi who, established in unity, worships Me dwelling in all beings, lives in Me whatever be his mode of life. O Arjuna, he who sees equality in all beings by analogy with himself, whether in pleasure or in pain, is regarded as the highest Yogi.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He who sees equality everywhere by analogy with himself, O Arjuna, whether in pleasure or in pain, he is considered the highest Yogi"—establishes universal compassion (*Sarva-bhuta-hita*) and Ahimsa as the ethical culmination of Vedantic realization.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Regarding all beings as dear as one"s own self is the root of the enlightened sage"s universal benevolence.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Beholding Krishna"s form in all creatures and loving them as oneself is the highest spiritual perfection.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "He is considered the highest yogi"—spontaneous non-injury and universal compassion are the natural characteristics of an Advaita sage.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of self-analogy in universal empathy and declaring such a sage as the highest yogi.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Realizing equal conscious nature in all souls and harming none is the supreme Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When a devotee loves all creatures seeing Lord Krishna"s presence within them, he becomes the highest yogi.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "By analogy with oneself"—feeling others" pain as one"s own and showing mercy is the crown jewel mark of all yogis.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
He is a perfect yogi who, by comparison to his own self, sees the true equality of all beings, in both their happiness and their distress, O Arjuna!
