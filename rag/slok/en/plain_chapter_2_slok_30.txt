Life Application:
Remind yourself that every person possesses an indestructible divine spark, inspiring universal respect and compassion.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He introduces another verse with 'atha...' -- that is, after having shown the Self's difficulty of comprehension. The purport of this topic is that grief and delusion are not to be indulged in, from the standpoint of the true state of things. He states the resulting conclusion that the embodied Self is not slain even though the body is being slain, with 'yasmat...' He divides the reason-portion with 'sarvasya...' He explains the half-verse that shows the resulting meaning, with 'tasmat bhishmadini...'

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, born in the line of Bharata! This embodied Self is ever indestructible in the bodies of all. Therefore, you ought not to grieve for all beings, that is, for any being whatsoever.

Explanation -- 'dehi nityam avadhyo'yam dehe sarvasya bharata' -- in the bodies of all beings, moving and unmoving alike -- humans, gods, animals, birds, insects, and the rest -- this embodied Self is ever 'avadhya,' that is, imperishable.

The word 'avadhya' has two meanings: (1) it ought not to be slain, and (2) it cannot possibly be slain. For example, the cow is avadhya, meaning the cow should never, under any circumstance, be killed, since killing a cow is a grave sin. But in the case of the embodied Self, it is not merely that 'It ought not to be slain' -- rather, this embodied Self can never be slain by any means whatsoever, nor can anyone slay It -- 'none can bring about the destruction of this imperishable one' (2.17).

'tasmat sarvani bhutani na tvam socitum arhasi' -- therefore you ought not to grieve for any being whatsoever; because this embodied Self can never be destroyed, and the perishable body does not remain stable even for an instant. Here the plural 'sarvani bhutani' conveys that no being at all should be left out -- that is, grief should not be felt for any being at all.

The body is indeed perishable, since its very nature is to perish; it is being destroyed every single moment. But one's own eternal true nature is never destroyed at all. If this truth is realized, grief becomes altogether impossible.

A note on this section -- The topic spanning verses 11 through 30 exists specifically to establish the discrimination between the embodied Self and the body -- the eternal and the transient, the real and the unreal, the imperishable and the perishable. For until one discriminates that the embodied Self is distinct and the body is distinct, no path -- karma-yoga, jnana-yoga, bhakti-yoga, or any other -- can be practiced. All theistic philosophers, whether Advaitin or Dvaitin or of any other school, accept this distinction between the embodied Self and the body; here the Lord wishes to make this distinction clear. What the Lord has said in this section is, for the most part, a matter of universal human experience -- the body changes, but the embodied Self does not change. The Lord has, quite deliberately, not used philosophical terms such as Atman-anatman, Brahman-jiva, Prakriti-Purusha, jada-chetana, Maya-avidya in this section, since people tend to treat philosophical terms merely as subjects for study, not for direct realization. Whoever truly discriminates between these two can never feel the slightest grief; whoever merely learns philosophical propositions does not find their grief removed.

Connection -- Arjuna's mind held both grief over the death of his kinsmen and fear of the sin of slaying his elders. To remove Arjuna's grief, the Lord has given this section from verse 11 to verse 30; now, to remove Arjuna's fear, He begins the next section on the duty of a kshatriya.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Now he concludes the means for removing the delusion common to all beings -- even though the body of every creature is being slain, this embodied one, that is, the Self conditioned by the subtle body, is not slain. 'Nityam' means fixed, certain. Hence, even referring to all beings, gross and subtle, that have taken the form of Bhishma and the rest, you ought not to grieve. The gross body is not to be grieved over because it is unavoidable; the subtle body is not to be grieved over because, like the Self, it too is indestructible. Neither the gross body, nor the subtle body, nor the Self is a fit object of grief -- this is the sense.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Just as, among beings ranging from gods down to immovable things, inequality is observed in their bodily portion -- in the ease or difficulty of destruction according to species, quality, place, and time -- so too inequality of happiness, sorrow, and the like is seen in the embodied Self as well. The word 'devadi' extends up to the individual self qualified by divinity and so on. Thus, a doubt might arise that inequality could also apply to characteristics such as eternality and non-eternality; to remove this doubt, he says 'dehi...' The phrase 'vadhyamane'pi' is supplied by implication, as in 'hanyamane sarire' (2.20). Otherwise, saying 'dehe sarvasya' would be pointless, since the mere word 'dehi' already establishes residence in a body. Here the word 'bhuta' extends up to the individual soul (kshetrajna). The reason for the doubt indicated by 'sarvani' and the rest is restated with 'visamakarani api' -- that is, on account of the differences among gods and so forth, and the differences in happiness and the like caused by them. 'By the nature already stated' means by the previously mentioned subtlety, uncuttable nature, and so on. 'Nityani ca' means that no doubt of inequality regarding eternality versus non-eternality should be entertained. 'Dehagatam tu vaisamyam' -- the inequality here refers only to the differing arrangements of gods and so forth as located in the body; the inequality of happiness and the like, even within the Self, arises as gradations in the particular states of adventitious knowledge conditioned by each respective body. The designation of conscious beings by terms like 'body' rests merely on the non-separate existence of the body -- this is the sense. To show the connection with the current topic, he states what is excluded by the word 'sarvani' -- 'not merely toward Bhishma and the rest.' Thus, beginning from 'asocyan anvasocas tvam' (2.11), the topic concluding that you ought not to grieve is here brought to its close.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Concluding the topic stated above, he teaches the absence of grief -- with 'dehi...' The embodied one is indestructible in everyone's body. Thus, all beings assume bodies, but the embodied Self (the Atman) is never truly born -- hence it is not fitting for you to grieve. By addressing him as 'Bharata,' it is conveyed that your grief arises from ignorance.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He concludes the topic under discussion -- with 'dehi...' 'How will these poor, weak ones be slain by me, who am strong? Without them, how will their sons and others survive?  The Lord now concludes the chapter of instruction on the immutable spirit soul. In describing the immortal soul in various ways, Lord Kṛṣṇa establishes that the soul is immortal and the body is temporary. Therefore Arjuna as a kṣatriya should not abandon his duty out of fear that his grandfather and teacher – Bhīṣma and Droṇa – will die in the battle. On the authority of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, one has to believe that there is a soul different from the material body, not that there is no such thing as soul, or that living symptoms develop at a certain stage of material maturity resulting from the interaction of chemicals. Though the soul is immortal, violence is not encouraged, but at the time of war it is not discouraged when there is actual need for it. That need must be justified in terms of the sanction of the Lord, and not capriciously.
