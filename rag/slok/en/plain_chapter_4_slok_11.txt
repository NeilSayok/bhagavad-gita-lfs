Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃstathaiva bhajāmyaham .
mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ

Word Meanings:
ये - who
यथा - as
मां - Me
प्रपद्यन्ते - surrender / approach
तांस्तथैव - them in the same way
भजाम्यहम् - I reward / accept
मम - my
वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते - follow the path
मनुष्याः - men
पार्थ - O son of Pritha
सर्वशः - in all ways

Life Application:
Respect the diverse ways people seek truth and divine connection, knowing that sincere devotion is always answered.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
In whatever manner or mood devotees approach and surrender unto Me, even so do I reward and respond to them accordingly; for all human beings, O Partha, follow My cosmic path in all respects.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Even so do I reward them"—Lord Vishnu is completely impartial, bestowing Moksha or material rewards according to each soul"s devotion and eligibility.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The Lord is free from partiality, bestowing either Mukti or worldly fruits upon seekers strictly according to their underlying disposition.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: In whatever spirit (seeking Mukti or material rewards) devotees surrender to Me, I reveal Myself and grant fruits corresponding to their devotion.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 4.11–4.12 jointly): In whatever manner men approach Me, I reward them accordingly; those seeking immediate success in actions worship lesser deities here, obtaining temporary rewards.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha"—clarifies that God is unattached and impartial (*Raga-dvesa-rahita*), granting results according to each seeker"s intent.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: God is the ultimate refuge of all beings; both desireless and desire-driven worshippers receive results matching their disposition.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Lord Krishna responds to the mood of His devotees; in whatever form one adores the Lord, He bestows His divine bliss in that very form.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Even so do I reward them"—though Ishvara dispenses karmic fruits, He remains spotless and equal, rewarding seekers strictly per their intent.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
In whatever way men approach Me, even so do I reward them; My path do men tread in all ways, O Partha.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how the Lord grants fruits corresponding to the specific desire and form through which He is worshipped.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Vasudeva is the sole bestower of fruits; He grants liberation to the salvation-seeker and material goods to the worldly practitioner.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Lord Krishna"s mercy is non-discriminatory; He surrenders Himself to the devotee according to the intensity of divine love.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Even so do I reward them"—God, like a wish-fulfilling tree (Kalpavriksha), bestows fruits matching the practitioner"s faith.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly. Everyone follows My path in all respects, O son of Prtha.
