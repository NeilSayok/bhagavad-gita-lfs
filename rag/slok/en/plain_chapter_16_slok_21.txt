Life Application:
Triple is the gate of hell, destructive of the self: lust, anger, and greed; therefore one should abandon these three.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The gates of hell are three: lust, wrath and avarice. They destroy the Self. Avoid them.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Triple is the gate of this hell, destructive of the self — lust, anger and greed; therefore one should abandon these three.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that heaven and sky are states of mind that can be experienced here in this life. Hell is a state of extreme agitation and sorrow. The three gates to this hell are desire (kama), anger (krodha), and greed (lobha). Constant contemplation on sense-objects leads to desire. When desire is obstructed, anger arises, which destroys intellectual capacity. If desire is fulfilled, it leads to greed and endless dissatisfaction, poisoning peace of mind. Since these three are closely linked, they constitute the highway to hellish existence and must be abandoned.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
To the hell, three-fold is the gate that ruins the Self : [They are]  desire, anger as well as greed.  Hence one should avoid these three.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Desire, wrath and greed - this is the triple gateway to Naraka, ruinous to the self. Therefore one should abandon these three.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
This door of hell, which is the destroyer of the soul, is of three kinds-passion, anger and also greed. Therefore one should forsake these three.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that although Asuri traits are infinite, they are all rooted in these three. By abandoning lust, anger, and greed, one effectively rejects the entire demoniac nature, paving the way for liberation.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Lust, anger and greed — these three gates of hell lead to the ruin of the soul; therefore, one must renounce these three.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that all Asuri tendencies are born from the desire for worldly pleasures and accumulation. Lust is the desire for sensual enjoyment, and greed is the desire for accumulating wealth and materials. When either of these is obstructed, anger arises. These three are the primary causes of a soul's degradation. Even if they appear to bring temporary worldly pleasure, they destroy the soul's ability to act for its spiritual welfare, leading it to hell. Renouncing them means not acting under their influence and keeping life aligned with scriptural discipline.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Those three which constitute the 'gateway of this hell' in the shape of demoniac nature, and are destructive of the self (Atman) - are known as desire, wrath and greed. The nature of these has already been explained. 'Gateway' (Dvara) means the path, the cause. Therefore, one should renounce these three. Therefore, since they constitute the cause of the extremely dreadful Naraka, one should wholly renounce this triad - desire, wrath and greed.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Idam, this; dvaram, door; narakasya, of hell-for entering it; which is the nasanam, destroyer; atmanah, of the soul; is trividham of three kinds. It is that by the mere entry into which the soul perishes, i.e., it ceases to be fit for attaining any human goal. hence it is said that it is the door which is the destroyer of the soul. Which is that? Kamah, passion; krodhah, anger; and also lobhah, greed. Tasmat, therefore; tyajet, one shoud forsake; etat trayam, these three. Since this door is the destroyer of the soul, therefore one should renounce this group of three-passion etc. This is a eulogy of renunciation.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that desire, wrath, and greed are the three gates of demoniac existence. Krishna gives this beneficial instruction to abandon them to prevent the self's destruction.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that although Asuri qualities are infinite, they can be summarized into these three. By abandoning desire, anger, and greed, the entire demoniac nature is abandoned. Abandonment means preventing their expression through discrimination and avoiding their future arising.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that among all demoniac defects, these three are the root causes and must be avoided at all costs because they lead the soul to lower species.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that entering these doors ruins the soul's ability to achieve any human goals (purusharthas). Therefore, anyone seeking welfare must abandon desire, anger, and greed.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the ruin of the self means failing to achieve the true nature of the soul (which is service and devotion to God). Because they lead to the most dreadful hell of demoniac existence, one must abandon them completely.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that desire means seeking personal pleasure, anger is heat of the heart without reason, and greed is seeking others' properties which destroys all qualities. One must avoid them to save the self.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the endless demoniac attributes are summarized into this triad. They destroy the soul's capacity for right exertion.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
There are three gates leading to this hell – lust, anger and greed. Every sane man should give these up, for they lead to the degradation of the soul.

 The beginning of demoniac life is described herein. One tries to satisfy his lust, and when he cannot, anger and greed arise. A sane man who does not want to glide down to the species of demoniac life must try to give up these three enemies, which can kill the self to such an extent that there will be no possibility of liberation from this material entanglement.
