Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
arjuna uvāca .
dṛṣṭvedaṃ mānuṣaṃ rūpaṃ tava saumyaṃ janārdana .
idānīmasmi saṃvṛttaḥ sacetāḥ prakṛtiṃ gataḥ

Word Meanings:
दृष्ट्वा - having seen
इदम् - this
मानुषम् - human
रूपम् - form
तव - Thy
सौम्यम् - gentle
जनार्दन - O Krishna
इदानीम् - now
अस्मि - I am
संवृत्तः - composed
सचेताः - with mind
प्रकृतिम् - to nature
गतः - restored.

Life Application:
Feel your mind regain its composure and natural state upon beholding the serene, gentle human form of God.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Arjuna said - O Janardana! Having seen this gentle human form of Yours, I have now become calm in mind and am restored to my own nature.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Arjuna said  Having seen this Thy gentle human form, O Krishna, now I am composed and am restored to my own nature.

दृष्ट्वा having seen? इदम् this? मानुषम् human? रूपम् form? तव Thy? सौम्यम् gentle? जनार्दन O Krishna? इदानीम् now? अस्मि (I) am? संवृत्तः composed? सचेताः with mind? प्रकृतिम् to nature? गतः restored.Commentary Arjuna says to Lord Krishna O Lord? I now behold Thy human form. Now my thoughts are collected and I am serene. I can speak. My senses perform their proper functions. My fear has vanished. Thou hast treated me as a mother would treat her erring child whom she embraces and nurses. Just as the calf rejoices when it beholds its mother who was missing for some days? so also I rejoice now when I behold Thy gentle form. I have drunk the nectar. Now I am alive.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Arjuna said: Seeing Thee in Thy gentle human form, my Lord, I am myself again, calm once more.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Due to the lack of necessary preparation to perceive and experience the object beyond time and space, it was natural that Arjuna was overcome with fear and delusion upon suddenly seeing such a vast cosmic Vishwarupa. But here he admits that upon seeing the calm, gentle human form of the Lord, he has become peaceful in mind and has regained his normal nature. Now the Lord Himself describes the devotion to God in the next verse.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Arjuna said  On seeing this gentle human form of Yours, O Janardana, now I have regained my nature and have now collected my thinking faculty.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Arjuna said  Having behold the human and pleasing form of Yours, O Krsna, I have now become composed in mind and I am restored to my normal nature.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Arjuna said  O Janardana, having seen this serene human form of Yours, I have now become calm in mind and restored to my own nature.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Thus, being comforted by the Lord, Arjuna spoke back to Him; this is introduced by [the words] 'Arjuna [said]'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna said - O Janardana! Having seen this gentle human form of Yours, I have now become calm in mind and am restored to my own natural state.

Commentary - 'drstvaidam manusam rupam tava saumyam janardana' - On seeing the form performing divine plays manifested in Your human form, even cows, animals, birds, trees, creepers, etc., become thrilled with joy (Footnote p. 613). On seeing such a gentle two-armed form, I have regained my senses, my mind has become steady - 'idanim asmi samvrttah sacetah'. The fear that had gripped me upon seeing the cosmic form has now vanished, all distress has gone, and I have attained my real state - 'prakrtim gatah'. Here, the purpose of saying 'sacetah' (with mind/conscious) is that when Arjuna's attention went towards the Lord's grace, he regained consciousness and began to think: where am I, and where is the Lord's astonishing and extraordinary cosmic form! I have no eligibility or right in this. In this, there is only the grace, and grace alone, of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Arjuna said  Having beheld this pleasing and unie form of Yours, human in configuration, endowed with grace, tenderness, beauty etc., the excellence of which is infinite, I have now become composed, and I am restored to my normal nature.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Drstva etc.  At the end of the act of withdrawing all, the Brahman assumes the highly tranil stage of the tattva.  Hence at the stage of withdrawl, gentleness is in the  Bhagavat.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
O Janardana, drstva, having seen; idam, this; saumyam, serene; manusam, human; rupam, form; tava, of Yours-gracious, as of my friend; asmi, I have; idanim, now; samvrttah, become;-what?-sacetah, calm in mind; and gatah, restored; prakrtim, to my own nature.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Then, having returned to his normal state, Arjuna said: 'drstva idam...' On seeing this gentle human form situated in human configuration, which is endowed with boundless and unsurpassed beauty, tenderness, grace, and good disposition, and whose form of hands, feet, face, stomach, etc. consists of pure eternal bliss, I have become composed in mind. At that time (during the cosmic form), however, I was distracted (without mind), as per the statements of formlessness and imperishability. Now, indeed, I have attained my own normal nature.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Then, being free from fear, Arjuna said: 'drstva...' Now I have become composed in mind, meaning free from agitation due to the absence of delusion caused by fear. Likewise, I have returned to my normal nature, meaning I have regained my composure/health due to the absence of distress caused by fear. The rest is clear.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Then, being free from fear, Arjuna said: 'drstva...' I have now become composed, meaning pleased in mind. I have returned to my normal nature and regained composure. The rest is clear.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having seen the desired form, Arjuna said: Having seen this directly visible, human-shaped, friendly, gentle, and cheerful form of Yours, I have now become composed, meaning pleased in mind, and have returned to my normal nature, meaning my natural state. By addressing Him as 'Janardana'—which means one who afflicts or destroys the bad people (demons)—he indicates that the fear produced by the vision of the cosmic form has now been removed by the sight of this gentle form.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
To dispel the delusion that 'manusam rupam' (human form) refers to a material/ordinary body born of karma, he explains the significance of the word 'idam' (this) by stating 'boundless and unsurpassed beauty, etc.' [in reference to Sri Ramanuja's commentary].

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having seen the manifested form, Arjuna submits: 'drstva...' O Janardana, Destroyer of ignorance! Having seen this gentle and compassionate form of Yours, which is visible before me and fit to be seen by humans, I have now become composed, meaning attentive/alert in mind. And I have attained my nature, meaning the state of devotion.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Then, being free from fear, Arjuna said: 'drstva...' Sacetah means free from agitation. Prakrtim gatah means attained composure/health. Samvrttah means I have become.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When Arjuna thus saw Kṛṣṇa in His original form, he said: O Janārdana, seeing this humanlike form, so very beautiful, I am now composed in mind, and I am restored to my original nature.

 Here the words mānuṣaṁ rūpam clearly indicate the Supreme Personality of Godhead to be originally two-handed. Those who deride Kṛṣṇa as if He were an ordinary person are shown here to be ignorant of His divine nature. If Kṛṣṇa is like an ordinary human being, then how is it possible for Him to show the universal form and again to show the four-handed Nārāyaṇa form? So it is very clearly stated in Bhagavad-gītā that one who thinks that Kṛṣṇa is an ordinary person and who misguides the reader by claiming that it is the impersonal Brahman within Kṛṣṇa speaking is doing the greatest injustice. Kṛṣṇa has actually shown His universal form and His four-handed Viṣṇu form. So how can He be an ordinary human being? A pure devotee is not confused by misguiding commentaries on Bhagavad-gītā because he knows what is what. The original verses of Bhagavad-gītā are as clear as the sun; they do not require lamplight from foolish commentators.
