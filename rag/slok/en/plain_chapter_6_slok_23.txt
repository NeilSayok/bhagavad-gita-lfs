Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
taṃ vidyād duḥkhasaṃyogaviyogaṃ yogasaṃjñitam .
sa niścayena yoktavyo yogo.anirviṇṇacetasā

Word Meanings:
तं - that
विद्याद् - one should know
दुःखसंयोगवियोगं - disconnection from union with sorrow
योगसंज्ञितम् - named yoga
स - he
निश्चयेन - with determination
योक्तव्यो - should be practiced
योगोऽनिर्विण्णचेतसा - yoga with cheerful mind

Life Application:
Practice meditation with unyielding determination and hope, breaking free from all association with pain and suffering.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Dis-union with pain"—direct realization of Lord Vishnu"s supreme bliss is the absolute end of all suffering (Yoga), to be accomplished through unwavering devotion.

Sri Ramanuja states: Know that state free from all contact of pain to be Yoga; practice it with steady conviction and a cheerful mind, unwearied by obstacles.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: When the mind, restrained by the practice of Yoga, attains absolute quietude, and when beholding the Self by the self, one rests satisfied within the Self alone; when one experiences that boundless joy which transcends the senses and is grasped solely by purified intellect, and established in which one never swerves from the Truth; obtaining which one regards no other gain as superior, and anchored in which one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow—know that severance from union with pain to be called Yoga. That Yoga should be practiced with unshakeable resolve and an undaunted heart.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Let that be known by the name of Yoga, the severance of union with pain. That Yoga should be practiced with determination and an un-depressed heart"—formulates the profound Vedantic definition of Yoga as *Duhkha-samyoga-viyoga* (dis-union from union with pain) and specifies the attitude required: resolute conviction (*Nischaya*) and undaunted spirit (*Anirvinna-chetas*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Indecision and mental depression are two great enemies of meditation; hence Yoga must be pursued with firm resolve and cheerful heart.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
