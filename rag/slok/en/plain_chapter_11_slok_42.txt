Life Application:
Implore pardon for any disrespect shown in jest, while resting, sitting, or dining together in informal moments.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When an ordinary person suddenly gets acquainted with the majesty of the Supreme Lord, the emotions that naturally arise in his mind are brought out with dramatic realism in these two beautiful verses. Until now, Arjuna did not consider Lord Krishna to be anything more than a wise cowherd, whom he had generously patronized with his royal friendship. But now, having obtained a true introduction to the infinite form of Krishna, the individual soul (Jivabhava) in Arjuna bows down before Him with firm devotion and respect, begging for mercy and forgiveness. There is a touch of extreme intimacy in these two verses. Here, intellectual philosophical reflection is beautifully blended with the emotional aspect of close familiarity. The purpose of the Gita itself is to make the sweet music of Vedic truths harmonize with the pleasant rhythm of the practical world. Through these emotional touches of close familiarity, Vyasa’s skillful pen suddenly brings the sublime, thought-provoking truths of Vedanta into the familiar atmosphere of a living room conversation. As a close friend, either due to carelessness or out of love, Arjuna, not knowing the glory of Lord Krishna, must have addressed Him by affectionate names, for which he now begs forgiveness from Him. Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
It is not only that whatever improper words were spoken should be forgiven, but also the disrespect shown to You for the sake of jest during play etc., should be tolerated - this is stated by 'yacca' (and whatever). Viharana (walking/recreation) means play or physical exercise. Shayanam (resting) means bed etc. Asanam (sitting) is illustrative of a seat, throne, etc. That is to say, while engaged in these activities. The word 'ekah' denotes being alone in privacy, hence it is explained as 'being out of sight of others'. Accepting the connection that 'whatever disrespect or slight was shown to You by me either in public or in private, all that...', it is explained by the word 'tat'. For requesting forgiveness, His immeasurableness is stated as the reason by the word 'aprameyam' (the incomprehensible/limitless).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Not knowing Your greatness and true nature, and thinking 'You are my friend', whatever I have said rashly (without thinking) either out of carelessness or even out of love, addressing You as 'O Krishna! O Yadava! O Friend!'; and O Achyuta! whatever disrespect has been shown to You by me in jest, while walking or playing, while sleeping or waking, while sitting or standing, or while eating, in private or in the presence of those friends, relatives etc. - for all that, I implore forgiveness from You, who are immeasurable.

Commentary: [When Arjuna is frightened upon seeing the extremely fierce form of the Cosmic Lord, he forgets the Krishna-form of the Lord and asks, 'Who are You in this fierce form?' But when he remembers that this is Lord Krishna Himself, then seeing the Lord's power etc., he remembers his past behavior of friendship and begs the Lord for forgiveness.]

'sakheti matva prasabham yaduktam he krsna he yadava he sakheti'—Those who are great, superior persons, are not addressed directly by name. For them, words like 'You' (respectful 'Aap'), 'Maharaj' (Your Majesty) etc. are used. But I sometimes called You 'O Krishna', sometimes 'O Yadava', and sometimes 'O Friend'. What was the reason for this? 'ajanata mahimanam tavedam' - The reason was that I did not know such greatness and form of Yours, that You are so extraordinary. That infinite millions of universes reside in a single part of Yours - this I did not know before. My sight did not even go towards Your power. I never thought or understood who You are and how You are.

Although Arjuna knew the Lord's form, greatness, and power even before, which is why he rejected an Akshauhini (division) of army and chose the unarmed Lord; yet, that infinite millions of universes are situated in a single part of the Lord's body - such power, form, and greatness Arjuna did not know before. When the Lord mercifully showed the Cosmic Form, only after seeing it did Arjuna's attention go towards the Lord's power, and he began to know the Lord to some extent. He got this strange feeling: 'Where am I, and where is this God of gods! Yet, due to carelessness or out of love, rashly, without thinking, whatever came to my mind, I said - 'maya pramadat pranayena vapi'. I did not exercise any caution while speaking!'

In reality, no one can ever fully know the Lord's glory, because the Lord's glory is infinite. If it could be fully known, it would no longer be infinite; it would become limited. When there is no end even to the opulences arising from the Lord's power, how can there be an end to the Lord and His glory? That is, there can be no end.

'yaccavahasarthamasatkrto'si viharasayyasanbhojanesu'—Considering You as an equal, ordinary friend, whatever insulting words I spoke, or disrespect I showed You while joking, while walking along the path, while sleeping or waking on the bed, while sitting or rising from the seat, or while eating; or, O Achyuta, whether You were alone at that time or in the presence of those friends, family members, civilized people etc., whatever disrespect I have shown to You - for all that, I implore forgiveness from You - 'eko'thavapyacyuta tatsamaksam tatksamaye tvamahamaprameyam'.

Such is the description of the friendship between Arjuna and the Lord that just as two friends play together, similarly Arjuna used to play with the Lord. Sometimes while bathing, Arjuna would splash water on the Lord and the Lord on Arjuna. Sometimes Arjuna would run after the Lord, and sometimes the Lord after Arjuna. Sometimes they would make each other laugh. Sometimes they would show each other their special skills. Sometimes if the Lord fell asleep, Arjuna would say, 'You have spread out so much to sleep, will no one else sleep? Are you the only one here?' Sometimes if the Lord sat on a seat, Arjuna would say, 'Will you sit on the seat alone? Will you let anyone else sit or not? You have established sole domain! Slide over a bit.' In this way, Arjuna behaved with the Lord with extreme familiarity. Now Arjuna, remembering those things, says, 'O Lord! I do not know how many times I have disrespected You. I do not even remember all of them. Although You did not pay attention to my disrespect, yet many insults were committed by me, therefore I implore forgiveness for all disrespect from You, who are immeasurable.' The significance of calling the Lord 'aprameya' is that even with divine vision, You do not fall within the scope of divine vision.

, Connection—Now in the next two verses, Arjuna describes the greatness and influence of the Lord and prays again for forgiveness.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
To show that explaining 'ekah' as 'paroksa' (indirectly/in private) is a far-fetched interpretation, its meaning and syntactic connection are shown—'ekah' etc. By taking the initial letter, the letter 'e' indicates 'eka' (the one). The letter 'ka' indicates 'karayitrtva' (the status of being the cause/prompter of action). This is derived from the root 'kr' containing an implied causative sense, with the suffix 'da'. Even so, though You are totally unworthy of disrespect, You have been disrespected.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Furthermore, having previously regarded You as possessed of Tamoguna (quality of darkness) because You were engaged in Tamasic activities like hunting, sport, etc., whatever disrespect was shown to You by me - for all that, I, being alone, now beg forgiveness of You.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
And whatever disrespect was shown for the sake of fun, during recreation, on bed, seat, and meals; recreation (vihara) means play or physical exercise, bed (shayya) refers to a cushioned mattress etc., seat (asana) means a throne etc., and meals (bhojana) means eating in a gathering; during these activities, You were disrespected, i.e., insulted by me. 'Ekah' means when You were alone in privacy, having left behind Your friends, or 'tatsamaksam', i.e., directly in the presence of those mocking friends. O Achyuta (eternally changeless)! I ask for forgiveness for that entire group of offences consisting of verbal insults and disrespectful behavior. From You, who are immeasurable, i.e., of inconceivable power. The meaning is that my offences, committed as I was ignorant of Your greatness, ought to be forgiven by the Lord who is of inconceivable glory, changeless, and supremely merciful.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Moreover - 'yacca' (and whatever). O Achyuta, whatever disrespect was shown to You in jest during play, etc. 'Ekah' means alone, i.e., when situated in privacy without friends; or 'tatsamaksam', i.e., even in front of those mocking friends, for all that multitude of offences, I beg forgiveness of You, who are immeasurable, of inconceivable glory.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
And whatever disrespect was shown for the purpose of jest, i.e., You were slighted. Recreation (vihara) means play or exercise, bed (sayya) refers to lying down, seat (asana) means a sitting place like a throne, and meals (bhojana) means eating. During these activities, 'disrespect' (asatkaranam) means a lower person behaving as an equal with a superior person. 'Ekah' means in private, where You were disrespected, or 'samaksam', i.e., directly in public; whatever disrespect and slight was shown by me, either in private or in public, for all that multitude of offences, I beg forgiveness of You. Earlier, thinking 'He is my cousin (maternal uncle's son)' and thus treating You as an equal and disrespecting You, now knowing You to be immeasurable, I ask for forgiveness—with this intent he says 'aprameyam' (the immeasurable). It should not be thought, 'Since I was disrespected, I will not forgive,' because even when disrespected, You never slip from Your supreme position - conveying this, he addresses Him as 'O Achyuta' (the infallible/changeless).

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The word 'mahimanam' (greatness) qualified by the word 'idam' (this) recapitulates the majesty under discussion; with this intent, it is explained as—'that majesty which consists in infinite power, boundless valour, being the Inner Self of all, being the creator etc., being ignorant of this by me...'. If the word 'pramada' (carelessness) is taken in the sense of ignorance, there would be redundancy with 'ajanata' (unknowing); therefore, implying the delusion of regarding You as an equal, it is explained as 'pramadat mohat' (due to delusion). Since friendship is the natural cause of familiarity, although the word 'pranaya' means love, it is appropriate to interpret it here as the long-standing familiarity conducive to it—with this intent, it is explained as 'pranayena ciraparicayena'. Since the spiritual friendship established by Upanishadic texts like 'sayuja sakhaya...' cannot be produced by delusion, the phrase 'sakheti matva' (thinking You to be a friend) refers to the worldly conception of companionship—with this intent, it is explained as 'mama vayasya iti matva' (thinking You to be my companion). The address 'O Krishna' etc. shows the nature of rash speaking; with this intent, showing the connection, he indicates the lack of modesty appropriate to the occasion, saying 'he krsna' etc. In 'yacca', to establish the multiplicity of disrespect implied by the word 'ca' (and), accepting a division of sentences by repeating 'yad asatkrto'si', the meaning is explained by 'yacca parihasartham' etc. For that reason alone, its multiplicity is stated by 'tat sarvam' (all that). Disrespect, even for the sake of jest, towards great personalities is indeed an offence; with this intent, the meaning implied by the second person is stated as—'You are always worthy of respect'. The meaning resulting from the word 'ekah' is 'in privacy/solitude'.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
And further, thinking You to be a friend, for the sake of jest with teasing words etc.—such as 'leaving me behind, You are wandering around alone' etc. Or in my presence during recreation such as gambling, hunting etc., displaying intimacy; by sleeping together on a bed, sitting together on a seat, eating together at meals etc., whatever disrespect or insult was shown to You, O Achyuta (the infallible Lord, who protects those accepted as His own)! I request forgiveness for all that from You, who are immeasurable (unfathomable). By His being immeasurable, the forgiveness of offences born of ignorance is indicated.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
And also whatever disrespect was shown to You in jest during recreation etc., i.e., You were slighted; either in private, i.e., in the absence of friends, or in their presence, i.e., in front of the circle of friends, You were disrespected—for all that I ask for forgiveness. Because You are immeasurable, of inconceivable nature, and compassionate. The meaning is that You have granted the supreme destination even to enemies like Shishupala and others.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Of cheats, I am the gambling of cheats, and I am the brilliance of the brilliant. I am victory, I am endeavor, and I am the strength of the strong.

 Although Kṛṣṇa is manifested before Arjuna in His universal form, Arjuna remembers his friendly relationship with Kṛṣṇa and is therefore asking pardon and requesting Kṛṣṇa to excuse him for the many informal gestures which arise out of friendship. He is admitting that formerly he did not know that Kṛṣṇa could assume such a universal form, although Kṛṣṇa explained it as his intimate friend. Arjuna did not know how many times he may have dishonored Kṛṣṇa by addressing Him “O my friend,” “O Kṛṣṇa,” “O Yādava,” etc., without acknowledging His opulence. But Kṛṣṇa is so kind and merciful that in spite of such opulence He played with Arjuna as a friend. Such is the transcendental loving reciprocation between the devotee and the Lord. The relationship between the living entity and Kṛṣṇa is fixed eternally; it cannot be forgotten, as we can see from the behavior of Arjuna. Although Arjuna has seen the opulence in the universal form, he cannot forget his friendly relationship with Kṛṣṇa.
