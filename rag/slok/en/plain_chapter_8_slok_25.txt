Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
dhūmo rātristathā kṛṣṇaḥ ṣaṇmāsā dakṣiṇāyanam .
tatra cāndramasaṃ jyotiryogī prāpya nivartate

Word Meanings:
धूमो - smoke
रात्रिस्तथा - night and
कृष्णः - Krishna
षण्मासा - six months
दक्षिणायनम् - of southern course
तत्र - there
चान्द्रमसं - lunar
ज्योतिर्योगी - light the yogi
प्राप्य - attaining
निवर्तते - returns

Life Application:
Recognize the dark path of smoke, night, and southern sun, by which the practitioner attains lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern solstice—thereby departing, the yogi attains the lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern solstice—thereby departing, the yogi attains the lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Smoke, night-time, the dark fortnight of the moon, and the six months of the southern solstice—departing by this pitriyana path, the fruitive worker reaches the lunar sphere and returns to earth upon merit exhaustion.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern solstice—thereby departing, the yogi attains the lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya did not provide a separate commentary on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: This Southern Path (Pitriyana) indicated by smoke and night-deities carries fruitive workers to lunar heavenly realms, from which they return to mortal world.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern solstice—thereby departing, the yogi attains the lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: The escorting deities of the Pitriyana path lead the performers of pious rituals to Chandraloka, whence they return upon exhaustion of merit.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Fire, light, daytime, the bright fortnight, and six months of northern solstice symbolize the luminous path by which knowers of Brahman reach Supreme Brahman. Smoke, night, dark fortnight, and six months of southern solstice symbolize the shadowy path by which souls reach lunar light and return.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Smoke, night, dark fortnight, six months of southern solstice—departing thereby, the yogi reaches lunar light and returns"—the Pitriyana path led by these time-deities carries ritualistic performers to heavenly lunar realms, from which they inevitably return to earth upon merit exhaustion.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not provide a separate commentary on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The southern path indicated by smoke and night leads fruitive workers to Chandraloka and brings them back.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Reaching lunar light he returns"—fruitive workers departing via the Dhumradi path enjoy heavenly pleasures and then return to cycle of rebirth.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern solstice—thereby departing, the yogi attains the lunar light and returns.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the Dhumradi Southern Path and Chandraloka destination.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Souls performing only pious deeds go via Dhumradi path to Chandraloka and return upon merit exhaustion.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Fruitive worshipers and workers reaching heaven via Dhumradi path inevitably return to worldly cycle.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Smoke, night"—the Southern Path brings fruitive workers back to mortal realm after enjoying merit.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The mystic who passes away from this world during the smoke, night, the fortnight of the waning moon, or the six months when the sun passes to the south reaches the lunar planet but again comes back.
