If they function together, is there harmony or conflict among them? Anticipating such questions, Lord Krishna answers in this verse of His Divine Song. He describes how these qualities function at different times. Each quality becomes dominant and powerful at that specific moment. Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains: Raising the doubt whether the qualities of Sattva, etc., increase simultaneously without mutual opposition, or sequentially due to opposition, he asks—'uktam iti'. For those desirous of the increase of Sattva, taking the side of sequence for the sake of subduing the others, he replies—'ucyate iti'. He explains the increase of Sattva itself—'tada iti', that is, during the state of disappearance (subdual) of Rajas and Tamas. He describes the manner of increase of Rajas and its function—'tatha iti'. He indicates the increase of Tamas also and its function—'tama iti'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, descendant of Bharata! Sattva increases by subduing Rajas and Tamas; Rajas increases by subduing Sattva and Tamas; and similarly, Tamas increases by subduing Sattva and Rajas.

Commentary: 'rajastamaścābhibhūya sattvaṃ bhavati bhārata' — Sattva increases by suppressing the tendencies of Rajas and Tamas. That is, it suppresses the tendencies of Rajas, such as greed, activity, undertaking of new works, restlessness, longing, and attachment to worldly pleasures and hoarding, and the tendencies of Tamas, such as error (pramada), laziness, unnecessary sleep, and ignorance. It creates transparency, purity, dispassion, desirelessness, generosity, and detachment in the inner self.

'rajaḥ sattvaṃ tamaścaiva' — Rajas increases by suppressing the tendencies of Sattva and Tamas. That is, it suppresses the tendencies of Sattva, such as knowledge, light, dispassion, and generosity, and the tendencies of Tamas, such as error, laziness, unnecessary sleep, and ignorance. It creates greed, activity, undertaking of works, restlessness, and longing in the inner self.

'tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajastathā' — Similarly, Tamas increases by suppressing Sattva and Rajas. That is, it suppresses the tendencies of Sattva, such as transparency, purity, light, and generosity, and the tendencies of Rajas, such as restlessness, agitation, and greed. It creates error, laziness, excessive sleep, and ignorance in the inner self.

By subduing two qualities, one quality increases; the dominant quality conquers man and, having conquered him, binds him. However, the Lord has given the reverse order here (from verses 6 to 10). That is, He first spoke of binding, then of conquering, and then of one quality increasing by subduing the other two. The purpose of giving such an order is — first, in the second verse, the Lord explained that those great souls who have severed their connection with Prakriti are not born even at the time of great creation, nor are they disturbed at the time of dissolution. This is because both creation and dissolution occur only due to the connection with Prakriti. But those who establish a connection with Prakriti are bound by the qualities born of Prakriti (14.5). On this, the natural question arises: what is the nature of those qualities and how do they bind man? In response, from verses 6 to 8, the Lord described the nature of the three qualities — Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas — and the manner in which they bind the soul. On this, the question arises: what do the three qualities do before binding? In response, the Lord explained that before binding, the dominant quality conquers man, and then binds him (14.9). Now the question arises: how does a quality conquer man? In response, the Lord said that one quality conquers man by subduing the other two (14.10). Thus, it becomes clear that the sequence kept by the Lord from the sixth to the tenth verses is indeed appropriate.

Relationship: When one quality increases by subduing the other two, what are the characteristics of that increased quality? To explain this, He first describes the characteristics of the increased Sattva quality.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Even though all the three Gunas of Sattva etc., are associated with the nature of the self conjoined with Prakrti in the form of body, yet owing to the dominance of previous Karmas and the differences in the food nourishing the body, Sattva etc., preponderate or are subdued by turn. Sometimes Sattva preponderates prevailing over Rajas and Tamas; sometimes Rajas preponderates prevailing over Tamas and Sattva, and sometimes Tamas preponderates prevailing over Rajas and Sattva.

He teaches that this changing preponderance of the Gunas can be inferred from the knowledge of the effects produced by them.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains: 'rajastamaśceti...' Sattva arises by overpowering the two qualities (Rajas and Tamas) due to unseen destiny. The same applies to the other qualities as well.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains: It is being stated when the qualities perform their respective functions: When Sattva arises, i.e. increases, by subduing both Rajas and Tamas simultaneously, then it performs its unique function mentioned earlier. Similarly, when Rajas arises by subduing both Sattva and Tamas, then it performs its function mentioned earlier. Likewise, when Tamas also arises by subduing both Sattva and Rajas, it then performs its function mentioned earlier.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains: He states the reason there — 'rajah...' etc. Sattva arises due to unseen destiny (adrishtha) by subduing, i.e., suppressing the two qualities of Rajas and Tamas. Then, it binds the individual to its own effects like happiness, knowledge, etc. Similarly, Rajas also arises by subduing the two qualities of Sattva and Tamas, and then binds the individual to its own effects like longing, action, etc. Likewise, Tamas also arises by subduing Sattva and Rajas, and then binds the individual to its own effects like negligence, laziness, etc.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains: Addressing the question of whether the qualities of Sattva, etc., increase simultaneously without mutual opposition, or sequentially due to opposition, he adopts the view of sequence for the sake of subduing the others for those who desire the increase of Sattva, and says — 'rajah...' etc. When Sattva arises, i.e. increases, by subduing (suppressing) both Rajas and Tamas, then, during the state of suppression of Rajas and Tamas, the established Sattva begins its own function of knowledge, happiness, etc. By addressing him as 'Bharata', it is indicated that one who is devoted (rata) to light (bha), i.e. Brahmavidya, should achieve the increase of Sattva which suppresses Rajas and Tamas. Similarly, when the quality of Rajas increases by subduing both Sattva and Tamas, then it begins its own function like action, longing, etc. Likewise, when the quality called Tamas increases by subduing both Sattva and Rajas, it then begins its own function like covering of knowledge, negligence, etc.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains: He removes the doubt of inconsistency in the subsequent text by saying 'dehākāra...' (having the form of the body). It means they are mutually opposed, taking the form of right and wrong knowledge, happiness and pain, attachment, etc. To avoid the possibility of inconsistency in their rise and subdual, he says 'prācīnakarmavaśāt' (due to past karmas), because the past karmas, which are the cause of God's grace or punishment, ripen at different times, making the corresponding flow of rise and subdual logical. Just as Ayurvedic experts say that wind, bile, and phlegm increase through substances that nourish them, and decrease by opposites, similarly here also it is due to differences in food (Sattvik, etc.) which will be described later, hence he says 'dehāpyāyanabhūtāhāravaiṣamyācca'. Just as in Vedic passages like 'Then the gods arose (abhavan)...', the word 'bhavati' here is used in the sense of arising (udbhava), hence he says 'udriktaṃ vartate' (remains dominant). This instruction on rise and subdual is for the purpose of subduing Rajas and Tamas when they arise.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains: Raising the objection: 'Since happiness, pain, etc. are caused by unseen destiny, how can these qualities perform their functions and cause changes?' In order to show that 'such power has been given to them by Me', He states it as an established fact — 'rajastama...' etc. Sattva arises by subduing (suppressing) the two qualities of Rajas and Tamas, which are of the nature of pain and ignorance. By addressing him as 'Bharata', it is indicated that just as by My will you conquer by defeating all, so also do these qualities. Similarly, Rajas also arises by subduing the two qualities of Sattva and Tamas. By the word 'eva' (even), it is expressed that even though Tamas has a greater capacity to delude, it also functions similarly. Likewise, Tamas arises by subduing Sattva and Rajas.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains: Raising the doubt as to when Sattva and other qualities manifest their respective functions, he says that it happens when there is mutual subdual — 'rajah...' etc. Sattva arises, i.e. increases, by subduing Rajas and Tamas. Similarly, Rajas also arises by subduing Sattva and Tamas. Likewise, Tamas also arises by subduing Sattva and Rajas.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Sometimes the mode of goodness becomes prominent, defeating the modes of passion and ignorance, O son of Bharata. Sometimes the mode of passion defeats goodness and ignorance, and at other times ignorance defeats goodness and passion. In this way there is always competition for supremacy.

 When the mode of passion is prominent, the modes of goodness and ignorance are defeated. When the mode of goodness is prominent, passion and ignorance are defeated. And when the mode of ignorance is prominent, passion and goodness are defeated. This competition is always going on. Therefore, one who is actually intent on advancing in Kṛṣṇa consciousness has to transcend these three modes. The prominence of some certain mode of nature is manifested in one’s dealings, in his activities, in eating, etc. All this will be explained in later chapters. But if one wants, he can develop, by practice, the mode of goodness and thus defeat the modes of ignorance and passion. One can similarly develop the mode of passion and defeat goodness and ignorance. Or one can develop the mode of ignorance and defeat goodness and passion. Although there are these three modes of material nature, if one is determined he can be blessed by the mode of goodness, and by transcending the mode of goodness he can be situated in pure goodness, which is called the vasudeva state, a state in which one can understand the science of God. By the manifestation of particular activities, it can be understood in what mode of nature one is situated.
