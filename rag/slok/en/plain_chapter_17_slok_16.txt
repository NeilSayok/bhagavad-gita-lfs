Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
manaḥ prasādaḥ saumyatvaṃ maunamātmavinigrahaḥ .
bhāvasaṃśuddhirityetattapo mānasamucyate

Life Application:
Maintain mental serenity, gentleness, silence, and self-restraint to cultivate deep purity of heart today.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Serenity, kindness, silence, self-control and purity - this is austerity of mind.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Mental austerity (Manasam Tapah) consists of five values: serenity of mind (Prasada), gentleness (Saumyatvam), silence (Mauna - quietening the noise of desire), self-control (Atma-vinigraha), and purity of intent (Bhava-samshuddhi). Serenity of mind is impossible for a person whose mind constantly runs after sense objects. Gentleness means benevolence toward all living beings.

The serenity of mind, the ietness, the taciturnity, the self-control, the purity of thought-all this is called mental austerity.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Serenity of mind, benevolence, silence, self-control, purity of mind - these are called austerity of the mind.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Tranillity of mind, gentleness, reticence, withdrawal of the mind, purity of heart,-these are what is called mental austerity.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that 'Saumyatvam' means absence of cruelty. One who is not cruel is called 'Saumya'. 'Maunam' means thoughtfulness or being reflective. This is also supported by the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad: 'Having known child-like simplicity and learning, then he becomes a Muni (silent sage)'. How else could there be mental austerity without these qualities?

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that mental austerity involves tranquillity of mind, benevolence, silence, self-control, and purity of nature. Silence here is not merely avoiding speech, but is the result of controlling thought (the cause), which prevents the mind from running after senses.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Serenity of mind, gentleness, silence, self-control, and purity of heart—these are called the austerity of the mind.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains: 'Manah-prasada' (serenity of mind) is the state of mind free from any anxiety, worry, or distress, attained when one is content with whatever situation God places them in. 'Saumyatva' (gentleness) is the benevolence in the heart that is reflected on the face. 'Maunam' (silence) refers to the quietening of the inner thoughts, rather than just the physical tongue. 'Atma-vinigrahah' (self-control) is keeping the mind firmly in checks. 'Bhava-samshuddhih' (purity of heart) means keeping our inner intentions and feelings pure and free from deceit.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Serenity of mind, viz., absence of wrath etc., practice of benevolence, viz., the direction of the mind for the good of others, silence, viz., contorl of speech by the mind; self-control, viz., focusing the activity of the mind on the object of contemplation; purity of mind, viz., absence of thought about subjects other than the self - these constitute the austerity of the mind.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that 'Saumyatvam' (gentleness) refers to the absence of cruelty. 'Maunam' refers to being reflective (mananashilata)—the deep contemplation of scriptural truths using logic, which is the nature of a Muni (sage). It is not merely the silence of speech, since the latter would overlap with the austerity of speech and lead to redundancy (punarukti-dosha) with self-control (atma-vinigrahah). Purity of nature (Bhava-samshuddhi) refers to the complete purity of intentions.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains the terms: 'Manah-prasada' is tranquillity of mind free from disturbance; 'Saumyatvam' is gentleness, desiring good for all; 'Maunam' is the state of a Muni (reflection on the Self); 'Atma-vinigrahah' is the withdrawal of the mind from all desires; and 'Bhava-samshuddhih' is purity of inner intentions, free from deceit. These constitute mental austerity.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that mental austerity consists of serenity of mind (prasada), gentleness (saumyatva), silence (mauna - control of thoughts), self-control, and purity of nature.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that mental austerity involves tranquillity of mind, benevolence (wishing well for all), reticence/mindful reflection (mauna), self-control, and purity of intentions without hypocrisy.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that 'Saumyatvam' is gentleness, which is cruelty-free state of mind. 'Maunam' is reflection, namely scriptural contemplation. Purity of heart (Bhava-samshuddhi) means having noble intentions, free from deceit.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that mental austerity is keeping the mind content and calm, maintaining gentleness, practicing silence (meditating on the Lord), self-control, and maintaining purity of intentions in all actions.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that 'Saumyatvam' is benevolence, which is shown by facial brightness. 'Maunam' is thoughtfulness, 'Atma-vinigrahah' is control of the mind, and 'Bhava-samshuddhih' is purity of inner intentions.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
And satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one’s existence are the austerities of the mind.

 To make the mind austere is to detach it from sense gratification. It should be so trained that it can be always thinking of doing good for others. The best training for the mind is gravity in thought. One should not deviate from Kṛṣṇa consciousness and must always avoid sense gratification. To purify one’s nature is to become Kṛṣṇa conscious. Satisfaction of the mind can be obtained only by taking the mind away from thoughts of sense enjoyment. The more we think of sense enjoyment, the more the mind becomes dissatisfied. In the present age we unnecessarily engage the mind in so many different ways for sense gratification, and so there is no possibility of the mind’s becoming satisfied. The best course is to divert the mind to the Vedic literature, which is full of satisfying stories, as in the Purāṇas and the Mahābhārata. One can take advantage of this knowledge and thus become purified. The mind should be devoid of duplicity, and one should think of the welfare of all. Silence means that one is always thinking of self-realization. The person in Kṛṣṇa consciousness observes perfect silence in this sense. Control of the mind means detaching the mind from sense enjoyment. One should be straightforward in his dealings and thereby purify his existence. All these qualities together constitute austerity in mental activities.
