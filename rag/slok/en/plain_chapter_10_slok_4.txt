Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
buddhirjñānamasammohaḥ kṣamā satyaṃ damaḥ śamaḥ .
sukhaṃ duḥkhaṃ bhavo.abhāvo bhayaṃ cābhayameva ca

Word Meanings:
बुद्धिर्ज्ञानमसम्मोहः - intellect, wisdom, non-delusion
क्षमा - forgiveness
सत्यं - truthfulness
दमः - selfrestraint
शमः - serenity
सुखं - happily
दुःखं - sorrow
भवोऽभावो - birth and death
भयं - fear
चाभयमेव - and fearlessness alone
च - and

Life Application:
Understand that intellect, wisdom, non-delusion, forgiveness, truth, sensory control, and calm arise from God alone.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Intelligence (the capacity to comprehend subtle truths), wisdom (discrimination between Self and non-Self), non-delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness, control of senses, control of mind, pleasure, pain, birth, death, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Intellect, wisdom, non-delusion, forgiveness, truth, self-restraint, calmness, happiness, pain, existence, non-existence, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Intellect, wisdom, freedom from doubt and delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness, control of the senses, calmness of mind, pleasure, pain, birth, death, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Intellect, wisdom, non-delusion, forgiveness, truth, sense-control, mind-control, pleasure, pain, birth, death, fear, fearlessness, non-injury, equanimity, contentment, austerity, charity, fame, and infamy—all these twenty distinct qualities of living beings originate from Me alone. Detailed commentary given under 10.5.

Intelligence, knowledge, freedom from delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness, control of senses, tranquility, pleasure, pain, existence, non-existence, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Intelligence, knowledge, freedom from delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness, self-restraint, calmness, pleasure, pain, existence, non-existence, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Intellect, wisdom, non-delusion, forgiveness, truth, self-restraint, calmness, happiness, pain, existence, non-existence, fear, and fearlessness—

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Buddhi" is the discernment of what ought and ought not to be done. "Jnana" is correct perception. "Tusti" is contentment. All these diverse mental and intellectual attributes of living beings originate from and remain subject to the Supreme Lord.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Intelligence (comprehension of subtle meanings), knowledge (ascertainment of the Self), non-delusion (discrimination), forgiveness, and other mental faculties are the Lord's manifestations, arising in living beings from the Lord according to their past karma.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Intellect, wisdom, non-delusion, forgiveness, truth, sense-control, mind-control, happiness, sorrow, birth, death, fear, fearlessness, non-injury, equanimity, contentment, austerity, charity, fame, and infamy—these twenty distinct qualities of living beings arise from Me alone.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "Buddhi" is the mind's power of deliberation. "Jnana" is decisive knowledge regarding conscious and unconscious entities. "Asammoha" is freedom from prior confusion. All these twenty diverse states of living beings arise solely subject to the Lord's divine will.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 10.2–10.5 jointly): Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 10.1–10.5 jointly): To foster the devotion explained earlier and to reveal the all-pervading divine nature and infinite cosmic manifestations (vibhutis), the Supreme Lord resumes His teaching. 'Paramam vachah' signifies the supreme, wisdom-bestowing word spoken for Arjuna's highest welfare.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Buddhi" is the internal organ's capacity to comprehend subtle matters. "Jnana" is knowledge of the Self and spiritual truths. "Asammoha" is absence of confusion. All these twenty diverse qualities of creatures arise from Me, the Supreme Lord of all, according to their past deeds.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Intelligence, wisdom, non-delusion, and all other mental qualities and emotional states are not independent; they arise from and remain entirely governed by Lord Hari, who alone is absolutely independent.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The twenty internal attributes of creatures—such as intellect, wisdom, happiness, and sorrow—are the manifestation of the Lord's inherent yoga-majesty, appearing from Him according to the varying fitness of souls.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: The twenty mental qualities—intellect (capacity to grasp subtle truths), wisdom (Self-ascertainment), non-delusion, etc.—arise distinctly in living beings from the Supreme Lord in accordance with their respective unseen latent karma.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: From intellect, wisdom, and non-delusion down to fame and infamy, all these twenty diverse mental qualities of creatures arise solely from Me, the Supreme Lord.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: These twenty modifications and states of the mind in living beings are projected by the Lord's supreme power and Maya; hence the Supreme Lord alone is their ultimate substratum.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The origin of these twenty qualities—intellect, wisdom, etc.—depends entirely on the Lord's divine will. Realizing His cosmic presence and infinite powers forms the firm foundation of Bhakti-yoga.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: The twenty mental traits—Sattvic, Rajasic, and Tamasic—found in living beings manifest distinctly in them by My divine will alone.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: The twenty mental qualities arise in living beings from Me, the Supreme Ruler, according to their unseen karma, enabling the aspirant to contemplate even these internal states as expressions of Divinity.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Intelligence, knowledge, freedom from doubt and delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness, control of the senses, control of the mind, happiness and distress, birth, death, fear, fearlessness—
