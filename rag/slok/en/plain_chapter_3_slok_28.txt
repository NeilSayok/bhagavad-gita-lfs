Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
tattvavittu mahābāho guṇakarmavibhāgayoḥ .
guṇā guṇeṣu vartanta iti matvā na sajjate

Word Meanings:
तत्त्ववित्तु - knower of truth however
महाबाहो - O mighty-armed one
गुणकर्मविभागयोः - of divisions of qualities and actions
गुणा - modes (senses)
गुणेषु - among modes (sense objects)
वर्तन्त - are operating
इति - thus
मत्वा - knowing
न - not
सज्जते - is attached

Life Application:
Observe sensory attractions calmly as natural physical interactions, remaining unperturbed as the detached observer within.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
But he who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions, knowing that qualities act amidst qualities, is not attached.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
But he who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions, knowing that qualities act amidst qualities, is not attached.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
But he who knows the truth (Tattvavit tu), O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions (Guna-karma-vibhagayoh), knowing that "qualities as senses are merely interacting with qualities as sense-objects" (Gunah guneshu vartante), is never attached.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: The Sage knows senses and objects are Gunas of Prakriti, while the soul is Vishnu"s eternal servant and unattached.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Recognizing that senses (Gunas) interact with sense-objects (Gunas) while the Self remains detached and non-acting, the Knower remains unattached.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
But he who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions, knowing that qualities act amidst qualities, is not attached.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: A Knower of truth, discriminating between Nature and Spirit, sees sense-activities as Gunas interacting with Gunas and remains unattached.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Prakriteh etc. and Tattvavit tu etc. He who knows the truth about Gunas and action realizes that Gunas act upon Gunas, remaining unattached.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "But he who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions, knowing that qualities act amidst qualities, is not attached"—contrasts the Sage"s non-attached wisdom with the ignorant ego-doership.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Knowledge of Guna-Karma division pierces the knot of nescience, bestowing detachment upon the Sage.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A Knower of Krishna-truth understands all acts as Gunas, keeping himself surrendered at Krishna"s feet.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Knowing the detached nature of Atman and the interplay of Gunas, the Sage remains untouched by ego-doership.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
But he who knows the truth, O mighty-armed, about the divisions of qualities and their functions, knowing that qualities act amidst qualities, is not attached.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis substantiating how the Knower of Guna-Karma divisions remains unattached by realizing Gunas interact with Gunas.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A Knower of Vasudeva-truth, understanding Gunas" operations, abides steadfast in Atman"s detachment.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When a seeker knows Gunas interact with Gunas, his mind abides in peaceful remembrance of Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Senses interact with objects, Atman is non-doer—this correct knowledge is the root of Moksha.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who is in knowledge of the Absolute Truth, O mighty-armed, does not engage himself in the senses and sense gratification, knowing well the differences between work in devotion and work for fruitive results.
