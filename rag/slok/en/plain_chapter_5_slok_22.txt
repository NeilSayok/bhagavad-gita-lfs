Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ye hi saṃsparśajā bhogā duḥkhayonaya eva te .
ādyantavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ

Word Meanings:
ये - those which
हि - certainly
संस्पर्शजा - born of sensory contact
भोगा - pleasures
दुःखयोनय - sources (wombs) of sorrow
एव - also
ते - they
आद्यन्तवन्तः - having beginning and end
कौन्तेय - O son of Kunti
न - not
तेषु - in them
रमते - takes delight
बुधः - the wise person

Life Application:
Recognize that pleasures dependent on physical objects are temporary, avoiding excessive attachment to fleeting comforts.

The pleasures that are born of contact are only generators of pain, for they have a beginning and an end, O son of Kunti; the wise man does not rejoice in them.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Sensory pleasures are sources of sorrow"—material enjoyment is perishable and ultimately painful; hence the seeker of Moksha delights only in the eternal bliss of Lord Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Sense-pleasures are breeding grounds for sorrow; one aspiring for the soul"s eternal bliss never seeks gratification in these perishable pursuits.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The pleasures that are born of contact are only generators of pain, for they have a beginning and an end, O son of Kunti; the wise man does not rejoice in them"—exposes the deceptive, ephemeral nature of sensory enjoyment (*Samsparshaja bhoga*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Recognizing the impermanence and sorrowful nature of contact-born pleasures is the initial step of spiritual discrimination.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Material pleasures that turn one away from Krishna are temporary and painful; a devotee relishes only the divine nectar of devotional service.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The wise man delights not in them"—because sensory happiness is mixed with the threefold suffering (Parinama, Tapa, Samskara), the enlightened person never gets attached to it.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing why intelligent seekers refrain from contact-born pleasures due to their painful and temporary nature.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A devotee of Vasudeva discards material pleasures like poison, drinking only the eternal divine nectar.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: By Krishna"s compassion, the wise understand that worldly pleasures are merely traps entangling the soul.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "They are only sources of pain"—sensory enjoyment seems pleasant initially but culminates in intense grief, hence the prudent avoid it.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
An intelligent person does not take part in the sources of misery, which are due to contact with the material senses. O son of Kunti, such pleasures have a beginning and an end, and so the wise man does not delight in them.
