Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who with unclouded vision sees Me as the Lord-God, knows all there is to be known, and always shall worship Me with his whole heart.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The person who has completely renounced the false identification with his body, mind, and intellect, and the objects, emotions, and thoughts experienced through them, is alone 'Asammudhah' (undeluded). To 'know Me thus' here does not mean intellectual knowledge, but direct intuitive realization of the Self. Realizing oneself as the Supreme Self is the true knowledge. Having given up identification with the non-Self, he who has established complete identity with Me is the supreme devotee, who worships Me with his whole heart. Identification with the beloved is considered the measure of love everywhere; greater love means greater identification. Therefore, even mathematically, complete identification means complete love, i.e., para-bhakti. This Purushottama, as consciousness, illuminates all events and the inner thoughts of all beings in all three periods of time; hence He is called all-knowing (Sarvavit). The devotee who achieves complete identity with this Purushottama is also called all-knowing. The subject matter of this chapter is the knowledge of the Divine Truth. Now, Lord Krishna praises this knowledge, which frees us forever from the bondages of physical pain, mental agitations, and intellectual anxieties.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains: In this verse, the Lord states the fruit of the knowledge described earlier. One who is free from the delusion of identifying with the non-self (body, etc.), and knows Me as the Supreme Self, becomes the knower of all because he has realized the Self of all. Such a person worships Me in every way—with all thoughts and feelings directed towards the Supreme Reality alone.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, scion of the Bharata dynasty! He who, free from delusion, knows Me thus as the Supreme Person, is all-knowing and worships Me with his whole being.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that the individual soul (Jiva) is an eternal part of God. Therefore, experiencing one's eternal relationship with the Lord (which has always existed) constitutes being undeluded (asammudhah). Delusion is the obstacle to knowing the world or God in truth. Real knowledge of any entity is possible only when there is no relationship based on attraction (raga) or aversion (dvesha). Assuming a relationship with perishable objects due to attachment or aversion is delusion. Knowing the world in truth leads to the realization of one's non-difference from God, and knowing God in truth leads to the realization of one's distinctness from the world. Knowing God as Purushottama means turning completely to Him, considering Him alone as one's own. When one realizes that whatever glory is seen or heard in the world belongs to the Lord alone, the attraction to the world disappears. The knower of God is called all-knowing (Sarvavit) because he has known the only Reality worth knowing. He then worships the Lord in all ways, which is unadulterated devotion (avyabhicharini bhakti). When the mind is detached from the perishable world and anchored in the Lord, and the intellect is anchored in faith, every activity of the devotee becomes worship of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
He who, without delusion, knows the Supreme Person as Myself, as already stated, i.e., knows Me, as differing in kind from the perishable and the imperishable Persons, because of My being immutable and because of My being distinguished from them as pervading, supporting and ruling etc., by nature - such a devotee knows all. He knows all that is to be known as the means of attaining Me. He worships Me in 'every way,' i.e., whatever ways of worshipping Me have been prescribed as the means of attaining Me, he worships Me by all these modes of worship. Whatever love is evoked in Me towards one approaching Me by all forms of knowledge having Me for their object, and whatever love is roused up in Me towards those who practise various modes of worship having Me for their object - all that in a unified form is generatd in Me towards one who has the knowledge of Me as described.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Bharata, O scion of the Bharata dynasty; yah, he who; asammudhah, being free from delusion; janati, knows; mam, Me, God, having the aforesaid alifications; purusottamam, the supreme Person; evam, thus, in the way described, as 'I am this One'; sah, he; is sarva-vit, all-knowing- he knows everything through self-identification with all-, i.e. (he becomes) omniscient; and bhajati, adores; mam, Me, existing in all things; sarva-bhavena, with his whole being, i.e. with his mind fixed on Me as the Self of all.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains: The knowledge of the Supreme Person is highly praised here. One who is free from delusion and knows the Lord as the Supreme Person, who transcends both Kshara and Akshara, becomes the knower of everything (Sarvavit). Because the Lord is the source and essence of all things, knowing Him is equivalent to knowing all. Such a knower worships the Lord with his entire being (Sarvabhavena), which means with undivided love and complete surrender, realizing that there is nothing else besides the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains: The Lord describes the fruit of this knowledge of Himself as the Supreme Person. One who is free from the delusion of superimposing non-self attributes on the Self, and knows Me, the Lord, as the Supreme Person (Purushottama), who is distinct from Kshara and Akshara as explained before, is the knower of everything (Sarvavit). Since the Lord is the Self of all, knowing Him means knowing everything. Therefore, he worships Me with his whole being (Sarvabhavena), in every possible manner, with his mind completely absorbed in Me. This knowledge is praised as the ultimate means to liberation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains: The Lord now states the fruit of this knowledge. One who is free from delusion (Asammudhah) and knows Me as the Supreme Person, who is distinct from the perishable and the imperishable, is the knower of everything (Sarvavit). He knows all that is to be known. Consequently, he worships Me with his whole heart and in all ways (Sarvabhavena), seeing Me as the Self of all.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati explains: The Lord describes the reward of knowing the Supreme Person. One who is free from the illusion of duality and knows Me as Purushottama, who is superior to the perishable effect and the imperishable cause, is all-knowing. He realizes the unity of the Self with the Supreme. Therefore, he worships Me with all his heart and with all feelings directed to Me as the inner Self of all.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains: The Lord declares that the person who is free from the delusion of considering Him as merely a human being or an ordinary deity, and knows Him as the Supreme Person, is the knower of everything. Knowing Him means knowing the ultimate truth of the Vedas and all scriptures. Consequently, he worships the Lord with his entire being (Sarvabhavena), which includes performing all duties as an offering to Him and engaging in all forms of devotion.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains: The Lord states that the undeluded devotee who knows Him as the Supreme Person is all-knowing. He knows that the Lord is the ultimate cause and the supreme object of worship. Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains: One who is undeluded knows the Lord as the Supreme Person, who is distinct from Kshara (the material universe) and Akshara (the unmanifest cause). Such a person is the knower of all because he has realized the ultimate source of everything. He worships Me with his entire being, recognizing Me as the Self of all.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Whoever knows Me as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, without doubting, is the knower of everything. He therefore engages himself in full devotional service to Me, O son of Bharata.

 There are many philosophical speculations about the constitutional position of the living entities and the Supreme Absolute Truth. Now in this verse the Supreme Personality of Godhead clearly explains that anyone who knows Lord Kṛṣṇa to be the Supreme Person is actually the knower of everything. The imperfect knower goes on simply speculating about the Absolute Truth, but the perfect knower, without wasting his valuable time, engages directly in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the devotional service of the Supreme Lord. Throughout the whole of Bhagavad-gītā , this fact is being stressed at every step. And still there are so many stubborn commentators on Bhagavad-gītā who consider the Supreme Absolute Truth and the living entities to be one and the same. Vedic knowledge is called śruti, learning by aural reception. One should actually receive the Vedic message from authorities like Kṛṣṇa and His representatives. Here Kṛṣṇa distinguishes everything very nicely, and one should hear from this source. Simply to hear like the hogs is not sufficient; one must be able to understand from the authorities. It is not that one should simply speculate academically. One should submissively hear from Bhagavad-gītā that these living entities are always subordinate to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Anyone who is able to understand this, according to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, knows the purpose of the Vedas; no one else knows the purpose of the Vedas. The word bhajati is very significant. In many places the word bhajati is expressed in relationship with the service of the Supreme Lord. If a person is engaged in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, in the devotional service of the Lord, it is to be understood that he has understood all the Vedic knowledge. In the Vaiṣṇava paramparā it is said that if one is engaged in the devotional service of Kṛṣṇa, then there is no need for any other spiritual process for understanding the Supreme Absolute Truth. He has already come to the point, because he is engaged in the devotional service of the Lord. He has ended all preliminary processes of understanding. But if anyone, after speculating for hundreds of thousands of lives, does not come to the point that Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead and that one has to surrender there, all his speculation for so many years and lives is a useless waste of time.
