Life Application:
Behold all celestial beings, sages, divine serpents, and creator spirits residing within the divine cosmic form.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Arjuna said: O almighty God! I see in Thee the powers of Nature, the various creatures of the world, the Progenitor on his lotus throne, the Sages and the shining angels.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
When Arjuna addresses Lord Krishna with the word Deva (shining one), he confirms the simile given by Sanjaya, who described the splendour of the Cosmic Form as resembling the light of a thousand suns. Listing the things seen in the Cosmic Form, Arjuna says: I see in Your body all the gods and hosts of diverse classes of beings. Sanjaya has also mentioned all of these earlier. From the descriptions given by both, it is clear that in that Cosmic Form, not only worldly things but also extraordinary, divine gods could be recognized. Arjuna also beholds Brahma, Vishnu, and Mahesh (Shiva) in it, along with many sages. Arjuna also sees many divine serpents. It is a poetic style that great poets, while describing the most sublime, suddenly start describing things of a grotesque or ridiculous nature. The sole purpose of this is to startle the readers and draw their attention to the subject matter. In this Cosmic Form, representation is found from Brahmaji down to the serpents. The principle of Vedanta is that what is in the microcosm is also in the macrocosm, or the individual is the totality. The great philosophers of the world have described and experienced this. But before this, no one had clearly and objectively demonstrated this philosophical principle. Vyasaji was the pioneer of this art, and till now no one has had the courage to emulate him in this difficult task. Arjuna now describes such a form, the details of which would make even the most courageous man feel as if he is losing his courage.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
How did Arjuna speak to the Lord?—this question is asked as 'how'. Satisfying this expected question, he introduces the verse with 'What was shown by you...'. Although gods are included in the hosts of diverse beings, they are mentioned separately because of their prominence. Although Brahma is the self of all gods, his separate mention from them is because of his being their creator; thinking thus, he says 'Moreover'. Sages and serpents are mentioned separately due to some differences. 'Divyan' (divine) is an adjective that qualifies both.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna said: O Lord! In Your body, I see all the gods, hosts of various kinds of beings, Brahma seated on his lotus throne, Lord Shiva, all the sages, and all the celestial serpents.

Commentary: pasyami devams tava deva dehe sarvams tatha bhuta-visesa-sanghan — The divine vision bestowed upon Arjuna by the Lord is so extraordinary that he is seeing even the celestial realm (devaloka) right before him. Not only that, he is seeing the entire three worlds. Not just the three worlds, but even the creator (Brahma), keeper (Vishnu), and destroyer (Shiva) of the three worlds are visible to him directly. Therefore, Arjuna describes: I see all the gods, hosts of living beings, and Brahma and Shiva.

'brahmanam isam kamalasanastham' — Arjuna says that he sees Brahma seated on the lotus; this proves that Arjuna is also seeing the stalk of the lotus and the source of the stalk, i.e., the original foundation, Lord Vishnu (who is reclining on the couch of Shesha). Apart from this, Arjuna is also seeing Lord Shiva, His Mount Kailash, and His abode, the banyan tree situated on Mount Kailash.

'rsims ca sarvan uragams ca divyan' — Arjuna is seeing all the sages residing on the earth and also the divine serpents residing in the netherworld (patala). In this verse, Arjuna's statement proves that he does not see the three worlds—heaven, earth, and the netherworld—as separate, but rather they appear to him divided into many parts yet together in one place—'pravibhaktam anekadha' (Gita 11.13). When Arjuna's gaze shifts from the three worlds, he also sees the desired abodes of the eligible souls, namely Brahmaloka, Kailash, and Vaikunthaloka, along with their lords (Brahma, Shiva, and Vishnu). All this is solely the effect of the divine vision bestowed by the Lord.

Special Note:

When the Lord said that this entire universe exists in a fraction of His being, Arjuna requested to see it. In response to Arjuna's request, the Lord says, 'See the entire universe, animate and inanimate, gathered in one place in My body'—'iha ekastham... mama dehe' (11.7). Sanjaya, endowed with divine vision by Sage Vyasa, also says the same: Arjuna saw the entire universe gathered in one place in the body of the Lord of lords—'tatra ekastham... devadevasya sarire' (11.13). Here Arjuna says, 'I see all the hosts of beings etc. in Your body'—'tava deva dehe'. Thus, while the word 'ekastham' (situated in one place) appears in the words of both the Lord and Sanjaya, it does not appear in Arjuna's words. The reason for this is that wherever Arjuna's gaze fell on the Lord's body, he immediately started seeing the Cosmic Form (Vishvarupa) there. At that moment, Arjuna's gaze did not go toward the chariot-driving form of the Lord at all. Wherever Arjuna looked, endless creations appeared; hence, Arjuna's sight was swept away in that direction. Therefore, Arjuna could not say 'ekastham'. He could say 'ekastham' only if he saw the Lord's chariot-driving body along with the Cosmic Form. Arjuna sees only the Cosmic Form, and therefore he describes only the Cosmic Form. The Cosmic Form appears so boundless to him that he sees no limit in space or time. The point is that when Arjuna's vision cannot even find the end of the Cosmic Form, how could his gaze go toward the Lord sitting as the charioteer?

The Lord is showing the Cosmic Form in one part of His body, which is why He used the word 'ekastham'. Sanjaya is seeing the Lord seated as the charioteer as well as the Cosmic Form situated in one part of His body, which is why Sanjaya used the word 'ekastham' (Footnote p. 583). Now the question arises: what was that one place in the eyes of the Lord and Sanjaya where Arjuna was seeing the Cosmic Form? The answer is that it cannot be determined that Arjuna saw the Cosmic Form in only a particular place of the Lord's body. The reason is that in every single pore of the Lord's body, infinite millions of universes are present (Footnote p. 584). The Lord had also said, 'Behold the entire universe, both animate and inanimate, in one part of My body' (Gita 11.7). Therefore, wherever Arjuna's gaze fell once, he began to see the entire Cosmic Form there.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He describes that very experience by saying 'pasyami' (I see) in seventeen verses, consisting of sixteen descriptions of the forms and one prayer. 'O Lord, Moon-like Sri Krishna, in Your body, in this Cosmic Form, I see all.' 'Hosts of various classes of beings' (bhutaviseshasanghan) refers to the different categories of beings, such as those born of the womb (jarayuja), etc.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Revealing his own experience, thinking, 'Oh! What a height of my great fortune! I am beholding the Cosmic Form shown by the Lord, which is invisible to all the worlds, through the divine vision granted by the Lord,' Arjuna spoke, starting with 'I see' (pasyami). 'I see' means I make it the object of my visual perception. 'O Lord (Deva), in Your body' (which is the Cosmic Form) I see 'all the gods' such as Vasus and others. 'And also hosts (groups) of various classes of beings', both stationary (inanimate) and moving (animate), of diverse configurations. 'And Brahma, the four-faced ruler' of all, 'seated on the lotus'—which means seated on the Meru pericarp in the center of the earth-lotus, or seated on the lotus arising from the Lord's navel. 'And all the sages', Vasistha and other mind-born sons of Brahma. 'And the divine', non-material (supernatural) 'serpents' such as Vasuki and others. The phrase 'I see' is connected with all these nouns.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He presents the speech itself by saying 'I see' (pasyami) in seventeen verses: 'O Lord, in Your body, I see the gods like Adityas and others. And also all the hosts of various kinds of beings, such as those born of the womb (jarayuja), those born of eggs (andaja), etc. And the divine sages like Vasistha and others, and the serpents like Taksaka and others. And also Brahma, the ruler and lord of the gods. What is he like? Seated on the lotus, meaning situated on Mount Meru in the pericarp of the earth-lotus, or seated on Your navel-lotus.'

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Revealing his own experience, Arjuna spoke: 'O Lord, in Your body, I see all the gods like Indra and others. And also I see the hosts of various kinds of beings. Furthermore, I see Brahma, the controller of creatures, seated on the lotus, and all the sages like Vasistha and others, and the divine serpents like Vasuki and others.' The verb 'I see' is to be connected with each of these. Even though the gods and others are included within the category of 'various kinds of beings', they are mentioned separately due to their excellence. Although Brahma is a god, he is mentioned separately because he is their creator.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He presents the speech itself by saying 'I see' (pasyami) in seventeen verses: 'O worshipful Lord, in Your expanded body, I see the gods like Indra and others who are playful by nature, and also all the hosts of the four classes of beings who are likewise playful. I see the divine sages like Narada and others, manifested for the sake of divine play, and also the tamasic serpents like Shesha and others. I see Brahma, the root source of all, seated on the lotus of Your navel, and Lord Mahadeva (Shiva). I see all of them.'

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Gods refers to Adityas and others. Various classes of beings means the four categories of living beings starting with the womb-born, and hosts means their groups. Brahma means the four-faced creator. Ruler (Isa) means the controller. 'Seated on a lotus seat' indicates the perception of distant objects. Serpents refers to Ananta and others residing in the netherworld (patala). Divine refers to Vasuki and others residing in Kailash etc. By this, the perception of hidden/obstructed objects is indicated.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Arjuna said: My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, I see assembled in Your body all the demigods and various other living entities. I see Brahmā sitting on the lotus flower, as well as Lord Śiva and all the sages and divine serpents.

 Arjuna sees everything in the universe; therefore he sees Brahmā, who is the first creature in the universe, and the celestial serpent upon which the Garbhodaka-śāyī Viṣṇu lies in the lower regions of the universe. This snake bed is called Vāsuki. There are also other snakes known as Vāsuki. Arjuna can see from the Garbhodaka-śāyī Viṣṇu up to the topmost part of the universe on the lotus-flower planet where Brahmā, the first creature of the universe, resides. That means that from the beginning to the end, everything could be seen by Arjuna, who was sitting in one place on his chariot. This was possible by the grace of the Supreme Lord, Kṛṣṇa.
