Slok:

mohāttasya parityāgastāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ

Life Application:
Renunciation of prescribed duty is not proper; abandonment of duty through delusion is Tamasic.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
It is not right to give up actions which are obligatory; and if they are misunderstood, it is the result of sheer ignorance.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The abandonment of obligatory, i.e. duty-bound actions is considered to be a very low-level Tamasic abandonment. The combined form of daily (nitya) and occasional (naimittika) actions is indeed called obligatory action (niyata karma). As long as a person lives as a member of his society, that society also provides him with the benefits of security and progress. Therefore, according to Hindu ethics, a person has no right to abandon his duties. If a person abandons his moral duties out of ignorance, even then it is not excusable. Just as ignorance of the law regarding compliance with the constitution and the natural laws of the physical world is not considered excusable, the same rule applies in the spiritual realm as well. If a person, due to ignorance and lack of discrimination, does not serve society by performing his duty, then this abandonment of his is a foolish, i.e., Tamasic abandonment.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The renunciation of the enjoined action does not stand to reason; and completely relinishing it, out of ignorance is proclaimed, on all sides, as an act of the Tamas (Strand).

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
But the renunciation of obligatory acts is not proper. Abandonment of these through delusion is declared to be Tamasika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Drawing upon what was said about the absolute obligatoriness of daily duties, and filling in what is expected, he introduces the subsequent verse: "tasmāt...". Objection: "But some person is observed abandoning even obligatory actions." To this he says: "mohat...". Ignorance means not knowing their purifying nature. He explains that the abandoning of daily duties by an ignorant person is due to delusion, with the words: "niyataṃ ca...". Raising the doubt, "If the abandonment of daily duties is caused by delusion, how is it Tamasic?", he says: "mohaśca...".

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Indeed, the renunciation of obligatory action is not proper. The abandonment of the same from delusion is declared to be Tamasic.

Commentary — [The Lord describes the three kinds of abandonment because Arjuna wanted to abandon actions in their physical form — "better it is to live in this world even on alms" (Gita 2.5). Therefore, by explaining the threefold abandonment, the Lord wanted to awaken Arjuna, and to show humanity in general that the physical abandonment of obligatory actions is not approved by (or pleasing to) the Lord. The Lord considers only Sattvik abandonment to be true abandonment. Through Sattvik abandonment, the relationship with the world is completely severed. Secondly, in the seventeenth chapter, the Lord has also spoken of the three kinds of faith, food, etc. according to the gunas. Therefore, here too, when Arjuna asked about the essence of abandonment, the Lord explained the three kinds of abandonment.]
niyatasya tu saṃnyāsaḥ karmaṇo nopapadyate — The word tu (but) is used here to indicate that this Tamasic abandonment is entirely contrary and completely inferior to the certain and best opinion about abandonment that the Lord stated in the previous verse. It is never proper to abandon obligatory actions because they are duties that must be performed. Abandoning obligatory duties means not accepting or performing actions such as performing sacrifices like bali-vaishvadeva, offering food and water to a guest according to householder duties, feeding Brahmanas and giving them charity (dakshina) on special festivals or on days of shradha-tarpana, and performing sandhya prayers in the morning and evening according to one's varnashrama.
mohāttasya parityāgastāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ — Giving up such obligatory actions out of foolishness, i.e., without any discrimination or thought, is called Tamasic abandonment. If it was necessary to attend a spiritual gathering (satsang), assembly, or committee, but one remained idle due to laziness, started resting, or fell asleep; or if one's parents are sick at home and one was going to call a doctor or bring medicine for them, but on the way, seeing people playing cards or dice, one also got involved in the game and forgot to call the doctor or bring the medicine; or if a case is going on in court and at the time of appearing, one got busy with jokes and amusements, and the time passed; or if one abandoned necessary bodily duties like cleanliness and bathing due to laziness and negligence — all these are examples of Tamasic abandonment.
What is the difference between prescribed actions (vihita karma) and obligatory actions (niyata karma)? All actions which the scriptures command to be done are called prescribed actions (vihita karma). A single person cannot possibly perform all those prescribed actions because the scriptures provide rules for fasting on all days of the week and all dates of the lunar month. If the same person fasts on all days or all dates, when will he eat? This proves that not all prescribed actions apply to a single individual. However, among those prescribed actions, that duty which is necessary for a person according to his varna (class), ashrama (station in life), and circumstances, is called obligatory action (niyata karma) for him. For example, among the four classes — Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra — whatever rules exist for livelihood and bodily maintenance for each class, all of them are obligatory actions (niyata karma) for that respective class.
Abandoning obligatory actions out of delusion makes that abandonment Tamasic, and abandoning them for the sake of pleasure and comfort makes it Rajasic. Performing obligatory actions by abandoning the desire for pleasure, desire for fruits, and attachment makes that abandonment Sattvik. The meaning is that getting entangled in delusion is the nature of a Tamasic person, getting entangled in pleasure and comfort is the nature of a Rajasic person, and performing duty carefully and without desires, free from both of these, is the nature of a Sattvik person. It is only through this Sattvik nature or Sattvik abandonment that one severs the relationship with action and its fruit. It does not happen through Rajasic and Tamasic abandonment, because Rajasic and Tamasic abandonment is not true abandonment at all.
Generally, people consider abandonment of actions in their physical form alone to be abandonment because that is what is visible to them. They do not know with what attitude a person is performing which work. But the Lord considers only the abandonment of internal desire, possessiveness, and attachment to be true abandonment, because these alone are the cause of birth and death (Gita 13.21). If external abandonment alone were considered true abandonment, then all dying persons would attain liberation because all their belongings are left behind, and what to say of other things, even the body called one's own is left behind and they usually do not even remember those things. Therefore, internal abandonment is the real abandonment. When there is internal abandonment, whether external objects remain with a person or not, he is not bound by them.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Obligatory acts consist of daily, and occasional ceremonies like the five great sacrifices; their abandonment is not proper, for without actions even the sustenance of the body would be impossible, as already stated:  'From no-work, not even the body can be sustained' (3.8). The sustenance of the body by eating the sacrificial remnants produces perfect knowledge. Otherwise, as declared in the statement, 'But the sinful ones eat sin' (3.13). The satisfaction that comes by eating food which is not the remnant of sacrifice and which is therefore of the form of sin, is productive of erroneous knowledge in the mind. For, as declared in the Sruti, 'The mind consists of food' (Cha. U., 6.5.4), the mind is sustained by food. Also, there is the Sruti text, 'When the food is pure, the mind becomes pure; when the mind is pure, remembrance becomes firmly fixed; and when remembrance is acired, there is release from all knots of the heart' (Ibid., 7.26.2). It is therefore proved by the Sruti that knowledge of the form of direct perception of Brahman, is dependent on the purity of food. Hence the great sacrifices and such other obligatory and occasional rites are worthy of adoption till one's death, as they help in the knowledge of the Brahman. The renunciation of these is therefore not proper. Thus, the relinishment of these acts which produce knowledge through the delusion that they bind the self, is rooted in Tamas.

Tamasika renunciation has its roots in Tamas. Since such renunciation has its roots in ignorance which is the effect of Tamas, such renunciation is said to have its roots in Tamas. For Tamas is the root of ignorance as has been stated:  'From Tamas arise negligence and delusion, and also, ignorance' (14.17). Ignorance is erroneous knowledge which is antagonistic to right knowledge. So, it will be taught, 'That reason which, enveloped in Tamas, regards wrong as right, and which reverses every value, O Arjuna, is Tamasika' (18.32). It is for this reason that the renunciation of obligatory and occasional actions are said to have their roots in erroneous knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.4–18.11 jointly): Niscayam etc.  upto abhidhiyate.  The conclusion here is  this :  Due to the manifoldness of the nature of the Strands, that have been defined earlier, the act of relinishment itself is performed with a certain mental disposition which is a modification of the Sattva, the Rajas and the Tamas (the Strands).  Because it reflects  (is contaminated by)  the nature of the person having the same (the said mental dispositon),  what is called the real (unalloyed)  relinishment is the performance of the actions  by the knowers of the Supreme Brahman by giving up desire to achieve fruits and by avoiding the craving and hatred on account of their eanimity to [the pairs of opposites like]  success and failure etc.  That is why [the Bhagavat] says :  'By the act of relinishment born of the Rajas or of the Tamas  (Strands), no connection with the fruit  [of relinishment] is attained'.  However, for an act of relinishing, born of the Sattva (Strand), there is the fruit in the form of honouring the purport of the scriptures.  The application of the term  'relinishment'  stands to reason, in fact, only in the case of a sage who has relinished his holding on the multitude of the Strands.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
He refutes the opinion of the thinkers with: "niyatasya...". The statement of the thinkers that sannyasa in the form of physical abandonment of actions prescribed by rules and controlled by the Vedas, even when associated with desire, should be performed, is not proper; because for the followers of the Vedas who are knowers of Brahman, there is never any abandonment of what is spoken in the Vedas in any part. If the abandonment of that Vedic action is done by a follower of the Shruti out of delusion, which is Tamasic ignorance, then due to being caused by delusion, that abandonment is declared to be Tamasic; because it arises from delusion which is the effect of Tamas, in accordance with statements like: "Negligence and delusion arise from Tamas..." (Gita 14.17). And it brings about sin of omission; this is the meaning of the prefix "pari".

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Thus, My own view was established in the words "Some say that sacrifice, charity and austerity should not be abandoned" (18.3). Now, He begins to refute the opponent's view, "Some wise men say that action, being full of defects, should be abandoned" (18.3), by explaining the threefold abandonment mentioned earlier: "niyatasya tu...". The abandonment of actions done for desired results (kamyakarmas), which are full of defects because they do not cause purification of the inner instrument and are causes of bondage, is indeed proper for one who seeks knowledge which is the cause of cessation of bondage. But the sannyasa (abandonment) of obligatory action (niyata), i.e., daily duties (nityakarma), which is free from defect and causes purification, is not proper for a seeker of liberation who desires purification of the inner instrument; because by both scripture and logic, it must be performed for the purification of his inner instrument. And so it was said earlier: "For a sage who wishes to ascend to Yoga, action is said to be the cause" (Gita 6.3). Objection: "Like actions for desired results, even daily duties such as Darsha-Purnamasa, Jyotishtoma, etc., are said by the Sankhyas to be full of defects, because they are mixed with the killing of animals and grains." And it cannot be argued that because the killing which is an auxiliary of a sacrifice falls under specific injunctions like "One threshes paddy" and "One immolates an animal dedicated to Agni-Soma", the general prohibition "Do not kill any living being" refers only to killing other than that; because both the injunction and the prohibition can apply together without contradiction, having different scopes. For by the prohibition, it is stated that killing is a cause of evil for a person, not that it is not for the sake of the sacrifice; and by the injunction, it is stated that it is for the sake of the sacrifice, not that it is not a cause of evil. Thus, because being helpful to the sacrifice and being a cause of evil for the person can exist together in the same act, even killing for the sake of a sacrifice is indeed prohibited, and therefore all sacrifices mixed with killing, like Darsha-Purnamasa and Jyotishtoma, are indeed defective. It is indeed possible for what is ordained to be also prohibited, and what is prohibited to be also ordained, just like the Shyena sacrifice. For example, even though the Shyena sacrifice is ordained by the injunction for malevolent purposes, "One desiring to harm an enemy should sacrifice with the Shyena", it is indeed a cause of evil because it falls under the prohibition "Do not kill any living being"; and only he who is willing to tolerate that evil and is under the control of attachment, aversion, etc., is qualified for it. The same applies to the Jyotishtoma sacrifice. And so it is said in the Mahabharata: "Japa (chanting) is said to be the supreme dharma among all dharmas, because the sacrifice of japa proceeds with non-injury to all living beings." Manu also, by saying, "A Brahmana shall indeed succeed by japa alone, there is no doubt about this; whether he performs other duties or not, a Brahmana is called friendly (maitra)" (Manusmriti 2.87), praising friendship and non-injury, has established indeed the defectiveness of killing. Since purification of the inner instrument is easily accomplished by such acts as chanting the Gayatri mantra, daily duties like Jyotishtoma, which are defiled by the defect of killing etc., should be abandoned even by one qualified for action who cannot tolerate the defect, just like the Shyena sacrifice. To this we say: Killing for the sake of a sacrifice is not a cause of evil, because there is no room for prohibition where an action is touched by an injunction. For, since an injunction prompts action by indicating that it is a means to achieve an object of strong desire, and since this would be inconsistent if it were a means to evil, it is implied that the object of the injunction which is the scope of prompting is indeed not a means to evil; therefore, it is not proper that the object of an injunction is a cause of evil. Indeed, being helpful to the sacrifice is not the direct meaning of the injunction so that there would be no contradiction, but it is through the prompting itself. But human activity, which is the object of prompting, has only human goal (purushartha) as its scope, and sometimes also makes the sacrifice its scope because the sacrifice, being a means to the human goal, has assumed the nature of a human goal; that is another matter. And human activity, occurring under the condition of a strong desire, does not imply that the consequence is a cause of good, nor does it reject that it is a cause of evil, but relies only on what is presented. For since one naturally acts towards the object of strong desire, there is no need for an injunction regarding heaven etc. For this very reason, it is indeed proper that the fruit of the ordained Shyena sacrifice—namely, malevolent practice in the form of killing the enemy—is a cause of evil, because the fruit is not the object of activity produced by the injunction. The prompting, however, relies on the instrument which is the meaning of the verbal root and is the object of activity produced by the injunction, and that prompting does not make a cause of evil its scope. Therefore, the general prohibition, being barred by the specific injunction, refers only to worldly killing which is not for the sake of a sacrifice and is rooted in attachment, aversion, etc.; hence, due to the difference between the Shyena and the Agni-Soma sacrifices, the absence of defect in the latter is established; for if even the Jyotishtoma, which is touched by an injunction, were the object of prohibition, then the taking of the Shodashi cup would also become a cause of evil, because of the prohibition "One does not take the Shodashi cup in the Atiratra sacrifice". Therefore, this (objection) is nothing; such is the Bhatta view (of Kumarila Bhatta). But according to the Prabhakara view, since activity towards the means to a fruit is established from attachment itself, the scriptural command (niyoga) is not the prompter; hence, since the Shyena is the object of activity born of attachment, and the injunction is indifferent, the fact that it is a cause of evil is not rejected by the injunction. But in the case of the Agni-Soma killing, which is an auxiliary of the sacrifice, since it is not a means to a fruit and there is no attachment, the injunction alone is the prompter. And since it rejects that its own scope is a cause of evil, it is the primary killing (done out of passion) that produces evil, and not that which is for the sake of the sacrifice; hence, the Jyotishtoma is not defective by being mixed with killing. This is identical (in result). However, there is this much difference: according to the Prabhakara view, the Shyena etc. are adharma (unrighteous) because they are excluded by the word "artha" (good/purpose) in the definition "Dharma is that good which is indicated by scriptural command". Whereas in the Bhatta view, only the fruit of the Shyena—namely, malevolence—is adharma because it is a cause of evil, but the Shyena itself, being ordained as a means to a desired object, is indeed dharma; and the exclusion by the word "artha" applies only to prohibited acts like eating poisoned meat (kalanja), etc. However, because it leads to an evil consequence, the wise do not treat the Shyena etc. as dharma in practice. Thus it is said: "That action which, even in its consequence, is not followed by evil, and is a cause of pure joy, is called dharma." But according to the Logicians (Naiyayikas), the meaning of an injunction consists of three things: feasibility by effort, being a cause of good, and not being a cause of evil. In the case of killing for the sake of a sacrifice, since there is no direct prohibition and no penance is prescribed, the fact that it is not a cause of evil is also indicated by the injunction, along with feasibility and being a cause of good; hence it is not a cause of evil. But since the Shyena, being a malevolent practice, is directly prohibited and penance is prescribed for it, it is known to be a cause of evil, and that aspect (not being a cause of evil) is not indicated there by the injunction; thus, the difference between the Shyena and the Agni-Soma sacrifices is established. By the Vedantins (Upanishadas), the Bhatta view is mostly adopted in worldly transactions. And so is the Sutra composed by the Lord Badarayana: "If it be said that [sacrificial work] is impure, we say no, because of scriptural authority" (Brahma Sutra 3.1.25). The literal meaning is: if it be said that the Jyotishtoma etc. are defective because they are mixed with the killing of animals dedicated to Agni-Soma, we say no, because of the scriptural words of injunction like "One immolates an animal dedicated to Agni-Soma". But the sentence praising japa does not indicate that killing for the sake of a sacrifice is adharma, because it does not have its purport there. Thus, the Sankhyas' view of the ordained as prohibited, the non-cause of evil as a cause of evil, dharma as adharma, and that which is to be performed as not to be performed, is delusion in the form of erroneous knowledge. Therefore, the abandonment of daily duties out of delusion is declared to be Tamasic. Delusion indeed is Tamas.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Now, He shows the promised threefold nature of abandonment in three verses beginning with "niyatasya...". The renunciation of actions done for desired results (kamyakarmas) is proper because they are binding. But the renunciation, i.e., abandonment, of obligatory action (niyata), namely daily duties (nityakarma), is not proper; because it is the cause of liberation through the purification of the mind. Therefore, the abandonment of that (obligatory action) can arise only from delusion, characterized by the thought "this is to be abandoned" even though it is worthy of adoption. And since delusion is Tamasic, that abandonment is declared to be Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having stated His own conclusion, He begins to show the threefold nature of abandonment. But the sannyasa, i.e., abandonment, of obligatory action (niyata), namely daily duty (nityakarma), by a seeker of liberation who is ignorant but qualified for rites, is not proper, i.e., is not logical; because it is self-contradictory to say "an action is obligatory, meaning it must be performed, and yet it is abandoned". The abandonment of that obligatory action, which is ordained by the Vedas as an absolute duty, due to delusion, i.e., not knowing its purifying nature, is declared to be Tamasic. The meaning is that delusion is Tamas, and this abandonment is caused by that.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Thus, by stating their purifying nature, the view that "actions should be abandoned as full of defects" (18.3) is refuted. The meaning stated with "Hear My decision..." (18.4) is strengthened in the form of a conclusion by this verse "etānyapi" for the sake of determining the absence of defect; therefore, there is no tautology. He connects the preceding and succeeding texts as cause and effect by saying: "Since...". To indicate that they share the same status as worship (upasana) indicated by the word "manisi", the word "api" is used; hence he says: "like worship, these actions also". To establish the purifying nature of actions through the pleasing of the Supreme Self, he says: "as a part of My worship". He avoids the tautology of the word "sanga" with the statement of abandonment of fruits by explaining it as: "possessiveness towards actions". By "certain" — the idea is that you should not doubt this again. By "best" — the idea is that all other views of those who are not omniscient, which are contrary to this and advocate the abandonment of the essential nature of action, are inferior and should be disregarded.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus stated His decided conclusion, He states the threefold nature mentioned before in three verses beginning with "niyatasya...". But the sannyasa, i.e., abandonment, of obligatory action (niyata), which is spoken of as an auxiliary to devotion, is not proper (na upapadyate); literally, it means "does not lead to the presence (upa) of the Lord". Therefore, the abandonment of such action, done out of delusion or error thinking that "abandonment of actions itself is for the sake of liberation", is declared to be Tamasic, i.e., of the nature of ignorance.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He states the threefold nature of abandonment promised earlier in the verse "niyatasya...". The word "tu" is for the purpose of showing distinction from the two views mentioned before. Because the sannyasa, i.e., physical abandonment, of obligatory action (niyata) which must be performed by a qualified seeker of liberation, is not proper, i.e., is not logical; since an ignorant person requires purification. This being so, the abandonment of that obligatory action due to delusion, i.e., ignorance, is declared to be Tamasic; because it is self-contradictory to say "it is necessary, and yet it is abandoned".

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Prescribed duties should never be renounced. If one gives up his prescribed duties because of illusion, such renunciation is said to be in the mode of ignorance.

 Work for material satisfaction must be given up, but activities which promote one to spiritual activity, like cooking for the Supreme Lord and offering the food to the Lord and then accepting the food, are recommended. It is said that a person in the renounced order of life should not cook for himself. Cooking for oneself is prohibited, but cooking for the Supreme Lord is not prohibited. Similarly, a sannyāsī may perform a marriage ceremony to help his disciple in the advancement of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If one renounces such activities, it is to be understood that he is acting in the mode of darkness.
