Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
iṣṭānbhogānhi vo devā dāsyante yajñabhāvitāḥ .
tairdattānapradāyaibhyo yo bhuṅkte stena eva saḥ

Word Meanings:
इष्टान्भोगान्हि - desired enjoyments indeed
वो - to you
देवा - the gods
दास्यन्ते - will give
यज्ञभाविताः - nourished by sacrifice
तैर्दत्तानप्रदायैभ्यो - without offering what is given by them
यो - he who
भुङ्क्ते - enjoys
स्तेन - a thief
एव - also
सः - he

Life Application:
Give back to society and nature for the blessings you receive, ensuring you do not consume selfishly without contributing.

"The gods, nourished by sacrifice, will grant you the desired enjoyments." He who enjoys their gifts without offering to them in return is verily a thief.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Enjoying resources provided by Supreme Lord Vishnu without offering them as Naivedya and Yajna-part is direct theft.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: One who consumes food and resources provided by the gods without offering back via Pancha-maha-yajna is designated as a thief.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
"The gods, nourished by sacrifice, will grant you the desired enjoyments." He who enjoys their gifts without offering to them in return is verily a thief.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Consuming food and garments given by the demigods (limbs of the Supreme Lord) without first offering them back in Yajna is stealing.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 3.12–3.13 jointly): "Istan etc. and Yajnasishtasinah etc."—the virtuous eating remnants of sacrifice are freed from all sins; but those who cook for their own sakes eat sin.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The gods, nourished by sacrifice, will grant you the desired enjoyments. He who enjoys their gifts without offering to them in return is verily a thief"—condemns consuming cosmic resources without discharging the debt to gods, ancestors, and nature.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Consuming God-given wealth without first surrendering it to Him is the root of unrighteousness.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Offering all Krishna-given objects in Divine Service and partaking of Prasada is duty; otherwise one incurs sin.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: A person who feeds himself without repaying debts to sages, gods, and ancestors becomes guilty of sin.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
"The gods, nourished by sacrifice, will grant you the desired enjoyments." He who enjoys their gifts without offering to them in return is verily a thief.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis proving why one who consumes resources without offering back to the demigods is considered a thief.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Consuming wealth without offering Yajna-share to Vasudeva makes the soul punishable like a thief.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When a seeker fails to utilize Lord-given gifts in Krishna"s service, he becomes guilty of theft.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Verily he is a thief"—one who cooks solely for himself abandoning the five great sacrifices is a thief.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
In charge of the various necessities of life, the demigods, being satisfied by the performance of yajna [sacrifice], will supply all necessities to you. But he who enjoys these gifts without offering them to the demigods in return is certainly a thief.
