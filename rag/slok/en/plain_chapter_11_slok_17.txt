Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Describing the Universal Form further, Arjuna tells what he saw in that inconceivable, ungraspable divine form. He saw there Lord Vishnu, wearing a crown and holding the conch, discus, and mace. According to the descriptions in the Puranas, the conch, discus, etc., are the insignias or symbols of Lord Vishnu. In Hindu scriptures, deities are described as possessing certain special weapons or symbols which also have specific meanings. These special insignias indicate their lordship and sovereignty over the world. A person who is the master of external circumstances and the ruler of the natural tendencies of the mind is, in reality, worthy of being called Lord or God. One who remains a slave to his mind and external attractions is weak; even if he wears a royal crown, his kingship is as transient as that of an actor playing the role of a king on stage wearing a fake crown. A ruler cannot achieve real authority or effectiveness without sense-control and mind-control. No one can lead a happy and powerful life without crowning oneself over the kingdom of one's own mind by conquering lower sensual tendencies. A self-controlled man alone is Vishnu, and he alone is entitled to the royal crown. The four-armed Vishnu holds a conch, discus, mace, and lotus in His hands. This is a symbolic metaphor. In India, the lotus flower is a symbol of peace, joy, auspiciousness, and happiness. The blowing of the conch calls man to his duty. If a generation of human beings does not listen to this higher call of their heart, then unrest, war, epidemic, famine, storm, communal hatred, and social disorder spread everywhere. This is the blow of the mace upon that generation, dealt to bring them to order and discipline. If there is a generation that, despite receiving such punishment, does not learn any lesson from it, then comes the discus — the wheel of time (Kala-chakra) — which destroys that unreformable generation. From the description given by Arjuna, it is known that the one supreme Truth is the substratum for everything from Brahma down to an ant. That Truth is always and everywhere one; only Its expression is diverse. The variation in the expression of Its divinity is due to the different gross and subtle conditioning equipment (upadhis) through which that Truth is manifested. This Universal Form is a mass of radiance shining everywhere, glowing like blazing fire and the sun, and very difficult to look at. In the description given in this verse, this line is the most expressive, giving us a clear understanding of the pure Consciousness (Purusha). This should not be misunderstood as physical light. Although this word is borrowed from worldly language, yet its use is intentional. Consciousness alone is that light in which we clearly see the feelings of our mind and the thoughts of our intellect. It is this Consciousness that illumines form and color through the eyes, and sound through the ears respectively. Therefore, it is but natural that the description of the Universal Form of Lord Krishna, who is infinite Consciousness, had to be made by Arjuna in faltering language in this manner — that the Universal Form is a mass of splendour, which blinds the senses, mind, and intellect, meaning that these conditioning equipments (upadhis) are unable to grasp It. Immeasurable (un-knowable): Although up to this point Arjuna has tried his best to describe the Cosmic Form and the feelings of the mind generated by its vision, yet in all these verses, a faint stream of disappointment seems to flow. Arjuna feels that he has not been able to express the fullness of the subject matter within the limits of language. Language can only describe that object which has been seen by the senses, experienced by the mind, or understood by the intellect. Here before Arjuna is a vision which he is experiencing, seeing, and himself understanding through intellect, and yet, what a strange experience that when he tries to bottle it up in language, it evaporates as if into vapor! Arjuna tries to describe it in the language of sensory experiences and feelings, but he is not satisfied with that description himself. A wonder-struck human being is trying to sing of that glory in the language of his intellect. But here also, being disappointed, he can only say, "O Lord, You are ever immeasurable — un-knowable." Although the poet has depicted the Cosmic Form in visual terms, yet he wants to make us understand that the Self, which is Truth-existence (Sat), is indeed the Seer, and It cannot become an object of knowledge even for the intellect. The Self is the Seer and the Knower, and not the seen or the object of knowledge (prameya). From this vision of Your divine Yoga, I infer that

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
By the statement 'blazing like fire and the sun' (analarka-dyutim), the doubt of limitation is dispelled by the word 'immeasurable' (aprameyam).

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He describes the Lord possessing the Universal Form in another manner: 'Moreover' (kiñca). He rules out finiteness/limitation by the word 'everywhere' (sarvataḥ). There is no contradiction between 'seeing' and 'difficult to look at' due to the difference in the eligibility of the observers. His vision is not just in the front, back, or sides, but everywhere, which is indicated by 'on all sides' (samantataḥ). He clarifies the state of being luminous with an illustration: 'blazing' (dīpta).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
I see You wearing a crown, mace, and discus (as well as conch and lotus). I see You as a mass of radiance, shining in all directions, having splendour like blazing fire and the sun, difficult to see with the eyes, and immeasurable in every way.

Commentary: 'kiritinam gadinam cakrinam ca' — I see You wearing a crown, mace, and discus. Here, the word 'ca' (and) should also include the conch and lotus. Its meaning appears to be that Arjuna is also seeing the four-armed form of Lord Vishnu within the Cosmic Form.

'tejorasim' — You are a mass of radiance, as if a whole collection of energy (infinite radiance) has gathered together. Sanjaya described this earlier, stating that even if thousands of suns were to rise simultaneously in the sky, they could not match the splendour of the Lord (11.12). Such is Your form of light. 'sarvato diptimantam' — Being self-luminous, You are shining in all directions. 'pasyami tvam durniriksyam samantad diptanalarkadyutimaprameyam' — Your splendour is like blazing fire and the sun. Just as the eyes are dazzled before the bright light of the sun, similarly, on seeing You, the eyes are dazzled. Therefore, You are difficult to look at. One cannot see You properly. [Here is a great wonder: the Lord had given divine vision to Arjuna, yet even with that divine vision, Arjuna is not fully able to look at the Universal Form! Such is the blazing form of the Lord!] You are immeasurable (infinite) from all sides, which means You are not an object of proof (measurement). None of the proofs like direct perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, postulation, non-perception, etc., work in defining You, because the power in all these proofs comes from You alone.

Connection: Now, in the next verse, Arjuna praises the Lord, seeing Him in formless-attributeless (nirguna-nirakara), formless-with-attributes (saguna-nirakara), and with-form-and-attributes (saguna-sakara) aspects.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Objection: If it is said 'blazing like fire and the sun' (analarka-dyutim), does it not make the earlier statement (that the word 'thousand' means 'infinite', etc.) inappropriate [since fire and sun are finite]?
Answer: No. This is said precisely to address this doubt and to provide assurance. In reality, the Lord possesses unlimited splendour, as has been declared by the Lord Himself; therefore he says 'anala...'. This is said to remove the doubt regarding the limitation of splendour that arises upon such a statement. And this [word 'immeasurable'], being a qualifier of the qualified, is connected with 'splendour' (dyuti).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Regarding 'kiritinam' (wearing crowns): Here, many crowns, etc., which are symbols of supreme lordship (parameṣṭhya-pada), are to be understood, because [the Universal Form] does not possess only single limbs [but many heads, arms, etc.].

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
He qualifies/describes the same Lord of the Universal Form in another manner: 'kiritinam' (wearing a diadem). I see You with my divine eye on all sides — You who wear a crown, mace, and discus, who are a mass of radiance shining everywhere, and therefore difficult to look at (meaning impossible to be seen without the divine eye). In the reading containing the letter 'ya' (i.e., durnirikṣyam), the prefix 'duḥ' acts as a negation, meaning virtually 'impossible to look at' (anirikṣyam). One whose splendour is like the splendour of the blazing fire and the sun; You who are immeasurable, meaning impossible to be limited or defined as 'He is like this or of this measure'. Therefore, there is no contradiction between 'difficult to look at' and 'I see' due to the difference in the capability of the observers.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Furthermore, 'kiritinam' (wearing a diadem) means wearing a crown; 'gadinam' means wielding a mace; 'cakrinam' means holding a discus; shining everywhere, in the form of a mass of radiance; and likewise, 'durnirikṣyam' means impossible to behold. The reason for that is: He whose radiance (tejas) is like the splendour of the blazing fire and the sun. Therefore, I see You on all sides as 'immeasurable' (aprameyam), meaning one whose nature is impossible to be determined or defined as 'He is like this'.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Furthermore, 'kiritinam' means one who possesses a special head ornament. 'gadi' means one who has a mace; 'cakri' means one who has a discus. Therefore, one whose splendour is like the splendour of the blazing fire and the sun everywhere, on all sides. Consequently, I see You who are 'durnirikṣyam' (visible with great difficulty), and who, despite possessing a crown, mace, etc., are 'immeasurable' (aprameyam), meaning impossible to be limited or defined.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Regarding 'kiritinam' (wearing a diadem): Disregarding the reading order of 'kiritinam' etc., he states the syntactic connection (anvaya) based on the order of perception and meaning, starting with 'tejorasim' etc. 'tejorasim' (a mass of radiance) indicates the nature of the possessor of attributes (dharmi). 'sarvato diptimantam' (glowing all around) refers to Him who possesses all-pervasive light, as in "by His light all this is illumined" (Katha Upanishad 2.2.15). 'samantat' (on all sides) is said to refer to the entire region of His divine body. 'diptanalarkadyutim' (splendour like blazing fire and the sun) is the cause for the difficulty in looking at Him. Here, by the word 'dyuti' (splendour), the light in the form of rays is intended; hence, there is no tautology with 'diptimantam'. Alternatively, the former ('diptimantam') refers to the all-pervasiveness, while the latter ('diptanalarkadyutim') refers to the extreme intensity which makes it difficult to look at Him. 'aprameyam' means impossible to be limited or measured by 'being like this' or 'being of this much measure'. Having stated the general perception up to this point, it is said specifically "You, who are immeasurable" (aprameyam tvam). He says, "I see You as wearing a crown, mace, and discus," with the intention that even while seeing a form never seen before, he recognizes the Lord by His extraordinary and unique symbols. Here, 'kiritinam' is an indirect indication (upalaksana) of the entire class of ornaments. Even there, the specific mention of the crown (kirita) is because it manifests His supreme lordship over all. There are indeed two kinds of crowns: 'kirita' and 'karandika'; among them, 'kirita' is the superior one to be worn. 'gadinam cakrinam' is an indirect indication of the entire class of weapons. Even there, the designation of the discus (cakra), etc., is due to its being well-known as the unique weapon of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Furthermore, 'kiritinam' means adorned with a crown, who is full of nectar/sweetness (rasatmakam). 'gadinam' means the possessor of the divine properties of all life-force (prana). 'cakrinam' means the holder of Sudarshana in the form of light/splendour. By the word 'ca' (and), holding the discus for the purpose of granting liberation is also indicated. 'tejorasim' means in the nature of a mass of splendour. 'sarvato diptimantam' means possessed of illuminating rays all around. To illustrate being filled with splendour and light, he gives the example: 'diptanalarkadyutim' — one whose splendour is like the splendour of the blazing fire and the sun. I see You who are immeasurable (aprameyam), meaning impossible to be measured, and who are difficult to look at on all sides.
