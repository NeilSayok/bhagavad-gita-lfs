Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
cañcalaṃ hi manaḥ kṛṣṇa pramāthi balavad dṛḍham .
tasyāhaṃ nigrahaṃ manye vāyoriva suduṣkaram

Word Meanings:
चञ्चलं - restless
हि - certainly
मनः - mind
कृष्ण - O Krishna
प्रमाथि - turbulent
बलवद् - powerful
दृढम् - dearly
तस्याहं - to him I am
निग्रहं - control
मन्ये - I consider
वायोरिव - like that of the wind
सुदुष्करम् - very difficult

Life Application:
Recognize that the mind is turbulent, powerful, and stubborn, requiring patient effort to harness like the wind.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The mind indeed is restless, O Krishna, turbulent, strong, and unyielding; I consider its control to be as difficult as controlling the wind.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "As difficult as holding the wind"—just as stopping the wind is difficult, controlling the mind by material force is impossible; it becomes possible solely through devotion to Vishnu.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The mind is not merely flickering, but turbulent, agitating body and senses, exceedingly strong and obstinate, making its control as difficult as holding back the wind.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: The mind is turbulent (pramathi) and powerful, agitating the body; restraining it is as difficult as holding back the wild wind with one"s bare hands.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: O Krishna, the mind is restless, turbulent, powerful, and obstinate; I deem its control to be as difficult as holding the wind.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The mind indeed is restless, O Krishna, turbulent, strong, and unyielding; I consider its control to be as difficult as controlling the wind"—explains the four qualities of mind (Chanchala, Pramathi, Balavat, Dridha) using the classical simile of holding back the tempest (*Vayor iva su-dushkaram*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The wind analogy highlights the invincible nature of the mind, which cannot be subdued without divine grace.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The mind is fiercely restless by nature; until surrendered at Krishna"s lotus feet, its mastery remains impossible.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "I consider its control difficult"—owing to the mind"s four flaws: restlessness, turbulence, strength, and obstinacy, controlling it is as hard as stopping the wind.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the fourfold characteristics of mind"s restlessness and the wind analogy.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The exceedingly powerful mind can be mastered solely through taking refuge in Lord Vasudeva.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker surrenders acknowledging his weakness, Lord Krishna Himself stills the restless mind.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Turbulent, strong, unyielding"—the mind agitating the senses can be subdued gradually only through discrimination and practice.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
For the mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Krishna, and to subdue it, it seems to me, is more difficult than controlling the wind.
