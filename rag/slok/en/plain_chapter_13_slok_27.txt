Life Application:
Know that whatever creature is born, unmoving or moving, arises from the union of field and knower of field.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Neither of the two, the Field (Nature) and the Knower of the Field (Purusha), is independently the sole cause of this world of change (moving and unmoving). Through the combination of these two, the universe is born, but this combination is not real, rather it is in the form of mutual superimposition (adhyasa) of each other's attributes. In the process of superimposition, on an existing substratum, another object is imagined due to delusion, such as the superimposition of a ghost on a pillar. In this type of superimposition, the deluded person sees the form, qualities, and actions of a ghost which is actually non-existent in the pillar. This is the superimposition of the ghost's attributes on the pillar. Similarly, even though the ghost is non-existent, it appears to that person as real (i.e. 'existing'), but its existence actually belongs to the pillar. This is the attribution of the pillar's existence to the ghost. Due to this mutual superimposition of qualities, the strange thing is that the person sees the false ghost but not the real pillar. This peculiar trick of the mind is called superimposition (adhyasa). In pure Consciousness, the Field is completely absent. The Field has neither its own existence nor consciousness. Yet, through their strange mutual combination, the creation of this moving and unmoving world seems to occur. We can experience the function of this superimposition within ourselves. Upon reflection, the diverse creation disappears, and we realize that Brahman alone is the supreme reality, the substratum upon which the play of Nature and Spirit (Purusha) is taking place. For example, a person is generally of a calm nature. But occasionally, a strong desire arises in his mind. By identifying with that desire, that person becomes lustful and commits a contemptible act, which he later regrets. In this example, the desire, the lustful person, and the regret all have their existence inherent in that person alone. Even though they are in him, he is not actually in them; because even without them, his existence remains. Yet, by identifying with that desire, he becomes the agent of deeds that deserve regret. Similarly, the Self being complete, there is the possibility of the Field or non-Self in It. Manifesting Nature and identifying with it, it becomes the individual soul (Purusha). This Purusha maintains its samsara through false attachments. In this situation, the only way to free oneself and realize one's complete nature is to discriminate between the Self and the non-Self with proof, and to remain separate from Nature, as a witness to its activities. The next verse explains the right vision obtained through discrimination.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
To introduce the subsequent text, he recalls the distant context by 'na' etc. The word 'adi' (etc.) means the beginningless, indefinable ignorance (avidya), false knowledge, and its impressions. After restating the distant context, he restates the immediate context by 'janma' (birth) etc. Since ignorance, in both distant and immediate ways, is the root of all evils, right knowledge which removes it must be expounded; hence he says 'atah' (therefore). Fearing that since right knowledge has already been declared multiple times, its repetition is futile, he states 'uktam' (it has been said), meaning that the repetition of an extremely subtle truth through different words is for the benefit of different qualified seekers. Since the Supreme Self is one everywhere, it has no superiority or inferiority; hence he says 'samam' (equally). He establishes His supremacy and Lordship by 'deha' (body) etc. The connection is with 'tam' (him) referring to the soul (jiva) etc. Fearing that when the support (body) is destroyed, the supported (self) also might be destroyed, he says 'tam ca' (and that) etc. The connection is that he qualifies it by the word 'avinasayantam' (non-perishing). He explains the significance of both adjectives in both cases by 'bhutanam' (of beings) etc. He doubts by 'katham' (how), as to how there is absolute difference despite the difference between perishing and non-perishing, since having attributes and being separate are common. To state that though beings possess attributes etc., the Supreme does not, and hence there is absolute difference, he declares birth to be the first among the modifications of being, by saying 'sarvesham' (of all) etc. He gives the reason for it by 'janma' (birth) etc. For without birth, subsequent modifications cannot occur, as they are observed only in that which has birth. Anticipating the objection that since some modifications may occur even after destruction, destruction is not the final modification, he says 'vinashat' (from destruction) etc. Having established that destruction is the final modification, he states the result by 'atah' (therefore) etc. The effects of birth etc., which have temporary existence, along with their substrata, is the meaning. He concludes the absolute difference between Ishvara and beings by saying 'tasmat' (therefore) etc., namely, being attributeless, free from all modifications of existence, immutable, single, and non-dual. He explains 'yah pasyati' (who sees) etc. by 'yah evam' (who thus) etc. He raises an objection against the statement that 'only he who sees Ishvara with the aforesaid attributes sees' by 'nanu' (but) etc. He resolves it by saying 'satyam' (true) etc., meaning that since one who is turned away from Ishvara and established in non-self sees falsely even if he seems to see, only one who is devoted to Ishvara possesses right vision; this is what is intended by the adjective. He explains this very point with an illustration by 'yatha' (as) etc. He concludes the meaning of 'yah pasyati' by 'itarah' (the other) etc., meaning, those who are different from those established in the Supreme Reality.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, best among the Bharatas! Whatever beings are born, whether moving or unmoving, know them to be born from the union of the Field (ksetra) and the Knower of the Field (ksetrajna).

Commentary:
yāvatsañjāyate ... kṣetrakṣetrajñasaṃyogāt — All stationary beings such as trees, creepers, grass, shrubs, bamboo, mountains etc., and all moving beings (terrestrial, aquatic, and aerial) such as humans, demigods, animals, birds, insects, fish, turtles etc., are all born only from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field.
The transient entities are the Field, and the one who knows this Field—who is birthless, deathless, and ever immutable—is the Knower of the Field. The Knower of the Field (the soul identifying with nature) assuming a relationship of 'I' and 'mine' with the body is what is meant by the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field. Due to this assumed union, this individual soul has to take birth in various moving and stationary wombs. This very union of the Field and the Knower of the Field was previously referred to by the term 'attachment to qualities' (guṇa-saṅgaḥ) in the twenty-first verse. The meaning is that by identifying with the constantly changing nature and its products (like the body), the soul also considers itself as subject to birth and death.
[The birth of moving and non-moving beings is mentioned here by the word 'sañjāyate' (is born), and their death will be spoken of in the next verse by the word 'vinaśyatsu' (perishing).]
tadviddhi bharatarṣabha — This Knower of the Field assumes a relationship with the Field, and hence its birth takes place. But when it does not assume a relationship with the body, its birth does not occur—understand this clearly.
Connection — In the previous verse, the Lord stated that by keeping a connection with the Field (body) and looking towards it, this soul falls into birth and death. Now the question arises: what should one do to get out of this cycle of birth and death? The Lord answers this in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Bharatarsabha, O scion of the Bharata dynasty; yavat kincit, whatever; sattvam, object;-as to whether they are without exception the Lord says-sthavara-jangamam, moving or non-moving; sanjayate, comes into being; viddhi, know; tat, that; as originating ksetra-ksetrajna-samyogat, from the association of the field and the Knower of the field.
Objection: What, again, is meant by this 'association of the field and the Knower of the field'? Since the Knower of the field is partless like space, therefore Its conjunction with the field cannot be a kind of relationship like coming together of a rope and a pot through the contact of their parts. Nor can it be an intimate and inseparable relation as between a thread and a cloth, since it is not admitted that the field and the Knower of the field are mutually related by way of being cause and effect.
Reply: The answer is: The association of the field and the Knower of the field-which are the object and the subject, respectively, and are of different natures-is in the form of superimposition of each on the other an also of their alities, as a conseence of the absence of discrimination between the real natures of the field and the Knower of the field. This is like the association of a rope, nacre, etc. with the superimposed snake, silver, etc. owing to the absence of discrimination between them. This association of the field and the Knower of the field in the form of superimposition is described as false knowledge. After having known the distinction between and the characteristics of the field and the Knower of the field according to the scriptures, and having separated, like a stalk from the Munjagrass, the above-described Knower of the field from the field whose characteristics have been shown earlier, he who realizes the Knowable (i.e. the Knower of the field)-which, in accordance with 'That is neither called being nor non-being' (12), is devoid of all distinctions created by adjuncts- as identical with Brahman; and he who has the firm realization that the field is surely unreal like an elephant created by magic, a thing seen in a dream, an imaginary city seen in the sky, etc., and it appears as though real-for him false knowledge becomes eradicated, since it is opposed to the right knowledge described above.
Since the cause of his rirth has been eliminated. therefore what was said in, 'He who knows thus the Person and Nature along with the alities৷৷.', that the man of realization is not born again (23), has been a reasonable statement. 
In 'He৷৷.will not be born again' (23) has been stated the result of right knowledge, which is the absence of birth owing to the destruction of ignorance etc., the seeds of worldly existence. The cause of birth, viz the association of the field and the Knower of the field brought about by ignorance, has also been stated. Hence, although right knowledge, which is the remover of that ignorance, has been spoken of, still it is being stated over again in other words:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Since all that was promised in 'tat ksetram yac ca...' (13.4) has already been explained, what is the purpose of the subsequent section? To this, he says 'punas ca' (and again). What is the reason for repeating what has already been said? To this, he says 'samyadi' (equality etc.). The term 'samya' (equality) is used because the attributes of Ishvara are principal compared to the attributes of both (Prakriti and Purusha). The description of the characteristics of Prakriti and Purusha is included in the promise of 'yadrak' (what it is like), and the description of Ishvara's characteristics is included in 'yat-prabhavah' (what His powers are).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Now, in order to state the method of inquiry for the discrimination of the self associated with Prakriti according to the Sankhya school, he says that any moving or unmoving being is born of the contact of Sat (Existence/Matter) and Chit (Consciousness/Spirit) by the verse beginning with 'yavat'. 'Sattvam' means any living being, moving or unmoving, that comes into existence. That is due to the relationship between the Field and the Knower of the Field. As stated in the Bhagavata Purana (10.87.31): 'Living beings come into existence through the union of both, like water-bubbles.' Due to the mutual connection of the Field and the Self, they are born here only in a state of union, never separated from each other; this is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Since the worldly existence (samsara) is caused by ignorance (avidya), liberation is possible through knowledge (vidya). To determine this truth, the worldly existence and the knowledge that removes it are elaborated until the end of the chapter. And the Lord explains in detail what was said previously in 'attachment to qualities is the cause of his birth in good and evil wombs' (13.21) by the verse starting with 'yavat'. O best of the Bharatas, know that whatever object or being, moving or unmoving, comes into existence, it is all born from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field. The 'Field' is the insentient, indefinable world of objects, characterized by existence and non-existence, consisting of ignorance and its effects. The 'Knower of the Field' is entirely distinct from it, the illuminator of it, self-luminous, absolute, existence-consciousness, unattached, indifferent, attributeless, and non-dual. The union between them is a false identification (adhyasa) in the form of coupling the real and the unreal, caused by mutual non-discrimination due to Maya. Know that all effects are born from that union alone. Therefore, the worldly existence, which is based on the ignorance of one's real nature, is fit to be destroyed by the knowledge of one's real nature, just like a dream; this is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Since Karma-yoga has been elaborated in the third, fourth, and fifth chapters, and Dhyana-yoga in the sixth and eighth chapters, and since meditation (dhyana) etc. has for its object the Self discriminated through Sankhya, the Lord elaborates Sankhya itself until the end of the chapter, beginning with the verse 'yavat' etc. Know that whatever living being or object is born, it all comes into existence from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field, which is a false identification (adhyasa) of identity caused by non-discrimination.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Here, the knowledge regarding the identity of the Knower of the Field and the Lord, as stated in 'Know Me also as the Knower of the Field' (13.2), is declared as the means to liberation by 'knowing which one attains immortality' (13.12). The Lord states the reason for that in the verse beginning with 'yavat'. Know that whatever object or being (sattvam), moving or unmoving, comes into existence, it is all born from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field. To indicate that 'you are qualified to know this', He addresses Arjuna as 'O best of the Bharatas' (bharatarṣabha).
Objection: The Lord's statement 'from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field' is inconsistent. Since the Knower of the Field is partless like space, its conjunction with the Field through the contact of parts (like a rope and a pot) is impossible. Nor can there be an inherent relationship (samavaya) between them (like thread and cloth) because they are not admitted to be mutually related as cause and effect. Also, their identity is impossible because they are of opposite natures like darkness and light.
Reply: No, this is not a valid objection. Just as a snake is superimposed on a rope, or silver on a mother-of-pearl, due to the lack of knowledge of their true nature, so too, there is a union between the Field and the Knower of the Field (which are the object and the subject, and of opposite natures) in the form of mutual superimposition (adhyasa) of each other's attributes, caused by the lack of discrimination between their real natures. Therefore, he who, in accordance with the scriptures, separates the Knower of the Field possessing the described characteristics from the Field, just like pulling out a stalk from the Munja grass, and sees Him as identical with Ishvara and free from all limiting adjuncts; and who has the firm realization that the Field is unreal like an elephant created by magic, a thing seen in a dream, an imaginary city, a double moon, or a snake in a rope, and yet appears as though real; for him, through right vision, false knowledge which is the cause of birth is eradicated, and liberation is attained, and not for anyone else. Hence, what was said in 'He who knows thus...' (13.23) is indeed reasonable.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Thus, the vision of the Self has been stated. For that purpose, the method of inquiry into the discrimination of Prakriti and Purusha will be spoken of in the verses starting with 'samam sarvesu' (13.28). This teaching is appropriate only when there is a perception of non-discrimination (between the body and the soul), otherwise it would be useless. And that non-discrimination does not occur without a defect; and the defect here is the specific contact that brings about the condition of being the enjoyer and the locus of enjoyment. This is what is stated in the verse 'yavat sanjayate' (whatever is born); hence the connection is introduced by the word 'atha' (now). By the word 'all', the sense of completeness of 'yavat' is expressed here. The view that explains the word 'yavat' in the sense of the relative pronoun 'yat' is incorrect, as it is non-expressive and serves little purpose. The word 'sattvam' here refers to a living being (jantu), as per the Amarakosha (3.3.212) which states: 'Sattva in the neuter gender refers to substance, life-breath, determination, and living beings'. The word 'sthavara' (unmoving) is used here to refute the view of the Jains who consider trees, shrubs, creepers, plants, grass, etc., as completely insentient merely due to the absence of the manifestation of consciousness. The phrase 'in the form of moving and unmoving' is used to show that moving and unmoving states, like childhood, youth, old age, etc., do not persist for the entire duration of the body. The phrase 'from mutual union' is stated to dispel the doubt of union with anything other than the Field and the Knower of the Field. To emphasize the essential point, it is stated 'from union alone'. Lest it be thought that another being is born from the union of the Field and the Knower of the Field, just as a son is born from the union of mother and father, he says 'only in a state of union'. To exclude the well-known relationship of separate existences between the Field and the Owner of the Field, he says 'but not'. In 'tad viddhi' (know that), the word 'tat' refers to birth.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Previously, under 'in the matter of causation of effect and instrument...' Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O chief of the Bhāratas, know that whatever you see in existence, both the moving and the nonmoving, is only a combination of the field of activities and the knower of the field.

 Both material nature and the living entity, which were existing before the creation of the cosmos, are explained in this verse. Whatever is created is but a combination of the living entity and material nature. There are many manifestations like trees, mountains and hills which are not moving, and there are many existences which are moving, and all of them are but combinations of material nature and the superior nature, the living entity. Without the touch of the superior nature, the living entity, nothing can grow. The relationship between material nature and spiritual nature is eternally going on, and this combination is effected by the Supreme Lord; therefore He is the controller of both the superior and inferior natures. The material nature is created by Him, and the superior nature is placed in this material nature, and thus all these activities and manifestations take place.
