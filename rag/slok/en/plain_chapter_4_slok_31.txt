Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yajñaśiṣṭāmṛtabhujo yānti brahma sanātanam .
nāyaṃ loko.astyayajñasya kuto.anyaḥ kurusattama

Word Meanings:
यज्ञशिष्टामृतभुजो - eating nectar of sacrifice remnants
यान्ति - attain
ब्रह्म - Brahman
सनातनम् - eternal
नायं - not this
लोकोऽस्त्ययज्ञस्य - world is for non-sacrificer
कुतोऽन्यः - how another
कुरुसत्तम - O best of Kurus

Life Application:
Live generously by offering your best before partaking, knowing that a life without sacrifice brings no true joy.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The eaters of the nectar of the remnant of sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. Not for the non-sacrificer is this world; how then the other, O best of the Kurus?

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The eaters of the nectar of the remnant of sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. Not for the non-sacrificer is this world; how then the other, O best of the Kurus?

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Those who partake of the ambrosial nectar of the remnants of sacrifice (Yajnashishtamrita) attain to the eternal Brahman. But for him who performs no sacrifice, O best of the Kurus, even this mortal world offers no happiness; how then can the higher world be attained by him?

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Go to eternal Brahman"—devotees who faithfully partake of the sacred remnants (Prasada) of Lord Vishnu"s Yajna reach the eternal abode of Vaikuntha.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Those who partake of sanctified remnants of Yajna attain eternal Brahman through mental purity; non-sacrificers lose both this world and the next.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The eaters of the nectar of the remnant of sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. Not for the non-sacrificer is this world; how then the other, O best of the Kurus?

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "Not for non-sacrificer is this world"—devotees partaking of Yajna-remnants reach eternal Brahman; one devoid of Yajna forfeits both earthly and celestial goals.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Yajna-etc. They enjoy the nectar of the remnant of sacrifice and go to eternal Brahman; for the non-sacrificer, not even this world is happy, how then the other world?

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The eaters of the nectar of the remnant of sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. Not for the non-sacrificer is this world; how then the other, O best of the Kurus?"—stresses that spiritual sacrifice is indispensable for both earthly well-being and ultimate Moksha.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Partaking of divine nectar (Prasada) purifies the practitioner"s intellect, enabling direct realization of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Loving devotees relishing Krishna"s holy remnants with exclusive devotion gain entrance into the Lord"s eternal realm.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Not for the non-sacrificer is this world"—one devoid of sacrifice lacks mental purity, missing spiritual welfare in both worlds.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how partaking of sacrificial nectar yields Brahman, while neglecting sacrifice forfeits happiness in both realms.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Enjoying sacred remnants of Yajna dedicated to Vasudeva delivers the seeker from nescience to the eternal realm.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Tasting Lord Krishna"s sacrificial grace releases the soul from material attachment, granting eternal Brahmanhood.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Not for the non-sacrificer is this world"—one devoid of sacrificial practice retains an impure mind, deprived of all human goals.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O best of the Kuru dynasty, without sacrifice one can never live happily on this planet or in this life: what then of the next?
