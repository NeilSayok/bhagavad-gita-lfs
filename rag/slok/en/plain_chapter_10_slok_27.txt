Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
uccaiḥśravasamaśvānāṃ viddhi māmamṛtodbhavam .
airāvataṃ gajendrāṇāṃ narāṇāṃ ca narādhipam

Word Meanings:
उच्चैःश्रवसमश्वानां - among horses, Uchchaihshravas
विद्धि - know
माममृतोद्भवम् - Me, born of nectar
ऐरावतं - Airavata
गजेन्द्राणां - among lordly elephants
नराणां - among men
च - and
नराधिपम् - king

Life Application:
Know God as Uchchaihshravas among horses, Airavata among noble elephants, and the monarch among human beings.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Among horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar; among royal elephants I am Airavata, and among men I am the King.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Know Me as Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar, among horses; Airavata among kingly elephants, and the King among men.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Of horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of the ocean of nectar; of lordly elephants I am Airavata, and of men I am the King.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Among horses, Ucchaihshravas born of nectar; among royal elephants, Airavata; and among human societies, the King (symbol of rule and protection) represents the Supreme Divine.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Among horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar; among royal elephants Airavata; and among men the King.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Among horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar; among royal elephants Airavata; and among men the King.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Know Me as Ucchaihshravas among horses, born of nectar; Airavata among royal elephants, and the King among men.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: The name 'Kapila' signifies Him who is of the nature of Supreme Bliss, by whom the universe is sustained and dissolved. As declared in Vedic Sruti (Svetasvatara Upanishad & Babhravya Samaveda), Lord Vishnu Himself is called 'Kapila' because He is the source of infinite bliss, protection, and dissolution.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The celestial horse Ucchaihshravas produced along with nectar from the ocean, Airavata among royal elephants, and the protective King among men are the Lord's chief manifestations.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Among horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar; among royal elephants I am Airavata, and among men I am the King.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Among horses, Ucchaihshravas born of nectar; among elephants, Airavata; and among human beings, the Sovereign Ruler (King) is My divine manifestation.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: The Lord describes His infinite divine opulences in prominence. The Supreme Self alone abides in the hearts of all living beings; whatever is glorious, beautiful, or mighty in the universe manifests from a fragment of the Lord's divine splendor.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: Among horses know Me to be Ucchaihshravas, born of nectar; among royal elephants Airavata; and among human beings the Sovereign King.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Etymologically, 'Kapila' represents 'Ka' (Blissful), 'Pi' (Protector of the world), and 'La' (Dissolver of creation). Among perfected beings, Sage Kapila is an incarnation of the omniscient Lord Vishnu who sustains all existence through eternal wisdom.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Ucchaihshravas born of nectar, Airavata among elephants, and the Ruler among men manifest the divine Presence.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Among horses, Ucchaihshravas born from the milk ocean; among lordly elephants, Airavata; and among men, the Sovereign Ruler is My divine manifestation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Know Me as Ucchaihshravas among horses; Airavata among royal elephants, and the King among men.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Ucchaihshravas among horses, Airavata among elephants, and the protective Sovereign among men symbolize God's supreme authority.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Ucchaihshravas among horses, Airavata among elephants, and the Sovereign King among men occupy supreme positions solely by the Lord's grace.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Among horses I am Ucchaihshravas; among elephants I am Airavata; and among men I am the King.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: The horse Ucchaihshravas, the elephant Airavata, and the protective King among men represent the Lord's divine Vibhuti.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Of horses know Me to be Uccaiḥśravā, produced during the churning of the ocean for nectar. Of lordly elephants I am Airāvata, and among men I am the monarch.
