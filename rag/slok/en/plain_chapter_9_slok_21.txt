Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
te taṃ bhuktvā svargalokaṃ viśālaṃ kṣīṇe puṇye martyalokaṃ viśanti .
evaṃ trayīdharmamanuprapannā gatāgataṃ kāmakāmā labhante

Word Meanings:
ते - they
तं - that
भुक्त्वा - having enjoyed
स्वर्गलोकं - heavenly world
विशालं - vast
क्षीणे - being exhausted
पुण्ये - merit
मर्त्यलोकं - mortal world
विशन्ति - they enter
एवं - thus
त्रयीधर्ममनुप्रपन्ना - following the three Vedas
गतागतं - going and coming
कामकामा - desiring enjoyments
लभन्ते - obtain

Life Application:
Seek eternal spiritual fulfillment rather than exhausting temporary merits in fleeting celestial realms.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
They, having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, upon the exhaustion of their merit, enter the mortal world; thus following the dogma of the three Vedas, desiring desires, they obtain coming and going.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
They, having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, enter the mortal world, on the exhaustion of their merit; thus abiding by the injunctions of the three Vedas, desiring desires, they attain to the state of going and returning.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Having enjoyed that vast celestial realm, they return to the mortal world upon the exhaustion of their merit; thus, adhering to the Vedic ritualistic path with material desires, they undergo repeated birth and death.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
They, having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, upon the exhaustion of their merit, enter the mortal world; thus following the injunctions of the three Vedas, desiring desires, they obtain coming and going.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Having enjoyed the vast heavenly world, they enter the mortal world on exhaustion of merit; thus following the three Vedas with desires, they obtain coming and going.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they enter the mortal world when their merit is exhausted; thus following the way of the three Vedas, desiring desires, they obtain coming and going.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they enter the mortal world when their merit is exhausted; thus following the way of the three Vedas, desiring desires, they obtain coming and going.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: Performers of sacrifices seeking desires worship demigods to enjoy heavenly realms, but return to earth upon exhaustion of merit. Conversely, those who worship the Supreme Lord residing within the demigods with unselfish devotion attain supreme liberation.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The reward of desired-driven rites is finite and perishable; as soon as merit decays, souls return to the mortal realm bound to repeated births.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they enter the mortal realm upon the exhaustion of their merit; thus adhering to the three Vedas, seeking enjoyments, they attain repeated birth and death.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Desire-bound ritualists, after enjoying vast heavenly pleasures, return to the mortal world upon merit-exhaustion; their path yields only endless repetition.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Desire-driven practitioners of the three Vedas attain heavenly delights after drinking Soma, but return to the mortal world when their merits are exhausted. Only unselfish devotees of the Lord attain eternal liberation.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Having enjoyed that vast heaven, they enter mortal world on merit loss"—desire-seeking ritualists enjoying vast celestial pleasures return to mortal earth when merit expires; adhering to triple Veda rites, they gain only endless coming and going, never mukti.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Elucidating the supreme truth, he clarifies that desire-driven worshippers of demigods remain bound to the cycle of coming and going, whereas unselfish worshippers of all-pervading Lord Vishnu attain eternal mukti.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Practitioners of the three Vedas with material desires enjoy celestial realms and return to earth; repeated birth and death is the fruit of desired-based rites, whereas pure devotees attain eternal freedom.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: Desire-driven performers of the three Vedas, purified by Soma rites, pray for heaven and enjoy celestial delights in Indra's realm; yet upon merit-exhaustion they return to earth, proving the impermanence of fruitive works.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Having enjoyed that vast heavenly world, they enter the mortal world on exhaustion of merit; thus desire-driven ritualists attain coming and going.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: The end of desire-driven rites is impermanent worldly rebirth; souls with exhausted merit fall back to earth, establishing the supremacy of unselfish non-dual wisdom.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: The reward of desire-driven rites is transient, cycling the soul repeatedly between heaven and earth.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Desire-driven devotees after enjoying heavenly blisses return to earth; true welfare lies solely in unselfish devotion.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: Returning to Samsara upon merit loss is the limitation of desire-based rites; eternal liberation comes only through unselfishness.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When they have thus enjoyed vast heavenly sense pleasure and the results of their pious activities are exhausted, they return to this mortal planet again. Thus those who seek sense enjoyment by adhering to the principles of the three Vedas achieve only repeated birth and death.
