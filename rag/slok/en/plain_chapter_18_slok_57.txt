Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Surrender all actions to Me mentally, i.e., with wisdom. The meaning of this sentence is to perform actions with the attitude of offering to God, by abandoning the sense of agency (doership) and attachment to results. This principle has been discussed in detail earlier. Become 'Matparah': One for whom I, the Supreme Lord, am the supreme goal, is called Matparah. Without understanding God as the goal of life, the feeling of surrender cannot arise in us. Therefore, Lord Krishna instructs Arjuna to be devoted to God. 'Buddhi Yoga': Since the attitude of offering (devotion) is of supreme importance in Karma Yoga, Lord Krishna has termed it as Buddhi Yoga. This also has been discussed before. 'Maccittah Bhava': He whose mind is established in Me, the Supreme Lord, is Maccittah. By surrendering all actions in Me and becoming Matparah, Karma Yoga and Jnana Yoga are indicated respectively, and now by the word Maccittah, Bhakti is indicated. It is a law of mental life that as we think, so we become. According to this rule, the devotee who constantly contemplates the Krishna-principle himself becomes the Supreme Lord Sri Krishna. This is the immutable spiritual nature. What happens if a person rejects this teaching of the Lord? Listen:

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Mentally surrendering all actions to Me, regarding Me as the supreme goal, and resorting to the Yoga of equanimity, ever fix your mind on Me.

Commentary: In this verse, the Lord teaches four key points:
1. Surrendering all actions to Me mentally: This means accepting with firm conviction that the mind, intellect, senses, body, and all worldly objects, people, and situations belong to the Lord alone. We must surrender even our sense of ownership over them.
2. Regarding Me as the supreme goal (Matparah): Knowing that the Lord is our only refuge, and that we have no other personal purpose or desire in this world.
3. Resorting to the Yoga of equanimity (Buddhi-yoga): Equanimity is the greatest quality in the Gita. One must remain undisturbed in pleasure and pain, success and failure.
4. Ever fixing the mind on Me (Maccittah satatam bhava): When a devotee completely surrenders himself to the Lord, his mind naturally rests in the Lord's feet. The remembrance of the Lord becomes constant.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'By your mind' means with thought which considers the self as belonging to Me and as controlled by Me. For, it has been declared:  'Surrendering all your acts to Me with a mind focussed on the self' (3.30). Surrendering all acts to Me along with agentship and the object of worship and regarding 'Me as the goal,' i.e., constantly contemplating that I alone am to be attained as the goal; performing all acts; and resorting to Buddhi-Yoga  - focus your mind on Me always. Buddhi Yoga here implies the mental attitude special to the seeker of salvation in regard to agency of works, the fruits etc.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Therefore, you too, mentally (with the mind imbued with Yoga and devotion), surrender all actions to Me, the supreme deity who is the actual agent, and contemplate. Although the word renunciation denotes abandonment, it refers only to the abandonment of the qualifiers (such as agency and ownership) and not the abandonment of the actions themselves (just like the phrase 'abandon the staff-bearing man' implies abandoning the staff, not the man). Thus, giving up the sense of doership, become Matparah: 'I am not the doer, my indwelling Lord is the primary doer who performs all actions, I am subservient to Him and act as He inspires me.' With this attitude, or by acting as directed by Me, become Matparah. Resorting to the Buddhi-yoga (the yoga of intellect) based on the aforementioned Sankhya-yoga, ever fix your mind on Me.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Since taking sole refuge in Me is the only means to liberation, and not mere performance of actions or renunciation of actions, therefore, you being a Kshatriya—resort to: 'Mentally (with a discriminating intellect) surrendering all actions meant for seen or unseen results to Me, the Lord, offering them in the manner described in "Whatever you do, whatever you eat..." (9.27). Being Matparah—one to whom I, the Lord Vasudeva, am the dearest and the supreme goal. Resorting to Buddhi-yoga—the yoga characterized by the intellect of equanimity mentioned before, which makes even action (the cause of bondage) a cause of liberation—accepting Me as the sole refuge, ever fix your mind on Me, the Lord Vasudeva, and not on a king, a beloved, or anything else.'

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
In all activities just depend upon Me and work always under My protection. In such devotional service, be fully conscious of Me.

 When one acts in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he does not act as the master of the world. Just like a servant, one should act fully under the direction of the Supreme Lord. A servant has no individual independence. He acts only on the order of the master. A servant acting on behalf of the supreme master is unaffected by profit and loss. He simply discharges his duty faithfully in terms of the order of the Lord. Now, one may argue that Arjuna was acting under the personal direction of Kṛṣṇa but when Kṛṣṇa is not present how should one act? If one acts according to the direction of Kṛṣṇa in this book, as well as under the guidance of the representative of Kṛṣṇa, then the result will be the same. The Sanskrit word mat-paraḥ is very important in this verse. It indicates that one has no goal in life save and except acting in Kṛṣṇa consciousness just to satisfy Kṛṣṇa. And while working in that way, one should think of Kṛṣṇa only: “I have been appointed to discharge this particular duty by Kṛṣṇa.” While acting in such a way, one naturally has to think of Kṛṣṇa. This is perfect Kṛṣṇa consciousness. One should, however, note that after doing something whimsically he should not offer the result to the Supreme Lord. That sort of duty is not in the devotional service of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. One should act according to the order of Kṛṣṇa. This is a very important point. That order of Kṛṣṇa comes through disciplic succession from the bona fide spiritual master. Therefore the spiritual master’s order should be taken as the prime duty of life. If one gets a bona fide spiritual master and acts according to his direction, then one’s perfection of life in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is guaranteed.
