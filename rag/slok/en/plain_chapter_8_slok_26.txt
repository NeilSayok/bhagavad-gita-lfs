Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
śuklakṛṣṇe gatī hyete jagataḥ śāśvate mate .
ekayā yātyanāvṛttimanyayāvartate punaḥ

Word Meanings:
शुक्लकृष्णे - bright and dark
गती - two paths
ह्येते - truly these
जगतः - of the world
शाश्वते - eternal
मते - are considered
एकया - by one
यात्यनावृत्तिमन्ययावर्तते - goes to liberation by other returns
पुनः - again

Life Application:
Know these two eternal paths of light and darkness, one leading to liberation and the other to rebirth.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Truly these two paths, the bright and the dark, are considered eternal for the world; by the one man goes to non-return, and by the other he returns again.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Truly these two paths, the bright and the dark, are considered eternal for the world; by the one man goes to non-return, and by the other he returns again.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
These two paths for the world—the bright path of knowledge (Devayana) and the dark path of action (Pitriyana)—are deemed eternal. By the first a seeker reaches non-return (liberation), while by the second one returns to the cycle of rebirth.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya did not provide a separate commentary on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: The bright (knowledge) and dark (action) paths are deemed eternal in samsara, guiding spiritual seekers to liberation and ritualists to rebirth.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Truly these two paths, the bright and the dark, are considered eternal for the world; by the one man goes to non-return, and by the other he returns again.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: The bright path bestows non-return (liberation) to the steadfast meditator, while the dark path brings rebirth to the ritualistic performer; both paths are eternal.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: These two paths (bright and dark) are considered eternal for the world; the jnana-yogi reaches non-return via the bright path, while the ritualist returns via the dark path.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "These two paths, the bright and the dark, are deemed eternal for the world; by one a man reaches non-return, by the other he returns again"—the luminous Devayana path (for knowledge/worship) grants liberation, while the dark Pitriyana path (for karmic rituals) leads back to mortal rebirth.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not provide a separate commentary on this verse.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The bright path leads to God"s realm and the dark path brings return; both paths are eternal.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "By one he goes to non-return"—the bright path of knowledge leads to liberation, while the dark path of karma leads to rebirth; both are eternal cosmic paths.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Truly these two paths, the bright and the dark, are considered eternal for the world; by the one man goes to non-return, and by the other he returns again.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural comparison of the two eternal Devayana and Pitriyana paths and their respective outcomes of non-return vs return.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: For the seeker of Mukti, the bright path grants eternal salvation, whereas the dark path brings repeated rebirth to ritualists.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Exclusive devotees of the Lord traveling by the bright path directly reach the supreme realm, never to return.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Deemed eternal"—understanding these two age-old paths inspires the seeker to choose the path of truth.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
According to Vedic opinion, there are two ways of passing from this world—one in light and the other in darkness. When one passes in light, he does not come back; but when one passes in darkness, he returns.
