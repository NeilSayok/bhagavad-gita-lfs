Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Continuing his earlier argument, Arjuna says that as unrighteousness increases, morality in society will gradually decline, and admixed castes (varnasankara) will be produced.

'Varna' is a word whose meaning has been so distorted that it has become a target of sharp criticism from today's educated people. Their criticism is justified if its distorted meaning is accepted. But what we see happening today in the country in the name of varna is a degenerate form of the Hindu way of life. In ancient times, the basis of the division into varnas was the mental and intellectual capacity and maturity of individuals in society.

Those who were intelligent and interested in study, teaching, and research were called brahmins; kshatriyas were those who had the capacity to lead the nation through statecraft and who took upon themselves the responsibility of protecting the nation from internal and external threats, bringing peace and prosperity to it. Those who served society through agriculture and commerce were called vaishyas. Those who could not perform any of these suitable functions were called shudras; their duty was service and labour. Our present-day social workers and officials, farmers, industrial workers and so on -- all of them fall within this same varna scheme when understood properly.

When we understand the varna system in its broader sense, we still see it manifested today in the form of numerous organizations. Hence, the objection to varnasankara means simply this: if an electrical engineer performs a surgeon's work in the operating theatre, it will prove hazardous to society; likewise, if a physician is appointed as an administrative and planning officer for a hydroelectric project, it will cause harm to society.

When there is moral decline in society, young men and women, drowned in uncontrolled desires, mingle with one another freely. Overcome by desire, they give not the slightest thought to social and cultural development. This is why Arjuna fears that varnasankara will cause the decline of society and culture.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
He shows the culminating fault resulting from admixture of castes, in 'Sankara...'. He then adds a further fault caused by the destruction of the family, in 'Patanti...'. He states the reason why the ancestors of those who destroy the family become bound for hell, in 'Lupta...': for lack of sons and other descendants to perform the rites, the offerings of the rice-ball (pinda) and water to them cease -- such ancestors are meant. And since there remains no cause for their release from the state of being a departed spirit (preta), falling into hell becomes inevitable for them, is the sense.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Krishna! With unrighteousness increasing greatly, the women of the family become corrupted; (and) O Varshneya! Once women are corrupted, admixture of castes (varnasankara) is produced.

Explanation: 'Adharmabhibhavat krishna... pradushyanti kulastriyah' -- by observing dharma, the inner faculty (antahkarana) becomes pure. When the inner faculty is pure, the intellect becomes sattvic. In a sattvic intellect, discernment of what ought and ought not to be done remains awake. But when unrighteousness increases within a family, conduct begins to become impure, which in turn makes the inner faculty impure. When the inner faculty becomes impure, the intellect becomes tamasic. When the intellect becomes tamasic, a person begins to regard what ought not to be done as duty and what ought to be done as not-duty -- that is, notions contrary to scriptural propriety begin to arise within them. Due to this perverted intellect, the women of the family become corrupted, that is, unchaste.

'Strishu dushtasu varshneya jayate varnasankarah' -- once women are corrupted, admixture of castes results. When a man and a woman are of different varnas, the offspring born from their union is called varnasankara.

Here Arjuna, by using the address 'Krishna,' is saying: since You are called 'Krishna' because You draw everyone toward Yourself, tell us -- toward which direction will You draw our family, that is, where will You lead it?

The purpose of the address 'Varshneya' is this: You are called 'Varshneya' because You took incarnation in the Vrishni lineage. But once our own family (lineage) is destroyed, to what family will our descendants belong? Hence, destroying the family is not proper.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The connection is: the admixture of castes caused by the family leads only to hell for the destroyers of the family. He states that it is not only the family-destroyers who fall into hell, but their ancestors as well, in 'Patanti...'. The word 'hi' here carries either the sense of 'indeed' or 'because.' For lack of sons and other descendants to perform the rites, the offering of the rice-ball and water to them ceases -- the connection is: the ancestors of the family-destroyers fall, bound for hell alone.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
What then results from that? To this he answers, in 'Adharma...': from the prevalence, the abundance, of unrighteousness, the women of the family become thoroughly corrupted, that is, become unchaste. Some say the corruption of women arises simply from association with their own fallen husbands who caused the family's destruction, as the smriti states: 'a woman associated with one tainted by a great sin is, until purified, to be regarded with suspicion.' And what results from their becoming corrupted, he answers, in 'Strishu...': once women are corrupted, admixture of castes results. Hinting that You alone ought to draw us away from war, the source of all this unrighteousness productive of every calamity, and not engage us in it, he addresses Him as 'Krishna.' By addressing Him as 'Varshneya' -- O one born in the Vrishni lineage -- he hints, suggesting: how is it that You, who know the propriety of the family, do not understand this? And knowing it, why do You remain indifferent?

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
By this too, no one thereafter would arise as a devotee -- he says this, in 'Adharmabhibhavat...': overwhelmed by unrighteousness, the women of the family, pervaded by unrighteousness, become corrupted -- that is, become tainted with faults such as adultery, is the sense. Once women are so corrupted, admixture of castes results. By the address 'Varshneya,' he conveys: such a state is improper for those born in a noble lineage.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
One might object: then how is it told that when Parashurama, son of Jamadagni, slew the kshatriyas, their widowed women repeatedly bore sons through brahmins? Or how could Dhritarashtra and the rest not themselves be of mixed birth? Anticipating this, he answers, in 'Patanti...'. The word 'hi' here signals a well-known Vedic teaching -- as in the shruti: 'For Agni there is no remainder born of another' -- meaning, an offspring born of another is not considered a 'remainder,' that is, a true continuation. As Yaska states: 'a child of another womb is not to be regarded, even in thought, as my own son.' From the scriptural statement 'we who sacrifice' (in the sense of 'we ourselves are the ones who perform sacrifice'), since the visible presence of a father and the rest is always subject to doubt, the certainty that 'this is indeed my father' is difficult to establish. There is also the mantric statement 'whoever I am, that same one I sacrifice as,' and the explanatory passage: 'we do not know this -- are we brahmins, or are we not brahmins?' Therefore, since it is the seed-giver alone, not the field-owner, who is entitled to receive the rice-ball and the rest, the lapse of the rice-ball-and-water rites necessarily brings about the fall of the ancestors. As for the smriti concerning a 'field-born son,' that pertains only to the establishment of a lineage in this world, and confers no benefit whatsoever upon the field-owner for the next world, since it would contradict the shruti cited above. And this admixture, if brought about by ourselves, would certainly afflict us in just this way, is the sense.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
An increase of unwanted population certainly causes hellish life both for the family and for those who destroy the family tradition. The ancestors of such corrupt families fall down, because the performances for offering them food and water are entirely stopped.

 According to the rules and regulations of fruitive activities, there is a need to offer periodical food and water to the forefathers of the family. This offering is performed by worship of Vishnu, because eating the remnants of food offered to Vishnu can deliver one from all kinds of sinful reactions. Sometimes the forefathers may be suffering from various types of sinful reactions, and sometimes some of them cannot even acquire a gross material body and are forced to remain in subtle bodies as ghosts. Thus, when remnants of prasadam food are offered to forefathers by descendants, the forefathers are released from ghostly or other kinds of miserable life. Such help rendered to forefathers is a family tradition, and those who are not in devotional life are required to perform such rituals. One who is engaged in the devotional life is not required to perform such actions. Simply by performing devotional service, one can deliver hundreds and thousands of forefathers from all kinds of misery. It is stated in the Bhagavatam (11.5.41): 'Anyone who has taken shelter of the lotus feet of Mukunda, the giver of liberation, giving up all kinds of obligation, and has taken to the path in all seriousness, owes neither duties nor obligations to the demigods, sages, general living entities, family members, humankind or forefathers.' Such obligations are automatically fulfilled by performance of devotional service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead.
