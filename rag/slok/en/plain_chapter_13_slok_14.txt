Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
'Sarvatah panipadam...' A highly qualified seeker (Uttama Adhikari) recognizes the unconditioned (Nirguna) nature of the Self, but for a medium seeker (Madhyama Adhikari), it is easier to understand the unknown and unmanifest through known and manifest objects. Although the hands and feet of living beings are made of inert matter, yet they appear sentient and functional. Behind all of them, the Self-principle that provides consciousness is one and the same everywhere. Therefore, it is said here that Brahman possesses all hands and feet. In the same way, all eyes, heads, and mouths are capable of performing their functions only because of this Consciousness. Hence, the Self is indicated as having eyes, heads, and mouths on all sides. Only when sustained by Consciousness do the actions of perception and thinking take place in living beings. Therefore, the conscious Brahman is said to have ears everywhere. 'It remains enveloping all' — When the conditioned nature and influence of the Self are shown here, one might assume that the Self exists only where the conditions (limiting adjuncts) exist, and not elsewhere. To remove such a contrary notion, it is most appropriately said here that the Supreme Truth remains enveloping everything. This verse reminds students familiar with Vedic literature of the famous Purusha Suktam of the Rigveda. The Lord says further:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anticipating the doubt that the Knowable (Brahman) directly possesses hands and feet without superimposition, the commentator says—'upadhi' etc. By the word 'all' qualifying the organs, and due to the similarity in the logic of the limiting adjuncts of the Knowable, the mind and intellect (antahkarana) are also included here; to explain this he says—'antahkarane ca' etc. Since the organs like ears etc. function as adjuncts of the Knowable through the mind and intellect, these two are also included here; to explain this he says—'api ca' etc. The result of including them is stated—'ityatah' etc. After explaining the literal meaning, he states the sentence meaning—'sarva' etc. To support the idea of imagined activity through limiting adjuncts, he quotes the scriptural passage—'dhyayati' etc. That Its activity is merely imagined and not real is shown through anticipation of the Lord's agreement—'kasmat' etc. The result of being devoid of all organs is stated—'atah' etc. If it is objected that the Upanishadic text depicts the Knowable as moving swiftly, how can It be unrelated to organ activities? He translates the text and shows its consistency with the context—'yastu' etc. If the mantra literally meant swift movement without assuming the qualities of organs, it would lead to contradiction; to explain this he says—'andhah' etc. The figurative eulogy (arthavada) has no intention of stating a literal meaning that opposes the context. Accepting that absence of all organs implies absence of their activities, and using the same reason, he states that It is actually free from all association—'yasmat' etc. Even though free from association, Its being the substratum of all is stated—'yadyapi' etc. He explains that It sustains all by Its mere existence—'sad' etc. Every superimposed object is cognized only through the intellect pervaded by existence (Sat), just as different superimposed moons are cognized as pervaded by the moon. To the objection that illusions like a mirage have no Sat, he replies—'na hi' etc. Even they cannot be without a substratum, which upon analysis is Sat alone; thus superimposition of all on Sat is consistent. He concludes the discussion of the existence of the Knowable Brahman as the substratum of all—'atah' etc. He gives another reason for the existence of the Knowable—'syadidam ca' etc. Its being the perceiver (upalabdhri) cannot be established if It were non-existent.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He (the Supreme Soul) has hands and feet everywhere, eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere, and ears everywhere. He exists in the world enveloping all.

Explanation —
'Sarvatah panipadam tat' — Just as all kinds of scripts exist everywhere in ink, hence a writer can write any script using ink; all kinds of ornaments exist everywhere in gold, so a goldsmith can make any ornament from any part of gold; similarly, the Lord has hands and feet everywhere. Therefore, wherever and whatever a devotee wants to offer to the Lord with devotion, the Lord's hands are present right there to accept it. Whether the devotee wants to offer externally or mentally, in the east or in the west, in the north or in the south, the Lord's hands are present right there to receive it. Similarly, wherever a devotee calls upon the Lord in any trouble—in water, on land, in fire—the Lord's hands are ready right there to protect him, i.e., the Lord protects him with His own hands right there. Wherever a devotee wants to apply sandalwood paste to the Lord's feet, offer flowers, or bow down, the Lord's feet are present right there. If thousands and millions of devotees want to worship different feet of the Lord at the same time, the Lord's feet are present right there according to their devotion.
'Sarvato'kshi-siro-mukham' — Wherever a devotee shows a lamp or performs Arati to the Lord, the Lord's eyes are right there. Wherever a devotee dances physically or mentally, the Lord sees his dance right there. The implication is that he who sees the Lord everywhere is never out of the Lord's sight (Gita 6.30). Wherever a devotee wants to apply sandalwood paste to the Lord's head or offer flowers, the Lord's head is right there. Wherever a devotee wants to offer food (bhog) to the Lord, the Lord's mouth is right there, i.e., the Lord eats the offering given by the devotee with devotion right there (Gita 9.26).
'Sarvatah-shrutimat' — Wherever a devotee prays aloud, whispers, or prays mentally, the Lord hears it with His ears right there.
Human limbs are not everywhere, i.e., where there are eyes, there are no ears; where there are ears, there are no eyes; where there are hands, there are no feet, and where there are feet, there are no hands, etc. But the Lord's senses and limbs are everywhere. Therefore, the Lord can hear with His eyes, speak with them, grasp with them, etc. The implication is that He can perform all activities with all limbs because all limbs are present in all His limbs. Even in His smallest part, all senses are present.
Saying that the Lord has hands, feet, eyes, head, mouth, and ears everywhere implies that the Lord is not far from any creature. This is because the Lord is fully present in all space, time, object, person, event, and circumstance. Saints have said: 'Aarati on all sides, worship on all sides. Rama is on all sides, there is no other.'
Just as a worldly man sees only the world everywhere—inside, outside, above, below—and sees nothing but the world; similarly, a person who knows the Supreme Truth sees only the Supreme Soul everywhere.
'Loke sarvam avrtya tisthati' — There are infinite creations, infinite universes, infinite glories, and in all of them, space, time, objects, persons, etc. are also infinite; all of these are within the Supreme Soul. The Supreme Soul remains pervading all of them. In the forty-second verse of the tenth chapter, the Lord has also said: 'I remain pervading this entire universe with a single fraction of Myself.'
Connection — Having described the Saguna-Nirakara (Brahman with attributes but without form) in the previous verse, now in the next three verses, His uniqueness, all-pervasiveness, and omnipotence are described.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Everywhere are Its hands and feet i.e., the self in Its pure form is able to perform everywhere the works of hands and feet. Its eyes, heads and mouths are everywhere; It performs everywhere the task of eyes etc. The Sruti declares; 'Without feet or hands, He moves swiftly and seizes things; He sees without eyes, He hears without ears?  (Sve. U., 3.19). It may be said that it means that the Supreme Brahman performs everywhere the task of hands, feet etc., even though He is devoid of hands and feet. If 'Brahman' is taken to mean the self, it can be asked how this power of the Supreme Brahman (namely, having hand, feet, eyes, etc., everywhere) can be attributed to the self, then the answer is that it is established in the Srutis that the pure individual self has the capacity of performing the task of hands, feet etc., because It is eal to Him. Sruti also declares:  'Then, the wise seer, shaking off good and evil, stainless, attains the supreme eality with Him' (Mun. U., 3.1.3). Sri Krsna will also teach later on:  'Resorting to this knowledge, It partakes of My nature' (14.2). It exists encompassing all things, whatever aggregate of things that exist in the world; It encompasses them. The sense is that in Its pure state, It is all-pervasive, as It has no limitation of space etc.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Tat, That-the Knowable; sarvatah-pani-padam, which has hands and feet everywhere-.
The existence of the Knower of the field is revealed through th adjuncts in the form of the organs of all creatures. And the Knower of the field is spoken of as such because of the limiting adjuncts of the field. The field, too, is diversely differentiated as hands, feet, etc. All diversity in the Knower of the field, caused by the differences in the adjunct-the field-, is certainly unreal. Hence, by denying it, the nature of the Knowable has been stated, in, 'That is called neither being nor non-being.' Although the unreal form is caused by the limiting adjuncts, still, for the comprehension of Its existence it is said, '(It) has hands and feet everywhere, etc., by assuming this as a ality of the Knowable. Thus, as is well known, there is saying of the people versed in tradition, 'The Transcendental is described with the help of superimposition and its refutation'. Everywhere the hands, feet, etc., which are perceived as limbs of all bodies, perform, their duties due to the presence of the power of the Knowable (Brahman). Thus the grounds for the inference of the existence of the Knowable are metaphorically spoken of as belonging to the Knowable. The others have to be explained similarly.
That Knowable has hands and feet everwhere. That which has eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere is sarvatoksi-siro-mukham. That which has ears every-where is sarvatah-srutimat: sruti means the organs of hearing; that which has it is sruti-mat. Tisthati, It exists, remains established; loke, in the multititude of creatures; avrtya, by pervading; sarvam, them all.
With this purpose is view, that as a result of the superimposition of the organs like hands, feet, etc., which are adjuncts, there may not be the misconception that the Knowable is possessed of them (adjuncts), the (next) verse is begun:

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Raising the doubt whether That is with form (sakara) or without form (nirakara), the commentator says—'sarvatah-panipadam' etc. It is indeed with form, whose hands and feet are the limits (ends) in all places. By the word 'anta' (ends), it is stated that the activities of movement and action appear everywhere as delimited according to His own will. By 'sarvato'kshi-siro-mukham', the predominance of knowledge and enjoyment are stated to be present everywhere. To describe the expansion of names, he says 'sarvatah srutimal loke', meaning He hears from all sides. To the doubt whether such a Being would be limited, he says 'sarvam avrtya tisthati', meaning He exists enveloping all. Although these attributes become manifest only after the creation of the universe, they are mentioned here first to establish their eternity.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Although the doubt about the non-existence of the unconditioned Brahman (since It is not the object of the concept 'Sat') was refuted by 'nor non-being', yet, to completely remove that doubt in detail, the Lord establishes Its existence as the conscious Knower of the field (Kshetrajna) through the limiting adjuncts of the organs of all living beings, saying 'sarvatah' etc. In all bodies, the hands and feet which are insentient are directed in their respective activities by that conscious Kshetrajna; hence That which has hands and feet everywhere is the Knowable Brahman. Since all insentient activities are preceded by a conscious substratum, there can be no doubt about the non-existence of this conscious Brahman, who is the cause of all insentient activities. Similarly, He is One whose eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere are directed by Him, and whose ears (organs of hearing) everywhere exist to be directed; thus It has eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere. In all living beings, the one, eternal, and all-pervading Brahman, enveloping all insentient objects, pervades them by Its existence and light through an illusory (adhyasika) relationship, and remains established as changeless; It is not connected with even a single atom of the defects or merits of the insentient universe superimposed on It. And how there is only one conscious, eternal, and all-pervading entity in all bodies, and not a separate one for each body, has been explained in detail earlier.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
If Brahman is thus different from Sat and Asat, then wouldn't it contradict Upanishadic texts like 'All this is indeed Brahman' and 'Brahman alone is all this'? Anticipating this objection, the Lord reveals His all-pervading selfhood (sarvatmata) through His inconceivable power (achintya-shakti), as celebrated in scriptural texts like 'His supreme power is revealed as indeed manifold, and His knowledge, strength, and action are natural', in five verses starting with 'sarvatah'. He is One whose hands and feet are everywhere; whose eyes, heads, and mouths are everywhere; who has ears everywhere, and exists in the world enveloping (pervading) all. The meaning is that through the limiting adjuncts of the activities of the hands, feet, etc. of all living beings, He remains as the basis of all transactions.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Since the Knowable, which is devoid of all attributes and inaccessible to speech, is not the object of the word 'Sat', a doubt about Its non-existence arose. Though this was briefly solved by 'neither being nor non-being', yet to establish the existence of the Knowable as the inner consciousness, the cause of the activities of the organs, and the provider of existence and light to the superimposed duality, the Lord details it. Just as the movements of insentient chariots depend on a conscious driver, so also the activities of the insentient organs of all living beings depend on the inner consciousness, which is Brahman itself. To remove the doubt about non-existence, the Lord says: 'He has hands and feet everywhere'. Even though hands, feet, etc. are located in the body, the activities of the organs are preceded by a conscious substratum because they are purposeful, like the movement of a chariot. Thus, through the limiting adjuncts of all organs, the existence of the Kshetrajna (Knower of the field) is revealed. To the question: even if the existence of a conscious entity is proved, how does it prove the existence of Kshetrajna? He replies: the conscious entity is called Kshetrajna only because of the limiting adjunct of the field (Kshetra); hence Its existence is indeed the existence of Kshetrajna. Since the field is divided in many ways as hands, feet, etc., therefore by that limiting adjunct, it is said that Kshetrajna has hands and feet. The description of the Knowable as devoid of attributes is because the host of qualities caused by the limiting adjuncts of the field is unreal (mithya); by removing that, the Knowable is defined. Speaking of them in the instruction on the Knowable is meaningful to assume them as attributes of the Knowable for the sake of teaching Its existence. As the traditional knowers say: 'The Transcendental is described with the help of superimposition and its refutation.' 'Sarvato'kshi-siro-mukham...' means He has eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere. 'Sarvatah-shrutimat' means He has ears everywhere. Having hands, feet, mouth, eyes, ears, etc. is an implication (upalaksana) for having all other organs of action, organs of knowledge, and the mind/intellect. The limbs perceived in all bodies are able to perform their activities due to the mere presence of the Supreme Soul who directs them. Therefore, they are indicators of the existence of the Knowable, and are figuratively spoken of as belonging to the Knowable. 'Loke sarvam avrtya tisthati' means in all living beings, He pervades all moving and non-moving objects by an adhyasika (superimposed) relation of existence, and remains changeless; He does not become endowed with those attributes.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that since the essential nature of the self is bodiless, partless, and devoid of organs, there is no physical possibility of hands and feet. This verse does not refer to the cosmic form (Visvarupa) described earlier, because the context establishes this as the individual self (Jiva). Therefore, the theory that the all-pervading Brahman is connected with hands and feet assumed by the Jiva's karma or by His own will is rejected. How then can the Jiva have hands and feet everywhere? The answer is that even though the Jiva is devoid of hands and feet, in its pure state it possesses the power to perform the functions of hands and feet. To support this, he quotes the Upanishadic text describing the Supreme Brahman: 'Without feet or hands, He moves swiftly and seizes...' (Sve. U. 3.19). 'Without feet or hands' represents the organs of action, and 'without eyes or ears' represents the organs of knowledge. How can this power of the Supreme Brahman be attributed to the Jiva who has limited power? In the state of liberation, due to attaining similarity with Brahman, the Jiva's knowledge-power is uncontracted, making this consistent. This similarity is in all respects except for the creation/maintenance of the universe (jagad-vyapara), as decided in the Brahma Sutras. 'Tisthati' (exists) here means that its pervasiveness does not lapse. How can the Jiva, which is atomic, remain all-pervasive? In its pure state, this pervasiveness is through its attributive knowledge (dharmabhuta-jnana), which expands in liberation, while its essential nature remains atomic.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that if Brahman were not the object of anything, Its knowability would be contradicted. Therefore, to describe Its form as the Knowable, the Lord says: 'He has hands and feet everywhere'. By these two attributes, His action-power (kriya-shakti) everywhere and His being worshipped by all are described. He has eyes, heads, and mouths everywhere; by these three attributes, His omniscience and His supremacy are revealed. 'Sarvatah-shrutimat' means He has organs of hearing everywhere, showing His compassion in hearing the praises of His devotees. 'Loke' implies among His own devotees. To the doubt that such a form would make Him limited, the Lord says: 'sarvam avrtya' (enveloping all), meaning He exists as all-pervading, endowed with all these organs.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that after describing the nature of the Knower of the field (Kshetrajna) as devoid of all attributes, the Lord now explains Its glory in the form of the cosmic form (Vaisvarupya), saying 'sarvatah' etc. He has hands and feet everywhere, inside and outside, in all directions. He exists in the world enveloping all. Just as a dreamer experiences the dream world through limbs made of mental impressions (vasanas), and the physical limbs of the waking state leave impressions of the gross world which cause the dream world, and vice versa; by the seed-and-sprout logic (bija-ankura-nyaya), they cause and contain each other. Similarly, the Consciousness conditioned by the ignorance colored by the mental impressions of all living beings reveals the cosmic subtle universe, and its limiting adjunct is the sum total of all limbs of all creatures in the universe. Keeping this in mind, the Bhashyakara (Shankaracharya) stated that the existence of the Knowable Brahman is established through the limiting adjuncts of the organs of all creatures. To the objection that if the adjuncts are unreal, introducing them is useless, he replies that the conditioned (Saguna) serves to indicate the unconditioned (Nirguna), just as a tree branch is used to point to the moon (shakha-chandra-nyaya). As the traditional saying goes: 'The Transcendental is described with the help of superimposition and its refutation.'

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Everywhere are His hands and legs, His eyes, heads and faces, and He has ears everywhere. In this way the Supersoul exists, pervading everything.

 As the sun exists diffusing its unlimited rays, so does the Supersoul, or Supreme Personality of Godhead. He exists in His all-pervading form, and in Him exist all the individual living entities, beginning from the first great teacher, Brahmā, down to the small ants. There are unlimited heads, legs, hands and eyes, and unlimited living entities. All are existing in and on the Supersoul. Therefore the Supersoul is all-pervading. The individual soul, however, cannot say that he has his hands, legs and eyes everywhere. That is not possible. If he thinks that under ignorance he is not conscious that his hands and legs are diffused all over but when he attains to proper knowledge he will come to that stage, his thinking is contradictory. This means that the individual soul, having become conditioned by material nature, is not supreme. The Supreme is different from the individual soul. The Supreme Lord can extend His hand without limit; the individual soul cannot. In Bhagavad-gītā the Lord says that if anyone offers Him a flower, or a fruit, or a little water, He accepts it. If the Lord is a far distance away, how can He accept things? This is the omnipotence of the Lord: even though He is situated in His own abode, far, far away from earth, He can extend His hand to accept what anyone offers. That is His potency. In the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.37) it is stated, goloka eva nivasaty akhilātma-bhūtaḥ: although He is always engaged in pastimes in His transcendental planet, He is all-pervading. The individual soul cannot claim that he is all-pervading. Therefore this verse describes the Supreme Soul, the Personality of Godhead, not the individual soul.
