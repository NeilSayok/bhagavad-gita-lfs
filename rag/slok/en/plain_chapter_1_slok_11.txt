Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
After speaking alone for so long, weighing the strength of both sides, King Duryodhana, ready for battle, now breaks through the dense clouds of despair in his mind and begins to command the army. His order is that every warrior and leader, remaining at his own post, should fight in disciplined fashion, and at the same time strive to keep Grandsire Bhishma safe. He fears that if the kings and tribal chiefs who have come from distant realms scatter here and there, victory will be difficult. A coordinated, simultaneous attack on all fronts is the backbone of any army's success. So, adopting sound strategy, he commands everyone that, though stationed at different posts, all should protect Acharya Bhishma.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Having said that his own army's strength lies in Bhishma, Duryodhana now requests Drona and the others -- as being subordinate to Bhishma -- to act accordingly. The word 'cha' (and) signals a distinct duty. At the start of battle, warriors are stationed at their positions in the battlefield in due order -- east, west, and so on, according to rank; these positions are called 'ayanas' (fronts). The commander stands in the middle, presiding over the whole army. Duryodhana says: without abandoning the assigned positions among all these -- you, Ashvatthama, Karna, and the rest -- being so stationed, protect the commander Bhishma alone, from every side; for in protecting him, our entire army is protected, since it is through him that the enemy's advance is checked.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Explanation: 'In all the fronts... you all indeed' — whichever front each of you has been assigned to, remain firmly stationed there and protect Grandsire Bhishma from every side, in every way.

By saying 'protect Bhishma from all sides,' Duryodhana wants to secure Bhishma's loyalty from within. A second implication is: when Bhishma fights, make sure Shikhandi does not come before him from any gap in the formation. If Shikhandi does appear before him, Bhishma will not raise weapons against him, because Shikhandi was a woman in a former birth, and in this birth too was first a woman before becoming a man; hence Bhishma regards him as a woman and has vowed never to fight one. Shikhandi was born by Shiva's boon for the sole purpose of slaying Bhishma. So, if Bhishma is protected from Shikhandi, he alone will destroy everyone, and victory will certainly be ours. With this thought Duryodhana asks all the great warriors to protect Bhishma.

Transition: seeing Duryodhana's spirit shaken because Dronacharya said nothing, Sanjaya next reveals, in the following verse, the affection Bhishma showed him.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said  Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within.

Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory.

Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra:  Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
At the start of the battle, the positions where warriors are stationed on the battlefield, arranged in due order -- east, west, and so on, according to rank -- are called 'ayanas' (fronts); the commander stands in the middle, presiding over the whole army. This being so, all of you, without abandoning your own allotted battlefield stationed by rank, being intent on the fight, watching from front, back, and sides, should protect only the commander Bhishma. The intent is: if the commander Bhishma is protected, then by his grace everything else will be well protected too.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Therefore he says you should act thus: 'in the fronts' means in the paths of entry into the battle-formation. Without abandoning your own allotted battlefield by rank, all of you should protect Bhishma from every side, in such a way that while he fights others, none may strike him from behind. The sense is: our very life depends on Bhishma's strength.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Duryodhana therefore requests: let all of you, being of secondary rank, protect only the commander Bhishma. The word 'cha' (and) signals a distinct duty. At the start of battle, warriors' positions are fixed in due order -- east, west, and so on -- according to rank; these are called 'ayanas' (fronts). Being carefully stationed at your own allotted place obtained by rank, all of you should protect from every side the commander Bhishma, who stands amid the whole army, engaged in battle -- for the purpose of repelling the enemy force; the sense being that if he remains unharmed, everything of ours remains protected.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Venkatanatha explains: Sanjaya, wishing to lead up to Krishna's direct answer later (BG 18.78, "where there is Krishna..."), reports every intervening detail, starting with "having seen." Seeing the Pandava army arrayed caused Duryodhana's loss of courage; the word "tu" signals this changed state. The plain sense of verse 10 is: our army, protected by Bhishma, is insufficient (aparyaptam) to conquer the enemy, while this army of the Pandavas, protected by Bhima, is sufficient (paryaptam) to conquer ours. But this reading seems odd, since elsewhere Duryodhana repeatedly asserts confidence in his own army's strength (protected by Bhishma, Drona, etc.), including in the Mahabharata's Udyoga Parva (55.1) and at the start of the second day's battle, where he uses nearly identical words but clearly means "our army is unlimited, theirs is limited." To resolve the contradiction, Venkatanatha offers four possible reconciliations: (1) a variant reading swapping Bhima/Bhishma; (2) a discontinuous construal reordering the phrases so the sense becomes "that (Pandava) army protected by Bhima is insufficient (to defeat us)"; (3) splitting the verse into two independent statements — "that is insufficient," "this is sufficient" — with the Bhishma/Bhima clauses supplying the reason (our side has a mighty protector, theirs a weaker one); (4) taking "paryaptam/aparyaptam" to mean "limited/unlimited" in number (our eleven akshauhinis versus their seven), so Duryodhana never doubts his own strength at all. Venkatanatha then defends why Bhima specifically is named opposite Bhishma: not because Bhima is the Pandava commander (that is Dhrishtadyumna), nor because he equals Bhishma in valor (the two are famously unequal — Bhishma himself said he alone could slay all the Pandavas but for divine protection), but because Bhima has vowed to kill all the sons of Dhritarashtra and possesses the reckless daring suited to that vow — hence he is the special object of Kaurava dread, just as Bhishma is named as the singular protector of all the Pandavas. As for the earlier objection that no reason was actually given for the respective strength of the enemy's and our own forces -- that too is a careless remark. For right at the outset (verse 1.3) the enemy's army was itself described as vast even though it is four akshauhinis fewer than ours; immediately after (ending with "the wise one," 1.3) its commander was described; then, from "here are heroes, great bowmen" (1.4) through "all are great chariot-warriors" (1.6), some nineteen men, exemplified by Bhima and Arjuna, were described as of unsurpassed valor. But on our own side, neither the army nor a commander was described. In enumerating our army's chief men, only seven were named, the rest being covered by the general phrase "and many other heroes." And though it was also said "life-abandoning for my sake" (1.9) rather than "eager to conquer for my sake" -- implying they are driven only by personal loyalty -- that is true; yet even this manner of speaking, describing warriors as "life-abandoning," itself suggests the opposing side's superior strength, since it implies desperation. Here Sanjaya's account of Duryodhana's words ends; next comes: "Then, to gladden him, the aged Kuru elder, the grandfather (Bhishma)..."

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
This being so, in reply to the question 'what then should be done,' he says 'in the fronts, and...': being stationed each in his own place, divided by rank, along the paths of entry into the formation, all of you should protect Bhishma from every side, in every way -- since our (whole) army is protected by protecting Bhishma. The word 'cha' (and) also signals that even those stationed beyond the entry-paths (elsewhere) share this duty. The word 'eva' (only/alone) signals that it is fitting for us too to be protected (by extension). The word 'hi' (indeed) signals the reasonableness of this.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
In the fronts -- that is, in the army as arrayed in formation -- at the entry-paths, each of you being stationed at your own place, protect the commander Bhishma, who stands in the middle. If this commander wavers, the entire army will become agitated; if he remains steady, the whole army too will stay firm. Hence he alone is worthy of protection -- this is the meaning.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
All of you must now give full support to Grandfather Bhīṣma, as you stand at your respective strategic points of entrance into the phalanx of the army.

 Duryodhana, after praising the prowess of Bhīṣma, further considered that others might think that they had been considered less important, so in his usual diplomatic way, he tried to adjust the situation in the above words. He emphasized that Bhīṣmadeva was undoubtedly the greatest hero, but he was an old man, so everyone must especially think of his protection from all sides. He might become engaged in the fight, and the enemy might take advantage of his full engagement on one side. Therefore, it was important that other heroes not leave their strategic positions and allow the enemy to break the phalanx. Duryodhana clearly felt that the victory of the Kurus depended on the presence of Bhīṣmadeva. He was confident of the full support of Bhīṣmadeva and Droṇācārya in the battle because he well knew that they did not even speak a word when Arjuna’s wife Draupadī, in her helpless condition, had appealed to them for justice while she was being forced to appear naked in the presence of all the great generals in the assembly. Although he knew that the two generals had some sort of affection for the Pāṇḍavas, he hoped that these generals would now completely give it up, as they had done during the gambling performances.
