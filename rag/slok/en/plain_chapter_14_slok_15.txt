Life Application:
Meeting death in passion, one is born among those attached to action; dying in ignorance, born in dull wombs.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Meeting death when Rajas (mode of passion) is predominant, he takes birth among people attached to action; and dying when Tamas (mode of ignorance) is predominant, he takes birth in the wombs of the senseless (animal kingdom etc.).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Meeting death in Rajas, he is born among those who are attached to action; and dying in Tamas, he is born in the womb of the senseless.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
In accordance with the principle stated earlier, if one dies when Rajas predominates, the individual soul is born in the world of human beings who are attached to action. For such a soul, this is the most suitable field to perform action and experience its fruits. On the contrary, if the soul leaves the body at a time when Tamas is predominant, then as a consequence, it attains birth in the wombs of the deluded, that is, as animals, birds, or plant life.
Some philosophers hold the view that once human status is attained on the ladder of evolution, we do not degrade into lower species. Undoubtedly, this is a comforting view, but it cannot be accepted as it is contrary to experienced facts. The reality is that even after providing the best conditions and environment for progress, not all humans utilize them equally to achieve a glorious cultural stature. The average son of a rich man gets favorable conditions right from the beginning of life; yet it is often seen that instead of properly utilizing his position, he ruins himself by living a life of heedlessness and luxury.
Even though endowed with intellect, how many of us act wisely? Some people in society even look at animals with envy and declare that animal life is superior and happier! This implies that in the view of a few bipeds, the life of quadrupeds represents a higher development! If a person holds such a view, then animal life is not condemnable to him but desirable, something he longs for. A sober man who does not drink considers a tavern a place of misery, but to a drunkard, the same place appears to be a haven of joy and peace.
For people of tamasic nature, to be born in animal species is a wonderful opportunity to experience joy, where they can fully express their tastes and tendencies. Thus, viewing it from a philosophical standpoint, we must accept without doubt or hesitation that tamasic people will experience full satisfaction only in an animal body. Therefore, it is said here that dying when Tamas is predominant, the soul takes birth in the wombs of the deluded.

Commentary -- Rajasi pralayam gatva karmasangisu jayate -- At the final moment, if for any reason in any person the mental modifications of Rajas like greed, activity, restlessness, craving, etc., get heightened and his body drops while contemplating those modifications, then that departed soul takes birth among human beings who are attached to action. If one who performed good deeds and held noble feelings throughout life happens to die when Rajas increases at the final time, then even after taking birth in human species after death, his conduct and feelings will remain good, and he will continue to do auspicious deeds. If one who led an ordinary life dies when Rajasic modifications like greed increase at the final moment, then upon coming into human species he will remain attached to objects, persons, and actions. If one whose life was dominated by lust, anger, etc., dies when Rajas increases, then even on taking birth in human species he will be of a distinctly demoniac nature (asuri sampatti). The point is that even after being born in the human world, humans become of three types due to the gradations of the qualities (gunas), i.e., humans of three distinct temperaments are formed. But one special thing to be noted here is that no matter what kind of conduct creatures born as humans after dying in heightened Rajas may possess, God-given discrimination (viveka) inherently exists in all of them. Therefore, every human being, by valuing this discrimination, can purify it through holy company (satsang) and self-study (svadhyaya), elevate themselves, and attain the Supreme God. Due to this God-given discrimination, all human beings without exception become eligible for God-realization. Tathā pralīnastamasi mūḍhayoniṣu jāyate -- At the final moment, if in any person for any reason temporary Tamas increases—that is, modifications of Tamas like heedlessness, delusion, darkness, etc., get heightened—and he dies while contemplating those modifications, then that person takes birth in dull/foolish species such as animals, birds, insects, moths, trees, creepers, etc. In these dull species, dullness exists in all, but it varies in degree; for instance, the dullness present in trees, creepers, etc. is far greater than that present in animals, birds, etc. If a person who did good works happens to die when a temporary modification of Tamas increases at the final moment and goes to dull species, even there his qualities and conduct will remain good, and his nature will be to do good actions. For example, when King Bharata's body dropped at the final moment in a modification of Tamas—that is, thinking of a deer—he became a deer of the dull species. But the renunciation and austerity performed by him during his human birth remained intact even in the birth of a deer. Even in the deer species he did not stay with his mother, ate dry leaves instead of green leaves, etc. Such vigilance is rare even among humans, which Sage Bharata possessed in his deer birth. Context -- Why do human beings who die when gunas temporarily increase at the final moment attain such states?—this is explained in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
(a) Meeting with death when Rajas is preponderant, one is rorn in the families of those who act for the sake of fruits for themselves. Being rorn in such families, he becomes alified to perform auspicious acts which constitute the way for attaining heaven and the like.

(b) Similarly, one who dies when Tamas is preponderant is born in the wombs of beings lacking in intelligence, namely, in the wombs of dogs, pigs etc. The meaning is that he is rorn as one incapable of realising any human end.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Dhanapati states the specific fruit resulting from the increase of Rajas. When Rajas is predominant, meeting dissolution, i.e., death, the embodied soul is born among people attached to action, i.e., human beings possessing attachment to work. Just as one who dies when Sattva or Rajas is predominant is born in Brahmaloka etc. or the human world as devas or humans respectively, similarly, when Tamas is predominant, the embodied soul is born in the wombs of stupid species like animals etc. — this is the meaning.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When one dies in the mode of passion, he takes birth among those engaged in fruitive activities; and when one dies in the mode of ignorance, he takes birth in the animal kingdom.

 Some people have the impression that when the soul reaches the platform of human life it never goes down again. This is incorrect. According to this verse, if one develops the mode of ignorance, after his death he is degraded to an animal form of life. From there one has to again elevate himself, by an evolutionary process, to come again to the human form of life. Therefore, those who are actually serious about human life should take to the mode of goodness and in good association transcend the modes and become situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. This is the aim of human life. Otherwise, there is no guarantee that the human being will again attain to the human status.
