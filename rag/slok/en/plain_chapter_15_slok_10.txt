Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
utkrāmantaṃ sthitaṃ vāpi bhuñjānaṃ vā guṇānvitam .
vimūḍhā nānupaśyanti paśyanti jñānacakṣuṣaḥ

Life Application:
The deluded do not see the soul departing, staying, or experiencing quality; those with eye of wisdom see.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The deluded do not perceive Him when He departs or stays or enjoys, conjoined with the Gunas; but they who possess the eye of knowledge behold Him.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The deluded do not see Him Who departs, stays and enjoys; but they who possess the eye of knowledge behold Him.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The ignorant do not see that it is He Who is present in life and Who departs at death or even that it is He Who enjoys pleasure through the Qualities. Only the eye of wisdom sees.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The deluded do not perceive Him when He departs or stays or enjoys, conjoined with the Gunas; but they who possess the eye of knowledge behold Him.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that the common intellect cannot perceive the subtle presence of the divine Consciousness (Atman) that animates the physical body. Just as an ordinary person sees a piece of art but cannot appreciate its depth like a critic, or sees a gem but cannot evaluate it like a jeweler, the ignorant see only the physical activities and miss the animating spark. Whether the soul is departing the body (Utkramantam), staying in it (Sthitam), or experiencing sensory objects under the influence of the three Gunas (Bhunjanam), the deluded remain blind to It. Only those equipped with the 'eye of knowledge' (Jnana-chaksuh), developed through discriminative reasoning (Viveka) and scriptures, can recognize this eternal Truth.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The deluded do not perceive;  [but] the men of knowledge-eye do see Him, as He dwells of rises up or enjoys what is endowed with Strands.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The deluded do not perceive It (i.e., the self) conjoined with the Gunas when departing or staying or experiencing. They who have the eye of knowledge see It.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Persons who are diversely deluded do not see it even when it is leaving or residing (in this body), or experiencing, or in association with the alities. Those with the eye of knowledge see.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya comments that the deluded are unable to perceive the Lord even though He resides in the body, departs from it, and illumines the Jiva's experiences. Only those who possess the eye of knowledge, endowed with devotion and understanding of the scriptures, can perceive the Supreme Lord as distinct from all material attributes.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that although the Atman is the closest entity to oneself and is the very source of cognition, the ignorant fail to see It because their minds are obsessed with external sense objects. Those with the eye of knowledge, trained in logical reasoning and Vedic testimony, perceive the Atman as distinct from the body-mind complex.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The deluded do not perceive Him when He departs or stays or enjoys, conjoined with the Gunas; but they who possess the eye of knowledge behold Him.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that the transitions of the Jiva—such as leaving the physical body (Utkramantam), residing in a body (Sthitam), and enjoying sensory pleasures in association with the three Gunas (Bhunjanam)—cannot be understood by the deluded who are attached to material possessions and enjoyments. Their vision is blinded by the darkness of ignorance (Tamoguna). However, the wise possess the 'eye of knowledge' (Jnana-chaksuh). They recognize that all movements, experiences, and changes belong to the body and the Gunas, while the self remains eternally stable, separate, and untainted.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The deluded do not perceive the Atman (self) as a form of knowledge separate from Its human and other configurations which are particular transformations of Prakrti, with which the self is conjoined when It is in embodied condition, experiencing the objects of the senses. The self also departs from the body when the body dies and assumes another body. The deluded or those who misconceive the body as the self do not understand all this. However, those who possess the eye of knowledge, i.e., have the knowledge concerning the difference between the body and the self, perceive the self as having a form different from the body in all conditions.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 15.9–15.11 jointly): Srotram etc. upto asetasah.  Mind :  the internal organ is hery implied.  Hence, due to His association of  the bodily existence, when He remains  [in the body], or rises up to seize another body, or enjoys the sense objects - at that time the deluded persons do not perceive Him, because they are not well enlightened.  But, considering everything as manifestation of the  [Self] awareness, the highly enlightened men recognize Him.  Thus they are men of unbroken contemplation.  Because they are engrossed in their exertion.
 
But in the case of those who have not mastered their self (mind),  even their effort does not  yield any fruit, because their passion has not yet been fully decayed. Indeed the seeds of corn, sown during the autumnal season are not capable of yielding a rich harvest, even though the means like water etc., get collected together. Therefore, it (the water etc.) does not constitute a collection of means.  Indeed totally different is the water  sent forth by the mass of  clouds that had been filled up in the month of Madhu.  Altogether different is the earth that  had been under the grip of the Cold-season (Sisira)  and has (now) brilliance  by the simple touch of the sun-rays.  In the same way, the effort of the men of uncontrolled  self, never gets to the stage of fulfilment of all reisities .  That is why those  who, even after receiving the means like the inititation etc.  necessary for the realisation of the Supreme Lord, continue to have their inner vision filled with the collection of the knots (granthi) of anger, bewilderment etc., in the manner as before-in their case, even the means does never bear any fruit.  This must be borne in mind.  For, it is has been said :

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Thus, the embodied soul, utkarmantam, when it is leaving the body-the body that was assumed earlier; or sthitam, while residing in the (present) body; or bhunjanam, experiencing sound etc.; or guna-anvitam, in association with, i.e. identified with, the alities called happiness, sorrow and delusion-even when, under such conditions, this one comes very much within the range of cognition; vimudhah, the persons who are diversely deluded as a result of their hearts being forcibly attracted by the enjoyments of seen and unseen objects; na, do not; anu-pasyanti, see. And the Lord regrets this saying, 'Alas! How sorrowful this is!'
Those others, again, jnana-caksusah, who have the eye of knowledge, [Jnana-caksuh means the scriptures supported by reasoning, which are the means of knowledge.] who have the insight of under-standing which has arisen from the valid means of knowledge, i.e., those having a clear vision; pasyanti, see this one.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha explains that the deluded fail to see the Supreme Ruler (Lord) who dwells within and governs the Jiva's transitions and experiences. The inability to see is due to delusion and the absence of spiritual vision, not because the Ruler does not exist.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that those who identify the self with the physical body and are blinded by material qualities (Gunas) cannot perceive the Jiva when it departs, stays, or enjoys. Those who have the eye of wisdom, distinguishing the soul from the body, perceive it in all states.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that the Jiva is fully eligible to be perceived in all conditions—whether departing from the body, staying in it, or enjoying objects of sound and touch conjoined with happiness and sorrow. Yet the deluded, whose minds are dragged away by desires for worldly enjoyments, fail to see It. Only the discriminative sages endowed with the eye of valid knowledge behold It.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the ignorant do not see the Jiva, who is distinct from the body-mind complex, even when it is departing to another body, staying in the present body, or experiencing sense objects in association with the senses. Only the wise, whose eye is knowledge, perceive It.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that although the Atman is extremely close and self-evident, the deluded cannot perceive It due to their intellect being stolen by the desire for visible and invisible sense pleasures. The eye of knowledge, born from scriptures supported by reasoning, is the sole means to realize this self-luminous Reality.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the deluded fail to see the Atman, which is of the nature of pure knowledge and distinct from the physical body, in all its states of staying, departing, or enjoying. They identify the self with the human or other bodily forms. The wise, possessing the eye of discrimination, see It as distinct.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that the deluded, lacking holy association (satsanga) and being self-absorbed in personal enjoyment, cannot perceive the Jiva. Those who have obtained the eye of knowledge through satsanga perceive the Jiva and its divine connection.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the transitions like departing and staying belong only to the limiting adjuncts (Upadhis), such as the senses and mind, and not to the Atman itself. The deluded cannot distinguish the Atman from the Upadhis, whereas the wise recognize the Atman as eternally free and unaffected.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The foolish cannot understand how a living entity can quit his body, nor can they understand what sort of body he enjoys under the spell of the modes of nature. But one whose eyes are trained in knowledge can see all this.

 The word jñāna-cakṣuṣaḥ is very significant. Without knowledge, one cannot understand how a living entity leaves his present body, nor what form of body he is going to take in the next life, nor even why he is living in a particular type of body. This requires a great amount of knowledge understood from Bhagavad-gītā and similar literatures heard from a bona fide spiritual master. One who is trained to perceive all these things is fortunate. Every living entity is quitting his body under certain circumstances, he is living under certain circumstances, and he is enjoying under certain circumstances under the spell of material nature. As a result, he is suffering different kinds of happiness and distress, under the illusion of sense enjoyment. Persons who are everlastingly fooled by lust and desire lose all power to understand their change of body and their stay in a particular body. They cannot comprehend it. Those who have developed spiritual knowledge, however, can see that the spirit is different from the body and is changing its body and enjoying in different ways. A person in such knowledge can understand how the conditioned living entity is suffering in this material existence. Therefore those who are highly developed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness try their best to give this knowledge to the people in general, for their conditional life is very much troublesome. They should come out of it and be Kṛṣṇa conscious and liberate themselves to transfer to the spiritual world.
