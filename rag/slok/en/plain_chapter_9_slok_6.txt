Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yathākāśasthito nityaṃ vāyuḥ sarvatrago mahān .
tathā sarvāṇi bhūtāni matsthānītyupadhāraya

Word Meanings:
यथाकाशस्थितो - as situated in space
नित्यं - always
वायुः - the wind
सर्वत्रगो - moving everywhere
महान् - the mighty
तथा - so
सर्वाणि - all
भूतानि - beings
मत्स्थानीत्युपधारय - know them abiding in Me

Life Application:
Understand that just as the mighty wind blowing everywhere rests in space, so all beings rest in God.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
As the mighty wind, blowing everywhere, rests always in the ether (akasha), know that in like manner all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
As the mighty wind, moving everywhere, rests always in the ether, so know thou that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
As the mighty wind blowing everywhere resides in space, even so know that all created beings dwell in Me.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
As the mighty wind moving everywhere rests always in the space, so know that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
As the mighty wind, moving everywhere, rests always in space, so know that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
As the mighty wind, moving everywhere, rests always in space, so know that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
As the mighty wind, moving everywhere, rests always in space, so know that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Just as ether supports the wind without being tainted by its attributes, the unmanifest Lord supports all beings while remaining immaculate.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
As the mighty wind, moving everywhere, rests always in the ether, so know that all beings rest in Me.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: As the mighty wind moving everywhere is supported by space and acts under space's envelope, so all beings exist and act solely under the divine will of the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: As space and wind share an inseparable relationship without space ever becoming tainted, so does the Supreme Lord sustain all beings while remaining ever untainted.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: To illustrate how unattached the Lord is, He gives an example: As the mighty wind moving everywhere rests in space without ever contacting or affecting space, so do all beings rest in Me without ever affecting My unattached Self.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Demonstrating by analogy, just as space is the container for wind, Lord Vishnu is the ultimate substratum of the universe, sustaining all while remaining immutable.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Space and wind illustrate how one can be the support of another without physical contact; in divine play, the Lord is the sole refuge of all creation.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: As wind residing in space is produced in and supported by space without ever affecting space, so all superimposed beings exist in the unattached Supreme Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Just as the great wind resting in space does not touch unattached space, so do all beings rest in Me without affecting My unattached Self.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: The analogy of space and wind proves that the Supreme Self is the absolute substratum, in which the entire universe rests without leaving any trace.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: By the principle of space and wind, it is clear that sustenance and governance of all beings depend on God's resolution, yet He remains completely untainted.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Just as the wind blowing everywhere rests supported by space, so do all beings rest absorbed in My supreme Form.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: The wind's dependence on space illustrates the Supreme Self's absolute detachment combined with universal substratumhood.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Understand that as the mighty wind, blowing everywhere, rests always in the sky, all created beings rest in Me.
