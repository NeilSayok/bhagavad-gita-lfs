Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
On this occasion, Arjuna understands the purity and divinity of Lord Krishna's power. Inspired by that, he bows down with respect and salutes Him, whom until now he knew only as the cowherd (Gopala) of Vrindavan. Although he was intelligent, the vision presented before him was too vast for him. To see it fully, analyze it, and assimilate it was extremely difficult. Now all he can do is surrender himself at the feet of the Lord and entreat Him, 'Please tell me who You are.' Giving his curiosity a more concrete shape, Arjuna indicates that he wants the answer to his question quickly: 'I want to know You.' It is a well-known fact that in spiritual texts, intense curiosity for the knowledge of Truth is given a very important place, because that indeed is the motivation of seekers. But here Arjuna's mind was anxious due to his immediate problem or challenge; therefore, it cannot be said that he, in reality, wants to know the actual divine truth of this vision. This curiosity of his is exaggerated by his emotions, and along with it, there is also the anxiety to know the outcome of the war. This fact becomes clear in his words: 'I am unable to know Your purpose.' The meaning of his curiosity is: what is the Lord's purpose in showing Arjuna the destruction of the Kauravas by assuming this terrible form? When he is intensely desiring an event to happen and signs appear before him which predict his certain victory in the war, he seeks confirmation of it from others. Here Arjuna is seeing the very event he wants to see happen, so he wants confirmation of it from the Lord's own mouth. Therefore, he asks this question. Destruction is but one expression of Truth. Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:

Just as someone, even though knowing a person's name etc., asks 'who are you?' to know their class or origin. If Arjuna did not know Him at all, then addressing Him as 'O Vishnu' (11.30) and 'You are the Imperishable' (11.18) etc. would not have been possible.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anticipating the doubt that since Arjuna had already seen the Lord's form, he should have no curiosity about it, the commentator says 'yatah' (since/because). By saying 'namo'stu' (salutations be to Thee), he indicates that one who wishes to receive instruction should show humility towards the teacher. He prays for the withdrawal of the fierce form by saying 'prasadam' (be gracious). To answer the potential objection: 'You yourself know Me, so why do you make such a request now? Observe My actions and understand accordingly,' Arjuna says 'na hi' (for indeed I do not know...).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Tell me who You are, who are of this fierce form? O Best of gods! Salutations to You. Please be gracious. I want to know You, the Primal One, in essence; because I do not know Your activity.

Commentary—'ākhyāhi mē kō bhavānugrarūpō namō'stu tē dēvavara prasīda'—You appear both as a divine form and as a fierce form; so who are You in reality, who have assumed such forms? Seeing the extremely fierce cosmic form, what else could Arjuna do out of fear except offer salutations? When Arjuna became completely unable to comprehend such a cosmic form of the Lord, he finally says, O Best among the gods! Salutations to You. Seeing the terrifying behavior of the Lord repeatedly licking everyone after taking them into His mouths, Arjuna prays, please be pleased (be gracious).

'vijñātumaticchāmi bhavantamādyaṃ na hi prajānāmi tava pravṛttim'—The first manifestation of the Lord was in the cosmic (universal) form. Therefore, Arjuna says, O Primal Lord (Adinarayana)! I do not know You clearly. I also do not know Your purpose/activity as to why You have appeared here? And so many warriors of our side as well as the opposing side are entering into Your mouths; hence, what do You actually want to do? The gist is that I want to know who You are and what You want to do, and please clarify this Yourself.

A question arises: the Lord's first manifestation was in the cosmic (universal) form, and now Arjuna is seeing the cosmic form in a certain aspect of the Lord—are these two cosmic forms the same or different? The answer is that the reality is known only to the Lord, but on reflection, it appears that the cosmic form which Arjuna saw also included this worldly cosmic form. Just as it is said that the Lord is all-pervading, it does not mean only that the Lord is pervading the entire universe, but rather the Lord is pervading beyond the universe as well. The universe is only in a fraction of the Lord, and infinite such creations are in a fraction of the Lord. Similarly, the cosmic form Arjuna is seeing contains this universe, and besides this, there is much more.

Connection—In the next verse, the Lord gives the exact answer to the question asked by Arjuna with prayer in the preceding verse.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
To dismiss the misconception that Arjuna is asking 'Who are You?' because he does not know His nature or attributes, the commentator says: 'dharmantara...' (another attribute). One might object that the question should then have been 'Are You endowed with a different attribute?', whereas asking 'Who are You?' is inappropriate. To this, the commentator says: 'Just as...'. Just as someone, despite knowing another's name etc. in daily dealings and seeing their form, asks 'Who are you?' to know their class or origin, similarly Arjuna, though knowing the Lord and His attributes, asks 'Who are You?' to know another attribute of His. Since the explanation of the question is currently being presented, it is referred to in the present tense.
Why could this not be a question from someone doubting the Lord's identity itself, wondering upon seeing the fierce form: 'Is this Rudra or Yama?' To this, the commentator says: 'If...'. (Arjuna addresses Him as) 'Your fierce rays are burning, O Vishnu!' (11.30), which shows he knew His identity.
Then, if the question is indeed about His attributes, why is the word 'antara' (other/different) appropriate? To this, he says: 'You...'. If he did not know the attributes of the Lord, then descriptions like 'You are the supreme Imperishable' (11.18) would not have been stated. Therefore, knowing the Lord and His attributes such as imperishability, the question is asked only to know other attributes of His. This is the meaning.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
In this verse starting with 'ākhyāhi', Arjuna (Dhananjaya), being unable to bear the sight of such an extremely terrifying form, seeks to know the purpose behind the manifestation of such a terrible form by the Lord who had begun to reveal His cosmic sovereignty, and also prays for His grace to assume a pleasing form again. The question arises because of his lack of understanding of the purpose behind displaying a terrifying form to a devotee like himself. Since the Lord was already known to him through teachings and direct perception, the question 'Who are You?' is not about His identity or name. Since the unknown aspect is specified here as 'for I do not know Your activity (purpose)', this question is asked out of a desire to know that very aspect; therefore it is interpreted as 'What do You intend to do?' This refutes the opinion of some who say that this question was asked due to the doubt whether some demon had come disguised as Krishna or if it was some other entity altogether. To set aside the general actions of the visible form at that time, the word 'intended' is used. By using the word 'Best of gods' (devavara), indicating His supremacy even over Brahma, Rudra, etc., the naturalness of offering salutations to the most exalted is shown, which is why the term 'Lord of all' (Sarveshvara) is used. As stated in the Ahirbudhnya Samhita: 'The Supreme is the master to be bowed to, all others are those who bow. This relationship of the one who bows and the one who is bowed to is natural (not driven by a specific self-interest).' The summarized meaning of the verse is 'to do this'. The result of the prayer 'be gracious' (prasīda) is 'and assume a pleasing form'. This will be elaborated later in 'show me that very form' (11.45) etc.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
'By causing distress to the entire world, I also experience distress, therefore be gracious to me'—thus Arjuna says in 'ākhyāhi'. Who are You, who were seen in the previous form, and who are You in this fierce form? Please tell this to me clearly. What is the means to obtain Your grace and description? To this, he says: 'O Best of gods', the foremost of gods. The gods are pleased by worship, but You are the Lord superior even to them, therefore salutations be to You. As indicated by passages like 'what seat is fit for Him whose seat is Garuda?', salutation itself is the means to gain Your grace. Therefore, be gracious, be pleased. If one asks what is the purpose of knowing Your nature when You are gracious, he says: 'vijñātum' (to know). I desire, I wish, to know You in detail, the Primal One, Purushottama, who are the root cause, along with Your divine plays (lilas). Because indeed, I do not understand Your activity (pravṛttim), Your actions and plays manifested here. When the knowledge of Your nature is attained, the knowledge of Your plays will also follow. By knowing Your plays, I shall worship You, hence 'I desire to know'—this is the import.
