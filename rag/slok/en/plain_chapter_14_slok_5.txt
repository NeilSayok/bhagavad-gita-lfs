Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The word 'Guna' being a technical term in spiritual science, it is difficult to translate it into any other language. Especially in English, there is no exact synonym for it. The reason is that Western psychology is still in its infancy. Only when their theoretical and practical observation and study of psychology are complete will they be able to understand the influence of these Gunas on the thoughts arising in the inner equipment (antahkarana) of every individual.

In spiritual literature, Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas—these three Gunas—are indicated by the colors white, red, and black, respectively. In Sanskrit, the word 'Guna' also means a rope. The implication is that these three Gunas of Nature are like ropes that bind the Self, which is of the nature of Existence-Consciousness, with the unreal and insentient non-Self. In short, these Gunas are three different types of mental temperaments under whose influence our mind constantly plays its game of reactions to changing circumstances.

These Gunas are born of Nature. They bind, as it were, the Self to the body, due to which It assumes the state of jiva (individual soul) and gets trapped in the ceaseless cycle of birth and death and the sorrows of the world. As mentioned earlier, these Gunas of Nature are not properties inhering in substances. We can only say that they are different mental moods that cause different individuals to behave differently.

This relationship between the Self and the non-Self is false, not real. The Self, free from the limitations of time and space, can never be truly bound to insentient limiting adjuncts projected like a dream. It always remains untouched by their defects, just as a post remains untainted by the ghost superimposed upon it, and a waking man remains untouched by the offenses of the dream-ego. Similarly, as long as the bondage caused by the three Gunas persists, it appears as though the Self has attained the state of jiva due to association with these non-Self adjuncts; but in truth, It always remains ever-free.

From the above explanation, it becomes clear how a clear understanding of the nature of these Gunas and the mechanism of bondage caused by them can grant us our charter of liberation.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Thus, by showing the creation of the world from the union of the Field (Kshetra) and the Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna), it was stated that Brahman Himself transmigrates due to ignorance (Avidya). Now, after restating the two queries raised at the beginning of the chapter, Anandagiri provides the answer through the subsequent text—'Which are the Gunas?' To the doubt as to why the word 'Guna' is applied to Sattva etc., he replies 'Gunah', because of their dependence. To the objection as to why the word 'Guna' should not signify inherence in a substance as in the case of color etc., he says it is not like color etc., because being of the nature of Prakriti, they are themselves the substrate of everything. If Gunas and Prakriti are spoken of separately, how can they be identical with Prakriti? To this he says there is no absolute difference between quality and substance, for in absolute difference identity is impossible, as between a cow and a horse. Nor is identity-in-difference possible. Why then are they defined as 'Gunas'? To this he replies: 'Therefore...'. The cause of their eternal dependence on the Knower of the Field is Avidya. The answer to 'which are the Gunas' has been given; now he answers 'how do they bind?'—to the Knower of the Field. He explains: making That (Kshetrajna) their support. Regarding the Prakritic nature of the Gunas, he says: 'and they...'. That from which birth takes place is 'sambhava'; those whose origin is Prakriti are 'Prakriti-sambhavah'. By this he excludes the Sankhya Prakriti called Pradhana, referring to it as 'God's Maya'. Explaining the verb 'nibadhnanti' with the qualification 'iva' (as it were), meaning they present the Self as possessing their modifications, he explains 'Mahabaho' as one with great and powerful arms. 'Dehinam' means the embodied soul who considers the body as the self. To the doubt how the immutable (Kutastha) can be bound, he says 'Avyayam', attributing it to the mystery of Maya (just as attributing atomicity to Mount Meru). To the objection that 'nibadhnanti' contradicts 'he is not tainted by sin', he asks 'Nanu'; and replies 'Parihritam' (refuted), since we have already resolved this objection by explaining the verb with 'iva' (as it were).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O mighty-armed one! Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas—these three Gunas born of Nature bind the indestructible embodied soul to the body.

Commentary: Sattvam rajastama iti gunah prakritisambhavah — In the third and fourth verses, the original Nature which was referred to as Mahad-Brahma, from that very primal Nature, these three Gunas—Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas—are born. Here the word 'iti' signifies that endless creations are produced by these three Gunas, and due to variations in their proportions, countless varieties of living beings arise; yet the Gunas are neither two nor four, but precisely three.

Nibadhnanti mahabaho dehe dehinamavyayam — These three Gunas bind the indestructible embodied soul in the body. Truth be told, these three Gunas do not bind anyone on their own initiative; rather, this soul itself gets bound by identifying and forming connections with these Gunas. The point is that by claiming the products of the Gunas—objects, wealth, family, body, temperament, thoughts, circumstances, actions, etc.—as one's own, this soul, though indestructible, gets bound, coming under the control of perishable objects and wealth, and becoming dependent despite being naturally free. For instance, when a person considers wealth as his own, its increase or decrease affects him; when he claims individuals as his own, their birth or death affects him; when he considers the body as his own, its changes affect him. This is how the Gunas bind the indestructible soul.

It is a matter of great wonder that while this soul inherently remains unchanged and indestructible, yet by coming under the influence of the Gunas and their mental modes, it behaves as if it were sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic. Goswami Tulsidas says: 'The soul is a part of God, indestructible, conscious, pure, and a natural reservoir of bliss.' (Ramcharitmanas 7.117.1). This indestructible nature of the soul never truly gets bound by the Gunas; but when it considers the perishable body as 'I', 'mine', and 'for me', then owing to its own belief it gets bound by the Gunas, and experiences difficulty in attaining the Supreme Reality (Gita 12.5). Bound in the body by the Gunas due to body-identification, it cannot know its true indestructible nature beyond the three Gunas. Even while bound to the body, the soul's essential indestructible nature remains exactly as it is, which the Lord indicates here by the word 'avyayam'.

Here, the word 'dehinam' implies that only due to identification, sense of possessiveness (mamatva), and desire regarding the body do the three Gunas bind this soul in the body. If there is no identification, possessiveness, or desire regarding the body, then it is truly of the nature of the Supreme Self.

Special Note: The soul establishes a relationship with the body in two ways: (1) Through identity (abheda-bhava) — placing oneself in the body, whereby one feels 'I am the body'; and (2) Through possessiveness (bheda-bhava) — placing the body in oneself, whereby one feels 'the body is mine'. By forming a relation through identity, the soul considers itself as the body, which is called ego/i-ness (ahanta); and by forming a relation through possessiveness, it considers the body as its own, which is called possessiveness (mamata). Thus, on connecting itself with the body, Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas—all three Gunas—strengthen ego and possessiveness in the body through their mental modes, binding the soul.

Just as after marriage, a person forms a relationship with his wife's entire family (in-laws), and her needs for clothes and ornaments appear as his own needs; similarly, when the relationship of 'I and mine' is formed with the body, the soul's connection with the whole world gets established, and it starts treating things needed for sustaining the body as its own necessities. By assuming identity with the temporary body, it desires to make the temporary body eternal, because the soul itself is eternal. Due to identifying with the body, the fear of death arises, because the body is destined to die. If there is no identification with the body, there will neither be a desire to remain eternal in the body nor a fear of death. Therefore, as long as there is a desire for physical permanence and fear of death, one remains bound by the Gunas.

The soul itself is indestructible, and the body is perishable. The body is separating from us at every moment naturally. What difficulty or effort can there be in disassociating from that which is already separating automatically? The only effort needed is not to grasp what is naturally falling away. By not grasping it, one will spontaneously experience one's indestructible, gunatita (transcendent) nature.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The three Gunas of Prakrti - Sattva, Rajas and Tamas - are inherent in the essential nature of Prakrti and are particular expressions of it. They can be known only through their effects such as 'brightness' etc. They are not apparent in the unevolved state of Prakrti but become apparent in its transformations as Mahat etc. They bind the self, which is conjoined with bodies such as those of divinities, men etc., composed of the modifications of Prakrti beginning with Mahat and ending with the elements. The self is immutable, i.e., It is not in Its pristine nature conjoined with the Gunas. But the Gunas bind It when residing in the body. The meaning is that they bind It by virtue of the limiting conditions of Its living in the body.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Objection: The knowledge promised to be imparted was stated in the previous two verses; why then is something irrelevant to the chapter being discussed in the subsequent text? To this Sri Jayatirtha replies: 'Bandha...' One who truly realizes 'I am bound' alone desires to know the means for its removal, and knowing it, practices it. Therefore, in order to facilitate the practice of the means for destroying bondage, to impart that knowledge, and to create curiosity, he first shows the manner of bondage caused by the three Gunas; hence there is no inconsistency.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Having explained through previous verses the Lila (divine play) of creation of living beings from His own Nature by His supreme will, Sri Vallabhacharya notes that the Lord now presents the Lila of bondage through fourteen verses detailing the Gunas that cause bondage—'Sattvam' etc. These Gunas along with their functions are not attributes of the Self, but belong to Prakriti ('Prakritisambhavah'); hence the Gunas themselves bind the conscious living entity in their respective bodies. It is the nature of Prakriti that brings about limitation in one who holds the ego-sense ('I-am-the-body'), and not in the unconditioned pure Self. The qualities present in Prakriti, which is an amsa (manifestation) of the Supreme Lord, are originally the Sat-Chit-Ananda attributes of the Lord Himself, which upon transforming in relation to inert matter become binding defects. Because they tightly bind, they are termed 'Gunas'. Thus He says 'nibadhnanti'. The Gunas such as Sattva arising from Prakriti bind the individual soul—the atomic conscious spark—even though it is indestructible (avyaya). By addressing Arjuna as 'Mahabaho', the Lord implies: 'Do you break that bondage!'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Thus, by refuting the atheistic Sankhya, it was explained that the union of the Kshetra and Kshetrajna is dependent on God. Now through fourteen verses beginning with 'Sattvam', it is explained as to which Guna creates what attachment, what the Gunas are, and how they bind. The Gunas named Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas are eternally dependent on the Purusha (Self), since all insentient objects exist for the sake of the sentient Self; they are not qualities inhering in substances like color etc. as held by the Vaisheshikas. Nor is a difference between quality and substrate intended here, for Prakriti itself consists of the three Gunas. Why then are they called 'born of Nature' (prakritisambhavah)? The balanced state of the three Gunas is called Prakriti or Maya of the Lord; when transformed into an unbalanced state through mutual subordination and dominance, they are said to be 'born of Prakriti'. In the body, which is the product of Prakriti (the aggregate of body and senses), these Gunas bind the embodied jiva who has superimposed identity with the body—even though the jiva is in reality immutable (avyaya) and free from all modifications. That is, while it remains immutable, they illusionarily display it as possessing their own modifications, just as water vessels display the sun in the sky as trembling through reflection-superimposition. That there is no real bondage has already been explained in verses such as 'even while residing in the body, O son of Kunti, he neither acts nor is tainted'.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Thus having described the origin of all beings from Prakriti and Purusha under the control of the Supreme Lord, Sri Sridhara Swami states that through fourteen verses beginning with 'Sattvam', he now elaborates on the Purusha's transmigration (Samsara) caused by contact with Prakriti. Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas are the three Gunas born of Prakriti (prakritisambhavah), meaning those whose manifestation is from Prakriti. Prakriti is the state of equilibrium of the Gunas; becoming manifested distinctly from that equilibrium, they bind the embodied soul (the conscious spark) residing through identification in the body (which is the product of Prakriti)—even though the soul is in reality immutable (avyaya) and changeless. The meaning is that they connect it with their respective products such as happiness, sorrow, delusion, etc.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Thus, by establishing in two verses the dependence of Prakriti and Purusha on God, the Sankhya view of their independence was refuted. Now through fourteen verses beginning with 'Sattvam', Dhanapati Suri explains what the Gunas are and how attachment occurs. Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas are the names of the Gunas. Here the word 'iti' is not a technical term denoting inherence in a substance like color etc., because being identical with Prakriti, they themselves are the substrate of all. Nor is a difference between quality and substrate intended here, for in absolute difference identity is impossible, as between a cow and a horse. Therefore, like qualities, they are eternally dependent on the Kshetrajna (Knower of the Field), since they manifest only by making Him their substrate. 'Prakritisambhavah': Prakriti is the Lord's Maya in the form of equilibrium of the three Gunas, which is the cause of their manifestation. These Gunas bind the embodied jiva in the body—the jiva who considers the body as the self, though in reality it is immutable (avyaya) and changeless as stated in 'being without beginning'. Just like attributing atomicity to Mount Meru, it is the glory of Maya that the bondage of the immutable is also illusory and not real; hence there is no contradiction with the earlier statement that 'the embodied one is not tainted'. Explaining 'Mahabaho' as addressing Arjuna with mighty arms, he notes: the knowledge 'I am immutable' alone is the means of liberation from gunic bondage, and not 'I am mighty-armed', for physical strength is useless here, whereas body-identification only causes bondage.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Objection: In the periodic creation etc., birth occurs through contact with matter (achit) caused by the resolve of the Supreme Being in accordance with past karma, and past karma yields its fruit as ordained by Him; at the end of that initiating karma the body perishes, and the soul is naturally pure. How then does it become subject to continuous creation? To this Sri Vedantadeshika introduces the section on bondage by Gunas: 'Evam...'—meaning in the manner stated in the previous two verses regarding aggregate (samashti) and individual (vyashti) creation. Attributes that define the essential nature never leave the substance; hence 'born of Nature' here implies their manifestation in unequal proportions during the effect-state. Thus he explains that they are essential characteristics inherently attached to Prakriti. The word 'iti' is used to highlight the difference between these Gunas and the qualities like sound etc., belonging to Prakriti in its evolved state. Refuting the Sankhya view that Sattva etc. themselves constitute the substance of Prakriti, he defines them as 'special inherent attributes'. By this, Shankara's statement that 'Guna is a technical term and not inhering in a substance' is refuted. To the doubt that Sattva etc. are not perceived directly, he replies that their effects—illumination, activity, and delusion—are directly observed, from which their specific causes are inferred, as confirmed by scripture. To the objection that if they are known solely through effects, they would cease to exist during dissolution, he says they remain unmanifest in the unevolved state of Prakriti, just as fragrance manifests in flowers upon transformation. How do the Gunas bind the Self which is naturally immutable (avyaya) and unsuited for connection with Gunas? He explains that 'avyaya' here means freedom from reduction/contraction of knowledge caused by contact with Gunas, and 'dehe' implies bondage due to the limiting adjunct of continuous bodily relation until liberation. By this, Shankara's view of 'they bind as it were' is rejected. The address 'Mahabaho' implies that this bondage of Gunas cannot be severed merely by physical strength of arms.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Objection: How is the bondage of the living soul (the seed) possible when residing in bodies created for divine sport (Lila) by Lila-Prakriti? To this Sri Purushottamji replies: 'Sattvam...' The Gunas named Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas are born of Prakriti (prakritisambhavah), meaning originating from Prakriti. Those Gunas bind—that is, bring under control—the embodied jiva who is the conscious spark of the Lord and free from destruction, who has manifested in that form and through that body for the enjoyment of the Gunas; because the soul is inclined toward sensory flavor/enjoyment (rasa).

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Having stated that Nature creates all beings under the refuge of God, Sri Neelkanth now explains how she binds them—'Sattvam...' Prakriti is the equilibrium state of Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas. Becoming activated in unbalanced proportions through mutual subordination and dominance, they are called 'born of Nature' (prakritisambhavah); they are not qualities separate from substance as held by the Vaisheshikas. O mighty-armed one, even though the embodied soul is immutable and changeless in the body, the Gunas acting like ropes bind him just as a rope ties a calf to a post.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Material nature consists of three modes – goodness, passion and ignorance. When the eternal living entity comes in contact with nature, O mighty-armed Arjuna, he becomes conditioned by these modes.

 The living entity, because he is transcendental, has nothing to do with this material nature. Still, because he has become conditioned by the material world, he is acting under the spell of the three modes of material nature. Because living entities have different kinds of bodies, in terms of the different aspects of nature, they are induced to act according to that nature. This is the cause of the varieties of happiness and distress.
