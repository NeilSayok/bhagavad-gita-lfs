Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
vidhihīnamasṛṣṭānnaṃ mantrahīnamadakṣiṇam .
śraddhāvirahitaṃ yajñaṃ tāmasaṃ paricakṣate

Word Meanings:
विधिहीनम् - without keeping to ordinance
असृष्टान्नम् - in which no food
मन्त्रहीनम् - devoid of Vedic mantras
अदक्षिणम् - devoid of priestly gifts
श्रद्धाविरहितम् - devoid of faith
यज्ञम् - sacrifice
तामसम् - Tamasic
परिचक्षते - declare.

Life Application:
Sacrifice contrary to rules, without food distribution, without chants, without gift, devoid of faith—is Tamasic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That sacrifice which is contrary to scriptural injunctions, in which no food is distributed, which is devoid of Vedic hymns, fees to priests, and faith, is declared to be Tamasic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
They declare that sacrifice to be Tamasic which is contrary to the ordinances of the scriptures, in which no food is distributed, which is devoid of Mantras, gifts and faith.

विधिहीनम् without keeping to ordinance? असृष्टान्नम् in which no food is distributed? मन्त्रहीनम् which is devoid of Mantras? अदक्षिणम् which is devoid of gifts? श्रद्धाविरहितम् which is devoid of faith? यज्ञम् sacrifice? तामसम् Tamasic? परिचक्षते (they) declare.Commentary A sacrifice performed by a Tamasic man is never guided by any consideration for the prescribed rites or incantation. You will find every irregularity in this sacrifice. No food is distributed. No fees which are prescribed in the scriptures are given to the priests. The Mantras are not changed properly. The hymns recited are defective in utterance and accent. Sometimes there is no recitation at all. There is no faith. A man who performs such a sacrifice does not get any merit.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Sacrifice that is contrary to scriptural command, that is unaccompanied by prayers or gifts of food or money, and is without faith - that is the product of Ignorance.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
A Tamasic sacrifice benefits neither the performer nor society. Food distribution (sharing resources with the needy) is essential for any Yajna. However, a Tamasic person ignores this along with scriptural hymns, proper recitation, and offering fees to the priests. It is characterized by absolute lack of faith and discipline.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
That sacrifice they declare to be of the Tamas (Strand) which is devoid of scriptural injunction, in which there is no [recitation of]  Vedic hymns, where no food and [sacrifical]  fee are distributed, and which is totally devoid of faith.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
That sacrifice which is bereft of authority, which uses offerings not sanctioned by the Sastras, which is performed without recitation of hymns and bereft of gifts and faith - that, they say, is marked by Tamas.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
They declare that sacrifice as 'done through tamas' which is contrary to injunction, in which food is not distributed, in which mantras are not used, in which offerings are not made to priests, and which is devoid of faith.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that a Tamasic sacrifice is performed contrary to scriptural rules, without distribution of food to Brahmanas, lacking proper pronunciation of Vedic hymns, without offering gifts to priests, and performed without any faith. It should be rejected.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Stale and dry, without scriptural injunctions, without food-distribution, without mantras, without gifts, and without faith is declared to be a Tamasic sacrifice.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that different sacrifices have specific rules, methods, materials, and orientations. A Tamasic sacrifice ignores all these rules. No food (Annam) is distributed to Brahmanas or the needy, out of laziness or stinginess. No Vedic or scriptural mantras are chanted, and no sacrificial fee (Dakshina) is given to the priests. Most importantly, it is completely devoid of faith (Sraddha) in the scriptures or the spiritual results.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
They say that sacrifice is Tamasa, which is bereft of the authority of injunction of Brahmanas of learning and good conduct as 'Do this sacrifice,' which is 'Asrstanna' viz., which uses offerings (materials) not sanctioned by the Sastras; which is performed without recitation of hymns; and which is bereft of gifts and faith.

Now, to explain the threefold division of austerities according to their source in the Gunas, Sri Krsna describes their differences in respect of the body, speech and mind:

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta explains that a sacrifice performed contrary to scriptural injunctions, devoid of proper rituals, where no food is distributed, and which is done without stabilizing the mind in faith, is declared Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Paricaksate, they delclare; that yajnam, sacrifice; as tamasam, done through tamas; which is vidhi-hinam, contrary to injunction, opposed to what is enjoined; asrstannam, in which food is not distributed-a sacrifice in which food (annam) is not distributed (asrstam) to Brahmanas; mantra-hinam, in which mantras are not used, which is bereft of mantras, intonation and distinct pronunciation; adaksinam, in which offerings are not made to priests as prescribed; and which is sraddha-virahitam, devoid of faith.
After that, now is being stated the three kinds of austerity:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that a Tamasic sacrifice violates scriptural rules. It lacks food distribution, correct intonation of mantras, appropriate priestly fees, and is performed with resentment or complete lack of faith. Unlike Rajasic sacrifice which may produce temporary results, a Tamasic one produces no spiritual merit at all.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that a Tamasic sacrifice is performed without scriptural rules, without distribution of food (Annam) to Brahmanas, devoid of correct intonations of mantras, without offering dakshina to priests, and performed without any faith.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that a Tamasic sacrifice is contrary to scriptures, lacks food distribution, has no correct chanting of mantras, lacks priestly gifts, and is devoid of faith. Such a sacrifice yields no positive results in this life or the next.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that a Tamasic sacrifice is performed without the approval of learned Brahmanas. It uses materials not sanctioned by scriptures, is devoid of correct mantras, is performed without appropriate gifts, and lacks faith. It results in downfall.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that a Tamasic sacrifice is devoid of Vedic rules, lacks offering of food to the deserving, lacks correct chanting of deity-invoking mantras, is without proper priestly fees, and is performed without faith. Devotees must shun such rituals.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that a Tamasic sacrifice is contrary to scriptural instructions. 'Asrstannam' means no food is distributed; it lacks Vedic mantras, dakshina, and faith.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Any sacrifice performed without regard for the directions of scripture, without distribution of prasādam [spiritual food], without chanting of Vedic hymns and remunerations to the priests, and without faith is considered to be in the mode of ignorance.

 Faith in the mode of darkness or ignorance is actually faithlessness. Sometimes people worship some demigod just to make money and then spend the money for recreation, ignoring the scriptural injunctions. Such ceremonial shows of religiosity are not accepted as genuine. They are all in the mode of darkness; they produce a demoniac mentality and do not benefit human society.
