Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Here, Arjuna expresses his desire in clear words: 'I wish to see You as before.' He is frightened upon seeing the Cosmic Form of the Lord, which He had assumed to demonstrate His oneness with the entire universe. When the attributeless, formless Principle or the concept of the Total (Samashti) propounded by Vedanta is directly experienced, only a rare few possess the intellectual capacity to grasp that Truth in its completeness and meditate upon it. Even if the intellect is occasionally able to grasp it, the devotee's heart usually cannot maintain identification (tadatmya) with it for long. At the level of the mind, Truth can be understood and enjoyed only through metaphors, never directly through its full majesty. In this verse, Arjuna describes the gentle form of Lord Vasudeva, which is the traditional form of Lord Vishnu in the Bhagavatam. In all the Puranas, the description of God is painted in metaphorical language, depicting Him as four-armed. To students of anatomy, this might seem like an extraordinary freak of nature. We forget that in reality, this is only a symbolic metaphor of Truth. These four arms of the Lord are symbols of the inner instrument (antahkarana chatushtaya)—namely, mind (manas), intellect (buddhi), memory/consciousness (chitta), and ego (ahankara). In the Puranas, the four-armed Lord is described as blue-complexioned (neela-varna) and wearing yellow garments (peetambara). The blue color signifies His infinity; a limitless entity always appears blue, like the cloudless summer sky or the deep ocean. The color of the earth is yellow. Thus, the meaning of Lord Vishnu's form is that the infinite Supreme Self, assuming limitation, plays the game of life through the inner instrument. It is also noteworthy that in all religions, God is described in the same manner. That Supreme Lord is omniscient and omnipotent. Man achieves success only through the strength of God's arm; therefore, the omnipotent Lord can only be indicated as four-armed. Lord Vishnu holds the conch, discus, mace, and lotus (shankha, chakra, gada, padma). Through the sound of the conch, the Lord calls everyone to come near Him. If man ignores this call of noble emotions in his heart, he is struck by the mace in the form of sorrow. Even after this, if man does not reform himself, the final result is decapitation by the discus, which means destruction in the form of not attaining the supreme goal of human life. On the contrary, if a person hears the call of divine life and follows it fully, he obtains the lotus. In Hinduism, the lotus flower is a symbol of spiritual perfection and peace. In Indian culture, it is also a symbol of prosperity. In Western countries, the white dove is considered a symbol of peace. In short, Arjuna wants the Lord to manifest in His gentle form and peaceful mood. For beginners and neophytes of Vedanta, it is difficult to maintain the pace of continuous, subtle philosophical thoughts. In such a state of intellectual fatigue, enthusiastic seekers need a reliable resting place where they can rest and rejuvenate themselves with fresh consciousness. This bed of peace is the personal, endowed, and gentle form of the Lord. Seeing Arjuna frightened, the Lord, withdrawing His Cosmic Form, reassures him with sweet words and says:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
To the query, 'What is that form which is to be shown?' in response to 'show me that very form', it is said: 'kirīṭinam' (wearing a crown). Taking the etymological derivation of the word 'cakrahastam' as 'one who has the discus in his hand', it is said: 'cakra-'. 'My desire should be fulfilled up to its fruition', thus it is said: 'yatah' (since). To the question, 'How can there be thousand-armedness when it is four-armed?', he answers: 'by the present (Cosmic form)'. To the question, 'While the Cosmic form is present, how can there be the state of the former form?', he says: 'by withdrawing (it)'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
I wish to see You in that very way, wearing a crown, holding a mace and a discus in hand. Therefore, O thousand-armed one, O Cosmic Form, assume that very four-armed form.

Commentary—'kirīṭinaṃ gadinaṃ cakrahastamicchāmi tvāṃ draṣṭumahaṃ tathaiva'—I desire to see You in that very form in which You wear a divine crown on Your head and hold a mace and a discus in Your hands.

The significance of saying 'tathaiva' (in that very way) is that because of my expressing the desire, 'draṣṭumicchāmi te rūpam' (11.3), You showed me the Cosmic Form. Now why should I leave any of my desire unfulfilled? Therefore, I now want to see that very gentle four-armed form that I have seen in Your Cosmic Form—'icchāmi tvāṃ draṣṭumahaṃ tathaiva.'

'tenaiva rūpeṇa caturbhujena sahasrabāho bhava viśvamūrte'—Arjuna uses the word 'eva' (only) here to exclude the Cosmic Form in which he saw the four-armed Vishnu form in the fifteenth and seventeenth verses. The meaning is that 'tena caturbhujena rūpeṇa' (with that four-armed form) are words meant to show the four-armed form, and the word 'eva' (only) is meant to exclude 'not with the Cosmic Form', and the word 'bhava' is for the prayer 'become'.

The purpose of all three words—'tadeva' in the previous verse, and 'tathaiva' and 'tenaiva' here—is that Arjuna was very frightened by the Cosmic Form. Therefore, using the word 'eva' three times, he says to the Lord that he wishes to see only Your Vishnu form, and not the Cosmic Form along with the Vishnu form. Therefore, please appear only in the four-armed form.

The address 'sahasrabāho' (O thousand-armed one) seems to imply: O Lord with thousands of arms! Please become four-armed; and the address 'viśvamūrte' (O Cosmic Form) seems to imply: O Lord of many forms! Please become one-formed. The significance is: please withdraw the Cosmic Form and become the four-armed Vishnu form.

Relation—In the thirty-first verse, Arjuna asked, 'Who are You of fierce form?' The Lord replied, 'I am Time (Death), engaged in destroying everyone.' Hearing this and seeing the extremely terrifying form, Arjuna felt that the Lord was in great anger. Therefore, Arjuna repeatedly prays to the Lord to be pleased. In order to remove this feeling of Arjuna, the Lord says—

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Describing that very form, he prays to the Lord with the words, 'kirīṭinam'. By this, it is understood that Arjuna used to see Lord Krishna adorned with the group of six divine attributes (shad-guna) and in the four-armed form. The two-armed form, however, was always visible to him. A fearless love/interest does not arise in a devotee who is mixed with Grace (Pushti-mishra-bhakta) by merely seeing the Akshara Brahman, which causes spontaneous activities. For those who are submerged in the nectar of the personal form of Lord Hari, who is beyond both the perishable (Kshara) and the imperishable (Akshara), and who is the great ocean of Rasa (taste/relish) and Bliss, there can never be attachment/relish anywhere else. Knowing this intention of Arjuna, just like that of the cowherds (Gopas), the Lord, reassuring him who was afraid, spoke again.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Specifying that very form, he says: 'kirīṭinam'. I wish to see You, who are wearing a crown, bearing a mace, and holding a discus in Your hand, in the same manner as I have seen You before. Therefore, O thousand-armed one, O Cosmic Form, withdrawing this Cosmic Form, manifest Yourself in that very four-armed form equipped with the crown, etc. By this, it is understood that Arjuna used to see Lord Krishna equipped with the crown, etc., even before. As for what was said earlier during the vision of the Cosmic Form, 'I see You with crowns, maces, and discuses,' that was with the intention of many crowns, etc. Or else: 'For so long, the one whom I saw as crowned, wielding a mace and a discus, and who was very pleasing, that very same Lord I now see as a mass of light difficult to look at'—thus the plural usage was employed there, and so there is no contradiction.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
He clarifies what was meant by 'that very form'. I wish to see You, who are wearing a crown, bearing a mace, and having the Sudarshana discus in Your hand. Therefore, in accordance with my desire, O thousand-armed one, O Cosmic Form, fulfilling my desire, concealing Your Cosmic Form again, and concealing Your thousand-armed state, manifest Yourself in that very form, which is the four-armed form of the son of Vasudeva, in that same former form.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Dispelling the misconception regarding the word 'tathaiva'—that 'desiring to see the former most wonderful form supporting the universe, I wish to see the former form in that very same way'—and implicitly resolving the doubt, 'How can a form belonging to a past state be displayed?', he explains 'tathaiva' through a different syntactic connection: 'tathaiva' means 'as before, crowned...'. The meaning is, 'I wish to see a form identical in nature to the one seen before.' In the word 'rūpeṇa' (by the form), since the third case ending (instrumental) cannot denote the instrument or means, it is explained as 'be endowed with that form'. The impossibility of one characterized by thousands of arms, bellies, and a Cosmic Form possessing a four-armed form is resolved through a distinction in time, indicated by the word 'idānīm' (now/at present).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O universal form, O thousand-armed Lord, I wish to see You in Your four-armed form, with helmeted head and with club, wheel, conch and lotus flower in Your hands. I long to see You in that form.

 In the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.39) it is stated, rāmādi-mūrtiṣu kalā-niyamena tiṣṭhan: the Lord is eternally situated in hundreds and thousands of forms, and the main forms are those like Rāma, Nṛsiṁha, Nārāyaṇa, etc. There are innumerable forms. But Arjuna knew that Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead assuming His temporary universal form. He is now asking to see the form of Nārāyaṇa, a spiritual form. This verse establishes without any doubt the statement of the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead and all other features originate from Him. He is not different from His plenary expansions, and He is God in any of His innumerable forms. In all of these forms He is fresh like a young man. That is the constant feature of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. One who knows Kṛṣṇa becomes free at once from all contamination of the material world.
