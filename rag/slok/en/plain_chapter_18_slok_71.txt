Life Application:
Listen to spiritual wisdom with an open heart and genuine trust to attain peace and liberation.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
The subject of the Gita is not something to be merely admired from a distance. To fully benefit from the knowledge taught in the Gita, the seeker's personality must be well-integrated at all levels. Therefore, Lord Krishna specially mentions two qualities here, endowed with which the listener will derive the maximum benefit from hearing: Faith (Shraddha) and Freedom from Envy (Anasuya). Faith does not mean blind belief; it is the capacity of the intellect to comprehend the subtle truth. Freedom from envy means being receptive without looking for defects or criticizing the teaching. Such a listener, even if he only hears this scripture, will be liberated from sins and attain the auspicious worlds of the righteous. This does not mean one has to wait for death to go to heaven; the experience of bliss is here and now. It is the duty of the teacher to see whether the student has correctly understood the knowledge or not. Therefore, Lord Krishna asks Arjuna in the next verse.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Even the man who listens to this with faith and without envy, he also, being liberated from sin, shall attain the auspicious worlds of the righteous. Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains the reward of the listener: 1. Listens with faith and without envy (śraddhāvānanasūyaśca śṛṇuyādapi): The listener must have faith in the truth of the Gita and should not find faults in the speaker or the text. 2. Liberated from sin (so'pi muktaḥ): Even if he only listens without studying or teaching, he is cleansed of past sins. 3. Attain the auspicious worlds of the righteous (śubhā.Nllokānprāpnuyātpuṇyakarmaṇām): He reaches the higher realms attained by those who perform righteous actions like sacrifices and charity. This highlights the greatness of hearing the Gita.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
A man who, with faith and without cavilling, hears the Gita when faught by a alified teacher, he too is, by such hearing, released from all evil incompatible with devotional life. He shall reach the Lokas, i.e., the realms of the hosts of My devotees who have done virtuous acts, and who will facilitate the growth of devotion in these new arrivals and lead them ultimately to liberation.

Whosoever shall declare etc :  Whosoever directs the mind [of the devotees] by face to face method, following the best procedure prescribed in the scriptures; i.e., he who shall impart  [this knowledge] - he becomes one with Me. In order to ascertaini whether or not the disciple has comprehended the meaning of the Scripture, the Lord asks (the following estion), the intention of the estioner beings, 'If it is known that it has not been comprehended, I shall again make him grasp it through other means.' Hery is shown the duty of the teacher that a student should be made to achieve his goal by taking the help of a different method.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Having stated the fruit of the teacher and the reader, the Lord now describes the fruit of the listener: 'sraddhavan...'. The man who listens to this dialogue with faith and free from envy (without finding faults or questioning the recitation) shall be freed from all sins. Even by merely hearing the syllables, he will reach the auspicious worlds of those who perform great sacrifices like Ashvamedha. If this is the fruit of mere hearing, the fruit of those who understand the meaning and practice it is beyond words.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

If someone hears this from another who is reciting, with faith and without envy (not thinking that the recitation is incorrect or done for show), he too is liberated from all sins and reaches the auspicious worlds of those who perform meritorious deeds like Ashvamedha.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
And one who listens with faith and without envy becomes free from sinful reactions and attains to the auspicious planets where the pious dwell.

 In the sixty-seventh verse of this chapter, the Lord explicitly forbade the Gītā’s being spoken to those who are envious of the Lord. In other words, Bhagavad-gītā is for the devotees only. But it so happens that sometimes a devotee of the Lord will hold open class, and in that class not all the students are expected to be devotees. Why do such persons hold open class? It is explained here that although not everyone is a devotee, still there are many men who are not envious of Kṛṣṇa. They have faith in Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. If such persons hear from a bona fide devotee about the Lord, the result is that they become at once free from all sinful reactions and after that attain to the planetary system where all righteous persons are situated. Therefore simply by hearing Bhagavad-gītā , even a person who does not try to be a pure devotee attains the result of righteous activities. Thus a pure devotee of the Lord gives everyone a chance to become free from all sinful reactions and to become a devotee of the Lord. Generally those who are free from sinful reactions, those who are righteous, very easily take to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The word puṇya-karmaṇām is very significant here. This refers to the performance of great sacrifices, like the aśvamedha-yajña, mentioned in the Vedic literature. Those who are righteous in performing devotional service but who are not pure can attain the planetary system of the polestar, or Dhruvaloka, where Dhruva Mahārāja is presiding. He is a great devotee of the Lord, and he has a special planet, which is called the polestar.
