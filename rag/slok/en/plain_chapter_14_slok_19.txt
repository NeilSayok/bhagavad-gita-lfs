Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
From the description given so far, such a tragic picture of the Soul emerges as if it can never be free from the bondage of these Gunas. A student of the Gita might feel disappointed at this point. As long as we sit in a train, the motion of the train is our motion. But as soon as we get down at our destination, we become stationary; only the train remains in motion. Similarly, identifying ourselves with the body and other limiting adjuncts (upadhis) and considering their modifications as our own, we experience sorrow, suffering, and bondage. The implication is that the bondage experienced by the Soul is born of ignorance (false) and not real. Therefore, transcending the ego sense in the adjuncts and abiding in the Self as their witness is freedom from the three Gunas.

In the practice of Nididhyasana (deep contemplation), one practices the removal of this identification and abiding in one's true nature. A seeker qualified for meditation will see the Self, that is, directly experience Himself as the Self. This Self is entirely free from all defects. But this 'seeing' is not like seeing an object like a jar or cloth; the Self is the seer of the senses, mind, and intellect, not their object. Seeing here implies conclusive knowledge after gaining which there remains no occasion for doubt or deliberation regarding the Truth.

BEHOLDS NO AGENT OTHER THAN THE GUNAS — The self-realized man not only recognizes his infinite nature, but also knows that the ego which hitherto boasted of agency is nothing other than these Gunas; that is, the ego is a product of those Gunas. These Gunas rule over our thoughts and determine their direction. Therefore, the subtle body in which the pride of agency and enjoyment resides is indicated here by the word 'Guna'.

AND KNOWS THAT WHICH IS BEYOND THE GUNAS — The mind, being inert by itself, can neither perform any action nor observe its own modifications. Therefore, the conscious Principle which imparts consciousness to it and makes it functional must be distinct from that mind. If water kept in a vessel shines like melted silver, it means it must have derived that luster from the sun. Water has no brightness of its own. Now if the sun's reflection in that water gets shattered, the cause is the nature of the water in the vessel, not that the sun itself is dancing in the sky! The Consciousness manifested in the limiting adjunct of the mind is called the individual jiva, which experiences the suffering of the limitations of the adjunct. The man who renounces the jiva-state and recognizes the Sat-Chit-Ananda Self, which is its original prototype, as his own nature, becomes forever free from the bondages of all limitations, tears of sorrow, and sighs of despair.

HE ATTAINS MY NATURE — According to the declaration of the Upanishad, the knower of the Self becomes the Self itself. 'My nature' refers to the nature of the Self. Lord Krishna should not be understood merely as Devaki's son or the flute-bearer Krishna of Vrindavan. Here Sri Krishna is teaching as the Self of all beings, and every student of the Gita should understand that his own inner Self is imparting instruction to the jiva.

A awake person creates a state in a dream where, as a dreamer, he becomes happy or sad by acquiring or losing objects. All these joys and sorrows belong to the dreamer residing within himself. When he wakes up from the dream, the dream world and its bondages dissolve, and the dreamer himself becomes the awake person. Imagine that during the dream state, the consciousness of the awake state comes to instruct that suffering dreamer; it would say this very verse: 'O Dreamer, when you see no performer of action other than the dreaming mind, and know within yourself that Principle which is beyond this mind, then you will attain this nature of Mine, that is, the consciousness of the awake state.'

Similarly, teaching here from the standpoint of Pure Consciousness, the Lord says that a person who abandons his personality of waking, dream, and deep sleep states and recognizes the Self-nature beyond them, can truly be called an awakened man of the Supreme Truth. He himself becomes the Self (mad-bhava).

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
'Beholds no agent other than the Gunas' means that one does not see any modifying/transforming agent other than the Gunas. Otherwise, it would contradict the Sruti text: 'When the seer beholds the Golden-hued Creator, the Lord, the Person, the source of Brahma' (Mundaka Up. 3.1.3). Also in Mokshadharma: 'I am not the agent, nor are you the agent; the Lord alone who is ever Supreme is the Agent.'

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Before answering questions like 'In which mode, how?', to explain how liberation from the Gunas occurs, Sri Shankara introduces the right knowledge that dispels false knowledge, repeating the previous context starting with 'purusasya...'. Liberation means the attainment of Brahmanhood through separation from the Gunas. To explain the verse presenting right knowledge, he takes up the phrase 'nanyam...'. The intended meaning of the word 'guna' referring to sattva etc. and their effects is explained as body, senses and objects. The word 'anu' means after attaining knowledge. After stating the literal meaning, he gives the implied meaning of the first half: 'guna eva...' meaning the Gunas transformed as body, senses and objects are the real agents of all actions (physical, vocal, and mental, prescribed or prohibited). 'Param' means distinct/different from. He clarifies this distinctness: 'guneti...' meaning the Self which is the attributeless Brahman. 'Madbhavam' means he attains the identity of Brahman and Self; his intrinsic Brahmanhood is manifested.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
When the wise (discerning) person beholds no agent of action other than the three Gunas and experiences himself as beyond the Gunas, he then attains My essential nature.

Commentary -- nanyam gunebhyah ... madbhavam so'dhigacchati -- There is no agent of action other than the Gunas; that is, all actions are taking place through the Gunas alone, and all modifications are happening within the Gunas only. The point is that Gunas alone are the cause of all actions and changes, and there is no other cause. That principle by which these Gunas are illumined is beyond the Gunas. Being beyond the Gunas, it is never contaminated by them; that is, Gunas and actions have no effect on it. The discerning seeker who knows that Principle through wisdom experiences himself as beyond, unattached to, and untouched by the Gunas, realizing that 'My connection with the Gunas never was, is not, will not be, and can never be.' Because the Gunas are changeable, while the Self never undergoes any change. He then attains My state, My spiritual nature. The meaning is that the mistaken idea of having a connection with the Gunas is erased, and his eternally established relationship with Me remains as it is.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
The seer has in the first place to totally subdue his Rajas and Tamas and stay in pure Sattva. This is accomplished through nourishment by Sattvika food and the performance of disinterested actions for the propitiation of the Lord. He then perceives 'no agent of action other than the Gunas' i.e., sees that the Gunas are themselves the agents according to their nature. Further he perceives what is 'other than the Gunas,' i.e., perceives the Gunas which are agents and the self who is not an agent of action. Such a seer attains to 'My state,' i.e., gains likeness with Me in transcending the three Gunas etc. The purport is this:  The self, pure in nature by Itself, gains agency through varius actions by contact with the Gunas springing from past Karmas. When one perceives the self in this way, namely, that the self by Itself is no agent of actions and is of the nature of infinite knowledge, then It attains to My likeness.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
'Beholds no agent other than the Gunas'—to refute the misinterpretation that independent agency belongs only to the Gunas and to none else, the commentator explains 'parinami' (it refers to agency of transformation). How is this established? To this he says 'anyatha'—otherwise, if agency of anyone other than the Gunas were denied, it would contradict the statement in Mokshadharma where the agency of the Supreme Lord is declared.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Having thus explained the pastime of bondage (bandha-lila) of all beings through the creation and divisions of the Gunas, the Lord now describes in two verses the pastime of liberation (moksha-lila) by transcending the Gunas through discrimination: 'nanyam...'. When the seer beholds no agent other than the Gunas—perceiving that the Gunas alone are the agents according to their respective tendencies—and knows the Supreme Self as superior to the Gunas, then that seer attains My state, the state of Akshara Brahman. As the Sruti declares: 'He who knows Brahman becomes Brahman Himself' (Mundaka Up. 3.2.9). Attributeless Brahman is itself imperishable; transcending these three Gunas, he attains immortality or enjoys the bliss of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Three topics were presented for discussion in this chapter. Among them, two topics have been explained: that the conjunction of Kshetra (body) and Kshetrajna (Self) is under God's control, and what the Gunas are and how they bind. Now what remains to be stated is how one gets liberated from the Gunas and what the characteristics of a liberated person are. Since the Gunas are of the nature of false knowledge, liberation from them is achieved through right knowledge—so He says: When the discerning seer does not perceive any agent other than the Gunas transformed into the shapes of effect (body), instruments (senses), and objects—that is, after inquiry realizes that the Gunas transformed into inner senses, outer senses, body, and objects are the sole agents of all actions; and knows the Self as superior to the Gunas transformed into various states—unaffected by the Gunas and their products, illuminating them just as the sun is untouched by water or its ripples, changeless, the witness of all, uniform everywhere, the one Knower of the Field (Kshetrajna)—then that seer attains My spiritual nature (identity with Me).

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Thus having detailed the worldly existence resulting from attachment to the Gunas of Nature, the Lord now shows liberation through discrimination from them: 'nanyam...'. When the seer, becoming wise, beholds no agent other than the Gunas transformed into the shapes of intellect etc., but sees that the Gunas alone perform all actions, and knows the Self as distinct from the Gunas and as their witness, he then attains My state, i.e., Brahmanhood.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
The attachment of the person, endowed with false knowledge situated in Prakriti, to the enjoyable objects of the nature of pleasure, pain, and delusion in the form 'I am happy, I am miserable, I am deluded' is the cause of samsara involving births in good and bad wombs. As was briefly stated in the previous (thirteenth) chapter: 'Purushah prakritistho hi bhunkte prakritijan gunan...' (13.21). Having detailed that very principle in this chapter beginning with 'Sattvam rajastama iti gunah...', liberation through right knowledge is now shown: when the witness beholds no agent other than the Gunas and knows the Self as transcendental to the Gunas, he attains My divine nature.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Objection: If in 14.18 ('urdhvam gacchanti sattvasthah') liberation is stated for one situated in Sattva, then stating liberation next for one who has transcended the three Gunas would be contradictory. To resolve this doubt, the context is introduced: Sattvika food increases Sattva. Transcending worldly Gunas and ascending through augmented Sattva is well reconciled. By conquering Rajas and Tamas entirely through Sattvika food and selfless worship of the Lord, when the seer beholds no agent other than the Gunas—realizing that the Gunas act according to their nature—and knows the Self as distinct from the agent Gunas and as non-agent, he attains My nature (paramam samyam / supreme likeness). Here 'non-agent' means agency in worldly Karmas (virtuous or sinful) is non-inherent to the Self, being caused by contact with Gunas, while the Self naturally is of infinite knowledge. 'Madbhavam' means gaining similarity/likeness with Me, avoiding false identity, as affirmed by 'mama sadharmyam agatah' (14.2) and the Sruti 'attains supreme similarity' (Mundaka 3.1.3).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus described the forms of the Gunas etc. created by His own will for His divine sport, the Lord states that he who is free from the notion of high or low in the higher, middle, or lower attributes, possesses knowledge of My divine sport, and is detached from contact with the Gunas, attains My devotion—to explain this, He says: 'nanyam...'. When at the time graced by the Lord's mercy, the discerning seer beholds no agent other than the Supreme Lord who is the root of all and who has manifested the Gunas for His divine sport, and knows the Supreme Person (Purushottama) as beyond the diverse forms of the Gunas, he then attains 'madbhavam', i.e., My devotion.
The underlying purport is this: Seeing the diverse varieties created by the Gunas and through knowledge of the glory of Purushottama, he beholds the Lord who is manifested as the creator of that variety everywhere just as the Lord Himself sees only His own Self in everything; seeing none else, he attains My divine state (devotion).

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The answer to how Nature binds the Spirit has been given. To answer how one attains liberation from it, the Lord says: 'nanyam...'. The seeing living entity (jiva) does not perceive the Self, which is pure consciousness (drik-svarupa), as the agent separate from the Gunas transformed into effects, instruments, and objects—that is, through discrimination he sees that 'the Gunas alone are the agents, I am not the agent'. And if he knows Me (the Supreme Lord), who am beyond the Gunas and the witness of their activities, then that Knower attains My nature, i.e., Brahmanhood. Otherwise, he falls into the state of the Gunas (samsara).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
When one properly sees that in all activities no other performer is at work than these modes of nature and he knows the Supreme Lord, who is transcendental to all these modes, he attains My spiritual nature.

 One can transcend all the activities of the modes of material nature simply by understanding them properly by learning from the proper souls. The real spiritual master is Kṛṣṇa, and He is imparting this spiritual knowledge to Arjuna. Similarly, it is from those who are fully in Kṛṣṇa consciousness that one has to learn this science of activities in terms of the modes of nature. Otherwise, one’s life will be misdirected. By the instruction of a bona fide spiritual master, a living entity can know of his spiritual position, his material body, his senses, how he is entrapped, and how he is under the spell of the material modes of nature. He is helpless, being in the grip of these modes, but when he can see his real position, then he can attain to the transcendental platform, having the scope for spiritual life. Actually, the living entity is not the performer of different activities. He is forced to act because he is situated in a particular type of body, conducted by some particular mode of material nature. Unless one has the help of spiritual authority, he cannot understand in what position he is actually situated. With the association of a bona fide spiritual master, he can see his real position, and by such an understanding he can become fixed in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness. A man in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is not controlled by the spell of the material modes of nature. It has already been stated in the Seventh Chapter that one who has surrendered to Kṛṣṇa is relieved from the activities of material nature. For one who is able to see things as they are, the influence of material nature gradually ceases.
