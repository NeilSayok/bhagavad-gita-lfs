Speaker: Arjuna

Slok:
amī hi tvāṃ surasaṅghā viśanti kecidbhītāḥ prāñjalayo gṛṇanti .
svastītyuktvā maharṣisiddhasaṅghāḥ stuvanti tvāṃ stutibhiḥ puṣkalābhiḥ

Life Application:
Observe celestial hosts entering the divine presence, offering praises with folded hands while singing hymns of peace.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Indeed, these hosts of gods are entering into You, and some, being afraid, praise You with folded hands. The groups of great sages and perfected ones, saying 'May it be well' (wishing auspiciousness), praise You with complete hymns.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Until now, the description of the Cosmic Form given by Arjuna was static, and at the same time, wonderful and fierce. Here, Arjuna describes the motion and action visible in the Cosmic Form. These groups of gods are entering the Cosmic Person and disappearing. If many people who are unfit for reformation are forcibly drawn towards the Cosmic Form and get lost in it, and others are waiting and watching this process, they will surely be terrified with fear. A man apprehensive of a certain calamity, when he sees no means of safety, in those moments of despair, always turns towards prayer. This psychological truth is beautifully expressed here in these words: 'some in fear extol You with folded hands.' And this is not all. The groups of great sages and perfected ones, due to the divine and inner peace attained from the maturity of their knowledge, remain undisturbed by the vision of this Cosmic Form and sing praises of this manifold Cosmic Person with excellent (abundant) hymns. They always recite auspicious benedictions, meaning wishing for the welfare of all. Due to their perfect knowledge, they know that the Lord assumes such an extremely fierce and terrifying form only when He wishes to completely reconstruct the universe. The perfected ones also know that in this plan of creation through destruction, there is no loss of any kind. Therefore, welcoming this process of destruction, they wish for a golden age for the world, which will surely come after this complete destruction.

In this verse, the creatures of the world are classified into three parts: superior, mediocre, and inferior. Inferior creatures perish just like that. They are the first victims of the process of death, and unfortunately, they are not even aware of this action so that they could resist it in any way. The mediocre people thoughtfully watch this process of decay and destruction and are also aware of it. They become apprehensive thinking about their fate. They do not know that there is actually no loss due to destruction, and they become terrified by the inevitable end of all beings. But different from these, there is another class of superior men, who have complete knowledge of the nature and behavior, i.e., the working of the totality. They are never touched by the fear that the events occurring in daily life might also happen to them. Those who recognize the nature of the ocean have no reason to be worried about the destruction of the waves. Similarly, when the perfected ones see the great destruction that occurs before the reconstruction of a dying culture, they recognize this great power of Truth and wish for peace and welfare for the future world created by God. From whichever perspective we study this poetry, we find how great a psychologist Vyasa himself is and how beautifully he has collected the knowledge of human behavior here, so that man can evolve quickly and reach his goal of perfection. Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
The purpose of the subsequent text starting with 'ami hi' is stated by 'atha' etc. Seeing the Lord displaying the certain victory of the Pandavas, Arjuna speaks, as indicated by 'seeing Him' (tam pasyan). The following series of verses is intended to expand on the Cosmic Form itself, as shown by 'moreover' (kinca). By splitting the word as 'asura-sanghah' (hosts of demons), it should also be said that Duryodhana and others, who are a burden to the earth, are entering You. Regarding the specific distinction between those standing ready to fight in both armies, it is said, 'there' (tatra) etc. He shows the manner in which great sages like Narada, who have come to the battlefield to watch and who, fearing the destruction of the universe, wish to prevent it, engage in praising the Lord, by 'in battle' (yuddhe) etc.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Those very hosts of gods are entering into You. Some of them, being afraid, are singing praises of Your names and qualities with folded hands. The hosts of great sages and perfected ones, saying 'May there be welfare! May there be auspiciousness!', are praising You with excellent hymns.

Commentary — 'ami hi tvam surasanghah visanti' — When Arjuna went to heaven, those very gods with whom he became acquainted there, Arjuna is referring to them here, saying that those very gods are seen entering Your form. All these gods arise from You, remain in You, and enter into You. 'kecid bhitah pranjalayo grinanti' — But among those gods whose lifespan is still quite remaining, such Ajanadevatas (within the Cosmic Form), on seeing the terrifying forms like Narasimha etc., being afraid, are singing Your name, form, pastimes, and qualities with folded hands. Although the gods are singing the praises of the Lord only out of fear of the incarnations like Narasimha etc., and the death-form Time (all of which are parts of the Cosmic Form); but to Arjuna, it appears as if they are afraid and praising upon seeing the Cosmic Form of the Lord. 'svastityuktva maharsisiddhasanghah stuvanti tvam stutibhih puskalabhih' — Auspicious prayers ('May there be welfare! May there be auspiciousness!') are being chanted by the seven sages, celestial sages, great sages, Sanaka and others, and the gods, and Your praises are being sung through very excellent hymns.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
These hosts of superior Devas beholding You as the foundation of the universe, rejoice and move towards You. Among them, some in fear, on seeing Your extremely terrible and wonderful form, 'extol,' namely pronounce sentences in the form of praise, according to their knowledge. Others, the bands of seers and Siddhas, knowers of the truth, higher and lower, saying 'Hail,' glorify You in hymns of abounding praise which are suitable to the Lord.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Now, seeing the Lord revealing Himself as the destroyer of the burden of the earth, Arjuna says 'ami' etc. These hosts of gods, the Vasus and others, who have descended in human form to relieve the earth of its burden, are seen to enter You while fighting. Likewise, by splitting the word as 'asura-sanghah' (hosts of demons), it should also be stated that Duryodhana and others, who are the burden of the earth, are entering You. In this way, some in both armies, being terrified and unable to flee, praise You with folded hands. Thus, seeing the portents of evil on the onset of the battle, saying 'May it be well for the whole world!', the hosts of great sages and perfected ones, like Narada and others who have come to watch the battle, praise You with complete and meaningful hymns that glorify Your supreme qualities, in order to avert the destruction of the universe.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Furthermore, 'ami hi' etc. These hosts of gods, filled with fear, enter You, i.e., take refuge in You. Among them, some who are extremely terrified stand at a distance, and with folded hands, praise You and pray, 'Victory to You! Protect us, protect us!' The rest is clear.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Now, seeing the Lord, who is intent on relieving the earth's burden and has set out to show the certain victory of the Pandavas in order to resolve Arjuna's doubt of whether 'we shall conquer them or they shall conquer us', Arjuna speaks. It seems the Acharya (Shankaracharya) has commented on the reading 'tvam' as 'these hosts of gods, the Vasus and others, who have descended in human form to relieve the earth of its burden, enter You while fighting'. Otherwise, the Bhashya reading 'tva' or 'tvam' would be expected. The commentator's statement that by splitting the word as 'asura-sanghah', it should also be said that Duryodhana and others, who are a burden to the earth, are entering You, should be understood as consistent only with the reading 'tva'. Among them, some who are terrified and unable to flee, praise Him with folded hands. Furthermore, the hosts of great sages and perfected ones, like Narada and others who have come to watch the battle, seeing the portents causing the destruction of the world as the battle stands imminent, praise Him with complete hymns, saying 'May there be welfare for the world!'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
In the phrase 'ami hi tva visanti', destruction is not intended, because its mention alongside prayers etc. indicates there is no imminent destruction here like that of the sons of Dhritarashtra, and also because the interpretation by others referring to the hosts of descended gods would contradict verses like 'viksante' [11.22]. Therefore, what is spoken of here is the act of approaching the Lord in service. With this intention, it is said, 'ami surasanghah' (these hosts of gods) etc. Since 'kecid bhitah' (some in fear) speaks separately of those who are afraid, this sentence (ami surasanghah) implies those who are free from agitation and are joyful. To express this, the arrival of all the gods, including Brahma, for service is mentioned first. The word 'utkristah' (superior) is the meaning implied by the word 'sura'. To rule out entry for the sake of destruction, the term 'hristamanasah' (joyful in mind) is used. To rule out the entry into the mouth mentioned later, 'tvatsamipam' (near You) is used. The word 'kecit' (some) requires a specific sub-group, and since 'surasanghah' is relevant and no other class is mentioned, it is said 'tesveva' (among those very gods). The words 'atyugram atyadbhutam ca' describe the present form of the Lord, which is the cause of their fear. Since 'puskalabhih' (complete hymns) is mentioned later, its absence is implied here (for those who are afraid), and with this intention it is said 'svajnananugunam' (according to their own knowledge). The words 'stutirupani' etc. supply the expected object for the verb 'grnanti'. To show that it refers merely to reciting scriptural praises, the word 'uccarayanti' (utter) is used. This also proves that 'kecit' refers to a specific group of gods, as the flight of other beings is described later. The distinct mention of 'maharshi' etc. and its implication is expressed by the word 'apara' (others). The hosts of great sages refer to Bhrigu and others; the hosts of perfected ones refer to Sanaka and other chief Siddhas. The cause of their elaborate praise, indicated by their status as great sages, is stated as 'paravaratattvayathatmyavidah' (knowers of the truth of higher and lower realities). Like 'jitam te' (victory to You) [Srimad Bhagavatam 3.13.34], it is either an auspicious benediction offered out of devotion, or the word 'svasti' uttered by a servant upon merely beholding the Master, followed by the praise which describes the excellence of His qualities. Therefore, the interpretation of 'svasti' by others as 'may there be welfare for cows and Brahmanas, or the world' is irrelevant to the context. Here, the 'completeness' (pauskalya) of the praise refers to declaring His established status as the Lord of all, and with this intent it is said 'bhagavadanurupabhih' (with hymns suitable to the Lord).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Furthermore, 'ami hi' etc. These hosts of gods (surasanghah) enter You, i.e., approach near You and take refuge in You. The word 'hi' indicates that it is indeed appropriate for the gods to take refuge in Purushottama (the Supreme Person). 'Kecit' (some) refers to others, namely the demons, who, being frightened, pray with folded hands (anjali), saying 'Protect us!' The hosts of great sages and perfected ones (maharshi-siddhasanghah), saying 'May there be welfare for us!', praise You with complete and elaborate hymns.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
He describes the distress (caused by the terrifying aspect of the Cosmic Form) itself by 'ami' etc. Because ('hi'), these 'asura-sanghah' (demonic hosts), i.e., Duryodhana and others who are parts of demons, driven by an unseen force (destiny), enter You for their death, just as moths enter fire. Some of them, being afraid, praise You with folded hands.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
All the hosts of demigods are surrendering before You and entering into You. Some of them, very much afraid, are offering prayers with folded hands. Hosts of great sages and perfected beings, crying “All peace!” are praying to You by singing the Vedic hymns.

 The demigods in all the planetary systems feared the terrific manifestation of the universal form and its glaring effulgence and so prayed for protection.
