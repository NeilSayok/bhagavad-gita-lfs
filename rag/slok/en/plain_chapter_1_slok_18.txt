Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O king! The King of Kashi, a supreme archer, the great warrior Shikhandi, Dhrishtadyumna, King Virata, the unconquered Satyaki, King Drupada, the five sons of Draupadi, and the long-armed son of Subhadra, Abhimanyu -- all of these, from every side, blew their own conches separately.

Explanation: 'Kashyashcha parameshvasah...shankhan dadhmuh prithakprithak' -- the great warrior Shikhandi was extremely valiant. In a former birth he was a woman (Amba, the daughter of the King of Kashi), and in this birth too was born to King Drupada as a daughter. Later this same Shikhandi obtained manhood from a Yaksha named Sthunakarna and became a man. Bhishma knew all this and still regarded Shikhandi as a woman; for this reason he would not shoot arrows at him. During the battle Arjuna put Shikhandi in front and shot arrows at Bhishma from behind him, bringing Bhishma down from his chariot.

Arjuna's son Abhimanyu was extremely valiant. During the battle he broke into the chakravyuha formation built by Drona and, through his valor, slew many warriors. In the end six great warriors of the Kaurava army surrounded him unfairly and attacked him with weapons; he died from a mace-blow to the head dealt by Duhshasana's son.

In describing the conch-blowing, Sanjaya, from the Kaurava army's warriors, named only Bhishma, whereas from the Pandava army's warriors, he named eighteen heroes -- Lord Krishna, Arjuna, Bhima and the rest. This suggests that Sanjaya's mind held no regard for the side of unrighteousness (the Kaurava army), and so he did not consider it fitting to describe that side at length. But because his mind held regard for the side of righteousness (the Pandava army), and reverence toward Lord Krishna and the Pandavas, he considered it fitting to describe their side at greater length, and found delight in doing so.

Transition: what effect the Pandava army's conch-blowing had upon the Kaurava army is told in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said  Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within.

Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory.

Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra:  Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
The names 'Panchajanya,' 'Devadatta,' 'Paundra,' 'Anantavijaya,' 'Sughosha,' and 'Manipushpaka' are given -- the point being that on the enemy's side, each conch is famous by its own name, whereas in your own army not a single conch is famous by a name of its own; this is stated to convey the enemy's superior distinction. The word 'Hrishikesha' conveys that, as the inner controller who impels all senses, He is the (true) helper of the Pandavas. The word 'Dhananjaya' conveys that, having conquered all kings in his conquest of the quarters and brought back wealth, Arjuna is in every way unconquerable. He whose deeds include the slaying of Hidimba and the like is called 'Bhimasena,' immensely strong from digesting vast quantities of food, hence 'Vrikodara.'

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Saubhadra is Abhimanyu, the mighty-armed. Some say the set of four qualifying epithets (from the previous verse) applies to each individually. By addressing him as 'lord of the earth,' it is hinted that, like the earth, Dhritarashtra is fit to bear sorrow. Others say: the address 'O Dhritarashtra, lord of the earth' conveys the sense 'listen calmly.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
To make vivid the exceptional character of these conches -- already stated to be divine -- their proper names Panchajanya and Devadatta are given. Similarly, the naming of all four conches belonging to Bhimasena and the others individually. The phrase 'blew separately, each his own' conveys that they blew in such a way that each single conch-sound alone would be enough to rend the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons; or alternatively, that they blew one after another in sequence, each to express his own particular delight. In the following verse's 'that uproar,' though it resounds through both sky and earth, the construal is that it specifically rent the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons alone -- for others it was, instead, a cause of joy. The phrase 'of all your sons' is used, together with the plural, to convey that not a single one among them possessed a steady mind. The verb 'vyadarayat' (rent asunder) is glossed word by word to bring out the intended sense -- 'bibheda' (split/pierced). As to how a mere sound, like a weapon, could rend hearts, this is explained by the phrase 'even now' -- the very thought, in those confident of their own army's victory, that this army might now be destroyed, is itself what constitutes the 'rending of hearts.' Since Dhritarashtra, eager to hear of his sons' victory, was asking about it, Sanjaya, in this manner already described, in effect told him something foreshadowing their defeat -- this is what is meant.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having thus named the chief warriors and their conches, he now names all the other great soldiers of that army, in 'Kashyashcha...' etc. Kashya is the King of Kashi, 'parameshvasah' (supreme, whose bow is supreme). Shikhandi is a great warrior, skilled in weapons and scriptures. By the word 'cha' (and), he too is supreme in archery. Dhrishtadyumna and the others are likewise all counted. Saubhadra (son of Subhadra), Abhimanyu, is long-armed, supremely capable in battle. 'Prithak prithak' -- standing each in a separate place, they blew their conches. The address 'O lord of the earth' to Dhritarashtra is meant to convey knowledge of the true nature of all of these.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The King of the earth, the King of Drupada; the sons of Draupadi, and the mighty armed son of Subhadra, each one of them also blew their respective conchshells.

 Sañjaya informed King Dhṛtarāṣṭra very tactfully that his unwise policy of deceiving the sons of Pāṇḍu and endeavoring to enthrone his own sons on the seat of the kingdom was not very laudable. The signs already clearly indicated that the whole Kuru dynasty would be killed in that great battle. Beginning with the grandsire, Bhīṣma, down to the grandsons like Abhimanyu and others – including kings from many states of the world – all were present there, and all were doomed. The whole catastrophe was due to King Dhṛtarāṣṭra, because he encouraged the policy followed by his sons.
