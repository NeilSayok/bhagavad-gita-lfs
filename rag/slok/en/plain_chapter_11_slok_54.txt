Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Regarding devotion, Acharya Shankara says that among all the means of liberation, devotion indeed is the supreme, and this devotion, through the investigation of one's own nature, becomes the Self. Identification with the beloved is the true measure of love. When a devotee, forgetting the existence of his individual ego, attains identification with his beloved Lord in love, the culmination of that love is called Para-bhakti (supreme devotion) or Ananya-bhakti (single-minded devotion). According to spiritual laws, a seeker of self-knowledge is compelled to abandon his lower nature along with the conditioning (upadhis). Only by abandoning identification with the non-self can the pure nature of the Self be realized. Only those seekers who can identify with the Truth that binds this world into a single thread can experience Me in this form, i.e., the Cosmic Form. The three successive stages in which Truth is realized are indicated by the Lord using these three words: knowing, seeing, and entering. First, a seeker needs intellectual knowledge of his goal and the means, which is indicated here by the word 'to know' (jnatum), and its means is listening (shravana). After obtaining some knowledge in this way, doubts arise in the mind; to resolve these doubts, systematic reflection (manana) on the acquired knowledge is essential. Upon resolution of doubts, one beholds the Truth (seeing). Thereafter, by the practice of deep meditation (nididhyasana), completely abandoning identification with false conditioning and becoming one with the nature of the Self is 'entering' (pravestum) into it. This experience of the Self is not of some object different from oneself, but of one's own true nature. The word 'entering' conveys the oneness of the seeker and the sought. The dream-sufferings of a dreamer end when he enters into the waking consciousness and becomes the waking man himself. The Lord Himself states the means to attain Him.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
'By what means then can the Lord be seen?' — thus (Arjuna) asks: 'How?' Pointing out that His vision is successfully attained through scriptural knowledge, he (the commentator) says: 'It is answered' (uchyate). He clarifies the meaning of the word 'tu' (but) to show that not mere devotion (in general) is the cause there, by saying 'of what kind (of devotion)' etc. He reveals the single-minded (ananya) devotion itself by saying 'by all (senses)' etc.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary: 'bhaktyā tvananyayā śakya ahamevaṃvidho.arjuna' — The word 'tu' is used here to indicate a means that is extraordinary compared to the means mentioned earlier. The Lord says, 'O Arjuna! In this four-armed form holding the conch, discus, mace, and lotus as you have seen Me, I cannot be seen through sacrifice, charity, austerity, etc., but can indeed be seen only through single-minded devotion.'
Single-minded devotion (ananya-bhakti) means having the refuge, support, hope, and faith in the Lord alone (Footnote p. 616). There should not be even the slightest dependence on any capability, strength, intellect, etc., other than the Lord. These must not have the slightest significance in the heart. This single-minded devotion is of the self itself; it is not through the mind, intellect, or senses. The meaning is that there should be an intense longing arising solely from one's own restlessness, and one cannot find peace for even a moment without seeing the Lord. Such inner restlessness of the self is the primary cause of attaining the Lord. In this restlessness and longing, the infinite sins of infinite lifetimes are burnt away. It is for those with such single-minded devotion that the Lord has said: 'For the devotee who constantly remembers Me with single-minded attention, I am easily attainable' (Gita 8.14) and 'For those single-minded devotees who worship Me by meditating on Me, I carry their yogakshema (gain and safety)' (Gita 9.22).
Another meaning of single-minded devotion is that whatever support one feels in doing bhajan-remembrance, practicing spiritual disciplines, or calling out with intense longing, even that support should not exist. Then why perform spiritual practice? It is only to destroy one's own pride, i.e., to eliminate the sense of one's own strength in performing spiritual practices. The point is that the attainment of the Lord does not happen by the strength of spiritual practice, but rather by the melting of the pride of spiritual practice. When the pride of practice melts, the pure grace of the Lord acts upon the seeker, meaning there remains no barrier to the arrival of that grace, and (by that grace) the Lord is attained.
'jñātuṃ draṣṭuṃ ca tattvena praveṣṭum' — By such single-minded devotion alone can I be known in reality, by single-minded devotion alone can I be seen, and by single-minded devotion alone can I be attained. Through knowledge (jnana) also, the Lord can be known in reality and attained (Gita 18.55), but the Lord is not bound to give His vision (darshana) to them.
The meaning of 'to know' (jñātum) is that I am known exactly as I am. Being known does not mean that I come under the control of his intellect; rather, his power of knowing is completely filled by Me. The meaning is that he knows Me in reality as 'Vasudeva is all' (Gita 7.19) and 'I am both existence and non-existence' (Gita 9.19).
The meaning of 'to see' (draṣṭuṃ) is that he can see Me in any qualified form (saguna form) he wishes, such as Vishnu, Rama, Krishna, etc.
The meaning of 'to enter' (praveṣṭum) is that he experiences his oneness with the Lord, or he enters into the eternal pastimes (nitya-leela) of the Lord. In entering the eternal pastimes, the devotee's desire and the Lord's will are primary. Although upon completely surrendering to the Lord, all desires of the devotee end, yet this is an extraordinary characteristic of the Lord that they fulfill whatever desire the devotee had of entering His pastimes. It is not that they fulfill only spiritual desires; rather, if the devotee had any worldly desire earlier, the Lord fulfills that as well. For example, according to his desire prior to seeing the Lord, Dhruva obtained a kingdom for thirty-six thousand years, and Vibhishana for a Kalpa. The meaning is that the Lord fulfills the desire of the devotee and then, according to His own will, leads him to real perfection, leaving nothing more to be done, known, or gained by the devotee.

Special Point:
The intense longing of devotion itself has such power that it creates a longing in the Lord as well to meet the devotee. No one has the power to obstruct this longing of the Lord. When the grace of the infinitely powerful Lord overflows toward the devotee, that grace removes all the devotee's obstacles, completely disregards the devotee's worthiness or unworthiness, and makes the Lord subordinate, so that the Lord immediately appears before the devotee.

Relationship: Now the Lord describes the means of single-minded devotion.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
To show that I, the Lord, am attainable by the devotion that arises when such a soul (jiva) is chosen by My own, the Lord says: 'By devotion' (bhaktya). Or, to the question, 'Why then are You not seen like this by all?', He replies: 'By devotion'. Even though I have descended for the restraint (nirodha) of all, My vision and knowledge occur to some only as a human being or as a partial incarnation etc., but not in reality (tattvena); because I can be known in reality, seen, and entered into the heart only by devotion. Of what kind of devotion? 'By single-minded' (ananyaya) — meaning, that which is independent of means like Vedic study and discourse, and is obtainable only through the association of those who have received My grace, which is to say, raganuga-bhakti (devotion of spontaneous attachment). Some say it is of the nature of Prameya-bhakti (grace-filled devotion) on the basis of the statement 'by devotion alone'. Because 'I am of this form' — attainable by devotion, 'like one who is dependent' (asvatantra iva). Or, I am indeed the performer of effortless deeds (aklishta-karma) endowed with the imperishable lordship of the Cosmic Form etc., the founder of the Pravaha, Maryada, and Pushti paths, possessed of extraordinary qualities, a highly enchanting body, and great compassion — even though I am the Supreme Person (Purushottama).

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
'If You cannot be seen through the Vedas, austerities, charity, sacrifices, etc., then by what means can You be seen?' To this, the Lord says: 'By devotion' (bhaktya). The word 'tu' (but) is used to exclude other means. Only by single-minded devotion, which is exclusive devotion to Me in the form of unsurpassed love, can I, who bear this divine form, be known through the scriptures, O Arjuna. (In 'śakya aham', the omission of the visarga is a Vedic usage, as before). Not only can I be known through the scriptures, but by single-minded devotion, I can also be seen in reality, i.e., realized directly in My true form, through the ripening of hearing (shravana), reflection (manana), and meditation (nididhyasana) on the Upanishadic statements. And thereafter, upon the cessation of ignorance and its effects through the direct realization of the true nature, I can be entered into in reality, i.e., attained in My very form. The address 'O Parantapa' (scorcher of foes) suggests the seeker's fitness for entering into Me due to his ability to subdue the enemy of ignorance.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
To the question, 'By what means then can He be seen?', the Lord replies here, 'By devotion' (bhaktya). But by single-minded devotion, which is exclusive devotion to Me, I, who am of this Cosmic Form, can be known in reality (paramarthato) through the scriptures, seen directly (pratyakshato), and entered into by attaining oneness (tadatmyena); and not by any other means.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
'If You cannot be seen by the aforementioned means, then by what means can You be seen?' To this, He says:
'By devotion' (bhaktya tu) — the word 'tu' (but/indeed) is used to exclude means other than devotion.
To the question, 'Is devotion alone the cause of Your vision?', He qualifies it:
'By single-minded' (ananyaya) — that devotion which is never directed to anything other than the Lord Vasudeva, characterized by supreme attachment to Ishvara, and which restrains the activities of all senses from going anywhere else other than Vasudeva.
'I, of this form' (ahamevamvidho) — I, the bearer of the Cosmic Form, can be known through scriptures by removing doubts/impossible notions (asambhavana), seen (realized directly) like you, and entered into in truth (tattvatah) — meaning, to attain liberation characterized by oneness, known as Jivanmukti (liberation while living) and Videhakaivalya (disembodied liberation), through direct realization of truth by hearing (shravana) etc.
Now, an objection: What then is the status of study of the Vedas etc. which are taught as means of knowledge by the Shruti text, 'Brahmins desire to know Him by the study of the Vedas, by sacrifices, by charity, and by austerity'?
Anticipating this, He answers with the two vocatives, 'O Arjuna!' and 'O Parantapa!' (O scorcher of foes), implying: When those Vedic study etc. are performed along with their limbs in this birth or in other births, they lead to purification of the mind. From that, single-minded devotion arises, equipped with shama (control of mind), dama (control of senses), etc. which scorches the inner enemies like the mind. From that arises the knowledge of My real nature. Thus, since they serve as means through the medium of devotion, there is no flaw (contradiction).

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
To show that there is no redundancy because this latter verse supports the former one, he introduces it by saying 'why?' etc. Since the Vedas in their literal form cannot be a direct means, their prohibition would be illogical. Therefore, explaining the intended meaning, he says, 'by Vedas, i.e., by teaching, preaching, study, hearing, and recitation.' Implying that the mention of charity and sacrifice also includes fire-offerings (homa), he says, 'by sacrifices, charity, fire-offerings, and austerities.' Since their status as means is only through devotion, as known from the Shruti text 'Brahmins desire to know Him by the study of the Vedas...' etc., he says 'bereft of devotion to Me' (destitute of devotion). The word 'evamvidha' (of this form) refers to being free from errors like treating Him as a mere human, and denotes His transcendental nature. Hence, he says, 'I as I truly exist.' Devotion is required not only for realization, but also for determining the scriptural meaning, as in 'having attained pure disposition through devotion, I know Janardana from scriptures', and even for the attainment that follows realization. With this in mind, both 'to know' (jnatum) and 'to enter' (pravestum), which were not present in the previous verse, are introduced here. 'In reality' (tattvatah) is applied to all three (knowing, seeing, entering) because it is universally required. 'Entering in reality' means complete attainment, i.e., experiencing Him in His entire true form. This excludes the attainment of only partial manifestations like Vyuha or Vibhava. The stages of attainment lower than direct liberation are remembered: 'Some reside in the worlds of Vishnu (salokya), others go near Him (samipya), others attain a form similar to His (sarupya), and others attain union (sayujya), which is said to be liberation.' In the commentary on 'This birth in this world is indeed very rare...' (6.42), Yadavaprakasha also says: 'This is the fruit for one who dies after attaining a minor yogic success...' All this is indicated here by the Lord by saying 'or of yogis' (6.42) etc. There is no fallacy of mutual dependence because devotion has different stages in being the cause of knowledge, vision, and attainment. A basic level of devotion, which is of the nature of minor favorability generated by the virtuous deeds of past lives and association with saintly people, and which conforms to proper hearing (shravana), helps in the rise of knowledge born of scriptures. But 'Para-bhakti' (higher devotion) filled with an intense desire to see Him is the cause of realization (direct vision). After realization, 'Parama-bhakti' (supreme devotion) characterized by intense absorption in complete experience becomes the cause of entering (attaining union). Here, the word 'ananyaya' should be understood as 'with no other purpose/goal', as explained earlier. Some say it means 'having no other deity'. The view of contemplating absolute identity (oneness) has already been refuted and is contrary to perception etc. Keeping all this in mind, it was summarized (in Gitartha-Sangraha 15): 'In the eleventh chapter, the vision of the direct realization of His own majesty is described; and it is stated that devotion is the sole means for knowing and attaining Him.' He removes the doubt that this is mere praise of devotion, by saying 'And so...' (referring to the Shruti). In passages like 'This Self cannot be obtained...', there is a prohibition of the mere (other means) without devotion, similar to 'the fire should not be piled on the earth'. It is established by the nature of the meaning and the strength of the supporting texts (upabrimhana) that the special quality which is the cause of being chosen (varaniyata-hetu), referred to by the word 'yat', is devotion itself. Here, if the word 'tanu' refers to the body, then His form etc. are implied, and if it refers to His nature, His body etc. are obtained by implication.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
'Then how are You able to be seen?' To this, He says: 'By devotion' (bhaktya). 'By devotion' means by adoration. 'By single-minded' (ananyaya) means unwavering, i.e., unbroken. I, of this form, can be known through the scriptures that clarify the meaning of the term 'tvam' (thou). I can be seen through meditation. Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
My dear Arjuna, only by undivided devotional service can I be understood as I am, standing before you, and can thus be seen directly. Only in this way can you enter into the mysteries of My understanding.

 Kṛṣṇa can be understood only by the process of undivided devotional service. He explicitly explains this in this verse so that unauthorized commentators, who try to understand Bhagavad-gītā by the speculative process, will know that they are simply wasting their time. No one can understand Kṛṣṇa or how He came from parents in a four-handed form and at once changed Himself into a two-handed form. These things are very difficult to understand by study of the Vedas or by philosophical speculation. Therefore it is clearly stated here that no one can see Him or enter into understanding of these matters. Those who, however, are very experienced students of Vedic literature can learn about Him from the Vedic literature in so many ways. There are so many rules and regulations, and if one at all wants to understand Kṛṣṇa, he must follow the regulative principles described in the authoritative literature. One can perform penance in accordance with those principles. For example, to undergo serious penances one may observe fasting on Janmāṣṭamī, the day on which Kṛṣṇa appeared, and on the two days of Ekādaśī (the eleventh day after the new moon and the eleventh day after the full moon). As far as charity is concerned, it is plain that charity should be given to the devotees of Kṛṣṇa who are engaged in His devotional service to spread the Kṛṣṇa philosophy, or Kṛṣṇa consciousness, throughout the world. Kṛṣṇa consciousness is a benediction to humanity. Lord Caitanya was appreciated by Rūpa Gosvāmī as the most munificent man of charity because love of Kṛṣṇa, which is very difficult to achieve, was distributed freely by Him. So if one gives some amount of his money to persons involved in distributing Kṛṣṇa consciousness, that charity, given to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness, is the greatest charity in the world. And if one worships as prescribed in the temple (in the temples in India there is always some statue, usually of Viṣṇu or Kṛṣṇa), that is a chance to progress by offering worship and respect to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. For the beginners in devotional service to the Lord, temple worship is essential, and this is confirmed in the Vedic literature ( Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 6.23): yasya deve parā bhaktir yathā deve tathā gurau tasyaite kathitā hy arthāḥ prakāśante mahātmanaḥ One who has unflinching devotion for the Supreme Lord and is directed by the spiritual master, in whom he has similar unflinching faith, can see the Supreme Personality of Godhead by revelation. One cannot understand Kṛṣṇa by mental speculation. For one who does not take personal training under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master, it is impossible to even begin to understand Kṛṣṇa. The word tu is specifically used here to indicate that no other process can be used, can be recommended, or can be successful in understanding Kṛṣṇa. The personal forms of Kṛṣṇa, the two-handed form and the four-handed, are described as su-durdarśam , very difficult to see. They are completely different from the temporary universal form shown to Arjuna. The four-handed form of Nārāyaṇa and the two-handed form of Kṛṣṇa are eternal and transcendental, whereas the universal form exhibited to Arjuna is temporary. The words tvad anyena na dṛṣṭa-pūrvam (verse 47) state that before Arjuna, no one had seen that universal form. Also, they suggest that amongst the devotees there was no necessity of showing it. That form was exhibited by Kṛṣṇa at the request of Arjuna so that in the future, when one represents himself as an incarnation of God, people can ask to see his universal form. The word na, used repeatedly in the previous verse, indicates that one should not be very much proud of such credentials as an academic education in Vedic literature. One must take to the devotional service of Kṛṣṇa. Only then can one attempt to write commentaries on Bhagavad-gītā . Kṛṣṇa changes from the universal form to the four-handed form of Nārāyaṇa and then to His own natural form of two hands. This indicates that the four-handed forms and other forms mentioned in Vedic literature are all emanations of the original two-handed Kṛṣṇa. He is the origin of all emanations. Kṛṣṇa is distinct even from these forms, what to speak of the impersonal conception. As far as the four-handed forms of Kṛṣṇa are concerned, it is stated clearly that even the most identical four-handed form of Kṛṣṇa (which is known as Mahā-viṣṇu, who is lying on the cosmic ocean and from whose breathing so many innumerable universes are passing out and entering) is also an expansion of the Supreme Lord. As stated in the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.48), yasyaika-niśvasita-kālam athāvalambya jīvanti loma-vila-jā jagad-aṇḍa-nāthāḥ viṣṇur mahān sa iha yasya kalā-viśeṣo govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi “The Mahā-viṣṇu, into whom all the innumerable universes enter and from whom they come forth again simply by His breathing process, is a plenary expansion of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore I worship Govinda, Kṛṣṇa, the cause of all causes.” Therefore one should conclusively worship the personal form of Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead who has eternal bliss and knowledge. He is the source of all forms of Viṣṇu, He is the source of all forms of incarnation, and He is the original Supreme Personality, as confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā . In the Vedic literature ( Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad 1.1) the following statement appears: sac-cid-ānanda-rūpāya kṛṣṇāyākliṣṭa-kāriṇe namo vedānta-vedyāya gurave buddhi-sākṣiṇe “I offer my respectful obeisances unto Kṛṣṇa, who has a transcendental form of bliss, eternity and knowledge. I offer my respect to Him, because understanding Him means understanding the Vedas, and He is therefore the supreme spiritual master.” Then it is said, kṛṣṇo vai paramaṁ daivatam: “Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead.” ( Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad 1.3) Eko vaśī sarva-gaḥ kṛṣṇa īḍyaḥ: “That one Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and He is worshipable.” Eko ’pi san bahudhā yo ’vabhāti: “Kṛṣṇa is one, but He is manifested in unlimited forms and expanded incarnations.” ( Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad 1.21) The Brahma-saṁhitā (5.1) says, īśvaraḥ paramaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaḥ anādir ādir govindaḥ sarva-kāraṇa-kāraṇam “The Supreme Personality of Godhead is Kṛṣṇa, who has a body of eternity, knowledge and bliss. He has no beginning, for He is the beginning of everything. He is the cause of all causes.” Elsewhere it is said, yatrāvatīrṇaṁ kṛṣṇākhyaṁ paraṁ brahma narākṛti: “The Supreme Absolute Truth is a person, His name is Kṛṣṇa, and He sometimes descends on this earth.” Similarly, in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam we find a description of all kinds of incarnations of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and in this list the name of Kṛṣṇa also appears. But then it is said that this Kṛṣṇa is not an incarnation of God but is the original Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself ( ete cāṁśa-kalāḥ puṁsaḥ kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam ). Similarly, in Bhagavad-gītā the Lord says, mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat: “There is nothing superior to My form as the Personality of Godhead Kṛṣṇa.” He also says elsewhere in Bhagavad-gītā , aham ādir hi devānām: “I am the origin of all the demigods.” And after understanding Bhagavad-gītā from Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna also confirms this in the following words: paraṁ brahma paraṁ dhāma pavitraṁ paramaṁ bhavān, “I now fully understand that You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the Absolute Truth, and that You are the refuge of everything.” Therefore the universal form which Kṛṣṇa showed to Arjuna is not the original form of God. The original is the Kṛṣṇa form. The universal form, with its thousands and thousands of heads and hands, is manifest just to draw the attention of those who have no love for God. It is not God’s original form. The universal form is not attractive for pure devotees, who are in love with the Lord in different transcendental relationships. The Supreme Godhead exchanges transcendental love in His original form of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore to Arjuna, who was so intimately related with Kṛṣṇa in friendship, this form of the universal manifestation was not pleasing; rather, it was fearful. Arjuna, who was a constant companion of Kṛṣṇa’s, must have had transcendental eyes; he was not an ordinary man. Therefore he was not captivated by the universal form. This form may seem wonderful to persons who are involved in elevating themselves by fruitive activities, but to persons who are engaged in devotional service the two-handed form of Kṛṣṇa is the most dear.
