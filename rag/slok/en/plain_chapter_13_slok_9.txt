Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Indifference to sense-objects: This does not mean escaping from the world. Remaining amidst objects, not thinking of them in the mind and not being attached to them, is the meaning of dispassion (vairagya). A person who runs away from sense-objects and sits in forests thinking about them is merely suppressing his desires; the Lord calls such a man a hypocrite.

Absence of Egoism: The emergence of individual ego (jivabhava) occurs only when we identify with the body and other adjuncts (upadhis) and their experiences. The essential prerequisite for abiding in one's pure Self-nature is that we destroy this false identification through discrimination. This process is like clearing weeds before plowing the land.

Perception of evil in suffering (Duhkha-dosa-anudarsanam): Dissatisfaction with the current condition alone can inspire us to attain a new, superior, and happier state. As long as people in a nation or society lack awareness that their present condition is extremely degraded and painful, they forget their sorrows and adapt themselves to living in that condition. That is why every political leader or social worker first makes people aware of their fallen and impoverished condition. Once awareness dawns, they enthusiastically prepare to strive for a higher joy and prosperous life.

The same method applies to cultural and spiritual domains. As long as a seeker is not fully aware of the bondages of his inner personality, he remains in the pit of self-created sorrow and never makes an effort to come out of it. The human body and mind have a remarkable capacity to adapt to circumstances. They accept even the most deplorable state and even begin to experience happiness in it.

Therefore, the seeker is advised here to reflectively perceive the defects of his current condition. Once he fully understands his bound state, all necessary qualities—spiritual curiosity, intellectual capacity, mental enthusiasm, and physical courage—will arise in him, enabling him to easily achieve spiritual perfection.

Perception of evil in birth, death, old age, and disease: Every body undergoes these modifications. Each of these modifications is a source of fresh sorrows. Becoming aware of the sorrows arising from all these modifications, the seeker becomes eager to attain liberation from them. This spirit of rebellion against sorrow is the motivating force that swiftly propels seekers to the summit of perfection.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Dispassion towards objects of the senses, absence of egoism, and repeated perception of evil in the form of suffering in birth, death, old age, and diseases.

Commentary—Indriyarthesu vairagyam—Absence of attraction of the senses towards all objects like sound etc. of this world and the next world is dispassion towards sense-objects. Even when the senses come into contact with objects and even while using objects through the senses according to scriptures for sustaining life, the seeker should not have passion, attachment, or fondness for objects.

Means: (1) Only due to passion for objects, their importance is seen, attraction to the world arises, and from this all sins occur. If our passion remains in objects, how will realization of Truth occur and how will our state in the Supreme Truth be attained? If passion is abandoned, abidance in the Supreme Truth will take place—reflecting thus brings dispassion towards objects.

(2) Very rich people, warriors, kings and emperors existed and enjoyed many delights, but what remained of them in the end? Nothing remained. Their bodies became weak and in the end all passed away. Reflecting in this manner also produces dispassion.

(3) Compared to those who have not enjoyed delights and lack materials of enjoyment, what specialty or distinction came to those who enjoyed many pleasures and are enjoying them? Nothing; on the contrary, those who enjoy pleasures are drowned in grief and anxiety. Reflecting thus also brings dispassion.

Anahankara eva cha—In every person's experience there is a vritti (thought pattern) 'I am'. This vritti, combining with the body, creates a sense of localized identity 'I am the body', which is egoism. Because of this, considering one's relationship with body, name, action, object, feeling, knowledge, renunciation, place, time etc., the jiva continues to take birth and die in high and low species (Gita 13.21). This egoism often remains far along in spiritual practice. In reality it has no existence, yet because of self-assumption it continues to be perceived as personality. The Lord's use of this term among the means of Knowledge signifies the complete removal of assumed egoism in body etc., because upon true realization of matter and spirit, it completely disappears. Every human being can become free from egoism; therefore the Lord speaks of abandoning egoism with the word 'anahankarah'.

When pride (abhimana) and egoism (ahankara) are used together, they denote different feelings. Pride arises from connection with worldly things. Similarly, seeing distinction in oneself regarding renunciation, dispassion, learning etc. produces pride. Considering the body itself as one's true nature produces egoism. Here, the word 'anahankarah' should be taken to mean the complete absence of both pride and egoism.

Upon awakening from sleep, a person first gets knowledge of the vritti 'Aham' i.e. 'I am'. Then arises the pride 'I belong to such body, name, caste, varna, ashrama etc.' This is a sequence. Similarly, in the spiritual path there is a sequence to the destruction of egoism. First, pride in wealth and objects connected with the physical body is destroyed. Then the pride of agency (kartritva-abhimana) connected with the organs of action is destroyed. After that, the egoism of being a knower (jnatritva) residing predominantly in the intellect is destroyed. Finally, the egoism of witnesshood (sakshitva) residing in the Aham-vritti is also destroyed. Then the all-pervading Sachchidanandaghana nature naturally remains everywhere.

Means: (1) Pride arises from the feeling of superiority in oneself. Pride occurs only when a person looks at others and thinks they are inferior compared to him. For instance, if there is only one millionaire in a village, seeing others he feels proud of being a millionaire. But if all others are billionaires, he does not feel pride in being a millionaire. Therefore, to eliminate the defect of pride, a seeker should never look at others' flaws, but rather look at his own flaws and remove them.

(2) Just as the one Self pervades this body, so it pervades other bodies—Sarvagatah (Gita 2.24). But through ignorance, man considers the all-pervading Self limited to his own body and assumes the body to be 'I'. Just as a man claims ownership over only that portion of money deposited by him in a bank and considers himself rich, similarly in one body he forms egoism 'I am the body', and claiming connection with time thinks 'I am in this time', claiming connection with space thinks 'I am here', with intellect thinks 'I am intelligent', with speech thinks 'I am a speaker', and so on. Not accepting such connections is the means to become free from egoism.

(3) Scriptures describe the Supreme Being as Sachchidanandaghana. Sat (Existence), Chit (Consciousness), and Ananda (Bliss)—these three are not different forms of the Supreme Being, but three names of the one Supreme Reality. Therefore a seeker, targeting the Supreme Being by any one of these attributes, can become non-conceptual (nirvikalpa). By becoming non-conceptual he experiences his self-established state in the Supreme Reality, and egoism is completely destroyed. This should be understood as follows:

(a) Sat—The Supreme Reality was always, is always, and will always be. It never changes, increases or decreases, but always remains as It is—reflecting thus through intellect and becoming steady and non-conceptual, the seeker's connection with intellect is severed, and he experiences his real state in that Sat-reality. Upon such experience, egoism no longer remains.

(b) Chit—Just as everyone's body etc. are objects of perception within 'Aham' (I), similarly 'Aham' itself (in the forms of I, you, this, and that) is an object of perception within Consciousness/Knowledge. By becoming steady and non-conceptual in that Consciousness, one experiences one's self-established state in the Supreme Reality. Then egoism no longer remains.

(c) Ananda—Seekers often attempt to know through the intellect even that Consciousness which illuminates intellect and egoism. In reality, calling subjects known or learned through intellect as 'knowledge' and considering oneself wise thereby is a mistake. How can the Reality illuminating the intellect be known through the intellect? Although the seeker has no instrument other than intellect by which Reality can be known, yet through intellect only the reality of the inert world can be known. That Reality which illuminates intellect cannot be known by intellect. To know that Reality, severance of connection even with intellect is necessary. Being established non-conceptually in the Supreme Reality illuminating intellect, connection with intellect is completely severed. Then only the Bliss-nature (where there is not even a trace of sorrow) Supreme Reality remains, which itself is Knowledge-nature and Existence-nature. Thus, becoming silent/non-conceptual in Truth, there is pure Bliss—such is the experience. Upon such experience, egoism no longer remains.

Janmamrtyujaravyadhiduhkhadosanudarsanam—Repeatedly seeing the painful evils of birth, death, old age, and diseases means: just as a pot bakes in a kiln, so before birth a child bakes in the digestive fire in the mother's womb. Due to salty, spicy, sharp, hot, and pungent food eaten by the mother, there is burning in the child's body. Microorganisms living in the womb also bite the child. The pain suffered by the mother during delivery cannot be measured. Similar pain is suffered by the child coming out of the womb. Thus repeatedly reflecting on the painful evils of birth, one should firm up the conviction that there is only suffering in it. Whoever is born must die—this is the rule. No one can escape it. At the time of death when life leaves the body, the pain is as if thousands of scorpions sting at once. When separated from wealth earned lifelong, house lived in lifelong, and family, with no possibility of meeting again, severe sorrow occurs due to attachment. Seeing the key to the safe (where wealth was hidden from family) handed over to family members causes unbearable agony. Thus one should repeatedly see the painful evils of death.

In old age the strength of body and organs decays, causing pain in moving, standing, and sitting. Food of all kinds is not digested. Being senior, one desires respect from family, but serving no purpose, family members disrespect and insult him. Then past memories return: 'I earned wealth, nurtured them, but today they insult me'—causing great sorrow. Thus one should repeatedly see the painful evils of old age.

This body is the abode of diseases—shareeram vyadhimandiram. Various diseases born of vata, kapha etc. occur in the body, causing great pain. Thus one should repeatedly see the painful evils of diseases.

Here 'seeing repeatedly' does not mean repeated worrying, but reflective examination. By repeatedly seeing i.e. reflecting on the sorrows of birth, death, old age, and diseases, attraction towards perishable objects naturally decreases, i.e. dispassion towards enjoyments occurs. The point is that seeing the painful evils of birth, death etc. is a cause for dispassion towards enjoyments, because birth occurs through attachment to enjoyments i.e. association with qualities (Gita 13.21), and birth is the cause of all sorrows. The Lord has called rebirth a house of sorrow—punarjanma duhkhalayam asashvatam (Gita 8.15).

All defects arise by assuming connection with inert objects like body, giving them importance, and taking refuge in them. Because the embodied soul is a part of the Supreme Being, it is inherently faultless. That is why sorrow and defects do not appeal to the soul, for they are not of its nature. The jiva suffers continuously due to self-created defects. Therefore the Lord is telling us to reflectively remove body-identification, the root cause of the painful evils of birth, death etc.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Vairagyam, non-attachment, the attitude of dispassion; indriya-arthesu, with regard to objects of the senses, viz sound etc., with regard to seen or unseen objects of enjoyment; eva ca, and also; anahankarah, absence of egotism, absence of pride; janma-mrtyu-jara-vyadhi-duhkha-dosa-anudarsanam, seeing the evil in birth, death, old age, diseases and miseries-seeing the evil in each one of them from 'birth' to 'miseries'. The evil in birth consists in lying in the womb and coming out of it; seeing, i.e. thinking, of it. Similarly, thinking of the evil in death; so also, seeing in old age the evil in the form of deprivation of intelligence, strength and vigour, and becoming an object of contempt. In the same way, thinking of the evil in diseases like headtache etc.; so also with regard to miseries arising from causes physical, natural and supernatural.
Or, duhkha-dosa may mean the miseries themselves which are evil. Seeing, as before, that (evil in the form of miseries) in birth etc.-birth is miserable, death is miserable, old age is miserable, diseases are miserable. Birth etc. are miserable because they cause misery; not that they are miseries in themselves. [Birth etc. are perceivable events, and as such are not miseries in themselves.] Thus, when one thinks of the evil in the form of miseries in birth etc. dispassion arises with regard to the pleasures in the body, organs and objects. From that follows the tendency of the organs towards the indwelling Self for the realization of the Self. The seeing of the evil in the form of misery in birth etc. is called Knowledge because it thus becomes a cuase of the rise of Knowledge.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Further—'Indriyarthesu' etc. Dispassion (Vairagya) is that mental modification characterized by desirelessness, which opposes attachment to sense-objects like sound etc., whether experienced (seen) or scriptural (unseen/heard). Even in the absence of self-praise, the pride arising in the mind that 'I am superior to all' is egoism (ahankara); its absence is non-egoism (anahankara). The word 'eva' (only) is used to exclude non-conjunction, and 'cha' (and) is used for aggregation. And this assists in Self-realization by becoming the cause of detachment from sense-objects.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Further, absence of attachment to sense-objects (sound etc.) of this world and the next is dispassion (Vairagya). The pride arising in the mind 'I am supreme' is egoism (Ahankara); its absence is non-egoism (Anahankara). Since pride destroys all mentioned and unmentioned merits, the word 'eva' (only) is used in the sense of restriction to highlight the absolute necessity of cultivating non-egoism. The word 'cha' is for aggregation. Thus, some interpret that the combined practice of these twenty attributes starting from humility is called Knowledge, not lacking even one. However, Acharyas do not everywhere show the sense of indeclinables due to simplicity. 'Janmamrtyujaravyadhiduhkhadosanudarsanam' means perceiving evil in each item from birth to sorrow. Or sorrow itself is evil; perception or reflection upon that evil of sorrow in birth etc. Birth etc. are sorrows because they cause sorrow, not by their own nature. Thus, repeatedly perceiving evil in the form of sorrow in birth etc., dispassion arises towards body, organs, and sense-enjoyments. From that, the organs incline towards the indwelling Self for Self-realization; thus being a cause of Knowledge, perceiving evil in birth etc. is called Knowledge. As stated in Vishnu Purana—O Maitreya! The wise man, knowing the threefold afflictions (internal, external, divine), equipped with knowledge and dispassion, attains absolute liberation. Internal affliction is of two kinds—physical and mental. Physical affliction is of many types, listen: headache, cold, fever, colic, fistula, tumor, piles, asthma, swelling, vomiting, eye-diseases, dysentery, leprosy, etc.; now listen to mental afflictions. Through such divisions internal affliction is known. Affliction caused by beasts, birds, men, spirits, serpents, demons etc. is external (adhibhautika). Affliction born of cold, heat, wind, rain, water, lightning etc. is divine (adhidaivika). O best of sages! Sorrow born of womb, birth, old age, ignorance, death, and hell is divided into thousands of types. The delicate soul in the womb, surrounded by immense filth, wrapped in amnion, with bent spine and neck bones, burning intensely from the mother's extremely sour, pungent, sharp, hot, and salty food, suffers severe pain. Unable to stretch or contract limbs, lying in a mire of feces and urine, choked of breath, conscious and remembering hundreds of past births, stays in the womb in extreme sorrow due to past karmas. Being born, with face smeared with feces, blood, urine, and semen, joints pressed by birth-winds, turned upside down by strong delivery winds, emerges with severe pain as if sawed. Falls upon the earth like a worm from a putrid sore. A man with body shattered by old age, loose limbs, falling teeth, covered with wrinkles and veins, dimmed eyes staring at the sky, hair protruding from nostrils, trembling body, exposed bones, bent spine, diminished digestive fire eating little, struggling to walk, stand, sit, or lie down, dull ears and eyes, face smeared with drool, facing death with agitated organs, pained by separation from objects experienced even recently, exhausted by speaking a single sentence, suffering from cough, asthma, and sleeplessness, raised and laid down by others, subjected to humiliation by servants, sons, and wife, remembering youth and sighing heavily in distress—experiencing such sorrows in old age, listen to the sorrows suffered at death as well: with swollen neck and limbs, severely trembling, repeatedly overcome by faintness, for a moment regaining consciousness, anxious with possessiveness 'what will happen to my gold, grain, and vessels after me', torn by fatal diseases like saws, bones severed by death-god's arrows, rolling eyes, thrashing hands and feet, drying palate, lips and throat making rattling sounds, throat choked by phlegm, afflicted by udana breath, filled with intense heat, thirst, and hunger, tormented by messengers of Yama, dies with great pain and then attains a body of torment. These and other fierce sorrows occur to men at death. These descriptions serve as illustrations for perceiving evil and sorrow in birth etc. Defect also includes the repulsive nature of the body filled with vata, pitta, kapha, feces, urine etc., and helplessness.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Dispassion towards external objects is indeed based on devotion to the Self; with this intention it is said—'towards objects other than the Self' etc. The state of dispassion is Vairagya; its nature is explained as revulsion attained by contemplating defects. Their defectiveness is due to being painfully acquired, mixed with pain, and perishable. Since pride in the form of egoism was forbidden earlier, here the word 'egoism' refers to removing the delusion of identifying the body with the self—hence 'in the non-self' etc. The prohibition of egoism is also to indicate the prohibition of possessiveness (mamakara), which is rooted in it and always mentioned alongside it—hence 'to demonstrate' etc. The perception of defects like birth arising from embodied existence serves to show the despicable nature of the body, as stated in Chandogya Upanishad (8.12.1), 'Indeed, there is no freedom from pleasure and pain for one who is embodied'—hence 'in being embodied' etc. Sorrow generated by birth, death, old age, and sickness is 'janma-mrtyu-jara-vyadhi-duhkha', and that itself is evil—this is the compound's meaning. Or all items from birth up to sorrow are evils because they cause sorrow and are themselves of the nature of sorrow. To show that these evils cannot be avoided while retaining the body (like husks and rice), their inevitability is stated. 'Anudarsanam' means repeatedly perceiving, hence he says 'contemplation' etc.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Detachment from the objects of the senses, absence of false ego, the perception of the evil of birth, death, old age, and disease;

 This process of knowledge is sometimes misunderstood by less intelligent men as being the interaction of the field of activity. But actually this is the real process of knowledge. If one accepts this process, then the possibility of approaching the Absolute Truth exists. This is not the interaction of the twenty-four elements, as described before. This is actually the means to get out of the entanglement of those elements. The embodied soul is entrapped by the body, which is a casing made of the twenty-four elements, and the process of knowledge as described here is the means to get out of it. Of all the descriptions of the process of knowledge, the most important point is described in the first line of the eleventh verse. Mayi cānanya-yogena bhaktir avyabhicāriṇī: the process of knowledge terminates in unalloyed devotional service to the Lord. So if one does not approach, or is not able to approach, the transcendental service of the Lord, then the other nineteen items are of no particular value. But if one takes to devotional service in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the other nineteen items automatically develop within him. As stated in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (5.18.12) , yasyāsti bhaktir bhagavaty akiñcanā sarvair guṇais tatra samāsate surāḥ. All the good qualities of knowledge develop in one who has attained the stage of devotional service. The principle of accepting a spiritual master, as mentioned in the eighth verse, is essential. Even for one who takes to devotional service, it is most important. Transcendental life begins when one accepts a bona fide spiritual master. The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, clearly states here that this process of knowledge is the actual path. Anything speculated beyond this is nonsense. As for the knowledge outlined here, the items may be analyzed as follows. Humility means that one should not be anxious to have the satisfaction of being honored by others. The material conception of life makes us very eager to receive honor from others, but from the point of view of a man in perfect knowledge – who knows that he is not this body – anything, honor or dishonor, pertaining to this body is useless. One should not be hankering after this material deception. People are very anxious to be famous for their religion, and consequently sometimes it is found that without understanding the principles of religion one enters into some group which is not actually following religious principles and then wants to advertise himself as a religious mentor. As for actual advancement in spiritual science, one should have a test to see how far he is progressing. He can judge by these items. Nonviolence is generally taken to mean not killing or destroying the body, but actually nonviolence means not to put others into distress. People in general are trapped by ignorance in the material concept of life, and they perpetually suffer material pains. So unless one elevates people to spiritual knowledge, one is practicing violence. One should try his best to distribute real knowledge to the people, so that they may become enlightened and leave this material entanglement. That is nonviolence. Tolerance means that one should be practiced to bear insult and dishonor from others. If one is engaged in the advancement of spiritual knowledge, there will be so many insults and much dishonor from others. This is expected because material nature is so constituted. Even a boy like Prahlāda, who, only five years old, was engaged in the cultivation of spiritual knowledge, was endangered when his father became antagonistic to his devotion. The father tried to kill him in so many ways, but Prahlāda tolerated him. So there may be many impediments to making advancement in spiritual knowledge, but we should be tolerant and continue our progress with determination. Simplicity means that without diplomacy one should be so straightforward that he can disclose the real truth even to an enemy. As for acceptance of the spiritual master, that is essential, because without the instruction of a bona fide spiritual master one cannot progress in the spiritual science. One should approach the spiritual master with all humility and offer him all services so that he will be pleased to bestow his blessings upon the disciple. Because a bona fide spiritual master is a representative of Kṛṣṇa, if he bestows any blessings upon his disciple, that will make the disciple immediately advanced without the disciple’s following the regulative principles. Or, the regulative principles will be easier for one who has served the spiritual master without reservation. Cleanliness is essential for making advancement in spiritual life. There are two kinds of cleanliness: external and internal. External cleanliness means taking a bath, but for internal cleanliness one has to think of Kṛṣṇa always and chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. This process cleans the accumulated dust of past karma from the mind. Steadiness means that one should be very determined to make progress in spiritual life. Without such determination, one cannot make tangible progress. And self-control means that one should not accept anything which is detrimental to the path of spiritual progress. One should become accustomed to this and reject anything which is against the path of spiritual progress. This is real renunciation. The senses are so strong that they are always anxious to have sense gratification. One should not cater to these demands, which are not necessary. The senses should only be gratified to keep the body fit so that one can discharge his duty in advancing in spiritual life. The most important and uncontrollable sense is the tongue. If one can control the tongue, then there is every possibility of controlling the other senses. The function of the tongue is to taste and to vibrate. Therefore, by systematic regulation, the tongue should always be engaged in tasting the remnants of foodstuffs offered to Kṛṣṇa and chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. As far as the eyes are concerned, they should not be allowed to see anything but the beautiful form of Kṛṣṇa. That will control the eyes. Similarly, the ears should be engaged in hearing about Kṛṣṇa and the nose in smelling the flowers offered to Kṛṣṇa. This is the process of devotional service, and it is understood here that Bhagavad-gītā is simply expounding the science of devotional service. Devotional service is the main and sole objective. Unintelligent commentators on the Bhagavad-gītā try to divert the mind of the reader to other subjects, but there is no other subject in Bhagavad-gītā than devotional service. False ego means accepting this body as oneself. When one understands that he is not his body and is spirit soul, he comes to his real ego. Ego is there. False ego is condemned, but not real ego. In the Vedic literature ( Bṛhad-āraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10) it is said, ahaṁ brahmāsmi: I am Brahman, I am spirit. This “I am,” the sense of self, also exists in the liberated stage of self-realization. This sense of “I am” is ego, but when the sense of “I am” is applied to this false body it is false ego. When the sense of self is applied to reality, that is real ego. There are some philosophers who say we should give up our ego, but we cannot give up our ego, because ego means identity. We ought, of course, to give up the false identification with the body. One should try to understand the distress of accepting birth, death, old age and disease. There are descriptions in various Vedic literatures of birth. In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam the world of the unborn, the child’s stay in the womb of the mother, its suffering, etc., are all very graphically described. It should be thoroughly understood that birth is distressful. Because we forget how much distress we have suffered within the womb of the mother, we do not make any solution to the repetition of birth and death. Similarly at the time of death there are all kinds of sufferings, and they are also mentioned in the authoritative scriptures. These should be discussed. And as far as disease and old age are concerned, everyone gets practical experience. No one wants to be diseased, and no one wants to become old, but there is no avoiding these. Unless we have a pessimistic view of this material life, considering the distresses of birth, death, old age and disease, there is no impetus for our making advancement in spiritual life. As for detachment from children, wife and home, it is not meant that one should have no feeling for these. They are natural objects of affection. But when they are not favorable to spiritual progress, then one should not be attached to them. The best process for making the home pleasant is Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If one is in full Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he can make his home very happy, because this process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is very easy. One need only chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare, accept the remnants of foodstuffs offered to Kṛṣṇa, have some discussion on books like Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam , and engage oneself in Deity worship. These four things will make one happy. One should train the members of his family in this way. The family members can sit down morning and evening and chant together Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. If one can mold his family life in this way to develop Kṛṣṇa consciousness, following these four principles, then there is no need to change from family life to renounced life. But if it is not congenial, not favorable for spiritual advancement, then family life should be abandoned. One must sacrifice everything to realize or serve Kṛṣṇa, just as Arjuna did. Arjuna did not want to kill his family members, but when he understood that these family members were impediments to his Kṛṣṇa realization, he accepted the instruction of Kṛṣṇa and fought and killed them. In all cases, one should be detached from the happiness and distress of family life, because in this world one can never be fully happy or fully miserable. Happiness and distress are concomitant factors of material life. One should learn to tolerate, as advised in Bhagavad-gītā . One can never restrict the coming and going of happiness and distress, so one should be detached from the materialistic way of life and be automatically equipoised in both cases. Generally, when we get something desirable we are very happy, and when we get something undesirable we are distressed. But if we are actually in the spiritual position these things will not agitate us. To reach that stage, we have to practice unbreakable devotional service. Devotional service to Kṛṣṇa without deviation means engaging oneself in the nine processes of devotional service – chanting, hearing, worshiping, offering respect, etc. – as described in the last verse of the Ninth Chapter. That process should be followed. Naturally, when one is adapted to the spiritual way of life, he will not want to mix with materialistic men. That would go against his grain. One may test himself by seeing how far he is inclined to live in a solitary place, without unwanted association. Naturally a devotee has no taste for unnecessary sporting or cinema-going or enjoying some social function, because he understands that these are simply a waste of time. There are many research scholars and philosophers who study sex life or some other subject, but according to Bhagavad-gītā such research work and philosophical speculation have no value. That is more or less nonsensical. According to Bhagavad-gītā , one should make research, by philosophical discretion, into the nature of the soul. One should make research to understand the self. That is recommended here. As far as self-realization is concerned, it is clearly stated here that bhakti-yoga is especially practical. As soon as there is a question of devotion, one must consider the relationship between the Supersoul and the individual soul. The individual soul and the Supersoul cannot be one, at least not in the bhakti conception, the devotional conception of life. This service of the individual soul to the Supreme Soul is eternal, nityam, as it is clearly stated. So bhakti, or devotional service, is eternal. One should be established in that philosophical conviction. In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.2.11) this is explained. Vadanti tat tattva-vidas tattvaṁ yaj jñānam advayam. “Those who are actually knowers of the Absolute Truth know that the Self is realized in three different phases, as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān.” Bhagavān is the last word in the realization of the Absolute Truth; therefore one should reach up to that platform of understanding the Supreme Personality of Godhead and thus engage in the devotional service of the Lord. That is the perfection of knowledge. Beginning from practicing humility up to the point of realization of the Supreme Truth, the Absolute Personality of Godhead, this process is just like a staircase beginning from the ground floor and going up to the top floor. Now on this staircase there are so many people who have reached the first floor, the second or the third floor, etc., but unless one reaches the top floor, which is the understanding of Kṛṣṇa, he is at a lower stage of knowledge. If anyone wants to compete with God and at the same time make advancement in spiritual knowledge, he will be frustrated. It is clearly stated that without humility, understanding is not truly possible. To think oneself God is most puffed up. Although the living entity is always being kicked by the stringent laws of material nature, he still thinks, “I am God” because of ignorance. The beginning of knowledge, therefore, is amānitva, humility. One should be humble and know that he is subordinate to the Supreme Lord. Due to rebellion against the Supreme Lord, one becomes subordinate to material nature. One must know and be convinced of this truth.
