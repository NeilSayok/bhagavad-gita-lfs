Life Application:
Arjuna asks: What is the state of those who perform sacrifice with faith but setting aside scriptural rules? Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas?

Arjuna said: O Krishna, those who set aside the ordinances of the scriptures but perform worship with faith, what is their condition (or standing)? Is it Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas?

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Arjuna asks this question continuing from the end of the previous chapter where Krishna emphasized the authority of the scriptures. Arjuna wants a detailed explanation of how to lead an effective and beneficial spiritual life and to clear up any misconceptions. In practical life, due to ignorance or lack of intellectual capacity, many cannot read or follow scriptural injunctions. Yet, they may have deep faith and devotion to a higher life. Hence, Arjuna's question is relevant. Here, 'Yajna' does not just mean Vedic fire rituals, but includes all selfless actions done for the spiritual and social welfare. Arjuna's query is whether a person who acts with faith and dedication, even without knowing the metaphysical truth, can attain peace, and where such faith belongs—Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that this chapter details the division of Gunas (qualities). Arjuna asks about those who act without knowing the scriptural injunctions, out of ignorance, but with faith. This is not about those who knowingly reject the scriptures out of disbelief, for such people are purely Tamasic. Arjuna asks: if they possess faith but lack scriptural knowledge, does their standing split into Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas? For if they were Sattvic, they would not be completely devoid of scriptural support, as true Dharma is never against the Vedas. The Lord explains the three kinds of faith to clarify this.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that after describing the state of believers and non-believers who have scriptural knowledge, Arjuna now asks about those who lack scriptural knowledge but perform worship with faith. Since they do not know the scriptural injunctions, they act based on the conduct of their elders. Do their actions lead to Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas? Anandgiri notes that this query seeks to understand the root and outcome of such faith-guided actions.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Arjuna said: O Krishna, those who set aside the ordinances of the scriptures but perform worship with faith, what is their standing? Is it Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas?

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains: The dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna is for the welfare of all beings, keeping the people of Kali Yuga in mind. In Kali Yuga, scriptural knowledge will be scarce, and finding true saints will be difficult because of hypocrisy and deceit. Thus, people who have good intentions and faith, but do not know the scriptural injunctions, act out of their inherent faith. What is their status? Krishna means 'one who attracts'—so Arjuna addresses Him as Krishna, asking where He will pull these individuals. Are they on the path of Sattva (divine nature) or Rajas/Tamas (demonic nature)? The Lord explains the division of faith in the subsequent verses to answer this.

Nistha means Sthiti. What is called Sthiti is that state in which one abides, has one's position or basis. Do they abide in Sattva, in Rajas or in Tamas?  Such is the meaning of the estion.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Tu, but; of Krsna, ka, what; is the nistha, state; tesam, of those-whosoever they may be; ye, who; being anvitah, endued; sraddhaya, with faith, with the idea that there is something hereafter; yajante, adore gods and others; utsriya, by ignoring, setting aside; sastra-vidhim, the unjunctions of the scriptures, the injunctions of the Vedas and the Smrtis? Is the state of those who are such sattvam, sattva; aho, or; rajah, rajas; or tamah, tamas? This is what is meant: Does the adoration of gods and others that they undertake come under the category of sattva or rajas or tamas?
By 'those who, endued with faith, adore by ignoring the injunctions of the scriptures' are here meant those who, not finding any injunction which can be characterized as 'enjoined by the Vedas' 'or enjoined by the Smrtis', worship gods and others by merely observing the conduct of their elders. But, on the other hand, those who, though aware of some scriptural injunction, discard them and worship the gods and others in ways contrary to the injunctions, are not meant here by 'those who, ignoring scriptural injunctions, adore৷৷.'
Why?
Because of the alifying phrase, 'being endued with faith'. For, it cannot be imagined that even when they are aware of some scriptural injunction about worship of gods and others, they discard this out of their faithlessness, and yet they engage in the worship of gods and others enjoined by those scriptures by becoming imbued with faith! Therefore, by 'those who, endued with faith, adore by ignoring the injunctions of the scriptures' are here meant those very ones mentioned earlier.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that in the previous chapter, it was declared that those who act under the impulse of desire, disregarding the scriptural injunctions, do not attain perfection, happiness, or the supreme goal. Arjuna now asks: what is the standing of those who act with faith but without scriptural knowledge? Is their standing Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic?

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that performers of action are of two kinds: those who knowingly discard scriptural injunctions and act out of desire (demonic nature), and those who follow scriptural injunctions with faith (divine nature). However, there is a third category of people who ignore the scriptures due to laziness but perform actions with faith based on the conduct of elders. They share traits of both divine (faith) and demonic (neglecting scriptures) natures. Arjuna asks which category they belong to—Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that Arjuna asks about those who perform sacrifices, charity, etc., with faith but without scriptural knowledge. Having heard that those who discard scriptural injunctions do not attain perfection, Arjuna wonders about the standing of these individuals who are full of faith. Is their state Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas?

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that Arjuna's question arises from the statement at the end of the previous chapter. He asks whether the standing of those who act with faith but without scriptural knowledge is Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic. Sanjaya addresses Krishna to seek clarification on the nature of this faith.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that Arjuna's question is to resolve the doubt about the status of those who act with faith but without scriptural knowledge. He addresses Krishna as 'Krishna' (meaning one who attracts or removes obstacles) to show that their welfare depends on His grace. The question is whether their standing is Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas, to determine the value and result of their efforts.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that faith without scriptural knowledge is analyzed here. In the previous chapter, it was said that action without scriptural injunction yields no fruit. Arjuna now asks: for those who have deep faith but lack scriptural knowledge, does their standing lead to any knowledge or spiritual fruit? Is their state Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas? He addresses Krishna to know if their reliance on traditional practices has any spiritual validity.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the question arises from the statement that the scriptures are the authority. If people perform worship with firm faith but without scriptural knowledge, following their family traditions or elders, what is their standing? Is it Sattvic, Rajasic, or Tamasic? Since they lack scriptural knowledge, their actions might seem like self-willed behavior (demonic), yet their faith seems divine. Arjuna seeks to resolve this contradiction.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Arjuna inquired: O Kṛṣṇa, what is the situation of those who do not follow the principles of scripture but worship according to their own imagination? Are they in goodness, in passion or in ignorance?

 In the Fourth Chapter, thirty-ninth verse, it is said that a person faithful to a particular type of worship gradually becomes elevated to the stage of knowledge and attains the highest perfectional stage of peace and prosperity. In the Sixteenth Chapter, it is concluded that one who does not follow the principles laid down in the scriptures is called an asura, demon, and one who follows the scriptural injunctions faithfully is called a deva, or demigod. Now, if one, with faith, follows some rules which are not mentioned in the scriptural injunctions, what is his position? This doubt of Arjuna’s is to be cleared by Kṛṣṇa. Are those who create some sort of God by selecting a human being and placing their faith in him worshiping in goodness, passion or ignorance? Do such persons attain the perfectional stage of life? Is it possible for them to be situated in real knowledge and elevate themselves to the highest perfectional stage? Do those who do not follow the rules and regulations of the scriptures but who have faith in something and worship gods and demigods and men attain success in their effort? Arjuna is putting these questions to Kṛṣṇa.
