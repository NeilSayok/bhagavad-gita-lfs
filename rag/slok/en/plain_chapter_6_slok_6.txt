Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
bandhurātmātmanastasya yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ .
anātmanastu śatrutve vartetātmaiva śatruvat

Word Meanings:
बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य - the self is friend of him
येनात्मैवात्मना - by whom self by self
जितः - is conquered
अनात्मनस्तु - but for the uncontrolled self
शत्रुत्वे - in enmity
वर्तेतात्मैव - behaves self itself
शत्रुवत् - like an enemy

Life Application:
Master your mind so it becomes your best ally, rather than letting an undisciplined mind act as your worst enemy.

Sri Madhavacharya states: "Conquered by the Self"—a self-controlled devotee"s mind becomes a friend receiving Lord Vishnu"s grace; an un-controlled mind falling into sense-objects turns into an enemy.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: He who controls the mind through discriminating intellect finds the mind to be a true benefactor (friend); otherwise, the unbridled mind inflicts harm like an enemy.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Let a man elevate himself by his own Self, let him not degrade himself; for the Self alone is the friend of oneself, and the Self alone is one"s enemy. The Self is the friend of him who has conquered himself by the Self; but for him who has not conquered himself, his own Self acts as an enemy in enmity.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "For him who has conquered himself by the Self, his Self is a friend; but for him who has not conquered himself, his Self acts as an enemy in hostility"—delineates how self-mastery turns the internal organ into a liberating ally, whereas lack of control turns it into an adversarial enemy.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Mastery over senses is the root of internal friendship; self-control enables mind absorption in God, while un-control brings ruin.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: The mind dedicated to Lord Krishna"s loving service is the soul"s true friend, liberating it from worldly suffering.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "For the unmastered self, hostility"—a controlled mind is the supreme means to Mukti, whereas an uncontrolled mind causes deep worldly bondage.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the mind acting as friend or foe based on sense-mastery versus sense-subjection.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A controlled mind aids in Vasudeva"s meditation and lifts the soul, while an uncontrolled mind causes downfall.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: The mind subdued through Lord Krishna"s grace becomes the ultimate benevolent companion of the soul.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "For the unmastered self, hostility"—for one enslaved by senses, his own mind is his greatest devastating enemy.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy.
