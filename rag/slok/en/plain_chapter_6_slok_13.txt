Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
samaṃ kāyaśirogrīvaṃ dhārayannacalaṃ sthiraḥ .
samprekṣya nāsikāgraṃ svaṃ diśaścānavalokayan

Word Meanings:
समं - equal
कायशिरोग्रीवं - body, head and neck
धारयन्नचलं - holding motionless
स्थिरः - steady
सम्प्रेक्ष्य - gazing at
नासिकाग्रं - tip of the nose
स्वं - one's own
दिशश्चानवलोकयन् - without looking around

Life Application:
Hold your body, neck, and head erect and still, focusing your gaze inward to settle your wandering attention.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, looking at the tip of his nose, without looking around—

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, motionless, gazing at the tip of his nose, without looking around in directions—

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect, balanced, and immovable, seated firm, fixing his gaze steadily at the tip of his nose without letting his eyes wander in any direction—

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, looking at the tip of his nose, without looking around—

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, looking at the tip of his nose, without looking around—

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, motionless, gazing at the tip of his nose, without looking around in directions—

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, motionless, gazing at the tip of his nose, without looking around in directions—

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Gazing at the tip of the nose"—maintaining physical stillness and fixing vision on the nose-tip grounds the mind in Vishnu-meditation.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Physical posture stability—holding body, head, and neck upright—greatly assists in steadying the internal organ during meditation.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, looking at the tip of his nose, without looking around—

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Keeping body, head, and neck aligned in a straight still line, fix the eyes on the tip of the nose to prevent mind-wandering.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, gazing steadily at the tip of his nose without looking around, serene-minded, fearless, firm in the vow of celibacy, with mind controlled, let him sit concentrating on Me, intent on Me as the Supreme Goal.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, gazing at the tip of his nose, without looking around"—prescribes physical stillness and focused visual gaze (Nasikagra Drishti) to prevent mind dispersion.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Straightness of body, head, and neck combined with nasal tip gaze is the primary physical discipline of Dhyana-Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Withdrawing vision from external forms to rest on the tip of the nose absorbs the mind solely in thinking of Krishna.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Without looking in directions"—avoiding wandering gaze quietens the visual sense, perfecting inward orientation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Holding the body, head, and neck erect and still, motionless, gazing at the tip of his nose, without looking around in directions—

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of maintaining erect spinal alignment and nasal gaze during meditation.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Immovable posture eliminates sensory flickering and focuses the mind on Lord Vasudeva.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When bodily restlessness subsides, the mind naturally settles into Lord Krishna"s divine form.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Gazing at the nose-tip"—gazing at the nose-tip avoids the flaws of closing the eyes completely (sleep) or opening them wide (distraction).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One should hold one"s body, neck and head erect in a straight line and stare steadily at the tip of the nose. Thus, with an unagitated, subdued mind, devoid of fear, completely free from sex life, one should meditate upon Me within the heart and make Me the ultimate goal of life.
