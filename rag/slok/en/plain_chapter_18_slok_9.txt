Life Application:
When prescribed duty is performed because 'it ought to be done', abandoning attachment and fruit—that is Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Sattvik men perform actions with the feeling that to perform obligatory actions is a duty, and to abandon them is an extremely humiliating and shameful act. They consider the abandonment of duty to be their death itself. The abandonment of men inspired in this manner is called Sattvik. There are some unavoidable constraints in actions. Lord Krishna's advice is only that we should continue performing our actions without these constraints. The implication is that only when there is attachment to the fruit of action can that action bind us; otherwise not. Therefore, true abandonment must be of attachment to fruits, not of actions. For this reason, the performance of duties without attachment is called Sattvik abandonment here. According to the Lord's teaching, the abandonment of selfishness, desire, attachment, etc., located in one's mind is indeed the true abandonment, through which we attain our nature of supreme bliss. This abandonment is somewhat like abandoning hunger by consuming food. By performing duties while abandoning ego and selfishness, a person destroys his desires (vasanas) and attains inner purification. Thus, abandonment makes a person even more powerful and efficient in action. Listen to how this Sattvik person attains Self-realisation:

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Abandonment of actions has been shown to be of two kinds: Tamasic and Rajasic. Now, He describes Sattvik abandonment, prefacing it with a question: "What then...?". By the word "eva" (only) in the statement "because it ought to be performed" (kartyavyam ityeva), any other consequence of the daily duty is prohibited. To the objection, "Since no fruit is heard of in the scriptural injunctions for daily duties, it is improper to say 'renouncing their fruit'", he (Sri Shankaracharya) says: "of the daily obligatory duties...". He states the meaning of "giving up the result" in another way: "Or...". For action performed according to an injunction is not fruitless, because that would make the injunction useless; hence, even in the absence of any scriptural fruit, one who performs daily duties according to the injunction, but who is ignorant of the Self, imagines a fruit in that action consisting of purification of the mind in accordance with statements like "he becomes of uninjured mind"; or, because the Smriti declares sin of omission (pratyavaya) for not performing it, he imagines that performing it destroys that sin for the doer; therefore, the Lord prevents both such imagined fruits and any other secondary fruits in daily duties by the words "giving up the result". Such is the meaning. He states the result of the possibility of stating the abandonment of fruits in daily duties: "Hence...". Having promised the threefold nature in "Renunciation, indeed..." (18.4) of the abandonment of actions and their fruits which is the context of the terms tyaga and sannyasa, and having explained two kinds in accordance with that promise, he raises the objection that the Lord has shown lack of skill by explaining the third kind in contradiction to it, in the words: "Well...". To show that a concluding statement contrary to the introduction is improper, he gives an illustration: "like...". He refutes the alleged lack of skill arising from the contradiction between the preceding and succeeding statements, with: "This is not wrong...". Because of the similarity of the abandonment of actions and the abandonment of fruits, both being abandonment, this statement is meant to praise the abandonment of fruits of actions by condemning the abandonment of actions themselves; by accepting this, there is no contradiction. To clarify what was said, he first explains the general nature of abandonment: "Between...". Since there is a common factor, he supports the view that the statement is for praise: "While on this...".

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna! That obligatory action which is performed by renouncing attachment and fruits, understanding that "only duty has to be performed", is considered to be Sattvik abandonment.

Commentary — kāryamityeva yatkarma niyataṃ kriyate.arjuna — Here, by attaching the two indeclinables iti and eva to the word kāryam, the meaning is that only duty has to be performed. In performing this, there is no attachment to the fruit, no selfishness, and no enjoyment of pleasure born of action. By performing action solely as duty in this manner, the doer's relationship with that action is severed. This being so, that action does not cause bondage, i.e., no relationship is established with the world. Bondage arises only from attachment to action and its fruit — "one who is attached to the fruit is bound" (Gita 5.12).
Even among scripturally ordained actions, those actions in which one is appointed according to place, time, class, station in life, and circumstances, are all called obligatory actions (niyata karma). For example, a monk should do this, a householder should do that, a Brahmana should perform such work, a Kshatriya should perform such work, etc. Those actions must be performed with promptness and enthusiasm, free from defects like negligence, laziness, disregard, indifference, etc. Therefore, the Lord has used the word samācara (perform properly) in various places in the context of Karma Yoga (Gita 3.9, 3.19).
saṅgaṃ tyaktvā phalaṃ caiva — The abandonment of sanga (attachment) implies that there should be no attachment, fondness, or possessiveness towards the action, the instruments of action, etc.; and the abandonment of the fruit implies that there should be no relationship with the consequence of action, i.e., no desire for the fruit. The meaning of both is that attachment to and desire for action and its fruit should be abandoned.
sa tyāgaḥ sāttviko mataḥ — By performing action understanding it solely as duty, renouncing attachment to and desire for action and its fruit, that abandonment becomes Sattvik. In Rajasic abandonment, actions are abandoned in their physical form out of fear of physical suffering, and in Tamasic abandonment, they are abandoned out of delusion. But in Sattvik abandonment, actions are not abandoned in their physical form; rather, actions are performed carefully, promptly, methodically, and without desires. Through Sattvik abandonment, one's relationship with action and the body-world in the form of fruits of actions is severed. In Rajasic and Tamasic abandonment, abandoning actions physically only shows a severance of relationship with actions externally, but in reality (internally), the relationship is not severed. The reason is that by abandoning actions out of fear of bodily pain, actions are indeed left behind, but the relationship with one's own pleasure and comfort remains intact. Similarly, by abandoning actions out of delusion, actions are indeed left behind, but the relationship with delusion remains intact. The implication is that physical abandonment of actions causes bondage, whereas performing actions promptly and methodically leads to liberation (severance of relationship).
Relationship — What attitude should be maintained in performing scripturally ordained actions like sacrifice, charity, austerity, etc. mentioned by the words etāni and api tu in the sixth verse, and in abandoning scripturally prohibited and desire-oriented actions, is explained in the next verse.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
When rites like obligatory and occasional ceremonies and the great sacrifices enjoined on one's station and stage in life, are practised for their own sake, as worship of Myself and as a duty, relinishing possessiveness and fruits - such abandonment is regarded as Sattvika. It is noted in Sattva. The idea is that it is rooted in the knowledge of the meaning of the Sastras as it really is. That Sattva generates the knowledge of things as they really are, has been taught in:  'From Sattva arises knowledge' (14.17), and it will be further declared:  'That reason by which one knows action and renunciation, what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, fear and fearlessness, bondage and release, O Arjuna, is Sattvika' (18.30).

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 18.4–18.11 jointly): Niscayam etc.  upto abhidhiyate.  The conclusion here is  this :  Due to the manifoldness of the nature of the Strands, that have been defined earlier, the act of relinishment itself is performed with a certain mental disposition which is a modification of the Sattva, the Rajas and the Tamas (the Strands).  Because it reflects  (is contaminated by)  the nature of the person having the same (the said mental dispositon),  what is called the real (unalloyed)  relinishment is the performance of the actions  by the knowers of the Supreme Brahman by giving up desire to achieve fruits and by avoiding the craving and hatred on account of their eanimity to [the pairs of opposites like]  success and failure etc.  That is why [the Bhagavat] says :  'By the act of relinishment born of the Rajas or of the Tamas  (Strands), no connection with the fruit  [of relinishment] is attained'.  However, for an act of relinishing, born of the Sattva (Strand), there is the fruit in the form of honouring the purport of the scriptures.  The application of the term  'relinishment'  stands to reason, in fact, only in the case of a sage who has relinished his holding on the multitude of the Strands.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Yat, whatever; niyatam karma, daily obligatory duty; kriyate, is performed, accomplished; iti eva, just because; it is karyam, a bounden duty; O Arjuna, tyaktva, by giving up; sangam, attachment; and phalam, the result; ca eva, as well; sah, that; tyagah, renunciation, giving up of attachment and (hankering for) the resutls of daily obligatory duties; matah, is considered; to be sattvikah, based on sattva, arising from sattva.
We said that the Lord's utterance is proof of the fruitfulness of daily obligatory duties. Or, even if the niyakarmas be understood (from the Lord's worlds) to be fruitless, still the ignorant man does certainly imagine that the nityakarmas (daily obligatory duites) when performed produce for oneself a result either in the form of purification of the mind or avoidance of evil. As to this, the Lord aborts even that imagination by saying, 'by giving up the result'. Hence it has been well said, 'by giving up attachment and the result'.
Objection: Well, is not the threefold relinishment of actions, also called sannyasa, under discussion? As regards this, the renunciation based on tamas and rajas have been stated. Why is the relinishment of attachment and (desire for their) results spoken of here as the third? This is like somody saying, 'Three Brahmanas have come. Of them two are versed in the six auxiliaries [The six auxiliaries are: Siksa (Phonetics), Kalpa (Code of Rituals and Sacrifices), Vyakarana (Grammar), Nirukta (Etymology), Chandas (Meter, Prosody), and Jyotisa (Astronomy).-Tr.] of the Vedas; the third is a Ksatirya!'
Reply: This is not wrong, for this is meant as a eulogy on the basis of the common factor of renunciation. Between renunciation of actions and renunciation. of hankering for results, there is, indeed, the similarity of the fact of renunciation. While on this subject, by condemning 'renunciation of actions' on account of its being based on rajas and tamas, the 'renunciation of desire for results of actions' is being praised on account of its being based on sattva, by saying, 'that renunciation is considered to be based on sattva.'

When it is pure and tranil, it becomes capable of contemplating on the Self. Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:

Whatever action ordained by class and station of life (varnashrama), such as daily and occasional ceremonies, great sacrifices, etc., is performed with the attitude that "it must be performed as My command, being of the form of My worship", O Arjuna of pure nature, He says that it should be performed in the manner mentioned: "abandoning attachment, i.e. possessiveness towards actions, and the fruit". Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Abandonment of actions has been shown to be Tamasic and Rajasic, which are to be rejected. Now, what kind of abandonment is to be accepted, namely Sattvik abandonment, is described: knowing that "it ought to be performed as a duty" even though no fruit is heard of in the scriptural command, whatever obligatory action (niyata), namely daily duty (nitya), is performed by abandoning attachment, which is the insistence on agency, and also the fruit, until the purification of the inner instrument is achieved—that abandonment is Sattvik, i.e., arising from Sattva, and is approved by the wise as worthy of acceptance. Objection: "But daily duties have no fruit at all, how then is it said 'giving up the result'?" Reply: From this very word of the Lord, it is known that daily duties have a fruit, because the performance of what is entirely fruitless is impossible. And Apastamba also shows the secondary fruit of daily duties: "Just as when a mango tree is planted for its fruit, shade and fragrance are produced as secondary results, in the same way, when dharma is practiced, prosperity and other objects are produced secondary to it" (Apastamba Dharma Sutra 1.20.3). And the Smriti declaring sin of omission (pratyavaya) for non-performance shows that the fruit of daily duties is the avoidance of that sin. And Shruti passages like "By dharma one destroys sin; therefore they say dharma is supreme", "By whatever sacrifice one wins, or even by a simple offering (darvi-homa), one indeed becomes of uninjured mind", and "They say: the sacrifice to the gods is better, or the sacrifice to the Self? One should say: the sacrifice to the Self indeed... he who knows: 'this limb of mine is purified by this, this limb is placed by this'..." show that the fruit of daily duties is the purification of the self, characterized by the destruction of sin which obstructs knowledge and the production of merit which is the fitness for knowledge; the meaning is that they should be performed by renouncing the desire for that. What was said earlier — that the words tyaga and sannyasa do not refer to different classes of meaning like the words "pot" and "cloth", but their meaning is indeed the abandonment of action associated with desire for fruits — that should not be forgotten. There, even though the desire for fruits exists, the abandonment of action out of delusion or fear of physical suffering is a qualified absence (visishta-abhava) caused by the absence of the qualified object (action itself); it is censured as Tamasic and Rajasic. Whereas, even though action is performed, the abandonment of the desire for fruits is a qualified absence caused by the absence of the qualification (desire); it is praised as Sattvik. Since being a qualified absence is common to both the absence of the qualified object and the absence of the qualification, there is no contradiction between the preceding and succeeding statements. And we have also said that the absence of both (action and desire) is Nirguna and should not be counted in this comparison. By this, the objection that "the Lord has shown a clear lack of skill because, having promised 'Abandonment indeed is declared to be threefold...' (18.4) and shown two kinds consisting of the abandonment of actions, He has not shown a third kind consisting of the abandonment of actions but instead shown the performance of action" "just like someone saying 'Three Brahmanas are to be fed; two are Katha and Kaundinya, and the third is a Kshatriya'" — is refuted. Because all three kinds have been explained before as belonging to the same class under the general category of abandonment, in the form of qualified absence. Therefore, pointing out a lack of skill in the Lord is itself a great lack of skill (on the part of the objector).

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having thus described the mode of Rajasic abandonment, He states that of Sattvik abandonment: "kāryam...". Whatever obligatory (niyata), namely daily duty, is performed by renouncing attachment, which is the conceit of agency, and also the fruit, with the understanding that "it ought to be performed as a duty" — that abandonment is regarded as Sattvik. Objection: "Since no fruit is heard of in the scriptural commands for daily duties, how is it said 'giving up the result'?" Reply: Understand that the Lord's word itself is the proof that daily duties have a fruit. Otherwise, the Lord's statement would be useless. Or, if action performed according to an injunction were fruitless, it would lead to the uselessness of the injunction. Even in the absence of any scriptural fruit, the ignorant person qualified for actions imagines that daily duties perform purification of the self and avoidance of sin for the doer; the Lord prevents even that imagination with the words: "giving up the result". To indicate that this abandonment alone is the cause of mental purification, the address "Arjuna" is used. Objection: "Having introduced the threefold nature of abandonment as 'abandonment of action is considered to be of three kinds', stating the abandonment of attachment and fruit as the third is improper; just like someone saying 'Three Brahmanas have come; two are versed in the six auxiliaries of the Vedas, and the third is a Kshatriya'." Reply: This is not wrong. Because the renunciation of actions and the abandonment of attachment and fruits share the general category of abandonment; and presenting the abandonment of attachment and fruit as the third is for the purpose of praising it as Sattvik, by condemning the abandonment of actions on account of its being Tamasic and Rajasic; thus, the threefold nature of abandonment promised by the Acharya (Sri Shankaracharya) has been shown in three verses. But some explain: "Abandonment, which is in the form of a qualified absence, is declared to be threefold: from the absence of the qualification, the absence of the qualified object, and the absence of both. To explain: one is the abandonment of the desire for fruits while action is performed. The second is the abandonment of action while the desire for fruits exists. The third is the abandonment of both the desire for fruits and action. Of these, the first is Sattvik and is ordained here as worthy of acceptance. The second will be described in the verse 'He attains the supreme perfection of actionlessness...' (18.49)." In this view, by including two in one and inserting the third in another place, the inability to fulfill the promise would show a great lack of skill in the Lord; hence, this should be noted.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Now, taking up that very subject which was recalled in "Abandonment indeed..." (18.4) etc., He strengthens its scriptural character by its being rooted in the knowledge of things as they really are, which is the effect of Sattva, in the verse: "kāryamityeva...". Since "obligatory action" is the context starting with "the renunciation of obligatory action is not proper" (18.7), the word "niyata" here also is a qualification of action; hence he (Sri Ramanuja) explains its meaning as: "daily and occasional ceremonies". Directly, obligatoriness belongs only to the purpose (fruit). Indeed, the means has obligatoriness only for the sake of that purpose; therefore, how can that which has no fruit have obligatoriness? To this he says: "as a part of My worship, it ought to be done". He states the intended meaning as: "for their own sake". The abandonment of agency is also to be contemplated here. Indeed, for this very reason, in the subsequent verse, the word "tyagi" is explained as "one who abandons attachment, fruits, and agency".

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He describes Sattvik abandonment: "kāryam...". Knowing that the obligatory action, which is an auxiliary of devotion, ought to be performed indeed as My command and as an absolute duty, and performing action by abandoning attachment, which is the conceit of agency, and the fruit, namely heavenly happiness etc., produced by it; that abandonment indeed is Sattvik, because by performing it as My command, there is no selfish fruit; hence it is Sattvik. Therefore, it is "regarded", meaning "approved by Me". Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Thus, in the two previous verses, Tamasic and Rajasic abandonments were described in their primary sense (of physical abandonment of action). The impossibility of secondary Tamasic and Rajasic abandonments (i.e. performing actions but mentally abandoning them under Tamas/Rajas) was indicated by the Lord Himself in the words "abandonment from delusion" and "abandons out of fear of physical suffering"; for this is indeed not possible. There is a contradiction in saying "he is deluded, and yet he performs action". If he performs action, he is not deluded; if he is deluded, he does not perform action. Similarly, if he fears physical suffering, he does not perform action; if he performs action, he does not fear physical suffering; therefore, saying "he performs action, and yet he fears physical suffering" is self-contradictory. Therefore, due to the impossibility of secondary Tamasic and Rajasic abandonments, they were not mentioned. But secondary Sattvik abandonment is indeed possible. Just as the redness in a crystal placed near a hibiscus flower exists for the wise only in appearance and not in reality, so it can be said that the agency of the self, which is dependent on God, exists for the wise only in appearance and not in reality. And thus, a person free from the insistence on agency performs action only in appearance and not in reality; hence, secondary Sattvik abandonment is possible. Therefore, He first describes that very abandonment, which is the cause of qualification for primary abandonment, in the words: "kāryam...". Whatever obligatory (niyata), namely daily duty, is performed, O Arjuna, with the restriction "only by abandoning attachment and the fruit" (tyaktvaiva), is for the purpose of excluding the non-abandonment view mentioned before. Such abandonment is regarded as Sattvik, as seen in the Vedas. And so says the Shruti: "All this, whatever moves in this moving world, is enveloped by the Lord. Enjoy by renouncing it; do not covet anyone's wealth" (Isha Upanishad 1). By "īśā", by the Lord, the Supreme Ruler who is the prompter of the self which is the doer of all actions and instruments, this world of the moving and non-moving located in the universe (jagatyam) is to be enveloped, covered, pervaded. Because everything is dependent on Him, therefore, enjoy (bhunjitha) the objects through renunciation (tyaktena), by abandoning the conceit of agency and enjoyment. "Do not covet" (ma gridhah), do not be greedy. "Anyone's wealth" (kasyasvid dhanam) — no one has ownership over it, hence greed there is in vain. Such is the meaning. In this way, when you perform actions like sacrifice by abandoning the conceit of agency, you will not be tainted by actions. That there is no other way for you than this is shown by the next mantra: "Performing actions indeed here, one should wish to live a hundred years. Thus it is in you, not otherwise than this; action does not cling to man" (Isha Upanishad 2). This very principal view of His own is shown by the Lord in the verse "etānyapi tu karmāṇi..." (18.6). Objection: "But daily duties have no fruit at all, what then is to be abandoned?" Reply: Know from this very word of the Lord that they too have a fruit. Because the performance of what is fruitless cannot be ordained by the Vedas. And Apastamba also shows the secondary fruit of daily duties: "Just as when a mango tree is planted for its fruit, shade and fragrance are produced secondary to it, in the same way, when dharma is practiced, prosperity and other objects are produced secondary to it." By the Smriti declaring sin of omission for non-performance also, the avoidance of that sin of omission is shown as the fruit. And by passages like "By dharma one destroys sin", a fruit is indeed seen even in daily duties; hence, that should be stated, and there is no defect whatsoever.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O Arjuna, when one performs his prescribed duty only because it ought to be done, and renounces all material association and all attachment to the fruit, his renunciation is said to be in the mode of goodness.

 Prescribed duties must be performed with this mentality. One should act without attachment for the result; he should be disassociated from the modes of work. A man working in Kṛṣṇa consciousness in a factory does not associate himself with the work of the factory, nor with the workers of the factory. He simply works for Kṛṣṇa. And when he gives up the result for Kṛṣṇa, he is acting transcendentally.
