Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yasya sarve samārambhāḥ kāmasaṅkalpavarjitāḥ .
jñānāgnidagdhakarmāṇaṃ tamāhuḥ paṇḍitaṃ budhāḥ

Word Meanings:
यस्य - whose
सर्वे - all
समारम्भाः - undertakings
कामसङ्कल्पवर्जिताः - free from desire and motive
ज्ञानाग्निदग्धकर्माणं - whose actions are burnt by wisdom
तमाहुः - call him
पण्डितं - wise man
बुधाः - the wise

Life Application:
Burn away selfish motives from your projects today, letting pure duty drive your daily initiatives.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He whose every enterprise and undertaking is totally free from worldly desire and egoic intent, and whose actions are completely burnt away in the fire of Self-knowledge—him the truly wise declare to be a Sage (Pandita).

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Whose actions are burnt in the fire of wisdom"—he whose prohibited and fruitive actions are consumed by the fire of Divine Knowledge is called a true Pandita.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: He whose ignorance and desire-driven intentions are consumed in the fire of Knowledge is acclaimed by the wise as a true Pandita.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: He whose activities are performed solely for world-welfare or God"s service, devoid of personal desire, is designated a Pandita by the wise.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Yasya etc. The actions, performed without desire for fruits and burnt by the fire of Knowledge, turn a person into a Sage (Pandita).

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage"—defines a true Sage (*Pandita*) as one whose actions are burnt away by Self-realization.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: The scorching of karmic latencies in the fire of Knowledge is the hallmark of Jivanmukti (liberation in life).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A loving devotee acting selflessly with mind dedicated to Krishna becomes a Pandita dear to the Lord, his karma consumed by wisdom.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Him the wise call a Pandita"—when accumulated and present karmas burn away in non-dual realization, the practitioner becomes a true Pandita.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
He whose undertakings are all devoid of desire and purpose, whose actions are burnt by the fire of Knowledge, him the wise call a Sage.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing why one whose endeavors are free from desire and whose actions are burnt in wisdom is called a Pandita.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: He whose karma is burnt by Vasudeva-bhakti and the fire of wisdom is alone a Pandita in the eyes of sages.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Immersed in Krishna"s divine love, when a practitioner works devoid of worldly longings, he is known as a wise Pandita.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Whose actions are burnt in the fire of Knowledge"—consuming ignorance-born karma in wisdom is the sole path to freedom.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One is understood to be in full knowledge whose every endeavor is devoid of desire for sense gratification. He is said by sages to be a worker whose fruitive action is burned up by the fire of perfect knowledge.
