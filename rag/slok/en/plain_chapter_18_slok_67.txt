Life Application:
Share sacred spiritual wisdom only with sincere listeners who demonstrate genuine faith, devotion, and respect.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Generally, at the end of all spiritual texts, the method of transmitting the scripture—that is, the description of the eligible seeker of knowledge—is given. Following this great tradition, in this verse, Lord Krishna explains to whom this knowledge should not be given. By this, the qualities of an eligible student of knowledge are also indicated. This does not mean that by mentioning these qualities, the Gita is meant to be kept hidden, but rather that the knowledge should be shared with those who are receptive and prepared to assimilate it. If we do not benefit much from the study of the scripture, we must certainly lack some necessary qualities. In that situation, we must perform self-purification through self-examination. Just as removing dust from a mirror makes the reflection clear, similarly, when the mind and intellect become pure and steady, Self-realization becomes clear. Now, the Lord describes the fruits obtained by the teacher and disseminator of this tradition.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
This scripture, which is the means to destroy the bondage of Samsara, should never be spoken to one who lacks austerity, nor to one who has no devotion towards God and Guru, nor to one who is unwilling to hear or serve, and definitely not to one who is envious of Me, the Supreme Lord. Only the deserving seeker who has devotion, humility, and desire to learn should receive this knowledge.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
This secret should never be spoken by you to one who is devoid of austerity, nor to one who is not a devotee, nor to one who does not wish to hear, and certainly not to one who cavils at Me. Commentary: The Lord sets the qualification of a fit recipient of this knowledge. Four qualifications are laid down by negating four types of unfit persons: 1. One devoid of austerity (Nātapaskāya): One who has no self-control over senses, mind, and speech. 2. One who is not a devotee (Nābhaktāya): One who has no love and reverence for God and Guru. 3. One who does not wish to hear or serve (Na cāśuśrūṣave): One who has no desire to listen to the truth or who does not perform service to the Guru. 4. One who cavils at Me (Na ca māṃ yo'bhyasūyati): One who finds fault with the Lord's statements and behavior, attributing human defects to Him. This rule is meant to preserve the sanctity of the teaching and protect the unfit from accumulating further negative karma by criticizing or mocking the supreme truth.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
I have taught you this most secret doctrine. This should not be imparted by you to someone who has not practised austere disciplines. Never should this be taught to someone who is not devoted to Me and to you, the teacher (i.e., when you have to play the role of a teacher of this doctrine). The meaning is that it should not be taught by you to someone who, though practising austerities, is not a devotee and does not serve Me. It is also never to be taught to one who has no wish to listen, even though he is a devotee. Nor should it be imparted to one who traduces Me, that is, who - when My nature, glories and attributes are described - discovers defects in them. The differences of case (from ablative to nominative form) is to teach that the last one is the most despicable character.

When the knot of sin is cut off through observing  austerities, then only the results of good act is ready to become ripe.  Hence, austerity comes first.  Due to austerity, faith is born. Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Idam, this Scripture; which has been taught by Me te, to you, for your good, for terminating mundane existence; an vacyam, should not be taught (-na is connected with the remote word vacyam-); atapaskaya, to one who is devoid of austerities. It should kadacana, never, under any condition whatsoever; be taught abhaktaya, to one who is not a devotee, who is devoid of devotion to his teacher and God, even if he be a man of austerity. Neither should it be taught even asurusave, to one who does not redner service-even though he may be a devotee and a man of austerity. Na ca, nor as well; to him yah, who; abhyasuyati, cavils; mam, at Me, at Vasudeva-thinking that I am an ordinary person; to him who, not knowing My Godhood, imputes self-adulation etc. to Me and cannot tolerate Me. He too is unfit; to him also it should not be imparted.
From the force of the context it is understood that the Scripture should be taught to one who has devotion to the Lord, is austere, renders service, and does not cavil. As to that, since it is seen (in a Smrti)-'to one who is intelligent or to one who is austere'-that there is an option between the two, it follows that this should be imparted either to an austere person given to service and devotion, or to an intelligent person endowed with them. It should not be imparted to an austere or even an intelligent person if he lacks service and devotion. It should not be taught to one who cavils at the Lord, even though he be possessed of all the good alities. And it should be taught to one whoserves his teacher and is devout. This is the rule for transmitting the Scripture.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Having taught the meaning of the Gita, the Lord regulates its transmission: 'idam te...'. This knowledge should not be spoken to one who lacks self-control or austerity. The other conditions are clear from the text.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Having summarized the entire meaning of the Gita, the Lord establishes the rule for its transmission: 'idam te...'. This supreme secret should never be spoken to one who is devoid of austerity, nor to one who lacks devotion towards God and Guru, nor to one who does not serve or wish to hear, and definitely not to one who is envious of Me. Only those who possess these virtues are qualified to receive this teaching.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Having taught the truth of the Gita, the Lord states the rule for its transmission: 'idam te...'. This truth of the Gita should never be spoken by you to one who is devoid of austerity (not performing his duties), nor to one who lacks devotion towards the Lord and the Guru, nor to one who is unwilling to hear or serve, and not to one who is envious of Me, the Supreme Lord, finding faults in Me due to a human outlook.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Having summarized the Gita, the Lord details the rule of transmission: 'idam te...'. This scripture, which destroys the cycle of birth and death, should not be spoken to one who lacks austerity, nor to one who has no devotion towards God and Guru, nor to one who does not serve, and certainly not to one who is envious of Me (attributing human faults to Me and not tolerating My divine status). It should be taught to one who is devoted, humble, and free from envy.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
The Lord states the qualifications for receiving this supreme scripture. It should not be taught to one who has not performed proper austerity, nor to one who lacks devotion towards the teacher (Guru) and the Lord, nor to one who is unwilling to hear or serve, and most importantly, not to one who is envious of the Lord, attributing defects to His divine nature. Teaching this to unqualified persons results in spiritual harm.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Having taught the essence of all scriptures, the Lord describes the fit recipient for the propagation of this path: 'idam te...'. This secret of all scriptures should not be spoken to one who lacks good conduct or austerity. It should never be spoken to one who is devoid of devotion to Me. 'Never' indicates it should not be spoken even to a devotee if he is associated with non-devotees. Nor should it be spoken to one who has no desire to listen or who does not serve, and certainly not to one who maliciously finds faults in Me, the Supreme Lord.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
The Lord concludes the paths of Sankhya and Yoga and describes the rule of transmission of this knowledge: 'idam te...'. One who lacks reflection or effort (atapaskaya), one who lacks faith or devotion (abhaktaya), one who does not serve the Guru (asusrushave), and one who is envious of the supreme Self, finding faults in Me—to such persons, this secret should never be revealed under any circumstances, even in times of great distress. Only those who possess devotion to God and Guru, who are straightforward, and who practice self-control are eligible to receive this secret knowledge.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
This confidential knowledge may never be explained to those who are not austere, or devoted, or engaged in devotional service, nor to one who is envious of Me.

 Persons who have not undergone the austerities of the religious process, who have never attempted devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, who have not tended a pure devotee, and especially those who are conscious of Kṛṣṇa only as a historical personality or who are envious of the greatness of Kṛṣṇa should not be told this most confidential part of knowledge. It is, however, sometimes found that even demoniac persons who are envious of Kṛṣṇa, worshiping Kṛṣṇa in a different way, take to the profession of explaining Bhagavad-gītā in a different way to make business, but anyone who desires actually to understand Kṛṣṇa must avoid such commentaries on Bhagavad-gītā . Actually the purpose of Bhagavad-gītā is not understandable to those who are sensuous. Even if one is not sensuous but is strictly following the disciplines enjoined in the Vedic scripture, if he is not a devotee he also cannot understand Kṛṣṇa. And even when one poses himself as a devotee of Kṛṣṇa but is not engaged in Kṛṣṇa conscious activities, he also cannot understand Kṛṣṇa. There are many persons who envy Kṛṣṇa because He has explained in Bhagavad-gītā that He is the Supreme and that nothing is above Him or equal to Him. There are many persons who are envious of Kṛṣṇa. Such persons should not be told of Bhagavad-gītā , for they cannot understand. There is no possibility of faithless persons’ understanding Bhagavad-gītā and Kṛṣṇa. Without understanding Kṛṣṇa from the authority of a pure devotee, one should not try to comment upon Bhagavad-gītā .
