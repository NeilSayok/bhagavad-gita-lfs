aparyāptaṃ tadasmākaṃ balaṃ bhīṣmābhirakṣitam .

Life Application:
Avoid overconfidence or self-doubt by evaluating your true readiness rather than making biased comparisons.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Our army, protected by Bhishma, is insufficient; whereas their army, protected by Bhima, is sufficient. Or, our army protected by Bhishma is unlimited whereas their army protected by Bhima is limited.

अपर्याप्तम् insufficient? तत् that? अस्माकम् ours? बलम् army? भीष्माभिरक्षितम् marshalled by Bhishma? पर्याप्तम् sufficient? तु while? इदम् this? एतेषाम् their? बलम् army? भीमाभिरक्षितम् marshalled by Bhima.Commentary The verse is differently interpreted by different commentators. Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
In the ancient Indian system of warfare, besides the commander-in-chief, there was a warrior who acted as the protector of the army. He required qualities like bravery, courage, and wisdom. In the armies of the Kauravas and Pandavas, Bhishma and Bhima were these protectors respectively.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anandagiri: The king (Duryodhana), in order to declare to his teacher yet another ground for his own freedom from fear, speaks the verse beginning 'aparyaptam.' Our army, numbering eleven akshauhinis and therefore vast, guarded on all sides by Bhishma -- of celebrated greatness and subtle intellect -- is 'paryapta,' capable of overpowering the enemy. Theirs, on the other hand, is small, numbering only seven akshauhinis, and is protected by Bhima of fickle mind and wanting in skill; hence it is 'aparyapta,' incapable of overcoming us. -- Or: this our army, headed by Bhishma, is 'aparyapta' -- immeasurable, unassailable, unshakeable -- while the Pandavas' army guarded by Bhima is 'paryapta,' limited, i.e. can be withstood. -- Or again (the reverse construction): that army of the Pandavas is 'aparyapta,' not sufficient against us; 'bhishmabhirakshitam' meaning that Bhishma is kept well-guarded for the purpose of repelling the enemy force; whereas this army of ours is 'paryapta,' able to overpower these Pandavas; 'bhimabhirakshitam' meaning that since Bhima is weak-hearted he is guarded to repel our force; therefore there is no cause whatever for fear on our part.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
That army of ours is aparyapta -- inadequate, incapable of conquering the Pandavas -- because its protector Bhishma favours both sides. But this army of the Pandavas is paryapta -- adequate, capable of conquering us -- because its protector is Bhimasena.

Commentary -- 'aparyaptam tadasmakam balam bhishmabhirakshitam': because of the fear born in Duryodhana's mind from adharma and injustice, he reflects about his own army thus -- though our army is larger, that is, four akshauhinis more than the Pandavas', it is yet incapable of gaining victory over them; for there is disunity in our ranks. It does not possess the unity (organization), fearlessness and unhesitating resolve that the Pandavas' army has. Our chief protector, Grandsire Bhishma, favours both sides -- within him there is regard for both the Kaurava and the Pandava armies. He is a great devotee of Krishna. In his heart he holds Yudhishthira in high esteem, and he has deep affection for Arjuna too. Therefore, though he stands on our side, inwardly he wishes well of the Pandavas. And it is this very Bhishma who is the commander of our army. In such a state, how can our army be a match for the Pandavas? It cannot.
'paryaptam tvidameteshaam balam bhimabhirakshitam': but this army of the Pandavas is capable of gaining victory over us. For there is no disunity in their ranks; rather, all are united of one mind. The protector of their army is the mighty Bhimasena, who has been defeating me since childhood. He alone has vowed to slay me together with my hundred brothers -- that is, he is bent upon our destruction. His body is hard as a thunderbolt. Though I once fed him poison, still he did not die. Such a Bhimasena is the protector of the Pandavas' army; therefore that army is truly capable and complete.
A doubt may arise here: for the protector of his own army Duryodhana named Bhishma, who is appointed to the post of commander; but for the protector of the Pandava army he named Bhima, who is not the commander. The resolution is that Duryodhana at this moment is not thinking about the commanders, but about the strength of the two armies -- which army's power is greater. From the very beginning the strength and prowess of Bhimasena has weighed heavily upon Duryodhana's mind. So for the protector of the Pandava army he names Bhima alone.
A special point -- On seeing the Kaurava army, Arjuna goes to no one but takes up his bow in hand (Gita 1.20); but on seeing the Pandava army, Duryodhana goes to Dronacharya and asks him to behold the well-arrayed army of the Pandavas. This proves that fear is seated in Duryodhana's heart. Even while inwardly afraid, he cunningly wishes to please Dronacharya and to incite him against the Pandavas -- because in Duryodhana's heart there is adharma, injustice and sin. It is a rule that a person who is unjust and sinful can never remain fearless and in peace. But within Arjuna there is dharma and justice; hence there is in Arjuna no cunning to serve his own ends, no fear, but enthusiasm and valour. That is why, filled with valour, he commands the Lord to inspect the armies: 'O Achyuta, station my chariot between the two armies' (1.21). The purport is that one who takes refuge in and reveres perishable wealth and possessions, and within whom there is adharma, injustice and ill-will, has no real strength; he is hollow within and can never be fearless. But one who abides in his own dharma and takes refuge in the Lord is never afraid; his strength is true, and he remains ever untroubled and fearless. Therefore seekers who desire their own welfare should wholly renounce adharma and injustice, and taking refuge in the Lord alone, perform their own dharma for the sake of pleasing the Lord. One should never resort to adharma by giving importance to material wealth and being ensnared by the lure of pleasures born of contact; for neither of these ever brings good to a person -- rather only harm.
Connection -- Now, to please Grandsire Bhishma, Duryodhana speaks to all the great warriors of his army.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Dhrtarastra said - Sanjaya said  Duryodhana, after viewing the forces of Pandavas protected by Bhima, and his own forces protected by Bhisma conveyed his views thus to Drona, his teacher, about the adeacy of Bhima's forces for conering the Kaurava forces and the inadeacy of his own forces for victory against the Pandava forces. He was grief-stricken within.

Observing his (Duryodhana's) despondecny, Bhisma, in order to cheer him, roared like a lion, and then blowing his conch, made his side sound their conchs and kettle-drums, which made an uproar as a sign of victory. Then, having heard that great tumult, Arjuna and Sri Krsna the Lord of all lords, who was acting as the charioteer of Arjuna, sitting in their great chariot which was powerful enough to coner the three worlds; blew their divine conchs Srimad Pancajanya and Devadatta. Then, both Yudhisthira and Bhima blew their respective conchs separately. That tumult rent asunder the hearts of your sons, led by Duryodhana. The sons of Dhrtarastra then thought, 'Our cause is almost lost now itself.' So said Sanjaya to Dhrtarastra who was longing for their victory.

Sanjaya said to Dhrtarastra:  Then, seeing the Kauravas, who were ready for battle, Arjuna, who had Hanuman, noted for his exploit of burning Lanka, as the emblem on his flag on his chariot, directed his charioteer Sri Krsna, the Supreme Lord-who is overcome by parental love for those who take shelter in Him who is the treasure-house of knowledge, power, lordship, energy, potency and splendour, whose sportive delight brings about the origin, sustentation and dissolution of the entire cosmos at His will, who is the Lord of the senses, who controls in all ways the senses inner and outer of all, superior and inferior - by saying, 'Station my chariot in an appropriate place in order that I may see exactly my enemies who are eager for battle.'

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Aparyaptam etc. For us, for Pandava army grauded by Bhimasena is unlimited, i.e., it is not possible to vanish; or it is not sufficient, i.e., very insignificant when compared with our army. This is the meaning. On the other hand, for these Pandavas, this army guarded by Bhisma, belonging to us, is limited i.e., it is possible to vanish; or it is sufficient i.e., too much; in other words it is not possible to be vanished in the war by these (Pandavas).

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Duryodhana too, beholding the army of the Pandavas guarded by Vrikodara (Bhima) and others, and his own army guarded by Bhishma and others, and perceiving the adequacy of that (Pandava) force for his own defeat and the inadequacy of his own force for defeating it, after reporting to his teacher, became dejected within himself.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Madhusudana Sarasvati: The king, again suspecting parity between the two armies, declares the superiority of his own. 'aparyaptam' -- endless, immeasurable, numbering eleven akshauhinis, and guarded on all sides by Bhishma of renowned greatness and subtle intellect -- such is our army, presided over by so worthy a man. But the army of these Pandavas is 'paryapta' -- limited, being not more than a mere seven akshauhinis -- and is guarded by Bhima of exceedingly fickle mind. Therefore victory will be ours alone; this is the intent. -- Or (the reverse construction): that army of the Pandavas is 'aparyapta,' not enough for us; of what kind? 'bhishmo'bhirakshitah' -- Bhishma is well guarded by us for the purpose of repelling it; that Pandava army is thus 'guarded-by-Bhishma.' And this our army is 'paryapta,' capable of overpowering these Pandavas; for Bhima, exceedingly weak-hearted, is the one against whom (our army) is guarded -- hence 'guarded-with-reference-to-Bhima' -- since Bhima, being wholly unfit, is protected by them to repel (us); therefore there is no cause of fear for us. And if you are thus fearless, why then do you prattle so much? To this the word 'tu,' indicating the special thing yet to be done, is the reply.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sridhara: Then, to the question 'what of that?', he says 'aparyaptam.' That army of ours, though furnished with such heroes and guarded by Bhishma, appears aparyapta -- incapable of fighting against them. But this army of these Pandavas, guarded by Bhima, appears paryapta -- capable. This is because Bhishma favours both sides, and so our army is incapable against the Pandava host; whereas Bhima favours one side only.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Dhanapati: To state a further ground for his own superiority, he says 'aparyaptam.' That (enemy) army is aparyapta -- incapable of overpowering our army -- because our army is guarded by Bhishma of renowned greatness, an exceeding hero; whereas this army of ours is paryapta -- capable of overpowering theirs -- because the enemy's army is guarded by Bhima, a mere boy. -- Or: 'tat,' being beyond full perception, denotes our army which cannot be wholly comprehended and is guarded by Bhishma; hence aparyapta -- not to be compassed, overpowered or shaken; while this (visible) army of theirs is limited, guarded by Bhima, hence paryapta -- able to be compassed, overpowered, shaken and withstood. -- Or: 'tat' means 'therefore'; this army of ours is aparyapta, i.e. not gathered here and there from all sides but largely our own; whereas theirs is gathered from all sides, paryapta, and so will fight our army whole-heartedly; hence our strength; this is the intent. -- Indeed our army, numbering eleven akshauhinis and guarded by Bhishma, is paryapta, capable of overpowering the enemy; while theirs, small, of only seven akshauhinis, guarded by Bhima of fickle mind and wanting in skill, is aparyapta, incapable of overpowering us. -- Or again: this our army is aparyapta -- not small (i.e. ample) -- and presided over by Bhishma; while theirs is paryapta -- small -- and presided over by Bhima; therefore victory will be ours alone. -- Or: that army of the Pandavas is aparyapta, not enough for us, because their army is guarded by Bhishma, who is kept engaged for the purpose of repelling (them); then this army of ours is paryapta, capable of overpowering theirs, because the weak-hearted Bhima is guarded by them to repel our force; so say some. But the view that 'though furnished with such heroes and guarded by Bhishma our army appears aparyapta, unable to fight against them, while this Pandava army guarded by Bhima appears paryapta, capable, because Bhishma favours both sides' -- that is to be disregarded, being in conflict with the context; for on hearing such a respectful royal speech, what did Bhishma do (he cheered the king)? -- and it conflicts with the commentator's own text.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Venkatanatha explains: Sanjaya, wishing to lead up to Krishna's direct answer later (BG 18.78, "where there is Krishna..."), reports every intervening detail, starting with "having seen." Seeing the Pandava army arrayed caused Duryodhana's loss of courage; the word "tu" signals this changed state. The plain sense of verse 10 is: our army, protected by Bhishma, is insufficient (aparyaptam) to conquer the enemy, while this army of the Pandavas, protected by Bhima, is sufficient (paryaptam) to conquer ours. But this reading seems odd, since elsewhere Duryodhana repeatedly asserts confidence in his own army's strength (protected by Bhishma, Drona, etc.), including in the Mahabharata's Udyoga Parva (55.1) and at the start of the second day's battle, where he uses nearly identical words but clearly means "our army is unlimited, theirs is limited." To resolve the contradiction, Venkatanatha offers four possible reconciliations: (1) a variant reading swapping Bhima/Bhishma; (2) a discontinuous construal reordering the phrases so the sense becomes "that (Pandava) army protected by Bhima is insufficient (to defeat us)"; (3) splitting the verse into two independent statements — "that is insufficient," "this is sufficient" — with the Bhishma/Bhima clauses supplying the reason (our side has a mighty protector, theirs a weaker one); (4) taking "paryaptam/aparyaptam" to mean "limited/unlimited" in number (our eleven akshauhinis versus their seven), so Duryodhana never doubts his own strength at all. Venkatanatha then defends why Bhima specifically is named opposite Bhishma: not because Bhima is the Pandava commander (that is Dhrishtadyumna), nor because he equals Bhishma in valor (the two are famously unequal — Bhishma himself said he alone could slay all the Pandavas but for divine protection), but because Bhima has vowed to kill all the sons of Dhritarashtra and possesses the reckless daring suited to that vow — hence he is the special object of Kaurava dread, just as Bhishma is named as the singular protector of all the Pandavas.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Purushottama: Having thus recounted all (the warriors), Duryodhana says that even this army of ours, though so guarded, appears to me incapable of fighting that army: hence 'aparyaptam.' Though guarded by Bhishma, our army appears aparyapta -- unable to fight against them. He says 'guarded by Bhishma' (and not by Drona) because Drona might at some time grow angry. And the Pandavas' army appears capable of fighting us: hence 'paryaptam.' This army of these Pandavas, guarded on every side by Bhima, appears paryapta -- capable. By the word 'tu' the alternative (that it is aparyapta) is set aside. -- Or: that renowned army of ours is 'aparyapta' -- exceedingly great; and moreover it is guarded on all sides by Bhishma. But their army, though abounding in heroes, is 'paryapta' -- limited, being only seven akshauhinis; and moreover it is guarded by Bhima.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Neelakantha: 'paryaptam' means 'encompassed on all sides, surrounded.' The Pandava army, being measured at only seven akshauhinis, is small, and can be surrounded by our larger army of eleven akshauhinis -- but ours cannot be surrounded by theirs; this is the meaning. And thus 'paryaptam' here bears the sense of 'able to be encompassed.'

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Our strength is immeasurable, and we are perfectly protected by Grandfather Bhīṣma, whereas the strength of the Pāṇḍavas, carefully protected by Bhīma, is limited.

 Herein an estimation of comparative strength is made by Duryodhana. He thinks that the strength of his armed forces is immeasurable, being specifically protected by the most experienced general, Grandfather Bhīṣma. On the other hand, the forces of the Pāṇḍavas are limited, being protected by a less experienced general, Bhīma, who is like a fig in the presence of Bhīṣma. Duryodhana was always envious of Bhīma because he knew perfectly well that if he should die at all, he would only be killed by Bhīma. But at the same time, he was confident of his victory on account of the presence of Bhīṣma, who was a far superior general. His conclusion that he would come out of the battle victorious was well ascertained.
