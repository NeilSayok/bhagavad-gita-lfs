Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yajante sāttvikā devānyakṣarakṣāṃsi rājasāḥ .
pretānbhūtagaṇāṃścānye yajante tāmasā janāḥ

Life Application:
Sattvic people worship celestial beings; Rajasic worship demigods and demons; Tamasic worship spirits and ghosts.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Sattvic men worship the gods; Rajasic men worship the Yakshas (demigods/nature spirits) and Rakshasas (demons); and other Tamasic people worship the Pretas (spirits of the dead) and the hosts of Bhutas (ghosts).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The Sattvic or the pure men worship the gods; the Rajasic or the passionate worship the Yakshas and the Rakshasas; the others (the Tamasic or the deluded people) worship the ghosts and the hosts of the nature-spirits.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The Pure worship the true God; the Passionate, the powers of wealth and magic; the Ignorant, the spirits of the dead and of the lower orders of nature.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Every person dedicates their devotion to some altar or ideal in life to fulfill their desires, which is scripturally called worship. This extends beyond formal rituals to anything a person reveres, such as wealth, fame, or power. A person's object of worship is determined by their faith. Sattvic people naturally worship gods representing noble and divine ideals due to their pure impressions. Rajasic people, being highly ambitious and active, worship Yakshas (keepers of wealth) and Rakshasas (forces of power and ambition). Tamasic people worship spirits (Pretas) and ghosts (Bhutas) to satisfy their lower-level desires and negative urges. By observing a person's field of action and object of worship, their inner quality (Sattva, Rajas, or Tamas) can be inferred.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
The men of the Sattva (Strand) perform sacrifice intending for the gods ;  the men of the Rajas (Strand) do for the spirits and the demons;  and the men of the Tamas (Strand)  perform sacrifices intending for the imps, the dead, and the ghosts.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
The Sattvika types worship the gods. The Rajasa types worship Yaksas and Raksasas. And the others, the Tamasa types, worship the departed ancestors and hosts of Bhutas.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Those having the sattva ality worship the gods; those having rajas, the demi-gods and ogres; and other people possessed of tamas worship ghosts and the hosts of spirits.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya explains that the Lord classifies who possesses which type of faith. The Sattvic worship the gods, the Rajasic worship Yakshas and Rakshasas, and the Tamasic worship Pretas and Bhutas.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that one can infer a person's Guna by their choice of worship: gods represent the Sattvic class; Yakshas (lords of wealth like Kubera) and Rakshasas represent Rajasic; and Pretas (souls of deceased who fell from their duties) and Bhutas represent Tamasic. Each of these classes grants desires corresponding to their respective nature.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Sattvic men worship the gods; Rajasic men worship the Yakshas and Rakshasas; and others, who are Tamasic, worship the Pretas and the hosts of Bhutas.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that Sattvic people worship the gods (specifically the five deities representing divine power: Vishnu, Shiva, Ganesha, Shakti, and Surya) out of duty and without selfish desire. Rajasic people worship Yakshas (associated with wealth collection) and Rakshasas (associated with power and destruction) to satisfy their material desires or harm others. Tamasic people worship Pretas (the departed spirits) and Bhutas (ghosts). Ramsukhdas notes that honoring deceased ancestors (Pitri-shraddha) as a scriptural duty without desire is Sattvic, not Tamasic. The worship of entities determines the state and conviction of the worshiper.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
'Those who have abundance of Sattva ality and are conjoined with Sattvika faith worship the gods. The meaning is this:  The faith in the worship (sacrifice) of the gods which causes supreme joy unmixed with pain is of Sattvika nature. The Rajasika types worship Yaksas and Raksasas. And the others, i.e., the Tamasika types, worship the departed ancestors and hosts of Bhutas. The faith born of Rajas brings about limited joy mixed with pain, while the faith born of Tamas gives rise to extremely limited joy which verges almost on pain.

Therefore, there is difference in fruits according to the Gunas regarding sacrifices etc., which are enjoined in the Sastras and associated with faith. However, no happiness whatsoever will result from penances, sacrifices etc., not enjoined in the Sastras and therefore antagonistic to My ?ndment. On the contrary, calamity results from them. Sri Krsna proceeds to explain this more fully.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta explains that these verses describe the demonic determination. Those who perform self-willed penances not ordained by the scriptures are senseless (because they lack discrimination) and torment both the body and the indwelling Lord. Hence, they are Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains that those of Sattvic nature worship authentic Sattvic deities. The same applies to the Rajasic and Tamasic, as noted in the Bhagavata Purana: those of Rajasic/Tamasic natures worship entities of similar dispositions. This distinction defines their worship.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that one can infer a person's faith from the objects of their worship. Those who lack scriptural wisdom but worship Sattvic deities like Rudra are Sattvic; those who worship Rajasic deities like Kubera (Yaksha) or Nairrita (Rakshasa) are Rajasic; and those who worship Tamasic entities like Pretas (spirits of deceased who fell from their dharma) and Bhutas (such as the Seven Mothers) are Tamasic.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that the classification of Sattvic, Rajasic, and Tamasic is demonstrated through the actions of the people. Sattvic people worship deities of Sattvic nature. Rajasic people worship Yakshas and Rakshasas of Rajasic nature. Others, who are Tamasic, worship Pretas and Bhutas. Therefore, a person's inner quality can be understood by their inclination toward these respective objects of worship.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that those of Sattvic faith worship the gods; the Rajasic worship Yakshas and Rakshasas; and the Tamasic worship Pretas and Bhutas. The choice of worship is the visible indicator of their hidden qualities.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the Lord classifies the objects of worship to make it easy to infer a person's faith. The Sattvic worship gods, the Rajasic worship Yakshas and Rakshasas, and the Tamasic worship Pretas and Bhutas. Since the Gunas of the deities are well-known, the type of faith can be determined by seeing who a person worships.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that Sattvic people worship deities like Surya and Indra. Rajasic people worship Yakshas and Rakshasas who guard wealth. Others, who are Tamasic (devoid of Sattva connection), worship Pretas and Bhutas. One's inclination toward these indicates their respective nature.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that this verse shows how the Sattvic, Rajasic, and Tamasic classes are determined based on their objects of worship.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Men in the mode of goodness worship the demigods; those in the mode of passion worship the demons; and those in the mode of ignorance worship ghosts and spirits.

 In this verse the Supreme Personality of Godhead describes different kinds of worshipers according to their external activities. According to scriptural injunction, only the Supreme Personality of Godhead is worshipable, but those who are not very conversant with, or faithful to, the scriptural injunctions worship different objects, according to their specific situations in the modes of material nature. Those who are situated in goodness generally worship the demigods. The demigods include Brahmā, Śiva and others such as Indra, Candra and the sun-god. There are various demigods. Those in goodness worship a particular demigod for a particular purpose. Similarly, those who are in the mode of passion worship the demons. We recall that during the Second World War a man in Calcutta worshiped Hitler because thanks to that war he had amassed a large amount of wealth by dealing in the black market. Similarly, those in the modes of passion and ignorance generally select a powerful man to be God. They think that anyone can be worshiped as God and that the same results will be obtained. Now, it is clearly described here that those who are in the mode of passion worship and create such gods, and those who are in the mode of ignorance, in darkness, worship dead spirits. Sometimes people worship at the tomb of some dead man. Sexual service is also considered to be in the mode of darkness. Similarly, in remote villages in India there are worshipers of ghosts. We have seen that in India the lower-class people sometimes go to the forest, and if they have knowledge that a ghost lives in a tree, they worship that tree and offer sacrifices. These different kinds of worship are not actually God worship. God worship is for persons who are transcendentally situated in pure goodness. In the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (4.3.23) it is said, sattvaṁ viśuddhaṁ vasudeva-śabditam: “When a man is situated in pure goodness, he worships Vāsudeva.” The purport is that those who are completely purified of the material modes of nature and who are transcendentally situated can worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The impersonalists are supposed to be situated in the mode of goodness, and they worship five kinds of demigods. They worship the impersonal Viṣṇu form in the material world, which is known as philosophized Viṣṇu. Viṣṇu is the expansion of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, but the impersonalists, because they do not ultimately believe in the Supreme Personality of Godhead, imagine that the Viṣṇu form is just another aspect of the impersonal Brahman; similarly, they imagine that Lord Brahmā is the impersonal form in the material mode of passion. Thus they sometimes describe five kinds of gods that are worshipable, but because they think that the actual truth is impersonal Brahman, they dispose of all worshipable objects at the ultimate end. In conclusion, the different qualities of the material modes of nature can be purified through association with persons who are of transcendental nature.
