Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
tatraikāgraṃ manaḥ kṛtvā yatacittendriyakriyaḥ .
upaviśyāsane yuñjyādyogamātmaviśuddhaye

Word Meanings:
तत्रैकाग्रं - there one-pointed
मनः - mind
कृत्वा - having done
यतचित्तेन्द्रियक्रियः - controlling thoughts and senses
उपविश्यासने - sitting on the seat
युञ्ज्याद्योगमात्मविशुद्धये - should practice yoga for self-purification

Life Application:
Sit quietly with a single-pointed focus, curbing your thoughts and senses to purify your inner awareness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
There, having made the mind one-pointed, with the actions of the mind and the senses controlled, let him, seated on the seat, practice yoga for the purification of the self.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Seated on that seat, making the mind one-pointed and mastering the actions of mind and senses, let him practice Yoga for the purification of the self.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "For self-purification"—concentrating the mind in direct meditation on the supreme holy form of Lord Vishnu is the true means to inner purification.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Restraining sense and mental activities upon the seat, the yogi renders the mind one-pointed to achieve total inner purification.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Seated there, withdrawing the mind from other objects, make it single-pointed on the Self to achieve pure Self-realization.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Remaining in solitude, alone, with body and mind controlled, free from desire and possessions, let the Yogi constantly engage his mind in meditation. In a clean spot, establishing a firm seat neither too high nor too low, covered with Kusa grass, a deer skin, and a cloth, making the mind one-pointed and mastering thought and sense, let him practice Yoga for self-purification.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Seated on that seat, making the mind one-pointed and mastering the actions of mind and senses, let him practice Yoga for self-purification"—highlights one-pointed concentration (Ekagrata) and mental/sensory restraint as prerequisites for inner purification through Dhyana.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: One-pointed concentration means shaping the mind into the divine form of God; controlling senses secures unhindered meditation.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Focusing the mind in constant remembrance of Krishna"s name and form is the primary means of purifying the soul.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Practice Yoga for Self-purification"—through Dharana and Dhyana, the filth of ignorance is washed away, yielding direct unbroken Self-realization.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
