Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Arjuna heard that through unalloyed devotion, any devotee can not only recognize the Lord's cosmic glory, but can also experience it directly within himself. Then, on the face of the Pandava prince, eagerness to attain that experience or state became visible. Although he did not ask a clear question, Lord Krishna, understanding it from the expression on his face, describes here how a seeker can attain this perfection in life.

There are five parts to the scheme taught by Krishna for a soul to attain Godhood. Those five parts or essential qualities are described in this verse. Those qualities are: (1) he who performs action with a spirit of dedication to God, (2) he whose supreme goal is God alone, (3) he who is a devotee of God, (4) he who is free from attachments, and (5) he who is free from enmity towards all beings.

In these five essential qualities, the summary of the entire practice of self-control is given. Through the unbroken remembrance of God, the attitude of non-attachment to the actions of all conditioning is strengthened. Enmity towards anyone occurs only when we consider them as separate. There can be no enmity between my own two hands. Only when unity is seen and experienced everywhere through the realization of the oneness of the Self, can complete freedom from enmity towards all beings be attained.

It is completely impossible to be totally non-attached at the levels of mind and intellect. The mind and intellect cannot exist without attachment to some object or person. Therefore, a seeker first learns to renounce attachment to sense objects through the spirit of dedication to God, and thereafter establishes his mind in God with devotion. To perfect this aspect, the previously mentioned qualities are certainly helpful.

Thus, looking back at the entire scheme, it will be found to be completely acceptable because it is thoroughly psychological. Each succeeding part is nourished by its preceding part. It is also clearly known from this verse that the great sacred pilgrimage of a spiritual seeker begins with performing action with a spirit of dedication to God. Thereafter, God Himself becomes the supreme goal of his life. The result of this will be supreme love for God. Naturally, his attachment to the temporary, finite objects of the world will end, and he will be able to perceive the Self. When, becoming the Self itself, he recognizes himself everywhere, in all beings, then he will have no enmity of any kind towards any creature.

According to the Gita, the test of the perfection of self-realization attained through spiritual practice is: love for all and hatred towards none.

Conclusion: 'Tat Sat' - thus in the Upanishads of the glorious Bhagavad Gita, the science of the Eternal, the scripture of Yoga, the dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna, ends the eleventh discourse entitled the 'Yoga of the Vision of the Cosmic Form'.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter, named 'Yoga of the Vision of the Cosmic Form', of the Upanishad of the Bhagavad Gita, which is in the form of a dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna, representing Brahma-vidya and Yoga-shastra.

The name of this chapter, 'Yoga of the Vision of the Cosmic Form', is meaningful. According to the technical terminology of Vedanta Shastra, the real meaning of the word 'Vishvarupa' used here is 'Virat-rupa'. The Self, identifying with an individual gross body, experiences the events of the waking state. The Self situated in this state is called 'Vishwa' in Vedanta. The same Self, identifying with the cosmic gross body, i.e., the universe, is called 'Virat'. Although the Lord has shown His Virat form here, still this chapter is named 'Vishvarupa-darshana-yoga'. This indicates the absolute oneness of Vishwa and Virat.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Raising the doubt that other means are not causes due to the qualification 'but by devotion' (bhaktyā tu), he says 'adhunā' (now) etc., which means collecting, summarizing, or bringing together. If 'mat-karma-kṛt' (working for Me) is said, 'mat-paramatva' (considering Me as supreme) is implied; to address the doubt of redundancy, he says 'karoti' (performs) etc. For one who is convinced that the Lord alone is the supreme goal, devotion to Him alone is established; to state this, he says 'tathā' (and so) etc. Suspecting that worship in all ways is not possible there due to being attracted by attachment to wealth etc., he says 'saṅga' (attachment) etc. Enmity consists in performing undesirable actions out of hatred, which occurs even towards those who have done no harm, and certainly occurs towards wrongdoers; raising this doubt, he says 'ātmanaḥ' (of oneself) etc. All this has been stated in brief for the sake of practice; the attainment of the Lord is inevitable for one who practices thus; concluding this, he says 'ayam' (this) etc. Thus, by explaining the meditation of the all-pervading, omniscient, and omnipresent Lord of the Cosmic Form, which bears the fruit of gradual liberation in accordance with the rule of 'mat-karma-kṛt' etc., the meaning of the word 'Tat' is established.

Thus ends the commentary on the eleventh chapter by Anandagiri, the disciple of the revered Suddhānanda, who was a paramahaṁsa parivrājakācārya.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Pandava! He who performs actions for My sake alone, remains devoted to Me, is My devotee, is completely free from attachment, and is free from enmity towards all living beings, that devotee attains Me.

Commentary
[Five points are mentioned in this verse. These five are also called 'Sadhana Panchaka'. These five points are divided into two categories: (1) Intimacy with the Lord and (2) Disconnection of relationship with the world. In the first category, there are three points - 'mat-karma-krt', 'mat-paramah', and 'mad-bhaktah'; and in the second category, there are two points - 'sanga-varjitah' and 'nirvairah sarva-bhutesu'.

'mat-karma-krt'—He who performs actions related to the Lord such as chanting, singing, meditation, association with saints, study of scriptures, etc., and also performs worldly duties obtained according to his varna, ashrama, country, time, circumstance, etc., only for My sake, i.e., for My pleasure alone, is 'mat-karma-krt'. Actually speaking, actions have two external forms—spiritual and worldly—but internally, the single attitude remains that all actions are to be done only for the Lord, with a single purpose. The meaning is that whatever actions the devotee performs through the body, senses, mind, and intellect, he does all of them only for the Lord. Because whatever he has, like body, mind, intellect, senses, ability, capacity to act, understanding, etc., all of it is given by the Lord and belongs to the Lord, and he himself also belongs to the Lord. He works only as an instrument for the pleasure of the Lord, according to the Lord's command, with the power given by the Lord. This is his being 'mat-karma-krt'.

'mat-paramah'—He who, considering Me alone to be the supreme, remains devoted to Me alone, i.e., whose supreme goal, supreme object of meditation, and supreme refuge is Me alone, such a devotee is 'mat-paramah'.

'mad-bhaktah'—He who is My devotee alone, i.e., who has established an unwavering relationship with Me, thinking 'I belong only to the Lord and only the Lord belongs to me, and I do not belong to anyone else nor does anyone else belong to me'. By being so, intense love for the Lord is awakened, because that which is one's own is naturally dear. In the awakening of love, the feeling of oneness (belonging) is primary. That devotee always and everywhere sees the Lord fully present in all countries, times, objects, beings, and in himself. From this perspective, since the Lord is in all countries, He is here too; since He is in all times, He is now too; since He is in all objects and beings, He is in me too; and since He belongs to everyone, He is mine too—he who holds such a feeling is 'mad-bhaktah'.

'sanga-varjitah nirvairah sarva-bhutesu yah'—By performing actions only for the Lord, being devoted only to the Lord, and becoming a devotee of the Lord alone, what happens is described by these words: he becomes 'sanga-varjitah', i.e., he has no attachment, sense of possession, or desire in the world. Attachment, possession, and desire are the causes of relationship with the world. As soon as exclusive love for the Lord arises, there is a complete absence of attachment etc.

Secondly, when the devotee realizes the reality that 'I am indeed a part of the Lord', then love for the Lord is awakened in him. When love is awakened, attraction (raga) completely disappears. Due to the complete absence of attraction and the presence of the feeling of the Lord everywhere, even if someone behaves very badly with him, beats him, or harms him, not the slightest enmity arises in his heart towards that wrongdoer. He accepts it as the Lord's will and grace. The Lord has called such a devotee 'nirvairah sarva-bhutesu'.

'sanga-varjitah' and 'nirvairah sarva-bhutesu'—The purpose of describing both of these is to show that his relationship with the world is completely severed. On the severance of the relationship with the world, the self-established Supreme Lord is realized.

'sa mam eti'—Such a devotee of Mine attains Me alone. In 'sa mam eti', knowing in essence, seeing, and attaining—all three points are included, which were described in the preceding (54th) verse. The meaning is that the purpose for which human birth took place is completely fulfilled.

Special Point
At the end of the ninth chapter, the Lord had said—'man-mana bhava mad-bhakto mad-yaji mam namaskuru. mam evaisyasi yuktvaivam atmanam mat-parayanah.' (9.34). Even after saying this, it remained in the Lord's mind how to explain the secret of His teachings, in what manner. To explain this, the Lord spoke the tenth and eleventh chapters. The jiva has taken refuge in the temporary, destructible, and ever-changing nature and its product, the body-world, due to which he has turned away from the indestructible and ever-unchanging Lord. The purpose of both these chapters—tenth and eleventh—is to remove this aversion and turn the jiva towards the Lord.

This human being has two powers—the power of contemplation and the power of sight. Of these, the power of contemplation should be applied to the glories (vibhutis) of the Lord. The meaning is that whatever excellence, greatness, uniqueness, or divinity is seen in any object, person, etc., and if the mind goes there, considering that excellence etc. as belonging to the Lord, one should contemplate only on the Lord. For this, the Lord spoke the tenth chapter.

Second, the power of sight should be applied to the Lord. The meaning is that just as in the divine, indestructible universal form of the Lord there are many forms, many shapes, many kinds of sights, similarly, this world is also a part of that universal form, and in this, the Supreme Lord alone is full in the form of many names, forms, shapes, etc. From this perspective, one should see everything as the form of the Supreme Lord. For this, the Lord spoke the eleventh chapter.

Arjuna also prayed twice for these two visions. In the seventeenth verse of the tenth chapter, Arjuna said, 'O Lord! In what various aspects should I contemplate upon You?' Then, to engage the power of contemplation, the Lord described His glories. At the beginning of the eleventh chapter, Arjuna said, 'I wish to see Your form,' so the Lord showed His universal form and gave Arjuna divine eyes to see it. The meaning is that a seeker should not waste his power of contemplation and sight anywhere other than on the Lord, i.e., if a seeker contemplates, he should contemplate only on the Supreme Lord, and whoever he sees, he should see them only as the form of the Supreme Lord.

Thus, with the pronunciation of the divine names 'Tat' and 'Sat', ends the eleventh chapter entitled 'Vishvarupa-darshana-yoga' in the Upanishad of the glorious Bhagavad Gita, the science of the Eternal and the scripture of Yoga, in the form of a dialogue between Sri Krishna and Arjuna.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Whosoever performs all acts like the study of the Vedas described above, considering them as several modes of worship, 'he works for Me.' For all these reasons he has no hatred for any one.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
In order to reconcile the contradiction between statements like 'I cannot be attained by the Vedas' and Upanishadic texts like 'by the recitation of Vedas' [Brhadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.22], the utility of Vedic recitation etc. as auxiliary parts of devotion is stated. Explaining the means of entry mentioned as 'praveṣṭum', the specific state of devotion that is the cause of attainment is distinguished: 'mat-karma-kṛt' etc. The actions mentioned in 'I cannot be attained by the Vedas' etc., when surrendered to the Lord, are called 'mat-karma' (My work); whereas chanting etc. having been mentioned earlier, are fit to be included in 'mad-bhakta' (My devotee) as they belong to devotion. With this intention, he says: 'study of the Vedas' etc. Due to the context of actions, the fruit that comes to mind as their effect is denoted here by the word 'mat-paramaḥ', hence he says: 'of all' etc. In place of food and drink for worldly people, chanting etc. are the means of sustenance for this devotee; with this intention, he says: 'unable to sustain his self' etc. The state of non-attachment in the highest stage of devotion is shown along with its cause: 'due to Me alone being dear' etc. Just as a thirsty person, regarding a straw obstructing a stream of nectar as a hindrance, rejects it, similarly he himself gets agitated by worldly attachment; with this intention, he says: 'unable to bear other attachments'. In such a peak stage of devotion, non-enmity is not merely due to obedience to scriptures, but due to the absence of the cause of enmity, there is an absence of its effect, hence he says: 'union and separation' etc. For a person who is attached to the Supreme Lord and detached from other things, being indifferent to worldly trivial pleasures and pains, enmity cannot arise towards those who cause or remove them. Moreover, for one who knows his own sins, another person cannot be the object of hatred (as he attributes his suffering to his own past deeds, not to others). Just as no sane person gets angry at a stick (but at the person wielding it), how can there be anger towards others who are understood to be dependent on the Lord? When one contemplates the presence of the Cosmic Lord in all other beings just like in oneself, how can there be any room for enmity? This is the meaning.

'Evambhūtaḥ' means of this nature. Concluding with reference to the earlier statement 'praveṣṭum' (to enter) [11.54], he says: 'Me as I am' etc. To refute the wrong interpretations of words like entry, attainment, etc., imagined by faulty doctrines (such as absolute destruction of the self), he clarifies the nature of attainment which is supreme liberation: 'with the destruction' etc. 'Destruction' (nirasta) means destroyed in such a way that it never sprouts again. 'Avidyā' refers to ignorance, erroneous knowledge, and their cause, karma. Since even in prior stages there is some cessation of ignorance, the word 'aśeṣa' (entire) is used to denote complete cessation. The word 'gandha' (smell/trace) is used to indicate the removal along with its latent impressions (vasanas). 'Madekānubhavaḥ' means that in accordance with the text 'the seer sees everything' [Chandogya Upanishad 7.26.2], the entire universe is the form of Me, who is the self of all. And another Shruti says: 'Those wise men, having attained the all-pervading Lord from all sides, with their minds absorbed, enter into everything' [Mundaka Upanishad 3.2.5] - this is the idea.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter in Tatparya-Chandrika, the commentary on Sri Gita-Bhashya, written by Sri Venkatanatha Vedantacharya, the lion among poets and logicians, and master of all systems of thought.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
My dear Arjuna, he who engages in My pure devotional service, free from the contaminations of fruitive activities and mental speculation, he who works for Me, who makes Me the supreme goal of his life, and who is friendly to every living being – he certainly comes to Me.

 Anyone who wants to approach the supreme of all the Personalities of Godhead, on the Kṛṣṇaloka planet in the spiritual sky, and be intimately connected with the Supreme Personality, Kṛṣṇa, must take this formula, as stated by the Supreme Himself. Therefore, this verse is considered to be the essence of Bhagavad-gītā . The Bhagavad-gītā is a book directed to the conditioned souls, who are engaged in the material world with the purpose of lording it over nature and who do not know of the real, spiritual life. The Bhagavad-gītā is meant to show how one can understand his spiritual existence and his eternal relationship with the supreme spiritual personality and to teach one how to go back home, back to Godhead. Now here is the verse which clearly explains the process by which one can attain success in his spiritual activity: devotional service. As far as work is concerned, one should transfer his energy entirely to Kṛṣṇa conscious activities. As stated in the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (1.2.255), anāsaktasya viṣayān yathārham upayuñjataḥ nirbandhaḥ kṛṣṇa-sambandhe yuktaṁ vairāgyam ucyate No work should be done by any man except in relationship to Kṛṣṇa. This is called kṛṣṇa-karma. One may be engaged in various activities, but one should not be attached to the result of his work; the result should be done only for Him. For example, one may be engaged in business, but to transform that activity into Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one has to do business for Kṛṣṇa. If Kṛṣṇa is the proprietor of the business, then Kṛṣṇa should enjoy the profit of the business. If a businessman is in possession of thousands and thousands of dollars, and if he has to offer all this to Kṛṣṇa, he can do it. This is work for Kṛṣṇa. Instead of constructing a big building for his sense gratification, he can construct a nice temple for Kṛṣṇa, and he can install the Deity of Kṛṣṇa and arrange for the Deity’s service, as is outlined in the authorized books of devotional service. This is all kṛṣṇa-karma. One should not be attached to the result of his work, but the result should be offered to Kṛṣṇa, and one should accept as prasādam the remnants of offerings to Kṛṣṇa. If one constructs a very big building for Kṛṣṇa and installs the Deity of Kṛṣṇa, one is not prohibited from living there, but it is understood that the proprietor of the building is Kṛṣṇa. That is called Kṛṣṇa consciousness. If, however, one is not able to construct a temple for Kṛṣṇa, one can engage himself in cleansing the temple of Kṛṣṇa; that is also kṛṣṇa-karma. One can cultivate a garden. Anyone who has land – in India, at least, any poor man has a certain amount of land – can utilize that for Kṛṣṇa by growing flowers to offer Him. One can sow tulasī plants, because tulasī leaves are very important and Kṛṣṇa has recommended this in Bhagavad-gītā . Patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyam. Kṛṣṇa desires that one offer Him either a leaf, or a flower, or fruit, or a little water – and by such an offering He is satisfied. This leaf especially refers to the tulasī. So one can sow tulasī and pour water on the plant. Thus, even the poorest man can engage in the service of Kṛṣṇa. These are some of the examples of how one can engage in working for Kṛṣṇa. The word mat-paramaḥ refers to one who considers the association of Kṛṣṇa in His supreme abode to be the highest perfection of life. Such a person does not wish to be elevated to the higher planets such as the moon or sun or heavenly planets, or even the highest planet of this universe, Brahmaloka. He has no attraction for that. He is only attracted to being transferred to the spiritual sky. And even in the spiritual sky he is not satisfied with merging into the glowing brahma-jyotir effulgence, for he wants to enter the highest spiritual planet, namely Kṛṣṇaloka, Goloka Vṛndāvana. He has full knowledge of that planet, and therefore he is not interested in any other. As indicated by the word mad-bhaktaḥ, he fully engages in devotional service, specifically in the nine processes of devotional engagement: hearing, chanting, remembering, worshiping, serving the lotus feet of the Lord, offering prayers, carrying out the orders of the Lord, making friends with Him, and surrendering everything to Him. One can engage in all nine devotional processes, or eight, or seven, or at least in one, and that will surely make one perfect. The term saṅga-varjitaḥ is very significant. One should disassociate himself from persons who are against Kṛṣṇa. Not only are the atheistic persons against Kṛṣṇa, but so also are those who are attracted to fruitive activities and mental speculation. Therefore the pure form of devotional service is described in Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (1.1.11) as follows: anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyaṁ jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam ānukūlyena kṛṣṇānu- śīlanaṁ bhaktir uttamā In this verse Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī clearly states that if anyone wants to execute unalloyed devotional service, he must be freed from all kinds of material contamination. He must be freed from the association of persons who are addicted to fruitive activities and mental speculation. When, freed from such unwanted association and from the contamination of material desires, one favorably cultivates knowledge of Kṛṣṇa, that is called pure devotional service. Ānukūlyasya saṅkalpaḥ prātikūlyasya varjanam ( Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 11.676). One should think of Kṛṣṇa and act for Kṛṣṇa favorably, not unfavorably. Kaṁsa was an enemy of Kṛṣṇa’s. From the very beginning of Kṛṣṇa’s birth, Kaṁsa planned in so many ways to kill Him, and because he was always unsuccessful, he was always thinking of Kṛṣṇa. Thus while working, while eating and while sleeping, he was always Kṛṣṇa conscious in every respect, but that Kṛṣṇa consciousness was not favorable, and therefore in spite of his always thinking of Kṛṣṇa twenty-four hours a day, he was considered a demon, and Kṛṣṇa at last killed him. Of course anyone who is killed by Kṛṣṇa attains salvation immediately, but that is not the aim of the pure devotee. The pure devotee does not even want salvation. He does not want to be transferred even to the highest planet, Goloka Vṛndāvana. His only objective is to serve Kṛṣṇa wherever he may be. A devotee of Kṛṣṇa is friendly to everyone. Therefore it is said here that he has no enemy ( nirvairaḥ ). How is this? A devotee situated in Kṛṣṇa consciousness knows that only devotional service to Kṛṣṇa can relieve a person from all the problems of life. He has personal experience of this, and therefore he wants to introduce this system, Kṛṣṇa consciousness, into human society. There are many examples in history of devotees of the Lord who risked their lives for the spreading of God consciousness. The favorite example is Lord Jesus Christ. He was crucified by the nondevotees, but he sacrificed his life for spreading God consciousness. Of course, it would be superficial to understand that he was killed. Similarly, in India also there are many examples, such as Ṭhākura Haridāsa and Prahlāda Mahārāja. Why such risk? Because they wanted to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and it is difficult. A Kṛṣṇa conscious person knows that if a man is suffering it is due to his forgetfulness of his eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, the highest benefit one can render to human society is relieving one’s neighbor from all material problems. In such a way, a pure devotee is engaged in the service of the Lord. Now, we can imagine how merciful Kṛṣṇa is to those engaged in His service, risking everything for Him. Therefore it is certain that such persons must reach the supreme planet after leaving the body. In summary, the universal form of Kṛṣṇa, which is a temporary manifestation, and the form of time which devours everything, and even the form of Viṣṇu, four-handed, have all been exhibited by Kṛṣṇa. Thus Kṛṣṇa is the origin of all these manifestations. It is not that Kṛṣṇa is a manifestation of the original viśva-rūpa, or Viṣṇu. Kṛṣṇa is the origin of all forms. There are hundreds and thousands of Viṣṇus, but for a devotee no form of Kṛṣṇa is important but the original form, two-handed Śyāmasundara. In the Brahma-saṁhitā it is stated that those who are attached to the Śyāmasundara form of Kṛṣṇa in love and devotion can see Him always within the heart and cannot see anything else. One should understand, therefore, that the purport of this Eleventh Chapter is that the form of Kṛṣṇa is essential and supreme. Thus end the Bhaktivedanta s to the Eleventh Chapter of the Śrīmad Bhagavad-gītā in the matter of the Universal Form.
