Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
saṃnyāsastu mahābāho duḥkhamāptumayogataḥ .
yogayukto munirbrahma nacireṇādhigacchati

Word Meanings:
संन्यासस्तु - renunciation, however
महाबाहो - O mighty-armed one
दुःखमाप्तुमयोगतः - hard to attain without yoga
योगयुक्तो - engaged in yoga
मुनिर्ब्रह्म - the sage to Brahman
नचिरेणाधिगच्छति - quickly attains

Life Application:
Engage in unselfish action first to purify your mind before attempting quiet, solitary contemplative practices.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni (sage) quickly goes to Brahman.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni quickly goes to Brahman.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
But, O mighty-armed Arjuna, true renunciation (Jnana Yoga) is exceedingly difficult to attain without first practicing selfless Karma Yoga; whereas the contemplative sage equipped with Karma Yoga quickly reaches Supreme Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "The Yoga-harmonized sage reaches Brahman"—the seeker equipped with Karma-Yoga dedicated to God quickly reaches Lord Vishnu"s supreme abode.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Renunciation without mental purification causes grief, whereas a contemplative sage who practices Karma-Yoga reaches Supreme Brahman without delay.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni quickly goes to Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Sannyasa without Karma-Yoga is difficult to accomplish; a Karma-Yogi rapidly attains direct perception of the Self (Brahman).

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni (contemplative sage) quickly goes to Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni quickly goes to Brahman"—explains Karma-Yoga as the indispensable preliminary stepping-stone (*Upaya*) to true Sannyasa (*Upeya*).

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Renunciation cannot be perfected without Karma-Yoga; thus Karma-Yoga is the primary portal to Knowledge and Devotion.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A devotee engaged in Karma-Yoga as service to Lord Krishna effortlessly realizes the nature of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Quickly reaches Brahman"—a sage whose heart is purified by Karma-Yoga swiftly enters Asamprajnata Samadhi and achieves Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without Yoga; the Yoga-harmonized muni quickly goes to Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing the difficulty of renunciation without Karma-Yoga and the swift liberation of a Karma-Yoga practitioner.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Karma-Yoga surrendered to Lord Vasudeva effortlessly grants the seeker direct intuition of Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Renunciation without service to Lord Krishna brings grief, whereas selfless devotional service swiftly unites the seeker with God.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Hard to attain without Yoga"—one who takes Sannyasa without inner purity risks spiritual downfall; hence Karma-Yoga is the primary refuge.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Merely renouncing all activities yet not engaging in the devotional service of the Lord cannot make one happy. But a thoughtful person engaged in devotional service can achieve the Supreme without delay.
