Life Application:
Fix your mind on God with supreme faith and steady devotion, becoming recognized as most perfectly united in yoga.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Right at the beginning of His reply, the Lord points out the three essential qualities, only by possessing which the definite benefit of devotion to God can be obtained. Generally, people hold the view that the path of devotion is extremely simple. But it is also equally true that whichever path a seeker chooses for themselves, that path does not seem difficult to them. The difference in paths is only due to the means, i.e., the limitations (upadhis) employed. A journey on the Grand Trunk Road cannot be made by a boat, nor a sea voyage by an airplane, nor can a distance be covered at a speed of sixty miles per hour by a bicycle—each vehicle has its own limitations. However, by using any means intelligently and carefully, one can reach the destination. Similarly, for self-development, every seeker chooses the path of Karma Yoga, Bhakti Yoga, or Jnana Yoga according to the predominance of one of the available limitations of body, mind, and intellect. Every seeker finds their chosen path to be the easiest.

Fixing the mind on Me—Both mind and intellect are of the nature of thoughts (vrittis), which is called the subtle body. It is not enough that the thoughts of the mind leisurely wander around the form of the Lord. They must actually penetrate that form, enter deep, and finally merge into oneness with the ideal of perfection. That form is merely a symbol of perfection. This process is indicated here by the word 'avesya' (fixing/absorbing). It does not mean a mere touch of the thought with the form, but a penetration of the form. In fact, the human mind assumes the shape, fragrance, and aura of its object of contemplation; thus, when a devotee meditates on the Lord with complete dedication and love, they temporarily disappear as an individual and attain the beauty and aura of the Lord, the beloved of their heart.

Worship Me, ever-steadfast (nitya-yukta)—The second quality necessary for a devotee to achieve self-development through the path of devotion is constant integration (nitya-yuktata). Being 'nitya-yukta' means having self-control at the time of regular, daily worship. Due to its extroverted nature, the mind abandons the goal and starts wandering in other objects. The art of stabilizing such a mind in the goal itself is called self-control. Although the Sanskrit word 'upasana' can be translated as worship, one should not take an extremely superficial meaning from it. By that word, we generally understand mechanical ritualistic worship. True 'upasana' is the internal action of identifying with the Supreme Self, through which we become the very form of the Supreme Self.

Endowed with supreme faith (para shraddha)—Usually, the word 'shraddha' is understood as blind faith, but that is incorrect. Shraddha means my faith in some unknown object through which that object becomes known to me in reality, in which I previously had only belief. Without such faith, a seeking devotee, even after years of practice, cannot achieve sufficient mental purification and self-divinisation.

Thus, the three essential and indispensable qualities mentioned in this verse for becoming a true devotee are: (1) supreme faith, (2) constant integration in worship, and (3) concentration of the mind on the object of contemplation. The Lord considers the person endowed with these three qualities as the most perfect (yuktatama).

So are other devotees not most perfect? It is not so, but listen to what has to be said about them.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Raising the alternative whether the question is about which of these two Yogas is easier to practice or which is the direct cause of liberation, the Lord speaks the answer in order, saying 'Sri Bhagavan...'. If it were the second alternative (which is the direct cause of liberation), it would not be a subject of inquiry, because that type of Yoga is going to be described (later) in the verse 'ye tv aksharam...'. If it is the first alternative (which is easier), to that He replies with 'ye tu...' (those who...). The phrase 'of all the masters of Yoga' refers to all yogis who master and practice yoga. The Lord is qualified as 'vimukta...' (free from), meaning He has discarded the vision of ignorance (avidya) and false understanding generated by the beginningless ignorance called darkness (timira), which is the cause of afflictions like attachment etc. He establishes their state of being 'ever-steadfast' (nitya-yuktatvam) by referencing the end of the previous chapter. The meaning expressed there, such as 'mat-karma-krit' (doing My work) etc.—proceeding with that resolve, meaning performing it as a regular practice, by that. 'They worship' means they constantly maintain remembrance of Me. He explains the state of being 'most perfect' (yukta-tamatvam) of the said worshippers by their constancy (nairantaryena). He clarifies this further with 'ahorātram' (day and night), meaning that day and night, being free from other objects, they meditate on Me alone with intense absorption.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Fixing their minds on Me, ever engaged in Me, those devotees who worship Me endowed with supreme faith, they are, in My opinion, the best among Yogis.

Commentary: [Without Arjuna asking, the Lord had given this very decision in the forty-seventh verse of the sixth chapter. But because it was not his own question, Arjuna could not grasp that decision. The reason is that a heard statement usually does not register unless it is in response to one's own question. Therefore, he asked this question in the first verse of this chapter.
Similarly, in the absence of a complete desire and eagerness to know a subject, and because it is not one's own question, the profound and important points regarding spiritual practice heard in satsang or read in scriptures usually do not register with seekers. If the same thing is explained in response to their question, they accept it with faith, considering it said specifically for them. Often, they do not understand the heard and read words as meant for themselves and ignore them, although the impressions of those words remain in them in a general way, which can be awakened with intense eagerness. Therefore, seekers should try to apply whatever they read and hear to their lives, considering it meant for them alone.]

'Mayyāveśya mano ye māṁ nityayuktā upāsate' — The mind is fixed where there is love. What is loved is thought of automatically.
'Nityayuktāḥ' means that the seeker himself should attach himself to God. 'God alone is mine and I belong to God' — this is the self attaching to God. When one's firm goal is God-realization, the mind and intellect automatically attach to God. On the contrary, if the self's goal is not God-realization, then despite efforts to fix the mind and intellect on God, they do not fully attach to Him. But when the self considers itself as belonging to God, then the mind and intellect automatically become absorbed in God. The self is the doer (karta) and the mind-intellect are the instruments (karana). Instruments depend on the doer. When the doer becomes God's, then the instruments of mind and intellect automatically attach to God.
The mistake the seeker makes is that instead of attaching himself to God, he practices fixing his mind and intellect on God. Without attaching oneself to God, it is difficult to fix the mind and intellect on God. That is why seekers have a widespread complaint that the mind and intellect do not fix on God. Concentration of mind and intellect can lead to achievements (like samadhi), but welfare will only come by the self attaching to God.
Upanasa (worship) means offering oneself to God—that I belong only to God and God alone is mine. By offering oneself to God, chanting, contemplation, meditation, service, worship, and all actions prescribed by the scriptures automatically happen only for God.

The body is a part of Nature (Prakriti) and the soul is a part of God. Free from identification, possessiveness, and desire for the body, senses, mind, intellect, and ego which are products of Prakriti, one who considers God alone as his own can say, 'I belong to God, God is mine.' Such a person does not establish any new relationship with God. Being conscious and eternal, the soul's relationship with God is self-evident. But forgetting that eternally established real relationship, the soul considered its relationship with Prakriti and its product, the body, which is unreal. Therefore, as long as the assumed relationship with Prakriti exists, so long is there a need to acknowledge one's relationship with God. As soon as the relationship with Prakriti is broken, one's real and eternally established relationship with God is revealed; its memory is regained — 'nasto mohah smrtirlabdha' (Gita 18.73).
Due to facing inertness (Prakriti), i.e., enjoying pleasure from it, the soul establishes a sense of 'I-ness' with the body, assuming 'I am the body.' Because of this assumed relationship with the body, he naturally considers the varna, ashrama, caste, name, occupation, and stages of life like childhood and youth as his own without having to remember them—meaning he does not consider himself separate from them.
The soul's belief in its relationship with the foreign body and world (made by mistake) is so firm that it is always remembered without effort. If it recognizes its real relationship with its kindred (conscious and eternal) Supreme Self, it can never forget God in any state. Then while rising, sitting, eating, drinking, sleeping, and waking, at every moment and in every condition, remembrance and contemplation of God happen automatically.
A seeker whose goal is not the accumulation of worldly pleasures and deriving joy from them, but solely to realize God, should be understood to have begun recognizing his relationship with God. After the complete recognition of this relationship, the seeker has absolutely no desire for worldly enjoyments and their accumulation through the mind, intellect, senses, body, etc.
In reality, although belonging solely to God, to whatever extent the soul desires pleasure from Prakriti, to that extent it has not firmly grasped this relationship with God. To that extent, its relationship is with Prakriti alone. Therefore, the seeker should turn away from Prakriti, consider himself as belonging only to God, and turn towards Him.
'Śraddhayā parayopetāste ye yuktatamā matāḥ' — A seeker's faith will be in whatever he considers supreme. When there is faith, i.e., when the intellect is convinced, he will shape his life naturally according to his established principle and will never deviate from it.
Where there is love, the mind is fixed, and where there is faith, the intellect is fixed. In love, the company of the beloved is primary, and in faith, obedience to command is primary.
By having love solely for God, the devotee experiences an eternal and constant relationship with God, and never experiences separation. That is why in the opinion of the Lord, such devotees are indeed the best knowers of Yoga.

Here, what is said by the plural term 'te me yuktatamā matāḥ' was already said by the singular term 'sa me yuktatamo mataḥ' in the forty-seventh verse of the sixth chapter.

Connection—In the preceding verse, the Lord declared the worshippers of the personal form of God as the best Yogis. On this, the question may arise whether the worshippers of the formless (Brahman) are not the best Yogis? In response, the Lord says:

Those who thus worship Me, focusing their minds on Me as their supreme goal, attain Me soon and easily. Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
There, declaring the conclusion, the Lord said 'Mayi...' (on Me). On Me—who is the ruler of all, endowed with all divine qualities, who is the captivator of Cupid himself, whose divine abode is faultless, infinite, and eternal, who is compassionate, who is supreme bliss, whose form and name are transcendental, who is the repository of countless attributes, who is the supreme truth, who is of the nature of rasa (nectar), who is the complete Supreme Person, who is the direct doer, who is the supreme deity, the Lord and the root-cause of all—those who, being ever-steadfast through the bond of pure love (or by whatever connection), fix their mind on Me and worship Me. Possessed of supreme faith that 'He alone is our object of worship, the repository of virtues, the most beloved Self, and none other'—they are considered by Me to be the most perfect.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
There, the omniscient Lord, seeing Arjuna's eligibility only for the contemplation of the qualified Brahman (Saguna-Vidya), prescribes it for him, along with the means graded according to competence. Therefore, first, in order to create interest in the contemplation of the personal Brahman (Sakara-Brahma-Vidya), praising them as the best, the Lord gave the answer beginning with 'Mayi...' (on Me). On Me—who am Lord Vasudeva, the Supreme Lord, the qualified Brahman—fixing the mind, meaning causing it to enter (Me) through exclusive surrender and boundless love. Just as lac is colored by vermilion and becomes one with it, making the mind one with My form; those who, being ever-steadfast (always diligent), endowed with supreme, excellent, Sattvika faith, worship (constantly contemplate) Me—who am the Lord of all the lords of Yoga, the omniscient, the repository of all auspicious attributes, and possessed of a form—they are considered by Me as the most perfect. For they, having their minds always attached to Me, being turned away from other objects, spend their days and nights meditating only on Me. Therefore, they alone are accepted as the most perfect.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
There, giving the answer that the first ones are the best, the Lord said 'Mayi...' (on Me). On Me—who am the Supreme Lord, endowed with attributes like omniscience—fixing the mind, making it one-pointed; those who, being ever-steadfast, i.e., devoted to Me through the performance of actions for My sake etc., and endowed with supreme faith, worship Me, are considered by Me to be the most perfect.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Raising the alternative whether the question is about which worship is better due to the ease of practice between the qualified (Saguna) and unqualified (Nirguna) worships, or due to being the direct cause of liberation; having the intention to say: 'the superiority of Nirguna worship as the direct cause of liberation has been and will be stated, but as for ease of practice, listen to the first (Saguna)', the Blessed Lord spoke. On Me—who am the Cosmic Form, the Supreme Lord, the controller (dancer) of the river of the three-fold Maya, the Self of all, the object of supreme love—those who, fixing their mind, being devotees, worship Me—who am the Lord of all the lords of Yoga, the omniscient, who has cast away the false vision of beginningless ignorance which is the cause of afflictions such as attachment etc., being 'nitya-yukta', i.e., ever-steadfast in the manner of 'He who does My work, has Me as his goal...'—endowed with supreme, excellent faith and resolved that 'our elevation is only through the worship of the Lord'—they are considered by Me to be the most perfect (yuktatama), meaning the best, because being turned away from other objects, they continuously fix their mind on Me and meditate on Me day and night. As for those who interpret 'Me matam' (in My opinion) as meaning that they are considered the most perfect in the view of the omniscient Lord, who looks upon the wise man as His own self and who out of compassion shows partiality even towards fools—in their view, the consistency of this statement in this context needs to be considered. For the devotees of the Lord, who are favored as the most perfect by the Lord due to His compassion and partiality, are engaged in the easy path of worship, hence they are called 'yuktatama'; however, their actual, absolute superiority is not established, as is evident.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
'Mayi' (on Me) etc. In the phrase 'fixing the mind on Me', if it were taken to mean merely directing the mind towards Me as the object of worship—which is otherwise untenable anyway (since worship cannot take place without an object)—then it would be purposeless, because even those who are devoted to the Imperishable (akshara-nishtha) also similarly focus their minds on the Lord as a means. Therefore, to distinguish this from that, it is explained as focusing the mind on Me 'as the object of attainment itself' (prāpya-viṣayam).

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
There, with the intention to declare that those who worship My manifest form as the Supreme Person (Purushottama) are the best, the Lord gave the answer beginning with 'Mayi...' (on Me). On Me, who am in manifest form—fixing the mind, which is always steady and free from desires, and immersing it completely from all sides with the attitude of seeing Me as the self of all. Those fortunate ones who are ever-steadfast, solely intent on My service, endowed with supreme faith characterized by exclusive love, and thus worship (serve) Me—they are considered by Me to be the most perfect (yuktatama), meaning the best. Such is the meaning.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Indicating the superiority of the attributeless Brahman by merely stating that it is difficult to attain, and expressing the praise of the personal Brahman in words, the Blessed Lord said 'Mayi...' (on Me). On Me—who am the qualified Brahman (Saguna-Brahman)—fixing the mind, meaning causing it to enter (Me); those who, being ever-steadfast (always diligent), worship (contemplate) Me, the Supreme Lord, endowed with faith, i.e., the belief in the existence of God, which is supreme (Sattvika) and of the nature of the conviction that 'surely this Supreme Lord, when worshipped, will deliver us'—they are considered by Me (meaning in the opinion of the omniscient Lord, who looks upon the wise man as His own self and who out of compassion shows partiality even towards fools) to be the most perfect.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Those who fix their minds on My personal form and are always engaged in worshiping Me with great and transcendental faith are considered by Me to be most perfect.

 In answer to Arjuna’s question, Kṛṣṇa clearly says that he who concentrates upon His personal form and who worships Him with faith and devotion is to be considered most perfect in yoga. For one in such Kṛṣṇa consciousness there are no material activities, because everything is done for Kṛṣṇa. A pure devotee is constantly engaged. Sometimes he chants, sometimes he hears or reads books about Kṛṣṇa, or sometimes he cooks prasādam or goes to the marketplace to purchase something for Kṛṣṇa, or sometimes he washes the temple or the dishes – whatever he does, he does not let a single moment pass without devoting his activities to Kṛṣṇa. Such action is in full samādhi.
