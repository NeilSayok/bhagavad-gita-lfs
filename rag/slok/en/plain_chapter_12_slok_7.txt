Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Here, Lord Krishna, describing the virtues or rules to be faithfully practiced by the worshippers of the Saguna (manifest) form, gives the assurance that the Lord Himself will deliver the dedicated seekers from this ocean of Samsara. A careful study of these rules will reveal how the seeker's mind gradually develops and reaches a divine and supreme state, after which he no longer requires any external assistance. In the beginning, to gain the necessary self-confidence for spiritual practice, a seeker needs assurance and encouragement from his teacher.

'Those who surrender all actions to Me'—To surrender or renounce all actions for an institution, an ideal, or a state means to destroy one's personal limitations and identify with one's ideal. Thus, a citizen representing his home country abroad as an ambassador holds a powerful personality because, through his speech, actions, and thoughts, he represents the entire nation. Similarly, when a devotee surrenders himself completely at the feet of the Lord and acts as the messenger or representative of the divine will, he becomes endowed with divine power. He remains conscious of the presence and grace of the Supreme Lord in his every action.

'Who regard Me as the supreme goal'—A dancer never forgets the rhythm and beat of the accompanying mridangam. A musician always remains aware of the shruti (pitch) of the tanpura. Similarly, a devotee is instructed to consider the Lord alone as the supreme goal of his life and always strive to achieve Him in life. Religion should not be understood as a pastime for spare time or a means of temporary relief from daily chores. In short, we are taught that to ascend to the higher peaks of cultural perfection, it is necessary to utilize the contacts, behaviors, and experiences of our life for the realization of that Supreme Lord whom we worship in His qualified, manifest form.

'By single-minded yoga'—All those efforts by which we can establish the identification of our mind with our goal of perfection are called Yoga. Raising one's mind from its current distractions and wasteful tendencies and directing it towards the greater goal of vast bliss and perfect knowledge is Yoga. This power is inherent in all of us, and we are always using it. But the outcome of Yoga depends on which goal we are moving towards. Unfortunately, our goal is often not divine; striving only for sensual pleasure is enjoyment (bhoga), not Yoga.

Usually, our goal keeps changing constantly, and for this reason, despite constant struggle, we do not reach any definite place. If a person has two places in mind for spending holidays but cannot decide his destination, he cannot reach anywhere. He will only waste his energy and time in vain. The term 'ananya-yoga' (single-minded yoga) used here means that in which the seeker's goal is definite and steady, and there is no other thought in his mind towards the goal—that is, in which there is oneness between the seeker and the sought.

The point to be noted here is that the disintegration of our mind can be caused by another feeling towards the goal, and also by the mind wandering in other subjects, leaving the object of meditation.

Thus, those devotees who (a) surrender all actions to Me, (b) consider Me alone as the supreme goal, and (c) worship and meditate with single-minded yoga, are My best devotees. It has been said before that the real meaning of worship is the effort to identify with the goal and become one with it. This is the seeker's goal and in this lies his fulfillment.

Lord Krishna assures that seekers endowed with these qualities need not worry during meditation about how they can go beyond their sorrows, distractions, and imperfections, because: 'I will become their deliverer.' This is the promise given by the Lord Himself. It is possible that even after years of long practice, if seekers do not reach anywhere near self-realization, they will become impatient. Therefore, the Lord's assurance is necessary. The Lord also promises here: 'I will soon become their deliverer.'

'Whose minds are fixed on Me'—Normally, the mind takes the shape of its object of contemplation. When, as a result of constant practice, a seeker develops the capacity to completely abandon alien tendencies and maintain a continuous flow similar to the object, his mind becomes infinite Brahman itself. It is the mind itself that creates the illusion of the limitations of our individual self (jiva-bhava), and it is this mind that directly experiences its infinitude as the Self. Both bondage and liberation belong to the mind. The Self is ever-free, never bound.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Anticipating the doubt as to what fruit is obtained by those who meditate on the Lord, He states the fruit with the words beginning with 'tesam' (for them). 'Samuddharta' means one who completely lifts them up (delivers them) by granting them the support of knowledge. 'Mrtyu' (death) is ignorance, as it is the cause of evils like death; and 'Samsara' is the transmigratory existence which is conjoined with that ignorance as its effect.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Partha! I quickly become the deliverer from the ocean of mortal Samsara for those devotees whose minds are absorbed in Me.

Commentary—'tesamaham samuddharta ... mayyavesitacetasam'—For those seekers whose goal, purpose, and object of meditation has become the Lord alone, and who with exclusive love have fixed their mind on the Lord, and who themselves have surrendered to the Lord, the term 'mayyavesitacetasam' is used.
Just as there is only water in the ocean, similarly there is only death in the world. There is no object born in the world that escapes the blows of death even for a single moment; that is, every born object is going towards death at every moment. Therefore, the world is called the 'ocean of mortal life' (mrtyu-samsara-sagara).
In humans, both favorable and unfavorable tendencies exist. The favorable and unfavorable tendencies in the events, situations, and objects of the world bind a person to the world by creating attraction and aversion (raga-dvesha) (Gita 7.27). It is observed that even seekers fall prey to attraction and aversion by forming favorable or unfavorable feelings towards a particular sect or saint, because of which they cannot cross the ocean of Samsara quickly. The reason is that for a seeker who desires the ultimate truth, sectarian bias is a great obstacle. Blind attachment to a sect binds a person. Therefore, in the Gita, the Lord has repeatedly emphasized rising above these dualities of attraction and aversion.
If a seeker-devotee directs all his favorability towards the Lord—i.e., establishes a relationship of exclusive love with the Lord alone, and directs all his unfavorability towards the world—i.e., serves the world and becomes indifferent to the desire for favorable results, then he can be liberated from the worldly bondage very quickly. Having favorable and unfavorable tendencies in the world is what binds a person to the world.
It is a general rule of the Lord that in whatever way a person surrenders to Him, the Lord shelters him in the same way—'ye yatha mam prapadyante tans tathaiva bhajamyaham' (Gita 4.11). Therefore, He says that although I am equally situated in all beings—'samo'ham sarvabhutesu' (Gita 9.29), yet I am the only beloved of those who perform all their actions for Me, and being devoted to Me, are constantly engaged in My meditation, repetition of My name, and contemplation; I Myself deliver such devotees very quickly and completely from the ocean of mortal Samsara.
Connection—The Lord described the worshippers of the Saguna form as the best yogis in the second verse, and in the sixth and seventh verses, He stated that He quickly delivers such devotees. Therefore, now the Lord, in order to make Arjuna such a best yogi, first describes the path of surrender (Samarpanayoga) in the eighth verse, and then in the ninth, tenth, and eleventh verses, He sequentially describes the practices of Abhyasayoga, action for the sake of the Lord, and the renunciation of the fruits of all actions.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Objection: In the Gita, the excessive distress of the worshippers of the unmanifest (avyakta) is apparent, but the absence of such distress for the worshippers of the Lord is not clearly seen; then how is all this stated? To this, he says: 'madupasakanam' (of My worshippers) etc. By the word 'bhaktanam' (of the devotees), it is indicated that they do not have great regard (or attachment) elsewhere. Here, by the words 'ananyenaiva yogena' etc., it is understood that they worship without the meditation on the unmanifest. By using only the word 'worship' (upasana), the absence of any need for extreme or extraordinary worship is shown. By 'na cirat' (ere long), it is indicated that even there (the result is quick). Saying 'I become their deliverer' is stated for the purpose of pre-empting and preparing the mind for the upcoming statements of the Gita. He also shows the agreement of the Sruti etc., with the words 'uktam ca' (and it is said). Among those (devotees) like the distressed ones, 'gatih' means the provider of the means (sadhana) of liberation.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
For those of such nature, I (become the deliverer), meaning that I am bound by relation to them. This is in accordance with the Bhagavata (9.4.63) statement: 'O Brahmana! Indeed, I am subservient to My devotees, as if I am not independent. Just as chaste wives bring a good husband under control, saintly devotees bring Me under control by their devotion.' If it is asked how such devotees are delivered since their devotion seems to have attributes (sopadhi), it is answered that because they are established in Me, they are free from attributes (nirguna). Thus He says: 'Mayyavesitacetasam' (of those whose minds are absorbed in Me)—I soon become their deliverer. The cause of such a state of mind is the inherent power of the object (the Lord's potency) which alone delivers them, as says the statement: 'The desire of those whose minds are absorbed in Me does not lead to worldly desires.' Nor should it be doubted that having conditional devotion makes them associated with attributes (saguna), because their desire, being directed towards the Lord, is shown to be seedless (without material consequences). Therefore, the Lord has said: 'By pure love alone, the Gopis, the cows, the trees, the beasts, and other dull-witted serpents and Siddhas easily attained Me.' (Bhagavata 11.12.8), and 'That which is fixed on Me is considered beyond the gunas (nirguna).' Otherwise, if it were conditional or if desire were a hindrance there, attainment of the Lord would not have been declared. Hence, we stop here. This has been explained in detail by Shrimat Prabhucharana (Sri Vitthalnathaji), and should be understood from there.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Objection: If the fruit of both (Saguna and Nirguna) worships is the same, then there would be superiority or inferiority based on the lesser or greater amount of distress; but that is not the case. For, the fruit of the knowers of Nirguna Brahman is becoming Brahman itself, which is the nature of consciousness and supreme bliss, through the cessation of ignorance (avidya) and its effects. On the other hand, the fruit for the knowers of Saguna Brahman, since there is no cessation of ignorance due to the absence of the knowledge of the substratum, is the enjoyment of special lordliness in the world of Brahma (Brahmaloka). Therefore, if greater effort is required to gain a greater fruit, it does not imply any inferiority (i.e., the extra effort in Nirguna worship is justified by its direct fruit).

Answer: This is not so. For, those who have destroyed all obstacles through Saguna worship, without the instruction of a teacher and without the pain of repeatedly practicing hearing, reflection, and meditation, attain the cessation of ignorance and its effects through the rise of the knowledge of truth by the Upanishadic sentences that manifest themselves through the grace of the Lord. Thus, at the end of the enjoyment of lordliness in Brahmaloka, they obtain supreme liberation (Kaivalya), which is the fruit of Nirguna knowledge. As the Sruti says: 'He perceives the Person lying in the body of all, who is higher than the highest life-principle (Hiranyagarbha).' This means that after attaining the lordliness of Hiranyagarbha, at the end of enjoyment, he realizes the supreme Self, who is distinct from and superior to Hiranyagarbha (the totality of all individual souls), and who resides in the cave of his own heart, through the self-revealed Vedic authority, and thereby becomes liberated. Thus, even without the aforementioned distress, the worshippers of Saguna Brahman obtain the fruit of Nirguna Brahman-knowledge by the grace of God. This is what He states in these two verses beginning with 'ye tu'.

The word 'tu' (but) is used to dispel the objection raised. 'Ye' (those who), dedicating all their actions to Me, the qualified Vasudeva, and being 'matparah'—i.e., those for whom I, the Lord Vasudeva, am the object of supreme love—meditate on and worship Me, the Lord Vasudeva, with 'ananyenaiva yogena' (single-minded devotion)—having no other support than Me, who am the essence of all beauty, the embodiment of bliss, with two or four arms, or filling the flute that captivates the minds of all people with the most beautiful seven notes, or holding the lotus, conch, mace, and discus in His tender hands, or in forms like Narasimha, or in the form of the most compassionate and beautiful Shri Raghunandana, or in forms like Varaha, or in the Cosmic Form as shown before; they keep up an uninterrupted flow of mental modifications directed towards Me. For those whose minds are thus completely absorbed in Me with one-pointedness, I, the Lord who is constantly worshipped, become without delay—in this very life—the deliverer from the ocean of Samsara which is fraught with death (which consists of false knowledge and its effects, and is extremely difficult to cross). How do I deliver them? By easily lifting them up and establishing them in the pure Brahman, which is the limit of all sublation, by granting them the support of knowledge. 'O Partha' is an address meant for reassurance.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
By stating that 'the worshippers of the Imperishable attain Me alone', their capability of directly attaining Me was shown. However, those former ones (the worshippers of the Saguna form) are liberated from Samsara through the knowledge granted by Me, without the excessive pain of extensive hearing (sravana) etc. With this intention, He speaks the two verses beginning with 'ye tu'. Those Saguna worshippers who dedicate all actions to Me, the Supreme Lord, and hold Me alone as the supreme goal (matparah) and not heaven etc., and who worship Me with single-minded yoga (samadhi)—having no other support than Me, the Cosmic Divine Lord, the repository of infinite virtues, who incarnates on earth in various forms—and meditate on Me, and whose minds are fully absorbed in Me, the Supreme Lord of Cosmic Form; for them, I soon become the deliverer from the ocean of Samsara which is fraught with death. The meaning is: being pleased with their exclusive devotion, I grant them Buddhi-yoga (the yoga of wisdom) and deliver them from Samsara, which consists of primal ignorance and its effects. As is said: 'With their minds fixed on Me, their lives surrendered to Me, enlightening one another...' (BG 10.9-10). By addressing him as 'O Partha', He implies: 'Just as I, won over by the devotion of you, the sons of Prtha, deliver you from various calamities, so do I deliver them.'

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
'Ye tu sarvani' (But those who all) etc. Dedicating all worldly activities like farming which are subsidiary to the maintenance of the body, along with their causes, and Vedic rites like Sandhyavandana which are prescribed with goals like heaven, by 'adhyatma-cetasa'—i.e., with a mind fixed on the supreme Self. 'Matparah' means those for whom I am the supreme goal to be attained; keeping this in mind, it is said 'they for whom I alone am the goal'. 'With no other purpose' (ananya-prayojanena) is indicative of the meditation on the Lord, as described in 'always chanting My glories' (BG 9.14) etc. In the means to attain the Lord, there arises the intellect of purpose; this is the injunction of that intellect, this is the idea. 'Of itself' (svayamevapryah) means not expecting any other fruit. In 'from the ocean of Samsara which is fraught with death' (mrtyubhuta-samsarakhyat), the appositional compound (samanadhikarana) is appropriate according to the Nisadasthapati rule. The adjective is used here to show its despicability, this is the idea. 'Being antagonistic to attaining Me' is the reason for treating it metaphorically as death, since immortality indeed exists when the Lord is attained. To avoid the error of connecting the negative 'na' separately with the verb, the connection is stated as 'deliverer in a very short time' (acirenaiva kalena samuddharta).

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Of those who are devoted to Me and whose minds are fixed on Me, O son of Pṛthā, assuredly I am the swift deliverer from the ocean of birth and death.

 It is explicitly stated here that the devotees are very fortunate to be delivered very soon from material existence by the Lord. In pure devotional service one comes to the realization that God is great and that the individual soul is subordinate to Him. His duty is to render service to the Lord – and if he does not, then he will render service to māyā. As stated before, the Supreme Lord can be appreciated only by devotional service. Therefore, one should be fully devoted. One should fix his mind fully on Kṛṣṇa in order to achieve Him. One should work only for Kṛṣṇa. It does not matter in what kind of work one engages, but that work should be done only for Kṛṣṇa. That is the standard of devotional service. The devotee does not desire any achievement other than pleasing the Supreme Personality of Godhead. His life’s mission is to please Kṛṣṇa, and he can sacrifice everything for Kṛṣṇa’s satisfaction, just as Arjuna did in the Battle of Kurukṣetra. The process is very simple: one can devote himself in his occupation and engage at the same time in chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. Such transcendental chanting attracts the devotee to the Personality of Godhead. The Supreme Lord herein promises that without delay He will deliver a pure devotee thus engaged from the ocean of material existence. Those who are advanced in yoga practice can willfully transfer the soul to whatever planet they like by the yoga process, and others take the opportunity in various ways, but as far as the devotee is concerned, it is clearly stated here that the Lord Himself takes him. The devotee does not need to wait to become very experienced in order to transfer himself to the spiritual sky. In the Varāha Purāṇa this verse appears: nayāmi paramaṁ sthānam arcir-ādi-gatiṁ vinā garuḍa-skandham āropya yatheccham anivāritaḥ The purport of this verse is that a devotee does not need to practice aṣṭāṅga-yoga in order to transfer his soul to the spiritual planets. The responsibility is taken by the Supreme Lord Himself. He clearly states here that He Himself becomes the deliverer. A child is completely cared for by his parents, and thus his position is secure. Similarly, a devotee does not need to endeavor to transfer himself by yoga practice to other planets. Rather, the Supreme Lord, by His great mercy, comes at once, riding on His bird carrier Garuḍa, and at once delivers the devotee from material existence. Although a man who has fallen in the ocean may struggle very hard and may be very expert in swimming, he cannot save himself. But if someone comes and picks him up from the water, then he is easily rescued. Similarly, the Lord picks up the devotee from this material existence. One simply has to practice the easy process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness and fully engage himself in devotional service. Any intelligent man should always prefer the process of devotional service to all other paths. In the Nārāyaṇīya this is confirmed as follows: yā vai sādhana-sampattiḥ puruṣārtha-catuṣṭaye tayā vinā tad āpnoti naro nārāyaṇāśrayaḥ The purport of this verse is that one should not engage in the different processes of fruitive activity or cultivate knowledge by the mental speculative process. One who is devoted to the Supreme Personality can attain all the benefits derived from other yogic processes, speculation, rituals, sacrifices, charities, etc. That is the specific benediction of devotional service. Simply by chanting the holy name of Kṛṣṇa – Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare – a devotee of the Lord can approach the supreme destination easily and happily, but this destination cannot be approached by any other process of religion. The conclusion of Bhagavad-gītā is stated in the Eighteenth Chapter: sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ One should give up all other processes of self-realization and simply execute devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. That will enable one to reach the highest perfection of life. There is no need for one to consider the sinful actions of his past life, because the Supreme Lord fully takes charge of him. Therefore one should not futilely try to deliver himself in spiritual realization. Let everyone take shelter of the supreme omnipotent Godhead, Kṛṣṇa. That is the highest perfection of life.
