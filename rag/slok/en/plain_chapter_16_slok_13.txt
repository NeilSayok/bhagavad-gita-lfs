Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
idamadya mayā labdhamimaṃ prāpsye manoratham .
idamastīdamapi me bhaviṣyati punardhanam

Life Application:
Avoid thinking greedily about what you acquired today and what more wealth you will claim tomorrow.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
This has been gained by me today; this desire of mine I shall fulfill; this is mine and this wealth also shall be mine in future.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
This I have gained today; tomorrow I will gratify another desire; this wealth is mine now, the rest shall be mine ere long;

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
'This has been gained by me today; I shall acquire this desired object. This is in hand; again, this wealth also will come to me.'

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains that this verse self-evidently depicts the mindset of the average materialist. In a competitive world, success is measured by wealth. Yet, acquiring wealth never satisfies; it only breeds more greed. A self-controlled sage (sthitaprajna) is content because external objects do not disturb his peace, whereas the demoniac person is perpetually restless, chasing an ever-growing thirst for wealth.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that their thoughts run contrary to discrimination (viveka). They constantly think about gold, land, and other possessions, planning how to expand their wealth and become famous as rich men.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
'This much has been gained by me today; and I shall obtain this desire; this wealth is mine, and this also shall be mine in future.'

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that Asuri individuals are obsessed with multiplying their wealth. They attribute their gains solely to their cleverness and hard work, never considering destiny or divine grace. Even while eating, resting, or performing prayers, their minds are constantly plotting how to open more shops, buy more land, and accumulate more property. They fail to realize that none of this wealth will accompany them at death.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
This land, sons etc., have I gained solely by my ability and not by the help of any higher force. I shall attain this desire also myself and not by good fortune or any other means. This wealth, gained solely by my ability, is with me. And this also shall be mine through my own ability.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 16.13–16.16 jointly): Idam adya etc. upto asucau.  Endowed with many thoughts etc.  For, they do not have any conviction. Into the hell and what is foul :  in the [hell] Avici and the  like and in the regular succession of  birth and death.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Idam, this thing; labham, has been gained; maya, by me; adya, today; prapsye, I shall acire; idam, this other; manoratham, desired object which is delectable to the mind. And idam, this; asti, is in hand; punah, again; idam, this; dhanam, wealth; api, also; bhavisyati, will come; me, to me, in the next year. Thery I shall become rich and famous.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati details their greed through their daydreaming. They count what they have already gathered and plan what they will acquire in the coming year, which ultimately leads to their downfall into a hellish state.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that their daydreaming is detailed here. They rejoice in what they have today and plan to obtain more desired objects in the future, which leads to their downfall.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that Asuri individuals constantly think about acquiring land, gold, and other materials, boasting that they will soon become the wealthiest and most famous people in the world.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that while a seeker examines his progress under God's will, the demoniac person boasts of his own capability (maya - 'by me') to acquire sons, land, and wealth, ignoring destiny.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that they believe their gains are due to their own hard work, not by chance. They plan to satisfy their minds with more wealth, not knowing that everything is temporary and controlled by a higher power.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the ropes of hope are described here. They lead the individual to think endlessly about acquiring worldly riches.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Today I have obtained all these opulences by My own effort. They were not gifted to Me by anyone else. I am the enjoyer and the lord of everything. Therefore, I will utilize this power to maintain and protect the living entities according to My will.
