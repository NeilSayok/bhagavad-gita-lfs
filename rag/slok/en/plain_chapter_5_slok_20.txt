Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
na prahṛṣyetpriyaṃ prāpya nodvijetprāpya cāpriyam .
sthirabuddhirasammūḍho brahmavid brahmaṇi sthitaḥ

Word Meanings:
न - not
प्रहृष्येत्प्रियं - should rejoice on pleasant
प्राप्य - having obtained
नोद्विजेत्प्राप्य - nor grieve on obtaining
चाप्रियम् - and unpleasant
स्थिरबुद्धिरसम्मूढो - steady intellect, undeluded
ब्रह्मविद् - knower of Brahman
ब्रह्मणि - in Brahman
स्थितः - established

Life Application:
Maintain your mental balance today whether you receive good news or bad news, refusing to be swept away by emotions.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Resting in Brahman, with steady intellect and undeluded, the knower of Brahman should not rejoice on obtaining what is pleasant, nor grieve on obtaining what is unpleasant.

Resting in Brahman, with steady intellect and undeluded, the knower of Brahman should not rejoice on obtaining what is pleasant, nor grieve on obtaining what is unpleasant.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who neither rejoices excessively upon obtaining what is pleasant, nor grieves upon encountering what is unpleasant—that steady-minded, undeluded knower of Supreme Brahman rests forever established in Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Steady intellect, undeluded"—the selfless seeker whose mind is fixed in Lord Vishnu transcends worldly joys and sorrows, remaining rooted in God.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Undisturbed by pleasure upon gaining the agreeable or grief upon encountering the disagreeable, the knower of Brahman rests unshakeably in Brahman.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Remaining equanimous toward material pleasant and unpleasant objects, the wise man, regarding the Self as supreme, abides in Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: In the case of the knower of Brahman who is resting in Brahman, with steady intellect and undeluded, he should not rejoice on obtaining what is pleasant, nor grieve on obtaining what is unpleasant.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Resting in Brahman, with steady intellect and undeluded, the knower of Brahman should not rejoice on obtaining what is pleasant, nor grieve on obtaining what is unpleasant"—describes the marks of a *Brahmavit* (Knower of Brahman) who transcends mental fluctuations.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Equanimity amidst favorable and unfavorable circumstances is the prime mark of being established in Brahman, lifting the sage above all dualities.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A loving devotee fixing his mind in Krishna"s divine form remains completely unaffected by worldly happiness and distress.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Steady-minded and undeluded"—the Knower of Brahman established in Nirvikalpa Samadhi remains unruffled by pleasant or painful encounters.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how freedom from joy in pleasure and grief in pain characterizes the Knower of Brahman.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A practitioner devoted to Vasudeva remains immutable amidst all material ups and downs, ever abiding in Brahman.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the practitioner"s heart is filled with Krishna"s love, no worldly occurrence can agitate him.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Rejoices not on obtaining pleasant"—maintaining an unattached and undisturbed mind amidst desirable and undesirable events is the true touchstone of Self-knowledge.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A person who neither rejoices upon achieving something pleasant nor laments upon obtaining something unpleasant, who is self-intelligent, who is unbewildered, and who knows the science of God, is already situated in transcendence.
