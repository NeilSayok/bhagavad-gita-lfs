Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
jñeyaḥ sa nityasaṃnyāsī yo na dveṣṭi na kāṅkṣati .
nirdvandvo hi mahābāho sukhaṃ bandhātpramucyate

Word Meanings:
ज्ञेयः - should be known
स - he
नित्यसंन्यासी - perpetual renunciant
यो - who
न - not
द्वेष्टि - hates
न - not
काङ्क्षति - desires
निर्द्वन्द्वो - free from dualities
हि - certainly
महाबाहो - O mighty-armed one
सुखं - easily
बन्धात्प्रमुच्यते - is freed from bondage

Life Application:
Cultivate inner renunciation by letting go of personal hatred and selfish craving while carrying out your daily tasks.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
He should be known as a perpetual Sannyasi who neither hates nor desires; for free from the pairs of opposites, O mighty-armed, he is easily set free from bondage.

He should be known as a perpetual renunciate (Nitya-Sannyasi) who neither hates nor desires any worldly object; for free from all pairs of opposites (dualities), O mighty-armed Arjuna, he easily breaks free from all worldly bondage.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "He should be known as a perpetual renunciate"—he who, freed from attachment and hatred, surrenders all work to Lord Vishnu is a true Sannyasi.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: One who is free from attachment and aversion while performing Karma is a perpetual renunciate, regardless of outward dress or lifestyle.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He should be known as a perpetual Sannyasi who neither hates nor desires; for free from the pairs of opposites, O mighty-armed, he is easily set free from bondage.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: A Karma-Yogi who is free from desire for fruits and hatred of difficulty is equal to a true Sannyasi, even while actively engaged in work.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: Therefore he alone is to be known as a perpetual Sannyasi who neither hates nor desires; for free from the pairs of opposites, O mighty-armed, he is easily set free from bondage.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "He should be known as a perpetual renunciate who neither hates nor desires; for free from the pairs of opposites, O mighty-armed, he is easily set free from bondage"—defines the true interior essence of Sannyasa as freedom from desire and hatred.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: A person free from dualities remains unattached to karma while acting; this is the zenith of perpetual renunciation.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A practitioner absorbed in Krishna"s love naturally becomes free from dualities, effortlessly escaping worldly bondage.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Easily freed from bondage"—relinquishing egoism and reward-seeking is true Sannyasa, by which the seeker effortlessly reaches Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of the interior state of perpetual renunciation characterised by freedom from desire, hatred, and dualities.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A seeker devoted to Vasudeva remains equanimous in all conditions, attaining the state of a Nitya-Sannyasi.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the seeker"s mind is absorbed in Lord Krishna, attraction and aversion dissolve naturally, making him a perpetual renunciate.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "He is to be known as perpetual Sannyasi"—the defining mark of renunciation is not external robes, but inner freedom from desire and hatred.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who neither hates nor desires the fruits of his activities is known to be always renounced. Such a person, free from all dualities, easily overcomes material bondage and is completely liberated, O mighty-armed Arjuna.
