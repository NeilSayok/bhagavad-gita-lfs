Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yato yato niścarati manaścañcalamasthiram .
tatastato niyamyaitadātmanyeva vaśaṃ nayet

Word Meanings:
यतो - wherever
यतो - from whatever cause
निश्चरति - wanders away
मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम् - restless and fickle mind
ततस्ततो - from that
नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव - restraining this in Self alone
वशं - under control
नयेत् - should bring

Life Application:
Whenever your restless mind wanders away today, gently bring it back under the control of self-awareness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
From whatever cause the restless and unsteady mind wanders away, from that let him restrain it and bring it under the control of the Self alone.

From whatever sense-objects—sound, form, taste, touch—this naturally restless and flickering mind strays away, let him repeatedly withdraw it from them and bring it under the direct control of the Supreme Self alone.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Bring it under control of the Self alone"—the single remedy to conquer mental flickering is anchoring it repeatedly in Lord Vishnu"s divine form.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Whichever sense-object the naturally flickering mind wanders toward, pull it back by practice of discrimination and dispassion, restraining it in the inner Self.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Whichever sense objects the restless mind moves toward due to habit, turn it back by contemplating the flaws in those objects and subject it to the Self.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: From whatever cause this restless and unsteady mind wanders away towards external sense-objects like sound and touch, let him restrain it from that and bring it repeatedly under the control of the Self alone.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "From whatever cause the restless and unsteady mind wanders away, from that let him restrain it and bring it under the control of the Self alone"—provides the exact psychological technique of Abhyasa: identifying mental wanderings, revealing their defective nature, and re-centering the mind in the Inner Self.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Viewing defects in worldly objects and supreme beauty in God"s form is the key to drawing back the mind.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Whenever the mind strays into worldly affairs, re-establish it immediately in the nectar of Krishna"s divine sports.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Restraining it from that"—severing sense attachment through Vairagya and Abhyasa to merge the mind in the Inner Self is the essence of Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis of overcoming mental restlessness through active restraint and anchoring in the Self.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: Using discriminating power, turn the flickering mind away from objects and master it at Lord Vasudeva"s feet.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: Whenever the mind runs toward sensory pleasures, divert it back into meditation on Lord Krishna"s lotus feet.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "From whatever cause it wanders"—turning mental waves away from objects to fix them in Brahman is spiritual practice.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
From wherever the mind wanders due to its restless and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self.
