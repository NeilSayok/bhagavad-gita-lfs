Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
karmendriyāṇi saṃyamya ya āste manasā smaran .
indriyārthānvimūḍhātmā mithyācāraḥ sa ucyate

Life Application:
Align your inner thoughts with your outer behavior, avoiding false pretenses of self-discipline while craving forbidden pleasures.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The man of deluded intellect who restrains the organs of action while continuing to dwell in his mind upon the objects of the senses is called a hypocrite (a pretender).

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
He who, restraining the organs of action, sits thinking of the sense-objects in mind, he of deluded understanding is called a hypocrite.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
He who remains motionless, refusing to act, but all the while brooding over sensuous object, that deluded soul is simply a hypocrite.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Mere physical inactivity leads nowhere, let alone to the state of perfection. If a seeker restrains his organs of action but does not intelligently withdraw his mind and intellect from brooding over sense objects, his spiritual growth is bound to be insecure and joyless.

Modern books on psychology confirm the truth of this statement. To keep dwelling mentally upon immoral and criminal acts is far more damaging than to commit them physically. It is the nature of the mind to repeat a thought again and again; by such constant repetition a deep impression (vasana) is carved into it, and thereafter whatever thoughts arise flow along the channel already cut. Once the direction of a man's thoughts is fixed, that becomes his very nature, and it expresses itself in every action he performs. Thus, by ceaseless brooding on sense objects, sensuous impressions are engraved deep in the mind, and driven helplessly by them the man is seen acting accordingly in the world.

One who outwardly parades himself as moral and idealistic while inwardly wallowing in low tendencies is not a true seeker of the spirit at all; he is, as declared here, deluded and a hypocrite. We all know that even when physical restraint has been achieved, restraining the mind's sensuous tendencies is difficult for the ordinary person.

Understanding that the ordinary person cannot by himself find the means of protecting himself from his own natural tendencies, the Lord goes on to say:

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Controlling organs of actions,  whosoever sits with his mind,  pondering over the sense objects-that person is a man of deluded soul and [he] is called a man of deluded action.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
He who, controlling the organs of action, lets his mind dwell on the objects of senses, is a deluded person and a hypocrite.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
One, who after withdrawing the organs of action, sits mentally recollecting the objects of the senses, that one, of deluded mind, is called a hypocrite.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Although actions cannot be given up entirely, still renunciation should be practised to the extent that one is able -- this is stated by the words 'karmendriyani'. To show that the mind alone is the operative cause, both the positive and the negative statements are given: 'remembering with the mind' and 'restraining with the mind'. Karma-yoga means the duties proper to one's own varna and ashrama; there is no rule that it refers to the duties of the householder alone, since sannyasa and the rest are also enjoined and since the word is used in a general sense.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Objection: In that case, just as for the knower of the Self, so also for the non-knower there would be no fault in not performing action, since even a fool who is incapable of controlling the aggregate of body and senses could take up renunciation. Anticipating this, he says 'yastu...'. His omission of the enjoined duty is what is referred to by the word 'tat' -- 'that is bad'; the sense being that it is hypocrisy. That very hypocrisy is then described by the words 'karmendriyani...'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
He who forcibly restrains the organs of action -- indeed all the senses -- and yet goes on brooding in his mind over the objects of the senses, that man of deluded intellect is called a hypocrite (one whose conduct is false).

Commentary -- 'karmendriyani samyamya ... mithyacarah sa ucyate' -- Here the word 'karmendriyani' does not refer only to the five organs of action (speech, hands, feet, the generative organ and the anus), but together with them to the five organs of knowledge as well (ear, skin, eye, tongue and nose); for without the organs of knowledge no action can be performed by the organs of action alone. Besides, if only the hands, feet and the like were restrained while the eyes, ears and so on were left unrestrained, complete hypocrisy would not even be established.

In the Gita the organs of knowledge are counted within the organs of action. That is why the Gita uses the word 'karmendriya' but nowhere uses the word 'jnanendriya'. In the eighth and ninth verses of the fifth chapter, the activities of the organs of knowledge -- seeing, hearing, touching and so on -- are included along with the activities of the organs of action, which proves that the Gita regards the organs of knowledge too as organs of action. The Gita also regards the activities of the mind as action -- 'sarira-van-manobhir yat karma prarabhate narah' (18.15). The purport is that since Prakriti itself is active, everything that is a product of Prakriti is active.

Although the word 'samyamya' means proper regulation of the senses, that is, bringing them under control, here the word does not mean bringing them under control but forcibly restraining them from outside. For if the senses had actually come under control, it could not be called hypocrisy.

The man of deluded intellect (devoid of discrimination between the real and the unreal) outwardly checks the activity of the senses by force, but in his mind he goes on contemplating the objects of those very senses, and he takes such a state to be actionlessness. Therefore he is called a hypocrite, that is, one whose conduct is false.

Though he has outwardly renounced the objects of the senses and thinks 'I am not performing action', even in such a state he has not really become free from action. For although he appears outwardly inactive, on account of egoism, mineness and desire, action in the form of enjoyment of objects is certainly going on in the shape of attached brooding upon them.

Worldly enjoyments can be experienced outwardly as well as mentally. Just as the impressions of enjoyment are stamped upon the inner instrument by outwardly enjoying objects with attachment, exactly the same impressions are formed by enjoying them mentally, that is, by brooding over them with attachment. Outward renunciation of enjoyments a man may accomplish even through mere reasoning, through regard for public opinion, or through fear of disorder in his affairs; but in enjoying them mentally no external obstacle arises. So he goes on enjoying them in the mind and falsely prides himself, 'I am a renouncer of enjoyments.' Enjoyment through the mind causes special harm, because there is special opportunity for indulging in it. Therefore the seeker should, just as he guards himself against outward enjoyments and renounces them, with special care renounce the mental brooding over enjoyments as well.

Arjuna too wishes to renounce actions in their very form, and asks the Lord why He engages him in this terrible action. In answer, the Lord here says that the man who, while retaining egoism, mineness, attachment and desire, merely renounces actions outwardly and considers himself actionless, is false in his conduct. The purport is that the seeker should not renounce actions in their very form, but should go on performing them diligently, free from desire and attachment.

Connection -- In the fourth verse the Lord declared renunciation of actions to be unnecessary from the standpoint of both Karmayoga and Sankhyayoga. Then in the fifth verse He said that no man in any state can remain even for a moment without performing action. In the sixth verse He declares the conduct of one who forcibly restrains the activity of the senses and considers himself actionless to be false. From this it is established that merely renouncing actions in their form does not amount to their real renunciation. Therefore in the next verse the Lord describes the mark of real renunciation.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
He whose inner and outer organs of senses are not conered because of his sins not being annulled but is none the less struggling for winning knowledge of the self, whose mind is forced to turn away from the self by reason of it being attached to sense objects, and who conseently lets his minds dwell on them - he is called a hypocrite, because his actions are at variance with his professions. The meaning is that by practising the knowledge of the self in this way, he becomes perverted and lost.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Karmendriyani etc.  If he does not act with his organs of action, then he necessarily acts with his mind.  At the same time he is the man of deluded action;  For, the mental actions can never be avoided totally.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Yah, one who; samyamya, after withdrawing; karma-indriyani, the organs of action-hands etc.; aste, sits; manasa, mentally; smaran, recollecting, thinking; indriya-arthan, the objects of the senses; sah, that one; vimudha-atma, of deluded mind; ucyate, is called; mithya-acarah, a hypocrite, a sinful person.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Objection: to say 'even so, karmendriyani' is inappropriate, since the remembering of sense objects by the mind, which belongs to the third alternative, has not been mentioned at all. To this he replies with 'tathapi...'. Although actions required for the maintenance of the body and the like cannot be abandoned, still renunciation should be practised to the extent possible -- that is, of such actions as sacrifices, whose abandonment is possible. The purport is: scripture does not enjoin what is impossible; hence in the smriti the word 'karma' will refer to actions other than those unavoidable ones.

If this doubt is removed by the verse itself, the second verse would become purposeless; to this he says 'mana eva...'. The mind alone is the operative cause of bondage and liberation, not the doing or non-doing of action. Therefore only restraint of the mind is to be practised, not abandonment of action -- this is what is being made known. When the mind is unrestrained, with reference to bondage the first is the positive concomitance and the second the negative; with reference to liberation it is the reverse. By this, the objection that the word 'manasa' is redundant since remembering is necessarily mental is likewise refuted.

Since in 'karmayogena yoginam' (3.3) the word karma-yoga was in context used of the duties of householders and the like, one might understand it in the same sense here; to remove that he says 'karmayogam...'. It is not intended as a rule that it means only the duties of the householder, or only of the forest-dweller, or only of the celibate student. By the word 'adi' in 'sannyasadi' is taken whatever is other than what has been so restricted; and the word karma-yoga is itself a general term. Earlier, in 'jnanayogena sankhyanam' (3.3), the duty of the order of ascetics was stated separately, so there even a general word was fixed in a particular sense. But here there is nothing of that kind -- this is the sense.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
It was said that by not performing action with the organs of action the state of actionlessness is attained; that too is not sound, because what is to be removed only by mental activity cannot have the nature described. This he states with 'karmendriyani...'. As it is said: 'In Sankhya renunciation is proclaimed; and that renunciation too is by the mind alone. Where there is no renunciation, there is the path of yoga; when yoga is perfected there is fulfilment. For that purpose a certain procedure is set forth in the Purana as well. The sages have declared it in many ways, but the fruit is one and it is not external. By abandoning action there is renunciation, but by remembering it with the mind sin arises again; therefore the performance of action is held to be the better course.' Hence the man who is himself a doer of action with the mind, that is, engaged in mental activity upon sense objects, is deluded of soul, and his conduct is declared to be futile.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
One who has somehow taken up renunciation merely out of eagerness, but whose mind is impure, does not partake of its fruit. For -- the deluded one, whose inner organ is defiled by attachment, aversion and the rest, merely out of eagerness restrains the organs of action, speech, hands and so on, that is, refrains from acting with the external organs, and sits remembering with a mind impelled by attachment the objects of the senses -- sound and the rest -- and not the truth of the Self, remaining devoid of action with the conceit 'I have renounced'. He is a hypocrite; since, lacking purity of mind, he is unfit for the fruit, he is called a man of sinful conduct. The dharma-shastra also declares: 'Here the shruti has enjoined the renunciation of all actions for the sake of discriminating the meaning of the word 'thou'; one who abandons that becomes fallen.' Hence it is established that a man of impure inner organ does not attain perfection by mere renunciation.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Therefore he censures the ignorant man who abandons action, with the words 'karmendriyani...'. He who restrains even the organs of action -- speech, hands and the rest -- that is, holds them in check, and yet sits remembering with the mind the objects of the senses under the pretext of meditating on the Lord, his mind having no steadiness in the Self because of its impurity, is called a hypocrite, that is, a man of deceitful conduct, a pretender.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Objection: by forcibly restraining the organs of action, will he not become a non-performer of action? Anticipating this, he says: one who is without knowledge of the Self, whose inner organ is impure, who forcibly restrains the organs of action and does not perform the enjoined duty but renounces out of eagerness -- he falls from both sides. This is stated with 'karma...'. The deluded one, whose mind is soiled by attachment, aversion and the rest, withdrawing the organs of action -- hands and the rest -- sits remembering with the mind the objects of the senses, sound and so on; he is called a hypocrite, a man of bad conduct, a man of sinful conduct.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
He states that there is harm in not performing action: 'anyatha...', that is, without practising karma-yoga. By the words 'manasa smaran' he gives the reason established by implication: 'avinashta...' and 'atma-vimukhikrita-manah'. In 'vimudhatma' the word atma denotes the mind; delusion means being turned away from the Self. The force of the word 'eva' is: he remembers precisely those very objects from which the senses were meant to be withheld. He then states the manner of the hypocrisy with 'anyatha sankalpya...'. Being otherwise than what one professes is ultimately falsity everywhere; and here, since his conduct is contrary to the jnana-yoga he had resolved upon, the conduct he claims as jnana-yoga is declared false. Not stopping at merely applying the name 'hypocrite', he shows that it culminates in positive harm, with 'atma...'. By the two words 'viparita' (perverted) and 'vinashta' (ruined) it is shown that when the means is perverted, the result too is perverted. In the second chapter, from 'dhyayato vishayan' up to 'buddhinashat pranashyati' (2.62-63), this very meaning has been elaborated -- and the word 'vinashta' recalls it.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
He says that one who abandons action out of ignorance is a pretender and does not obtain the fruit of sacrifice, with the words 'karmendriyani...'. Restraining, that is, holding back, the organs of action -- hands, feet and the rest -- he who sits remembering with the mind the objects of the senses, as though absorbed in meditation upon the Lord, merely to make a display before people, is deluded of soul and a hypocrite, that is, he is called a pretender, one who acts falsely.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Objection: I shall accomplish purity of mind by meditation alone, preceded by renunciation; what need is there of actions? Anticipating this, he says 'karmendriyani...'. The deluded one, whose mind is overrun by attachment and the rest, restraining -- that is, holding in check -- the organs of action, speech and the rest, sits, that is, seats himself in solitude under the pretext of meditation; he is a hypocrite. His observance of postures, restraints and the like is false, a mere sham, because it is fruitless. The reason is given: 'indriyarthan manasa smaran' -- since he grasps the objects of the senses, sound and the rest, through the ear and the other organs and remembers them with the mind, he is therefore a hypocrite. While brooding over sense objects he displays before the world his own steadfastness in yoga; hence he is a deceiver. The sense is that there is no means of purifying the mind other than action.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
One who restrains the senses of action but whose mind dwells on sense objects certainly deludes himself and is called a pretender.

 There are many pretenders who refuse to work in Kṛṣṇa consciousness but make a show of meditation, while actually dwelling within the mind upon sense enjoyment. Such pretenders may also speak on dry philosophy in order to bluff sophisticated followers, but according to this verse these are the greatest cheaters. For sense enjoyment one can act in any capacity of the social order, but if one follows the rules and regulations of his particular status, he can make gradual progress in purifying his existence. But he who makes a show of being a yogī while actually searching for the objects of sense gratification must be called the greatest cheater, even though he sometimes speaks of philosophy. His knowledge has no value, because the effects of such a sinful man’s knowledge are taken away by the illusory energy of the Lord. Such a pretender’s mind is always impure, and therefore his show of yogic meditation has no value whatsoever.
