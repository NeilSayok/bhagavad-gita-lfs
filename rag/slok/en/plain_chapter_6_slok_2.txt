Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
yaṃ saṃnyāsamiti prāhuryogaṃ taṃ viddhi pāṇḍava .
na hyasaṃnyastasaṅkalpo yogī bhavati kaścana

Word Meanings:
यं - which
संन्यासमिति - as renunciation
प्राहुर्योगं - they call yoga
तं - that
विद्धि - know
पाण्डव - O Pandava.
न - not
ह्यसंन्यस्तसङ्कल्पो - without renouncing selfish desire
योगी - yogi
भवति - accrues
कश्चन - anyone

Life Application:
Understand that true spiritual practice requires giving up selfish planning and obsessive personal motives.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Know that which is called sannyasa to be yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a yogi who has not renounced desire for the fruit of action.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Know that which is called sannyasa to be yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a yogi without renouncing mental resolve.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Know that which scriptures designate as Sannyasa (Renunciation) to be one with Yoga, O Pandava; for no one can truly become a Yogi without discarding all selfish motives and mental resolves.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "What is called Sannyasa"—action performed with devotion to Lord Vishnu is called Sannyasa by sages; renouncing selfish resolve is the essential hallmark of both.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: True Sannyasa and Karma-Yoga are declared non-different because both fundamentally demand the renunciation of worldly mental resolve (Sankalpa).

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Know that which is called sannyasa to be yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a yogi who has not renounced desire for the fruit of action.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Know that Karma-Yoga is essentially identical to Sannyasa, for without renouncing worldly thoughts concerning the body, none can be a practitioner of Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He who performs his bounden duty without desiring the fruits of action is a true Sannyasi and Yogi, not he who merely relinquishes sacred fire or actions. Know that which is called Sannyasa to be Yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a Yogi without renouncing mental resolve.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Know that which is called sannyasa to be yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a yogi without renouncing mental resolve"—establishes that both Sannyasa and Karma-Yoga share the essential feature of abandoning worldly desire.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Renunciation of Sankalpa means abandoning desire for results and egoistic agency, without which spiritual success in Yoga is impossible.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: Becoming free from mental resolves through total self-surrender at Lord Krishna"s lotus feet is real Yoga and Sannyasa.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "No one who has not renounced Sankalpa"—without giving up desires for fruitive actions, no seeker can ascend the plane of Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami states: Know that which is called sannyasa to be yoga, O Pandava; for no one becomes a yogi without renouncing mental resolve.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Authoritative scriptural exposition of the non-difference between Sannyasa and Yoga, and the flaw of unrenounced desires.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: He who acts for Lord Vasudeva"s pleasure alone renounces Sankalpa and becomes truly qualified for Yoga.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: As long as fruitive resolves persist in the mind, exclusive Yoga at Lord Krishna"s lotus feet cannot be attained.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "No one who has not renounced Sankalpa"—relinquishing the mental craving for sense enjoyment is the gateway to Yoga.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
What is called renunciation you should know to be the same as yoga, or linking yourself with the Supreme, O son of Pandu, for one can never become a yogi unless he renounces the desire for sense gratification.
