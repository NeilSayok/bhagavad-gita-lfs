Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
ajñaścāśraddadhānaśca saṃśayātmā vinaśyati .
nāyaṃ loko.asti na paro na sukhaṃ saṃśayātmanaḥ

Word Meanings:
अज्ञश्चाश्रद्दधानश्च - the ignorant and faithless
संशयात्मा - doubting person
विनश्यति - is ruined
नायं - not this
लोकोऽस्ति - world is
न - not
परो - nor the next
न - not
सुखं - nor happiness
संशयात्मनः - for the doubting soul

Life Application:
Overcome persistent self-doubt and cynicism, cultivating healthy trust so you can experience peace and happiness.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting self goes to destruction. For the doubting self, there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting self goes to destruction. For the doubting self, there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
But the ignorant (Ajnaj), the faithless (Asraddadhanas), and the doubting soul (Samsayatma) go to complete destruction. For the doubting soul, there is neither this world, nor the world beyond, nor any happiness whatsoever.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "The doubting soul perishes"—a soul harboring doubts regarding Lord Vishnu and the Guru"s teachings falls into spiritual downfall.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: A doubting soul goes to total ruin, forfeited from all human goals, securing happiness neither in this world nor liberation in the next.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting self goes to destruction. For the doubting self, there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "The doubting soul perishes"—one who harbors doubts in divine teachings perishes without goals; he enjoys neither temporal happiness nor spiritual Mukti.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: "Sraddhavan etc. and Ajnasca etc."—a faithful, devoted, and self-controlled seeker attains Knowledge and supreme peace; whereas the ignorant, faithless, and doubting soul perishes, inheriting neither this world nor the next.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting self goes to destruction. For the doubting self, there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness"—condemns doubt (*Samsaya*) as the worst spiritual disease that ruins both mundane and transcendent life.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Doubt is the greatest enemy of the soul, depriving it of all spiritual and temporal wealth.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: One who doubts Krishna"s supreme divinity turns away from the nectar of devotion and is completely lost.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "The doubting self perishes"—ignorance and doubt impede Self-realization; hence unwavering trust in the Guru"s word is mandatory.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting self goes to destruction. For the doubting self, there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis showing how ignorance, faithlessness, and doubt ruin a person in this life and the life hereafter.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A practitioner who entertains doubt regarding Vasudeva and Guru-teachings can never attain Mukti; his fall is certain.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: As long as doubt is not resolved at Krishna"s holy feet, true spiritual joy remains unattainable.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "The doubting self perishes"—the veil of doubt is the ultimate enemy of Knowledge and discrimination.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
But ignorant and faithless persons who doubt the revealed scriptures do not attain God consciousness; they fall down. For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next.
