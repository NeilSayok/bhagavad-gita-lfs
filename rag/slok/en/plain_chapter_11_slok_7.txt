Life Application:
See the entire moving and unmoving universe united within the supreme spirit, encompassing all your questions.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Firstly, the Lord trains the enthusiastic seeker's courageous mind so that the imperishable wealth of curiosity to know develops in it. Thereafter, His effort is to transform this curiosity into intense longing or inquiry. For this purpose only, He mentions the forms visible in the universal form. By this device, the seeker's mind becomes focused at one place with complete intensity. This is the purpose of this verse. On carefully reflecting upon this verse, it will be known that here Vyasaji has given the outline of devotion as described in Bhaktishastra.

'Ihaikastham' means here at this very one place. By these words, Sri Krishna shows the entire moving and non-moving (animate and inanimate) universe in His body. Sri Krishna Himself clarifies the word 'iha', saying, 'in My body'. The physical world, along with all moving and non-moving entities, was to be shown compressed and situated within the bodily form of Sri Krishna. As we have seen in the introduction to this chapter, the concept of space was not entirely eliminated from Arjuna's mind; rather, only the concept of the collective space (Samashti Akasha) equivalent to Lord Sri Krishna's limited body was retained in his mind. When Arjuna looks through this mind, he sees the entire universe in the Lord's body itself, in a miniature form, while keeping its diverse dimensions intact.

Although the word 'charachara' (moving and non-moving) is so comprehensive that its mention indicates the entire universe, yet to increase Arjuna's enthusiasm, He says, 'behold also whatever else you wish to see.' According to human nature, Arjuna's mind was anxious about his immediate problems; therefore, it was natural that his curiosity was to know future events. Initially, his effort was more towards seeing the solution to his problems and less towards realizing the oneness pervading multiplicity. In the chapter on Vibhuti Yoga, the one Supreme Self was shown in all, and here all are to be shown in the one Supreme Self.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
'Not only is My form as the Adityas, Vasus, etc., capable of being seen by you, but you also deserve to see the entire universe situated in My body,' thus says the Lord in the verse beginning with 'na' (or 'iha'). The two locative case-endings are mutually connected. The locative case within the compound is also connected there itself. The relationship is: 'If you desire, then see it here itself'.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
O Arjuna, the conqueror of sleep! Behold now the entire universe, including the moving and the unmoving, in one part of this My body. Besides this, see whatever else you desire to see.

Commentary—
'Gudakesha'—Arjuna is called 'Gudakesha' because of his mastery over sleep. The significance of addressing him here with this name is to say, 'Behold My universal form with alertness and without laziness.'

'Ihaikastham jagatkrtsnam pasyadya sacaracaram, mama dehe'—At the end of the tenth chapter, the Lord had said that He stands supporting the entire universe with a single fraction of Himself. Upon this, a desire arose in Arjuna's mind to see the universal form. Therefore, the Lord says, 'Behold the entire universe, with moving and non-moving entities, in one part (fraction) of this My body, which is sitting before you holding the reins and whip of the horses.' To see it in one part means that wherever you cast your glance, you will see infinite universes there. Behold 'adya'—right now, at this very moment, without any delay, the entire universe including moving beings like humans, gods, Yakshas, demons, ghosts, animals, birds, etc., and non-moving entities like trees, creepers, grass, plants, etc., along with inert matter like earth, mountains, sand, etc.

'Yaccanyaddrastumicchasi'—All things were present in the Lord's body, i.e., whatever has passed in the past and whatever is to happen in the future, all those things were present in the Lord's body. Therefore, the Lord says, 'See whatever else you wish to see.' What else did Arjuna want to see? Arjuna had a doubt in his mind as to whether victory in the war would be ours or the Kauravas' (Gita 2.6). Therefore, the Lord says, 'Behold that also in one part of this My body.'

Special Point
Just as in the tenth chapter, after hearing from the Lord that 'he who knows in truth this glory and power of Mine becomes established in unwavering devotion,' Arjuna praised the Lord, prayed, and asked about His glories; similarly, hearing from the Lord that 'the entire universe is situated in one fraction of Mine,' Arjuna has prayed to be shown the universal form. If the Lord had not said on His own that 'the entire universe is situated in a single fraction of Mine,' Arjuna would not have desired to see the universal form. When he would not have desired it, how would he have prayed to see it? And if he had not prayed, how would the Lord have shown His universal form? This proves that the Lord, out of His own grace, wants to show Arjuna His universal form.

A similar thing happened at the beginning of the Gita. When Arjuna asked the Lord to place the chariot between the two armies, the Lord placed the chariot in front of Grandsire Bhishma and Drona, and said to Arjuna, 'Behold these Kurus'—'kurun pasya' (1.25). The purpose of this seems to be that the Lord gracefully wanted to reveal the Gita. Because if the Lord had not said so, Arjuna would not have grieved and the teaching of the Gita would not have begun. The implication is that the Lord has revealed the Gita out of His own grace.

Connection—In three verses, the Lord commanded Arjuna four times to see His form with the word 'pasya'. Accordingly, Arjuna opens his eyes wide and looks, and also wants to see; but Arjuna does not see anything. Therefore, in the next verse, the Lord explains the reason for Arjuna not being able to see, and giving him divine eyes, commands him to behold the universal form.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Moreover, by saying 'Behold here the entire universe concentrated in one in this imperishable form', the statement 'I stand supporting this entire universe with a single fraction of Myself' (10.42) is corroborated. By stating that the entire universe is in My body which is the imperishable itself (Akshara), and by actually perceiving this universe, its falsity (unreality) is also refuted. For, the teacher's delusion can never be asserted, as that would lead to absurdity (rendering the teaching useless). Therefore, Vyasa said: 'There is no non-existence of external objects, because they are perceived; and because of difference in characteristics, it is not like a dream' (Brahma Sutras 2.2.28-29). By 'in My body', it is declared that His own body is the substratum of the manifest world. 'Whatever else you desire to see', behold all of that.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Moreover, He says 'iha'. Behold right now, here in this body of Mine, the entire universe together with all moving and non-moving entities, which is impossible to see even by wandering here and there for billions of years, situated in one place as a limb of Mine. And whatever else you desire to see—such as the causal form which is the support of the universe, the special states of the universe, and victory or defeat—behold all of that.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Not only this much, but behold here in My body, concentrated in one place as a single limb, the entire universe together with all moving and non-moving entities, right now. And whatever else you wish to see, such as victory or defeat, see that too; or rather, behold it for the resolution of your doubt: 'Whether we shall conquer them, or they shall conquer us'. By 'whatever else', it is meant the causal form which is the support of the universe, the special states of the universe, and that which is past, future, distant, hidden, gross, and subtle. By addressing him as 'Gudakesha', the Lord indicates that he should remain alert to see.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
By the use of the singular form 'iha dehe' (here in My body), a single body to be displayed is intended. With this view, it is said, 'here in My single body'. Since the oneness of the body is intended by the singular case and by the specification of the form to be shown, the word 'ekastham' (situated in one) implies its location in a single part of that body. The view held by some that the universe is just a limb of the one whole is refuted because the Lord's form is non-material (aprakrita); with this in mind, it is said: 'even there, located in a single spot'. Or, since the entire universe is said to be concentrated in one place, its location in a single portion of the body is implied. By 'whatever else you wish to see', the victory of the Pandavas or the sons of Dhritarashtra is also implied. By the force of conjunction, that too is said to be located in a single part of His body; hence he says: 'that also'.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Moreover, He says 'iha'. Behold right now in this very birth, here in My body, concentrated in one place, the entire universe containing all moving and non-moving entities, along with inert matter and individual souls, which is impossible to be seen in its entirety even in millions of births, even though it appears full of contradictions. And see right now, at this very moment, whatever else you wish to see, such as death and killing, which arises from the thought, 'How shall I kill them who are all manifestations of Mine?'

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
O Gudakesha, conqueror of sleep! Behold here in My body, concentrated in one place—situated even in a single part of My body such as the tip of a fingernail—the entire existing universe. And whatever else that is past, future, distant, hidden, gross, or subtle, behold all of that here.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
O Arjuna, whatever you wish to see, behold at once in this body of Mine! This universal form can show you whatever you now desire to see and whatever you may want to see in the future. Everything – moving and nonmoving – is here completely, in one place.

 No one can see the entire universe while sitting in one place. Even the most advanced scientist cannot see what is going on in other parts of the universe. But a devotee like Arjuna can see everything that exists in any part of the universe. Kṛṣṇa gives him the power to see anything he wants to see, past, present and future. Thus by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna is able to see everything.
