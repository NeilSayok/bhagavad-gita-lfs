Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
naiva kiñcitkaromīti yukto manyeta tattvavit .
paśyañśruṇvanspṛśañjighrannaśnangacchansvapañśvasan

Word Meanings:
नैव - never at all
किञ्चित्करोमीति - I do anything
युक्तो - steadfast yogi
मन्येत - should think
तत्त्ववित् - knower of truth
पश्यञ्शृण्वन्स्पृशञ्जिघ्रन्नश्नन्गच्छन्स्वपञ्श्वसन् - seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, moving, sleeping, breathing

Life Application:
Remember that your physical senses automatically process experiences while your true inner observer remains serene and untouched.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "I do nothing at all"—the individual soul performs all activities by Lord Vishnu"s impulsion; recognizing oneself as non-doer is true wisdom.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Realizing the Self as non-doer during all sensory functions of seeing, hearing, and touching, the sage of Truth remains ever unattached.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: Experiencing that the senses function among their respective objects while the Self is non-doer and unattached, the yogi remains free from doership.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta states: He who is devoted to Yoga, pure in self, master of his body and senses, who realizes his own Self as the Self of all beings, is not tainted though acting. The knower of Truth, centered in Yoga, while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing eyes, thinks: "I do nothing at all; the senses merely move among their objects." He who performs actions offering them to Brahman, abandoning attachment, is not stained by sin, even as a lotus leaf is untouched by water. Yogis perform actions without attachment, with body, mind, intellect, and senses alone, solely for the purification of the self.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: ""I do nothing at all," for so the knower of Truth should think, centered in the Self even while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, going, sleeping, breathing"—expounds the natural non-doership (*Akartritva*) of the enlightened soul during bodily functions.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Sensory functions are subordinate to Nature and God; the individual jiva is not an independent agent.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A practitioner absorbed in Krishna-remembrance regards all bodily activities as the Lord"s sportful dispensation.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "Knower of Truth"—a sage directly realizing the unattached Immutable Self experiences non-agency in all sensory actions.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis demonstrating how a Knower of Truth rejects egoistic doership during seeing, hearing, touching, and eating.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A wise devotee surrendered to Vasudeva perceives Self-non-agency and God"s supreme agency in all sensory moves.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the practitioner engages his senses in Lord Krishna"s service, the sense of "I-ness" completely vanishes.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "The knower of Truth should think"—unshakeable conviction of Self-non-agency in all activities is the essence of Knowledge-devotion.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
A person in the divine consciousness, although engaged in seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, moving about, sleeping and breathing, always knows within himself that he actually does nothing at all.
