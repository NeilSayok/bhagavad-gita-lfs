Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
tyaktvā karmaphalāsaṅgaṃ nityatṛpto nirāśrayaḥ .
karmaṇyabhipravṛtto.api naiva kiñcitkaroti saḥ

Word Meanings:
त्यक्त्वा - having abandoned
कर्मफलासङ्गं - attachment to fruits of action
नित्यतृप्तो - ever satisfied
निराश्रयः - without dependence
कर्मण्यभिप्रवृत्तोऽपि - though engaged in action
नैव - not indeed
किञ्चित्करोति - does anything
सः - he

Life Application:
Cultivate inner independence, feeling complete within yourself regardless of how your daily work turns out.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Having abandoned attachment to the fruit of action, ever content, depending on nothing, though engaged in action he does not do anything at all.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Having completely relinquished all attachment to the fruits of action, ever satisfied in the Bliss of the Self (Nitya-triptah), and independent of all worldly dependencies (Nirasrayah)—even though fully engaged in worldly activities, he performs no action whatsoever in truth.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhavacharya states: "Ever content, taking sole refuge"—the devotee depending solely on Lord Vishnu works with an ever-satisfied mind, so his work causes no bondage.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandagiri states: Renouncing attachment to fruits, ever content in the Self, a wise person, though seemingly active due to Prarabdha, is in truth a non-doer.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Having abandoned attachment to the fruit of action, ever content, depending on nothing, though engaged in action he does not do anything at all.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Sri Ramanuja states: "He does not do anything"—renouncing fruits, attachment, and doership, one who performs action is deemed a wise non-doer even while working.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 4.20–4.21 jointly): Renouncing attachment to action-fruits, ever content, without desire, with mind controlled, performing action by the body alone, he incurs no sin.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Sri Shankaracharya states: "Having abandoned attachment to the fruit of action, ever content, depending on nothing, though engaged in action he does not do anything at all"—shows that total absence of ego and expectation (*Nityatripta-Nirasraya*) renders work non-action.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha states: Eternal contentment and independence characterize the inner state of a Karma-yogi, proving his non-agency.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya states: A loving devotee ever satisfied at Krishna"s feet remains a non-doer and untainted even while working in worldly eyes.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati states: "He does not do anything at all"—detached, independent, and ever-content, the practitioner working out Prarabdha remains a non-doer without ignorance.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanapati states: Scriptural analysis confirming how one who abandons fruit-attachment and remains ever content acts without doership.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha states: A seeker anchored in Vasudeva abandons fruit-attachments to stay ever content, so karma binds him not.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states: When the soul immerses in Krishna"s eternal bliss, no material object can attract or entangle it.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelakantha states: "Ever content, taking no refuge"—abiding in the bliss of one"s true Self bestows genuine non-doership.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
All detached from all attachment to the results of his activities, ever satisfied and independent, he performs no fruitive action, although engaged in all kinds of endeavors.
