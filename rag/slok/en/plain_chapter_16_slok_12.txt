Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

Slok:
āśāpāśaśatairbaddhāḥ kāmakrodhaparāyaṇāḥ .
īhante kāmabhogārthamanyāyenārthasañcayān

Word Meanings:
आशापाशशतैः - by a hundred ties
बद्धाः - bound
कामक्रोधपरायणाः - given over to lust
ईहन्ते - strive
कामभोगार्थम् - for sensual enjoyment
अन्यायेन - by unlawful means
अर्थसञ्चयान् - hoards of wealth.

Life Application:
Bound by hundreds of ties of hope, given to lust and anger, they strive to amass hoards of wealth unjustly for enjoyment.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
Bound by hundreds of ties of hope, given over to lust and anger, they strive to amass wealth by unjust means for the enjoyment of desires.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
Bound by a hundred ties of hope, given over to lust and anger, they strive to obtain by unlawful means hoards to wealth for sensual enjoyments.

आशापाशशतैः by a hundred ties of hope? बद्धाः bound? कामक्रोधपरायणाः given over to lust and anger? ईहन्ते (they) strive (to attain)? कामभोगार्थम् for sensual enjoyment? अन्यायेन by unlawful means? अर्थसञ्चयान् hoards of wealth.Commentary They murder people and rob them of their wealth in order to have sensual enjoyments. They amass wealth for sensepleasure only? but not for doing righteous actions. They have no mercy. They are very cruel. They are held in bondage by a hundred ties of expectation. They harbour in their hearts a craving for all kinds of sensual objects. Various sorts of desires crop up in their mind. When their desires are not gratified they become furious. They acire wealth by unjust means. Hope or expectation binds a man to the wheel of Samsara.,Therefore hope is likened to a cord or a rope. There is no end to their cravings. Though they possess enormous wealth their cravings are not appeased. They multiply daily. These people become hopeless victims of greed.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
Caught in the toils of a hundred vain hopes, the slaves of passion and wrath, they accumulate hoards of unjust wealth, only to pander to their sensual desire.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
Bound by a hundred ties of hope, given over to lust and anger, they strive to obtain by unlawful means hoards of wealth for sensual enjoyments.

Commentary: Swami Chinmayananda explains the active behavior of the materialist. Bound by hundreds of expectations (asapasasataih baddhah), they lose their mental balance and become slaves to lust and anger (kamakrodhaparayanah). When desires are blocked, anger is natural. To satisfy their endless greed, they abandon all moral values and seek to amass wealth by unjust means (anyayena). This description perfectly critiques modern industrial societies, which focus solely on material growth at the cost of ethical values, leading to conflict and self-destruction.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
Being bound by hundreds of ropes of longing; and being devoted to their desire and anger, they seek, by unjust means,  hoards with wealth, for the purpose of the gratification of their desires.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Bound by hundreds of fetters of hopes, given over to desire and anger, they strive unjustly to gather wealth for the gratification of their desires.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
Bound by hundreds of shackles in the form of hope, giving themselves wholly to passion and anger, they endeavour to amass wealth through foul means for the enjoyment of desirable objects.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that 'hopes' act as bindings that drag a person away from liberation. Being enslaved by desire and anger, they amass wealth through illegal means (like theft or deception) solely to satisfy their senses.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
Bound by hundreds of shackles of hope, given over to lust and anger, they strive to amass hoards of wealth by unjust means for the enjoyment of sensual pleasures.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that Asuri individuals are tightly bound by countless expectations (expecting wealth, status, respect). The chains of hope drag them in all directions, making them restless. They believe that life is meaningless without desire and anger. Driven by this mindset, they employ unjust means like dishonesty, tax evasion, cheating, and stealing from temples or the helpless to accumulate wealth, believing that one cannot become rich by honest means.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
They are 'bound by hundreds of fetters of hope,' viz., bound by hundreds of fetters in the form of hope. They are given over to 'desire and anger,' viz., they are intent solely on desire and anger. To satisfy their sensual desires, they endeavour for wealth through immortal means.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Etam etc. upto arthasancayam :  Their anxiety ends only at the time of dissolution i.e. never ceases, becaue the rise and dissolution never end.  This much alone :  For them the highest goal to be achieved is but the gratification of desires, and when this (aim) is just ruined, there arises anger.  Hence the Lord says 'Devoted to their craving and anger'.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Baddhah, bound, being impelled, being lured from all sides; asa-pasa-sataih, by hundreds of shackles in the from of hope-the hopes themselves are the shackles; by hundreds of these; kama-krodha-parayanah, giving themselves wholly to passion and anger, having passion and anger as their highest resort; ihante, they endeavour; artha-sancayan, to amass wealth; anyayena, through foul means, i.e. by stealing others' wealth, etc.; kama-bhoga-artham, for the enjoyment of desirable objects-in order to enjoy desirable objects, not for righteous acts.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that Asuri people are bound by endless chains of hopes. Addicted to passion and anger, they strive to amass wealth through unjust means like theft or cheating, driven by insatiable greed that only increases with acquisition.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that bound by hundreds of shackles of hopes and drawn in all directions, Asuri individuals seek to amass wealth through foul means like theft for the sake of sensory enjoyment.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that the shackles of hope drag Asuri individuals away from their ultimate welfare. Devoted to lust and anger, they strive to gather wealth by committing sins like stealing others' property.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that hope relates to fruits of action. Bound by these hopes and devoted to desire and anger, they employ illegal means to accumulate wealth, as lawful means cannot satisfy their unchecked desires.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji explains that being bound by the chains of hope, they rely on petty deities or corrupt methods. Regarding sensual pleasure as the only goal, they accumulate wealth through theft, violence, and deceit.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that they seek to satisfy their desires by accumulating riches through deceit and fraud, having no faith in moral principles.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Bound by hundreds of desires, entangled in the net of delusion, addicted to sense enjoyment and attached to the gratification of their own lust, they descend into a hellish existence.

 The demoniac accept that the enjoyment of the senses is the ultimate goal of life, and this concept they maintain until death. They do not believe in life after death, and they do not believe that one takes on different types of bodies according to one’s karma, or activities in this world. Their plans for life are never finished, and they go on preparing plan after plan, all of which are never finished. We have personal experience of a person of such demoniac mentality who, even at the point of death, was requesting the physician to prolong his life for four years more because his plans were not yet complete. Such foolish people do not know that a physician cannot prolong life even for a moment. When the notice is there, there is no consideration of the man’s desire. The laws of nature do not allow a second beyond what one is destined to enjoy. The demoniac person, who has no faith in God or the Supersoul within himself, performs all kinds of sinful activities simply for sense gratification. He does not know that there is a witness sitting within his heart. The Supersoul is observing the activities of the individual soul. As it is stated in the Upaniṣads, there are two birds sitting in one tree; one is acting and enjoying or suffering the fruits of the branches, and the other is witnessing. But one who is demoniac has no knowledge of Vedic scripture, nor has he any faith; therefore he feels free to do anything for sense enjoyment, regardless of the consequences.
