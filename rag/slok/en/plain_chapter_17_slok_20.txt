Speaker: Sri Bhagavan

dātavyamiti yaddānaṃ dīyate.anupakāriṇe .
deśe kāle ca pātre ca taddānaṃ sāttvikaṃ smṛtam

Life Application:
Charity given to worthy person, at right place and time, expecting nothing in return, 'duty to give'—is Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Tejomayananda:
That gift which is given to one who does nothing in return, with the idea that 'it is a duty to give', in a fit place and time to a worthy person, is considered Sattvic.

Commentary by Swami Sivananda:
That gift which is given to one who does nothing in return, knowing it to be a duty to give in a fit place and time to a worthy person, that gift is held to be Sattvic.

Commentary by Shri Purohit Swami:
The gift which is given without thought of recompense, in the belief that it ought to be made, in a fit place, at an opportune time and to a deserving person - such a gift is Pure.

Commentary by Swami Chinmayananda:
A gift is considered Sattvic when it is given with the attitude that 'giving is a duty'. The recipient must be someone who cannot do anything in return. Due consideration should also be given to place, time, and the worthiness of the recipient. Giving food in a famine-stricken area is an example of proper place and time.

Commentary by Dr.S.Sankaranarayan:
A gift which is given with the thought  that  'One must give'  and is given in a proper place, and at correct  time to a worthy person, incapable of obliging in return-that gift is held to be of the Sattva.

Commentary by Swami Adidevananda:
Gifts given with the feeling, that it is one's own duty to give to one who makes no return, at the proper place and time to the deserving person - that is said to be Sattvika.

Commentary by Swami Gambirananda:
That gift is referred to as born of sattva which gift is given with the idea that it ought to be given, to one who will not serve in return, and at the (proper) place, (proper) time and to a (proper) person.

Commentary by Sri Madhavacharya:
Sri Madhvacharya did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Anandgiri:
Sri Anandgiri explains that Sattvic charity is performed with the sole intention that 'giving is my duty,' without expecting any fruits or reward in this life or the next.

Commentary by Swami Ramsukhdas:
That gift which is given to one who does nothing in return, knowing it to be a duty to give in a fit place and time to a worthy person, that gift is held to be Sattvic.

Commentary: Swami Ramsukhdas explains that 'datavyam' means giving solely out of a sense of duty, realizing that the things we possess belong to the universe. It should be given to an 'anupakari'—someone who has not helped us, does not help us, and is not expected to help us in future. 'Deshe' (place) means giving where there is a scarcity, or in holy places like Ganga. 'Kale' (time) means when there is a need, or on auspicious occasions like eclipses. 'Patra' (recipient) means someone who lacks the object, or a virtuous person who will use it properly. This action breaks the bond of ownership.

Commentary by Sri Ramanuja:
Gifts given without thought of return of favours and with the feelings, 'These gifts must be given,' at the proper places and time to a worthy person who makes no return - such gifts are said to be Sattvika.

Commentary by Sri Abhinav Gupta:
Sri Abhinavagupta (commenting on verses 17.20–17.22 jointly): Datavyam etc. upto udahrtam.  With the thought that  'One must give' :  thinking that the [scriptural] injunction  'One must give' is to be obeyed in order to avoid sin.  Very much vexed : because of the fault of [giving] very little. A gift is converted into a bad one by offending its recipient, and so on.  Thus the activities of the worldly men are explained on the basis of their three-fold intentions born of the Sattva and so on.

Commentary by Sri Shankaracharya:
Tat, that; danam, gift; is smrtam, referred to; as sattvikam, born of sattva; yat, which gift; is diyate, given; with the idea in mind datavyam iti, that it ought to be given without consideration; anupakarine, to one who will not serve in return, and even to oen who can; and dese, at the (proper) place-in holy places like Kuruksetra etc. ; kale, at the (proper) time-during Sankranti [During the passage of the sun or any planetary body from one zodiacal sign into another.-V.S.A.] etc.; and patre, to a (proper) person-to one who is versed in the Vedas together with their six branches, and such others.

Commentary by Sri Jayatritha:
Sri Jayatirtha did not comment on this sloka.

Commentary by Sri Vallabhacharya:
Sri Vallabhacharya explains the threefold division of charity promised earlier in three verses starting with this one. The meaning of this verse is straightforward.

Commentary by Sri Madhusudan Saraswati:
Sri Madhusudan Saraswati explains that a gift should be given out of the conviction that 'it must be given because of scriptural injunctions,' and not for any reward. It should be given to an 'anupakarine' (one who will not return the favor). The seventh case in 'deshe, kale, patre' is used in the sense of the dative case (to a worthy person in a holy place at a holy time). A worthy person is one who is endowed with knowledge and penance, and is capable of protecting himself and the donor.

Commentary by Sri Sridhara Swami:
Sri Sridhara Swami explains that a gift should be given with the firm resolve 'I must give,' to one who cannot return the favor. The seventh case in 'patre' is used in association with place and time, meaning a recipient who is a Brahmana endowed with penance and Vedic learning. Alternatively, 'patre' means a protector, one who protects the donor from all calamities through his spiritual strength.

Commentary by Sri Dhanpati:
Sri Dhanpati explains that a gift must be given with the mindset 'it ought to be given,' to one from whom no return is expected. It must be given in a holy place like Kurukshetra, at a holy time like Sankranti, and to a worthy recipient who is well-versed in the Vedas.

Commentary by Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha:
Vedantadeshikacharya Venkatanatha explains that the Sattvic gift is free from desire for fruits like heaven. 'Anupakarine' indicates no expectation of visible returns. A worthy recipient (patra) is defined as one who is pure in conduct, not merely by birth or learning alone.

Commentary by Sri Purushottamji:
Sri Purushottamji states that a gift should be given out of duty to those in distress (anupakari), at holy places like Kurukshetra or during wedding occasions, and to those well-versed in Vedas, or even to a hungry person who is in need.

Commentary by Sri Neelkanth:
Sri Neelkanth explains that the first 'danam' refers to the object being given, and the second 'danam' refers to the act of giving. Giving to a worthy person in a proper place and time constitutes Sattvic charity.

Commentary by A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada:
Charity given out of duty, without expectation of return, at the proper time and place, and to a worthy person is considered to be in the mode of goodness.

 In the Vedic literature, charity given to a person engaged in spiritual activities is recommended. There is no recommendation for giving charity indiscriminately. Spiritual perfection is always a consideration. Therefore charity is recommended to be given at a place of pilgrimage and at lunar or solar eclipses or at the end of the month or to a qualified brāhmaṇa or a Vaiṣṇava (devotee) or in temples. Such charities should be given without any consideration of return. Charity to the poor is sometimes given out of compassion, but if a poor man is not worth giving charity to, then there is no spiritual advancement. In other words, indiscriminate charity is not recommended in the Vedic literature.
